# Q123: What is the sixth commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q123 faithfully states the sixth commandment, directly paralleling the Shorter Catechism and WLC Q134. The only difference is the modernized translation "You shall not murder" rather than "Thou shalt not kill," which reflects current standard English Bible translations (ESV, NASB, NIV, CSB) and more precisely renders the Hebrew *ratsach*. The proof text (Exodus 20:13) is identical across all three documents. This is a simple, definitional question with no theological content to omit or restructure.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What is the sixth commandment? A. The sixth commandment is, Thou shalt not kill (Ex. 20:13)."
- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves the complete content of the Shorter's answer.
- Structural changes: None. Both present the question and commandment quotation in identical structure.
- The translation from "Thou shalt not kill" to "You shall not murder" is a modernization that actually improves precision. The Hebrew verb *ratsach* refers specifically to unlawful killing (murder), not to all taking of life (which would be *harag* or *hemit*). The KJV's "kill" was accurate in 17th-century English when "kill" carried a stronger connotation of unlawful slaying, but in modern English "murder" better conveys the original meaning.
- Proof texts: Both cite Exodus 20:13 — identical.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God) — "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables; the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man."
- The 1689 does not expound individual commandments but affirms the Decalogue as the summary of the moral law, with the latter six commandments governing duty to man. The sixth commandment falls within this framework.
- Omissions: N/A — the Confession does not address individual commandments specifically.
- Structural changes: N/A.
- The BLC's statement of the sixth commandment is fully consistent with the 1689's affirmation that the moral law was "delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 134. Which is the sixth commandment? A. The sixth commandment is, Thou shalt not kill."
- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC tracks the WLC exactly in structure, differing only in modernized English ("You shall not murder" for "Thou shalt not kill") and question wording ("What is" for "Which is").
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this is an area where no Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement exists.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The only difference is translation modernization, which is expected and appropriate for the BLC's stated purpose.
- Key phrases dropped: None. The WLC answer is a single sentence quoting the commandment, and the BLC preserves this in full.

## Detailed Notes
- This is a purely definitional question — it simply states the commandment text. There is no theological exposition to evaluate for omissions or structural changes. The real theological work happens in the subsequent questions about what the sixth commandment requires and forbids.
- The BLC's choice of "murder" over "kill" is theologically significant in a positive way: it aligns with the broader Reformed understanding (visible in the WLC's own Q135-136) that the sixth commandment does not prohibit all taking of life. The WLC explicitly allows exceptions for "public justice, lawful war, or necessary defence" (Q136). Using "murder" in the commandment statement itself makes this distinction clearer from the outset.
- The proof text citation of Exodus 20:13 is the standard and only necessary reference for a question that simply states the commandment.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Exodus 20:13** — The sixth commandment from the Decalogue. The Hebrew verb *ratsach* is used, which specifically denotes unlawful, unauthorized killing — hence modern translations render it "murder" rather than the broader "kill." This is the sole proof text cited by the BLC, the Shorter, and the WLC, which is entirely appropriate for a question that simply identifies the commandment text.
