# Q72: What are the benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q72 attempts to merge the Shorter Catechism's simple list of benefits with the WLC's richer "communion in glory" framework, but in doing so it omits **"perseverance therein to the end"** — a critical benefit explicitly named by the Shorter Catechism and grounded in an entire chapter of the 1689 Confession (Chapter 17). This is a serious omission of a core Particular Baptist distinctive. Additionally, the BLC subtly restructures the WLC's "communion in glory with Christ" into "communion with Christ," weakening the eschatological and glorification dimensions of the original.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What are the benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification?
> A. The benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification, are assurance of God's love, peace of conscience (Rom. 5:1, 2, 5), joy in the Holy Spirit (Rom. 5:5, 17), increase of grace (Pr. 4:18), and perseverance therein to the end (1 John 5:13; 1 Pet. 1:5).

The BLC preserves four of the five benefits from the Shorter: assurance of God's love, peace of conscience, joy in the Holy Spirit, and increase of grace. However:

- **Omissions:**
  - **"Perseverance therein to the end"** is entirely absent. This is the most serious omission. The Shorter Catechism names perseverance as a benefit that flows from justification, adoption, and sanctification *in this life*. The 1689 Confession devotes all of Chapter 17 to this doctrine, and it is a hallmark of Particular Baptist theology (contra Arminianism). The BLC drops this and substitutes "hope of glory" (drawn from the WLC), which is a related but distinct concept. Hope of glory is the subjective expectation; perseverance is the objective divine guarantee. They are not interchangeable.
  - The Shorter's proof texts for perseverance (1 John 5:13; 1 Peter 1:5) are absent from the BLC.

- **Structural changes:**
  - The Shorter presents a straightforward list of five benefits. The BLC restructures these into a two-tier framework borrowed from the WLC: "first-fruits of communion with Christ" and then "as a down payment on this communion" the specific benefits. This repackaging is not inherently problematic, but it introduces a complexity the Shorter avoids and creates the structural occasion for the perseverance omission — perseverance doesn't fit neatly into a "first-fruits/down-payment" eschatological framework, so it was apparently dropped.
  - The BLC adds "hope of glory" (from the WLC), which partially compensates for the loss of perseverance by pointing toward the eschatological dimension, but "hope of glory" addresses the believer's confident expectation rather than God's preserving work.

- **Additions:**
  - "First-fruits of communion with Christ mainly in the form of interest in the glory Christ fully possesses" is new material drawn from the WLC's Q83 framework, expanding the Shorter's simpler presentation. This is a legitimate expansion.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

**Chapter 17 (Of Perseverance of the Saints), Paragraph 1:**
> "Those whom God hath accepted in the beloved, effectually called and Sanctified by his Spirit, and given the precious faith of his Elect unto, can neither totally nor finally fall from the state of grace; but shall certainly persevere therein to the end and be eternally saved, seeing the gifts and callings of God are without Repentance, (whence he still begets and nourisheth in them Faith, Repentance, Love, Joy, Hope, and all the graces of the Spirit unto immortality)..."

**Chapter 18 (Of the Assurance of Grace and Salvation), Paragraph 1:**
> "...such as truely believe in the Lord Jesus, and love him in sincerity, endeavouring to walk in all good Conscience before him, may in this life be certainly assured that they are in the state of Grace; and may rejoyce in the hope of the glory of God which hope shall never make them ashamed."

**Chapter 18, Paragraph 3:**
> "...it is the duty of every one, to give all diligence to make their Calling and Election sure, that thereby his heart may be enlarged in peace and joy in the holy Spirit, in love and thankfulness to God..."

- **Omissions:**
  - The 1689's entire Chapter 17 teaches that perseverance is a benefit flowing from effectual calling and sanctification — exactly what this catechism question asks about. The BLC's silence on perseverance creates a gap between the catechism and the Confession it is meant to exposit.
  - The Confession explicitly connects perseverance to the graces listed: "he still begets and nourisheth in them Faith, Repentance, Love, Joy, Hope, and all the graces of the Spirit unto immortality" (17.1). The BLC lists some of these graces but omits the perseverance framework that sustains them.

- **Structural changes:**
  - The 1689 treats assurance (Chapter 18) and perseverance (Chapter 17) as distinct but related doctrines. The Shorter Catechism names both as benefits. The BLC names assurance but not perseverance, collapsing this important distinction.

- **Agreement:**
  - The BLC's language about "assurance of God's love" aligns with Chapter 18.1's teaching about being "certainly assured."
  - "Peace of conscience" and "joy in the Holy Spirit" correspond to Chapter 18.3's "peace and joy in the holy Spirit."
  - "Increase of grace" corresponds to Chapter 13.3's teaching that "Saints grow in Grace."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 83. What is the communion in glory with Christ which the members of the invisible church enjoy in this life?
> A. The members of the invisible church have communicated to them in this life the firstfruits of glory with Christ, as they are members of him their head, and so in him are interested in that glory which he is fully possessed of; and, as an earnest thereof, enjoy the sense of God's love, peace of conscience, joy in the Holy Ghost, and hope of glory; as, on the contrary, sense of God's revenging wrath, horror of conscience, and a fearful expectation of judgment, are to the wicked the beginning of their torments which they shall endure after death.

- **Solidarity:** MODERATE
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:**
  - The WLC speaks of "members of the invisible church"; the BLC speaks of "the elect." This is a legitimate Baptist adjustment reflecting different ecclesiological emphasis.
  - The WLC frames this as "communion in glory with Christ"; the BLC adjusts to "communion with Christ." While this could be an intentional simplification, "in glory" is theologically significant — it specifies the eschatological nature of the communion being discussed and ties back to Q82's framework of communion in glory "in this life, immediately after death, and at last perfected at the resurrection."

- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC's contrasting clause about the wicked ("as, on the contrary, sense of God's revenging wrath, horror of conscience, and a fearful expectation of judgment, are to the wicked the beginning of their torments which they shall endure after death") is entirely omitted. While this may be a deliberate structural choice (addressing the wicked elsewhere), the WLC includes it for pedagogical contrast — showing that the benefits of the elect are the precise opposite of the wicked's experience. This loss reduces the pastoral force of the answer.
  - The WLC says "sense of God's love"; the BLC says "assurance of God's love." The WLC's "sense" is experiential and subjective — the felt experience of God's love. "Assurance" is a more cognitive and doctrinal term. The BLC's substitution draws from the Shorter Catechism's phrasing, which is legitimate, but it subtly shifts the register from experiential to doctrinal.

- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - **"As they are members of him their head"** — The WLC's explicit language about union with Christ as Head/members is dropped. The BLC has "communion with Christ" but lacks this organic, corporate image.
  - **The entire contrasting clause about the wicked** (noted above).
  - **"Communion in glory"** — reduced to simply "communion." The word "glory" in the WLC's framework is not merely decorative; it ties this question to the broader WLC structure of communion in glory (Q82-90).

- **BLC additions relative to the WLC:**
  - "Increase of grace" — present in the Shorter but absent from the WLC Q83. The BLC appropriately includes this from the Shorter, which is its primary standard.
  - "Down payment" modernizes the WLC's "earnest" — an acceptable and helpful update.

## Detailed Notes
- The most significant issue is the omission of perseverance. The Shorter Catechism's five benefits form a carefully constructed list: assurance, peace, joy, growth, and *preservation*. The BLC replaces the fifth with "hope of glory" from the WLC, creating a hybrid that loses the Shorter's completeness. A faithful larger catechism should include *both* perseverance (from the Shorter) and hope of glory (from the WLC), since expanding and encompassing both standards is exactly its purpose.
- The BLC's phrase "mainly in the form of interest in the glory Christ fully possesses" closely mirrors the WLC's "interested in that glory which he is fully possessed of," showing clear WLC dependence.
- The BLC's use of "down payment" for the WLC's "earnest" is a good modernization — both translate the Greek *arrabon* (2 Corinthians 1:22), and the BLC even cites this verse.
- The shift from the WLC's "communion in glory" to "communion" is not trivially explained by Baptist-Presbyterian differences. Both traditions affirm present communion in glory with Christ. This appears to be an inadvertent weakening.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Ephesians 2:5-6** (cited for "first-fruits of communion with Christ... interest in the glory Christ fully possesses"): Strong text — speaks of being raised and seated with Christ in heavenly places, supporting the "already" dimension of eschatological glory.
- **Romans 5:5; 2 Corinthians 1:22** (cited for "assurance of God's love"): Romans 5:5 ("God's love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit") supports the WLC's "sense of God's love" more naturally than "assurance." 2 Corinthians 1:22 ("who has also put his seal on us and given us his Spirit in our hearts as a guarantee/earnest/down payment") is well-placed for the "down payment" language.
- **Romans 5:1-2; Romans 14:17; 2 Peter 3:18** (cited for "peace of conscience, joy in the Holy Spirit, increase of grace, and hope of glory"): Romans 5:1-2 supports peace and hope of glory. Romans 14:17 ("the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking but of righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Spirit") supports joy in the Holy Spirit. 2 Peter 3:18 ("grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord") supports increase of grace.
- **Missing proof texts:** The Shorter's perseverance proof texts (1 John 5:13; 1 Peter 1:5) are absent, consistent with the omission of perseverance itself. Proverbs 4:18 (the Shorter's proof for "increase of grace") is also not cited.
- The BLC's proof texts are generally well-chosen for what they do cover, but the absence of perseverance texts is a telling gap.
