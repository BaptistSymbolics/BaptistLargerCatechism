# Q56: How is Christ to be exalted in His imminent return to judge the world?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
The BLC answer is virtually identical to WLC Q. 56, faithfully preserving all key doctrinal content of the answer itself. However, the **question** introduces the word "imminent" ("His imminent return to judge the world"), replacing the standards' neutral phrasing "coming again to judge the world." This imports a specific eschatological position (imminency) not found in the 1689 Confession, the Shorter Catechism, or the WLC. The 1689 explicitly teaches that the day is "unknown to Men" (Ch. 32.3), which is a broader and more cautious formulation than "imminent." This is a novelty that goes beyond the confessional standards.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. "Wherein consisteth Christ's exaltation?"
> A. "Christ's exaltation consisteth in his rising again from the dead on the third day, in ascending up into heaven, in sitting at the right hand of God the Father, and in coming to judge the world at the last day." (1 Cor. 15:4; Mark 16:19; Eph. 1:20; Acts 1:11; 17:31)

- Omissions: None from the answer content. The Shorter's phrase "coming to judge the world at the last day" is faithfully expanded.
- Structural changes: The BLC appropriately expands this single clause into a full question, as befits a Larger Catechism.
- **Key concern**: The Shorter says "coming to judge the world at the last day" — no imminency language. The BLC question changes this to "His imminent return to judge the world," introducing a qualifier absent from the Shorter.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 8, Paragraph 4:
> "...and there sitteth at the right hand of his Father, making intercession; and shall return to judge Men and Angels, at the end of the World."

Chapter 32 (Of the Last Judgement), Paragraph 1:
> "God hath appointed a Day wherein he will judge the world in Righteousness, by Jesus Christ; to whom all power and judgement is given of the Father; in which Day not only the Apostate Angels shall be judged; but likewise all persons that have lived upon the Earth, shall appear before the Tribunal of Christ; to give an account of their Thoughts, Words, and Deeds, and to receive according to what they have done in the body, whether good or evil."

Chapter 32, Paragraph 3:
> "As Christ would have us to be certainly perswaded that there shall be a Day of judgement, both to deter all men from sin, and for the greater consolation of the godly, in their adversity; so will he have that day unknown to Men, that they may shake off all carnal security, and be always watchful, because they know not at what hour, the Lord will come; and may ever be prepared to say, Come Lord Jesus, Come quickly, Amen."

- Omissions: The BLC answer does not address several 1689 elements, though these belong to other questions:
  - Judgment of "Men and Angels" (Ch. 8.4) — only "the world" is mentioned
  - "All power and judgement is given of the Father" (Ch. 32.1) — the Father's delegation of judgment is not stated
  - Accountability for "Thoughts, Words, and Deeds" (Ch. 32.1) — not mentioned
  - The dual purpose of the doctrine: deterring sin and consoling the godly (Ch. 32.3)
  - The day being "unknown to Men" (Ch. 32.3)
- Structural changes: None in the answer.
- **Key concern**: The 1689 carefully teaches that the day is "unknown to Men" and that believers should "be always watchful, because they know not at what hour, the Lord will come." This is the Confession's way of motivating readiness without committing to "imminency" as a doctrinal position. The BLC's use of "imminent" in the question goes beyond — and arguably narrows — the Confession's teaching. "Unknown" and "imminent" are distinct concepts: one emphasizes epistemic humility about timing, while the other asserts that the return could happen at any moment (which carries specific theological commitments about the relationship of Christ's return to preceding prophetic events).

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q. 56: "How is Christ to be exalted in his coming again to judge the world?"
> A. "Christ is to be exalted in his coming again to judge the world, in that he, who was unjustly judged and condemned by wicked men, shall come again at the last day in great power, and in the full manifestation of his own glory, and of his Father's, with all his holy angels, with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trumpet of God, to judge the world in righteousness."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC answer is nearly verbatim with the WLC answer, with only minor modernizing changes.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is not a topic where Baptist-Presbyterian differences apply).
- Unexpected divergences: The **question itself** diverges by adding "imminent" — "His imminent return" vs. the WLC's "his coming again." This is not a Baptist-Presbyterian issue but a novel addition.
- Key phrases dropped: None from the answer. The WLC's content is fully preserved.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "imminent" problem**: The word "imminent" in the question is the sole significant concern. It introduces a specific eschatological commitment (imminency doctrine) that is debated among Reformed Baptists. Some hold to strict imminency (Christ could return at any moment with no preceding signs), while others hold that certain events must precede the return. The confessional standards deliberately avoid this debate by using neutral language ("coming again," "return," "at the end of the World," "unknown to Men"). A catechism meant to exposit the 1689 should follow the Confession's careful neutrality rather than importing a contested position.

2. **Answer fidelity**: Setting aside the question's wording, the BLC answer itself is excellent. It preserves the WLC's structure and content with only two minor modernizations:
   - "the full manifestation of His own and His Father's glory" vs. WLC's "his own glory, and of his Father's" — same meaning, slightly rephrased
   - "accompanied by a shout of command" vs. WLC's "with a shout" — a reasonable translation update reflecting the Greek *keleusma* (a word of command)

3. **Recommended fix**: Change the question from "How is Christ to be exalted in His imminent return to judge the world?" to "How is Christ to be exalted in His coming again to judge the world?" This would bring the question into alignment with all three standards and eliminate the novelty.

## Scripture Analysis
The proof texts are well-chosen and closely follow the WLC's citations:
- **Acts 3:14-15** — supports "unjustly judged and condemned by wicked men" (WLC uses same reference)
- **Matthew 24:30; 2 Thessalonians 1:9-10** — supports "come again at the last day in great power" (reasonable update from WLC's Matt. 24:30 alone; 2 Thess. 1:9-10 is also used by the 1689 Confession at Ch. 32.2)
- **Luke 9:26; Matthew 25:31** — supports the manifestation of glory with holy angels (WLC cites the same)
- **1 Thessalonians 4:16** — supports the shout, archangel's voice, and trumpet (identical to WLC)
- **Acts 17:30-31; 2 Thessalonians 1:6-8** — supports "judge the world in righteousness" (Acts 17:31 is shared with WLC; 2 Thess. 1:6-8 is an appropriate addition)

Note: None of the cited proof texts specifically teach "imminency" as a doctrine, further highlighting that the word "imminent" in the question is unsupported by the BLC's own scripture references.
