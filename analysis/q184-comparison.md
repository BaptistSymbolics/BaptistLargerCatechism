# Q184: What does the preface of the Lord's Prayer teach us?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q184 is substantially faithful to the Shorter Catechism and WLC Q189, preserving all major theological elements. It shows strong structural solidarity with the WLC. Two minor concerns emerge: (1) the BLC replaces the WLC's "our interest therein" with "the benefits to us from that goodness," which subtly shifts emphasis from the believer's personal stake in God's fatherly goodness to a more general reception of benefits; and (2) the Shorter Catechism's compressed phrase "as children to a father, able and ready to help us" includes God's ability and readiness to help, which the BLC omits in favor of the WLC's more elaborate structure.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What doth the preface of the Lord's prayer teach us?
> A. The preface of the Lord's prayer, which is Our Father which art in heaven (Mt. 6:9), teacheth us to draw near to God with all holy reverence and confidence, as children to a father, able and ready to help us (Rom. 8:15; Lk. 11:13; Is. 24:8); and that we should pray with and for others (Acts 12:5; 1 Tim. 2:1, 2).

- **Omissions:** The Shorter's phrase "able and ready to help us" is absent from the BLC. The Shorter explicitly teaches that approaching God as Father includes confidence in His ability and readiness to help. The BLC instead speaks of "the benefits to us from that goodness," which is more passive and less personal. The Shorter's "all holy reverence" is rendered as simply "reverently" in the BLC—a minor simplification.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter compresses two ideas into one motion: we draw near "with all holy reverence and confidence, as children to a father, able and ready to help us." The BLC separates these into multiple sections: confidence in fatherly goodness (section 2), reverence and childlikeness (section 3), heavenly feelings (section 4), and sovereign awareness (section 5). This expansion follows the WLC's structure rather than the Shorter's compressed form, which is appropriate for a "Larger" catechism.
- The Shorter's proof text 1 Tim. 2:1, 2 (pray for kings and those in authority) is absent from the BLC's final section on praying "with and for others."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraph 3:
> "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance; and when with others, in a known tongue."

Chapter 12, Paragraph 1 (Of Adoption):
> "All those that are justified, God vouchsafed, in, and for the sake of his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the Grace of Adoption; by which they are taken into the number, and enjoy the Liberties, and Priveledges of Children of God; have his name put upon them, receive the Spirit of Adoption, have access to the throne of Grace with boldness, are enabled to cry Abba, Father, are pitied, protected, provided for, and chastned by him, as by a Father; yet never cast off; but sealed to the day of Redemption, and inherit the promises, as heirs, of everlasting Salvation."

- **Omissions:** None of significance. The 1689 does not directly address the preface of the Lord's Prayer, but its teaching on prayer (Ch. 22.3) and adoption (Ch. 12.1) align well with the BLC's content. The BLC's emphasis on fatherly goodness, childlike reverence, and sovereign awareness are all consistent with the Confession's teaching on prayer and adoption.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC appropriately connects the Lord's Prayer preface to the Confession's doctrines of adoption (addressing God as Father) and prayer (reverence, confidence).
- The 1689's teaching on adoption (Ch. 12.1) provides the theological foundation for the BLC's "confident of His fatherly goodness" and "reverently and in every way like a child." The connection is sound.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q189:
> Q. 189. What doth the preface of the Lord's Prayer teach us?
> A. The preface of the Lord's Prayer (contained in these words, Our Father which art in heaven,) teacheth us, when we pray, to draw near to God with confidence of his fatherly goodness, and our interest therein; with reverence, and all other childlike dispositions, heavenly affections, and due apprehensions of his sovereign power, majesty, and gracious condescension: as also, to pray with and for others.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC follows the WLC's structure and content very closely, preserving the same sequence of theological elements: fatherly goodness → reverence/childlikeness → heavenly affections → sovereign power/majesty/condescension → praying with and for others.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected or found. This question does not touch on baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC's "our interest therein" is replaced with "the benefits to us from that goodness." The WLC's phrase implies a personal, covenantal stake in God's fatherly goodness—believers have an "interest" (a share, a right) in that goodness by virtue of adoption. The BLC's "benefits to us from that goodness" is more focused on what flows from God's goodness rather than the believer's personal claim to it. This is a subtle but real shift from covenantal entitlement to general benefit.
  - The WLC's "all other childlike dispositions" becomes "in every way like a child." The WLC's phrasing treats reverence as one of several childlike dispositions; the BLC's phrasing separates reverence from childlikeness ("reverently and in every way like a child"), which is a minor structural change but does not alter the theology.
  - The WLC's "gracious condescension" becomes "His graciousness in allowing us to approach Him." The BLC's rendering unpacks the meaning helpfully but loses some of the richness of "condescension" as a theological term (God stooping down to meet His creatures).
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "our interest therein" — replaced rather than dropped, but the replacement ("benefits to us from that goodness") lacks the covenantal precision of the original.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Interest therein" vs. "benefits to us from that goodness"**: This is the most theologically significant change. In Reformed theology, "interest" carries covenantal weight—it means the believer has a personal stake, a right grounded in adoption, to claim God's fatherly care. The BLC's "benefits to us from that goodness" is not wrong, but it shifts from the believer's standing (interest/right) to the outcomes (benefits). This subtly weakens the connection to adoption theology.

2. **"Able and ready to help us"**: The Shorter Catechism's inclusion of this phrase teaches that approaching God as Father means confidence not only in His goodness but specifically in His power (able) and willingness (ready) to help. The BLC does address sovereign power later in section 5, but the Shorter's phrase connects ability/readiness directly to the fatherly metaphor in a way the BLC does not.

3. **Section structure**: The BLC's division into six TOML sections effectively mirrors the WLC's theological progression. This is appropriate for a Larger Catechism.

4. **"Gracious condescension"**: The BLC's rendering "His graciousness in allowing us to approach Him" is a reasonable modernization but loses the full theological freight of "condescension"—which in Reformed usage means not merely permission to approach but God's active stooping to accommodate Himself to creatures.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts align closely with the WLC's:
- **Matthew 6:9** — Identical (the preface text itself)
- **Luke 11:13** — Matches WLC note 1202 (fatherly goodness)
- **Romans 8:15** — Matches WLC note 1202 (Spirit of adoption, confidence)
- **Isaiah 64:9** — Matches WLC note 1203 (childlike dispositions); BLC uses same verse
- **Psalm 123:1** — Matches WLC note 1204 (heavenly affections)
- **Lamentations 3:41** — Matches WLC note 1204 (heavenly affections)
- **Isaiah 63:15-16** — Matches WLC note 1205 (sovereign power/condescension)
- **Nehemiah 1:4-6** — Matches WLC note 1205 (sovereign power/condescension)
- **Acts 12:5** — Matches WLC note 1206 (praying with/for others)

Additional BLC proof texts not in WLC:
- **Psalm 95:6-7** — Added for reverence/childlikeness; appropriate and fitting
- **Psalm 104:1; Psalm 113:4-6** — Added for sovereign power/majesty; appropriate supplements
- **Zechariah 8:21** — Added for corporate prayer; appropriate

The Shorter's proof text **1 Timothy 2:1, 2** (prayer for kings/authority) is absent from the BLC. The WLC's note 1206 also lacks it, citing only Acts 12:5, which the BLC follows. This is not a concern since the BLC follows the WLC here.
