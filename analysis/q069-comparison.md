# Q69: What is repentance unto life?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q69 closely follows the WLC Q76 structure and covers most key elements of repentance, but it introduces two significant theological shifts: (1) it replaces "saving grace" with "act of God's free grace," which changes repentance from an ongoing disposition to a one-time divine action; and (2) it omits the critical phrase "by Faith in Christ" from the 1689 Confession's definition, which specifies the instrumental means through which the sinner exercises repentance. The BLC also drops "endeavouring" from the Shorter Catechism's "full purpose of and endeavour after new obedience," weakening the active human response.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is repentance unto life?
> A. Repentance unto life is a saving grace (Acts 11:28), whereby a sinner, out of a true sense of his sin (Acts 2:37, 38), and apprehension of the mercy of God in Christ (Joel 2:12; Jer 3:22), doth, with grief and hatred of his sin, turn from it unto God (Jer 31:18, 19; Ez. 36:31), with full purpose of and endeavour after new obedience (2 Cor. 7:11; Is. 1:16, 17).

- **Omissions:**
  - **"Saving grace" replaced with "act of God's free grace"**: The Shorter says repentance is "a saving grace," identifying it as a grace that saves. The BLC says "an act of God's free grace," shifting the emphasis from repentance as an ongoing saving grace (a disposition wrought in the believer) to a discrete divine act. This is a substantive change, not mere modernization.
  - **"Endeavour after" dropped**: The Shorter says "with full purpose of and endeavour after new obedience." The BLC says "purposing to walk continually with Him according to this new obedience in every way." While "purposing" captures the "full purpose" element, the explicit "endeavour after" — the active striving component — is lost. The Shorter teaches that repentance involves both resolving AND actively working toward obedience; the BLC retains only the resolving.
  - **"Out of a true sense of his sin" replaced**: The Shorter's compact "true sense of his sin" is expanded (following the WLC) into recognizing both the "danger" and "filthiness and repulsiveness." This is appropriate expansion, not an omission.
- **Structural changes:**
  - The BLC adds explicit mention of the Spirit and the Word of God as the means through which repentance is wrought (sections 2-3), which the Shorter leaves implicit. This follows the WLC pattern and is a faithful expansion.
  - The BLC's phrase "Understanding that in Christ, God is merciful to those who repent" adequately captures the Shorter's "apprehension of the mercy of God in Christ."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XV, Paragraph 3:
> "This saving Repentance is an evangelical Grace, whereby a person being by the Holy Spirit made sensible of the manifold evils of his sin, doth, by Faith in Christ, humble himself for it, with godly sorrow, detestation of it, and self abhorrency; praying for pardon, and strength of grace, with a purpose and endeavour by supplies of the Spirit, to walk before God unto all well pleasing in all things."

Also Chapter XV, Paragraph 1:
> "Such of the Elect as are converted at riper years, having sometimes lived in the state of nature, and therein served divers lusts and pleasures, God in their Effectual Calling giveth them Repentance unto Life."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"By Faith in Christ" omitted**: This is the most significant omission. The 1689 explicitly states that the sinner "doth, by Faith in Christ, humble himself." This phrase identifies faith as the instrumental means through which the penitent exercises repentance — repentance works through faith. The BLC mentions understanding God's mercy in Christ but never specifies that repentance operates *by faith in Christ*. This is a key Reformation distinctive: faith and repentance are inseparable, with faith being the instrument through which repentance is exercised.
  - **"Humble himself" omitted**: The 1689 includes self-humbling as a component of repentance. The BLC has no equivalent.
  - **"Self abhorrency" omitted**: The 1689 includes "self abhorrency" alongside godly sorrow and detestation. The BLC has "filthiness and repulsiveness of their sins to God" but lacks the reflexive self-abhorrence that the Confession includes.
  - **"Praying for pardon, and strength of grace" omitted**: The 1689 explicitly includes prayer for pardon and for grace as part of repentance. The BLC has no equivalent.
  - **"By supplies of the Spirit" omitted**: The 1689 specifies that the endeavour after obedience is "by supplies of the Spirit," acknowledging the believer's dependence on the Spirit for ongoing obedience. The BLC omits this.
  - **"Evangelical Grace" vs. "act of God's free grace"**: The 1689 calls repentance an "evangelical Grace" — a grace of the gospel. The BLC's "act of God's free grace" loses the specifically evangelical (gospel-connected) nature of repentance.
- **Structural changes:**
  - The 1689's structure places "by Faith in Christ" as the means through which the sinner humbles himself, creating a faith-repentance nexus. The BLC restructures this so that understanding God's mercy comes as a separate section rather than being the means through which repentance operates.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q76:
> "Repentance unto life is a saving grace, wrought in the heart of a sinner by the Spirit and Word of God, whereby, out of the sight and sense, not only of the danger, but also of the filthiness and odiousness of his sins, and upon the apprehension of God's mercy in Christ to such as are penitent, he so grieves for and hates his sins, as that he turns from them all to God, purposing and endeavouring constantly to walk with him in all the ways of new obedience."

- **Solidarity: STRONG** — The BLC clearly follows the WLC Q76 structure closely, section by section.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected or present on this topic. Repentance is not an area of Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - **"Saving grace" changed to "act of God's free grace"**: The WLC says "saving grace." The BLC changes this to "act of God's free grace." This is unexpected because there is no Baptist theological reason to alter this phrase. "Saving grace" identifies repentance as a grace that participates in salvation; "act of God's free grace" shifts the meaning to emphasize the divine action rather than the salvific character of the grace.
  - **"Endeavouring" dropped**: The WLC says "purposing and endeavouring constantly." The BLC says "purposing to walk continually." The loss of "endeavouring" removes the active striving component.
  - **"To such as are penitent" dropped**: The WLC specifies God's mercy is apprehended as being "to such as are penitent." The BLC says "to those who repent," which is roughly equivalent but slightly less precise in its participial construction.
  - **"In all the ways of new obedience" changed to "according to this new obedience in every way"**: The WLC's "in all the ways of new obedience" speaks of walking in the various paths of obedience. The BLC's "according to this new obedience in every way" is somewhat awkward and shifts from walking *in* the ways to walking *according to* obedience, a subtle but real structural change.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "Saving grace" (changed without theological reason)
  - "Endeavouring constantly" (active striving removed)
  - "Sight and sense" simplified to just awareness/recognition
  - "Odiousness" replaced with "repulsiveness" (acceptable modernization)

## Detailed Notes

### "Saving grace" vs. "Act of God's free grace"
This is the most theologically consequential change. Both the Shorter Catechism and the WLC identify repentance as "a saving grace." The 1689 calls it "an evangelical Grace." All three standards identify repentance as a *type of grace* — specifically, one that saves or pertains to the gospel. The BLC's reformulation to "an act of God's free grace" subtly changes the genus: repentance is no longer identified as a grace (i.e., a disposition or capacity given to the believer) but as an act of God. While it is true that repentance is ultimately from God (as all three standards affirm), the standards carefully balance divine sovereignty with human agency — God grants the grace, and the sinner, by that grace, repents. The BLC's formulation risks collapsing this balance by making repentance purely a divine act rather than a divinely-granted human response.

### Omission of "by Faith in Christ"
The 1689 Confession's inclusion of "by Faith in Christ" as the instrumental means of repentance is theologically significant. It teaches that repentance is not an independent work but operates through faith. This is a Reformation distinctive that guards against making repentance a meritorious human work. Its absence from the BLC is notable, particularly since the BLC is supposed to exposit the 1689.

### Missing elements of prayer and self-humbling
The 1689's inclusion of "praying for pardon, and strength of grace" and "humble himself" with "self abhorrency" are not optional decorations but substantive elements of what repentance looks like in practice. Their omission narrows the definition.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts substantially overlap with those of the WLC, demonstrating awareness of the WLC's scriptural basis.
- 2 Timothy 2:25 and Acts 11:18 are appropriately cited for repentance as God's gift, though they better support "saving grace" than "act of God's free grace."
- Zechariah 12:10 properly supports the Spirit's role.
- The addition of Philippians 3:7-8 for the filthiness of sin is a reasonable addition, though it more directly addresses counting things as loss for Christ rather than recognizing sin's filthiness.
- The BLC's citation of Luke 15:7-10 for God's mercy toward penitents is a reasonable addition.
- 2 Chronicles 7:14 is cited for the purpose of new obedience, which is acceptable but less directly supportive than the WLC's references.
- The absence of a proof text for "by Faith in Christ" corresponds to the omission of that concept itself.
