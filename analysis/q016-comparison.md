# Q16: How does God execute His decrees?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q16 faithfully mirrors the Baptist Shorter Catechism's answer to the parallel question on the execution of God's decrees, expanding it with the qualification from both the 1689 Confession and the WLC regarding God's infallible foreknowledge and the free and immutable counsel of His will. The answer maintains strong solidarity with WLC Q14, reproducing its language almost verbatim in modernized form.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. How doth God execute his decrees?
> A. God executeth his decrees in the works of creation and providence.

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's complete answer is preserved in the BLC's first section.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC retains the identical structure: God executes His decrees through creation and providence.
- The BLC expands the Shorter by adding the qualifying phrase about infallible foreknowledge and the counsel of His will. This expansion is appropriate for a "Larger" catechism and is drawn directly from the 1689 Confession and WLC.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 3, Paragraph 1 (Of God's Decree):
> "God hath Decreed in himself from all Eternity, by the most wise and holy Councel of his own will, freely and unchangeably, all things whatsoever comes to passe..."

Chapter 4, Paragraph 1 (Of Creation):
> "In the beginning it pleased God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, for the manifestation of the glory of his eternal power, wisdom, and goodness, to Create or make the world, and all things therein..."

Chapter 5, Paragraph 1 (Of Divine Providence):
> "God the good Creator of all things, in his infinite power, and wisdom, doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all Creatures, and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence, to the end for the which they were Created; according unto his infallible foreknowledge, and the free and immutable Councel of his own will; to the praise of the glory of his wisdom, power, justice, infinite goodness and mercy."

- Omissions: The BLC does not include the doxological conclusion from 1689 5.1 ("to the praise of the glory of his wisdom, power, justice, infinite goodness and mercy"), but this is appropriate for a catechism answer as opposed to a confessional statement. The fuller description of providence (uphold, direct, dispose, govern) is also not included, but this is expected since that content belongs to subsequent questions about providence specifically.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC correctly links the execution of decrees to creation and providence, then qualifies this with the language of foreknowledge and immutable counsel drawn from 1689 5.1.
- The phrase "according to His infallible foreknowledge and the free and immutable counsel of His own will" is drawn almost verbatim from 1689 5.1, modernized only in capitalization and spelling.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q14:
> Q. How doth God execute his decrees?
> A. God executeth his decrees in the works of creation and providence, according to his infallible foreknowledge, and the free and immutable counsel of his own will.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question deals with the doctrine of God's decrees and providence, where Baptists and Presbyterians are in full agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The BLC reproduces WLC Q14 essentially verbatim in modernized form.
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every element of the WLC answer is preserved.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC splits the WLC's single-sentence answer into two sections with separate proof texts, which is a formatting choice rather than a theological change. The first section covers the core statement (creation and providence), while the second section covers the qualification (foreknowledge and counsel).
- This two-section approach allows for more targeted Scripture citations: Revelation 4:11 and Isaiah 40:12-31 for creation and providence, and Ephesians 1:11, Psalm 148:8, Daniel 4:35, and Acts 4:24-28 for the counsel of God's will.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Revelation 4:11** — Supports God's creative work and sovereign purpose ("for thy pleasure they are and were created").
- **Isaiah 40:12-31** — A comprehensive passage on God's sovereign power in creation and providence.
- **Ephesians 1:11** — Directly supports the language of "the counsel of his own will," the key proof text used by both the 1689 (3.1 and 5.1) and the WLC.
- **Psalm 148:8** — Fire, hail, snow, and vapor fulfilling God's word; supports the execution of decrees in providence.
- **Daniel 4:35** — God does according to His will among the inhabitants of the earth; supports the free and immutable counsel of His will.
- **Acts 4:24-28** — God's foreknowledge and determinate counsel in the crucifixion of Christ; directly supports both "infallible foreknowledge" and "counsel of His own will."
- The proof texts are well chosen and appropriately support each section's theological claim.
