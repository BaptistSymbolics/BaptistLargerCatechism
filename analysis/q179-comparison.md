# Q179: How does the Spirit help us to pray?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q179 is a close modernization of WLC Q182 that faithfully preserves the structure and key theological content. However, the BLC renders the WLC's precise term "graces" as "gifts," which subtly shifts from the language of internal spiritual dispositions (graces) to something that could be read as external endowments. This is a minor but noteworthy imprecision, as "graces" in the original carries specific theological weight tied to the Spirit's sanctifying work in the heart.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question on how the Spirit helps us pray. The Spirit's assistance is mentioned within the definition of prayer itself:

> Q. What is prayer?
> A. Prayer is an offering up our desires to God (Ps. 62:8), by the assistance of the Holy Spirit (Rom. 8:26), for things agreeable to his will (1 Jn. 5:14; Rom. 8:27), in the name of Christ (Jn. 16:23), believing (Mt. 21:22; James 1:6), with confession of our sins (Ps. 32:5, 6; Dan. 9:4), and thankful acknowledgments of his mercies (Ph. 4:6).

- Omissions: N/A — the Shorter folds the Spirit's role into the definition of prayer rather than treating it separately. The BLC's separate question is an appropriate expansion following the WLC pattern.
- Structural changes: None. The Shorter's phrase "by the assistance of the Holy Spirit" is consistent with the BLC's more detailed exposition of that assistance.
- The BLC's treatment is a natural expansion of the Shorter's brief reference to the Spirit's assistance.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraph 3:

> "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance; and when with others, in a known tongue."

- Omissions: The 1689 lists specific dispositions required in prayer: "understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance." The BLC does not enumerate these, instead using the general phrase "the gifts that are needed to pray properly." This is not a direct omission since Q179 is about the Spirit's help rather than the requirements for prayer (which may be treated in a prior or subsequent question), but the Confession's specificity provides important content that is only gestured at here.
- Structural changes: None. The 1689's statement that prayer must be "by the help of the Spirit" is exactly what Q179 expounds.
- The BLC is consistent with the Confession's teaching that the Spirit is essential for acceptable prayer. The Confession's emphasis that prayer must be "according to his Will" aligns with the BLC's language about understanding "for what" to pray.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q182:

> Q. 182. How doth the Spirit help us to pray?
> A. We not knowing what to pray for as we ought, the Spirit helpeth our infirmities, by enabling us to understand both for whom, and what, and how prayer is to be made; and by working and quickening in our hearts (although not in all persons, nor at all times, in the same measure) those apprehensions, affections, and graces which are requisite for the right performance of that duty.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC is a direct modernization of WLC Q182, preserving its question, structure, parenthetical qualification, and proof texts.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None present; this is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None structurally.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"apprehensions, affections, and graces"** → BLC has "grasp, feel, and experience the gifts." The WLC's triad is precise: "apprehensions" (cognitive understanding), "affections" (heart dispositions), and "graces" (Spirit-wrought virtues). The BLC's "grasp" reasonably maps to "apprehensions," and "feel" to "affections," but "experience the gifts" does not cleanly correspond to "graces." The WLC's "graces" refers to internally wrought spiritual qualities (faith, love, fervency, etc.), while "gifts" in modern English more naturally suggests things received or bestowed rather than character dispositions. This is a subtle but real shift in connotation.
  - **"requisite for the right performance of that duty"** → BLC has "needed to pray properly." This is a reasonable modernization, though "duty" is dropped — a minor loss, as the WLC's framing of prayer as a "duty" reinforces the obligation element.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Graces" vs. "gifts"**: This is the most notable change. In Reformed theology, "graces" in the context of prayer refers to the Spirit-wrought dispositions enumerated in the 1689 (understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, perseverance) and similar lists. These are internal qualities the Spirit works in the heart. "Gifts" in modern usage can carry this meaning but more commonly suggests something externally granted rather than internally cultivated. A rendering like "graces" (retained) or "qualities" or "dispositions" would more precisely capture the original.

2. **"Experience the gifts"**: The WLC says the Spirit works graces that are "requisite" — they are necessary qualities. The BLC's "experience the gifts" introduces an experiential dimension not present in the original. The WLC does not say we experience graces but that the Spirit works them in us. This subtle shift could be read as moving toward a more subjective/experiential understanding of prayer.

3. **"Infirmities" vs. "weakness"**: The BLC's "weakness" (singular) is a reasonable modernization of "infirmities" (plural), though the plural captures the multifaceted nature of our incapacity in prayer.

4. **Parenthetical qualification**: The BLC faithfully preserves the important pastoral qualification "(although not in everyone, nor all the time, nor to the same degree)" — this is well done and matches the WLC's "(although not in all persons, nor at all times, in the same measure)."

5. **"Working and quickening"** → "works in and makes our hearts alive": Good modernization of "quickening."

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC uses Romans 8:26-27; Psalm 10:17; Psalm 80:18; Zechariah 12:10.

The WLC proof texts (footnote 1167) include: Romans 8:26-27; Psalm 10:17; Zechariah 12:10.

The BLC adds **Psalm 80:18** ("Then we will not turn back from you; revive us, and we will call on your name"), which is not in the WLC. This is a reasonable addition supporting the "quickening/making alive" concept and is theologically appropriate.

The proof text alignment is very strong, with only the addition of Psalm 80:18.
