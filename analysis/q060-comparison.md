# Q60: Are the elect only effectually called?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q60 closely follows WLC Q68 in both structure and content, faithfully teaching that all and only the elect are effectually called. The BLC's expansion of the Spirit's work ("some of the same ways He works in the elect") is a helpful modernizing gloss on the WLC's "common operations of the Spirit." However, the BLC omits the 1689 Confession's important teaching that the non-elect "neither will, nor can truly come to Christ" and "cannot be saved," replacing the language of inability with language focused solely on the non-elect's culpability ("from their own willful neglect"). The 1689's double emphasis on both culpability and inability is an important Reformed distinctive that the BLC weakens.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter's question on effectual calling (Q. "What is effectual calling?") defines the nature of effectual calling but does not address the exclusivity question of who is and is not effectually called:

> "Effectual calling is the work of God's Spirit, whereby convincing us of our sin and misery, enlightening our minds in the knowledge of Christ, and renewing our wills, he doth persuade and enable us to embrace Jesus Christ freely offered to us in the gospel." (2 Tim. 1:9; 2 Thess. 2:13, 14; Acts 2:37; Acts 26:18; Ez. 36:26, 27; John 6:44, 45; Phil. 2:13)

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel to compare)
- Structural changes: N/A
- The BLC appropriately addresses a topic the Shorter does not cover, filling the gap that exists between the Shorter and the Larger catechism traditions.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 10, Paragraph 4:

> "Others not elected, although they may be called by the Ministry of the word, and may have some common operations of the Spirit, yet not being effectually drawn by the Father, they neither will, nor can truly come to Christ; and therefore cannot be saved: much less can men that receive not the Christian Religion be saved; be they never so diligent to frame their lives according to the light of nature, and the Law of that Religion they do profess."

- Omissions:
  - **"not being effectually drawn by the Father"** — The 1689 explicitly roots the non-elect's failure in God's sovereign non-drawing. The BLC shifts the entire causal weight to the non-elect's "willful neglect and contempt." While the BLC's language is not wrong (it reflects the WLC's framing), the 1689's phrase preserves the Reformed balance between divine sovereignty and human responsibility more explicitly.
  - **"they neither will, nor can truly come to Christ"** — The 1689 teaches both unwillingness ("will not") and inability ("cannot"). The BLC says they "never truly come to Christ," which states the result without affirming the underlying inability. This is a weakening of the 1689's anthropology.
  - **"and therefore cannot be saved"** — The 1689 draws the explicit soteriological consequence. The BLC omits this.
  - **"much less can men that receive not the Christian Religion be saved"** — The 1689's statement about the insufficiency of natural religion/other religions is entirely absent. This is expected given the catechism's narrower scope on this question, but worth noting.
- Structural changes: The 1689 structures the non-elect's condition as: (1) external call received, (2) common operations of Spirit experienced, (3) but not effectually drawn by the Father, (4) therefore they will not and cannot come to Christ. The BLC restructures this as: (1) external call, (2) Spirit's common operations, (3) from their own willful neglect they remain in unbelief. The 1689's causal chain (divine non-drawing → inability → not coming) is replaced with a purely culpability-focused chain (human neglect → remaining in unbelief).

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q68:

> "Q. 68. Are the elect only effectually called?"
> "A. All the elect, and they only, are effectually called: although others may be, and often are, outwardly called by the ministry of the Word, and have some common operations of the Spirit; who, for their wilful neglect and contempt of the grace offered to them, being justly left in their unbelief, do never truly come to Jesus Christ."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None in this question (no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology involved).
- Unexpected divergences:
  - The WLC says "they only" while BLC says "only they" — trivial word order change, no theological impact.
  - The WLC says "outwardly called" while BLC says "externally called" — synonymous modernization.
  - The WLC's "some common operations of the Spirit" becomes the BLC's expanded "the Spirit may work in them in some of the same ways He works in the elect." This is an interpretive gloss that makes the meaning clearer but slightly shifts the emphasis — the WLC's "common operations" is a technical term in Reformed theology distinguishing common grace from special/saving grace. The BLC's phrasing could be read as suggesting the Spirit's work in the non-elect is qualitatively similar to his work in the elect (differing only in degree), which moves toward a more Amyraldian reading. However, this is a plausible and defensible interpretation of "common operations."
  - The WLC says "being justly left in their unbelief" (passive — God justly leaves them). The BLC says "they are justly allowed to remain in their unbelief" — similar sense but "allowed to remain" is slightly softer than "left." The WLC's "left" carries a stronger sense of judicial abandonment.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - None of major significance. The BLC tracks the WLC very closely on this question.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The BLC's expansion of "common operations"**: The phrase "some of the same ways He works in the elect" is theologically loaded. Reformed theology has historically distinguished between the Spirit's *common* operations (illumination, conviction, temporary faith, partial reformation) and his *saving* operations (regeneration, effectual calling, perseverance). The BLC's language could blur this distinction by suggesting the difference is merely quantitative rather than qualitative. The proof texts chosen (Matt. 7:22; 13:20-21; Heb. 6:4-6) are appropriate and support the traditional reading.

2. **Missing "inability" language**: Both the WLC and the 1689 teach that the non-elect's failure to come to Christ involves both culpability (willful neglect) and inability (not being drawn by the Father, cannot come). The BLC preserves the culpability side but not the inability side. This is worth noting because the inability of the non-elect to come to Christ is a key Reformed distinctive against Arminianism.

3. **"Justly allowed to remain" vs. "justly left"**: The BLC's phrasing is softer. "Left in unbelief" evokes Romans 1:24-28 (God "gave them up") more directly than "allowed to remain."

4. **Strong structural parallel to WLC**: Despite these concerns, the BLC follows the WLC's four-part structure faithfully: (a) the affirmation of exclusivity, (b) the external call to others, (c) common operations of the Spirit, (d) the non-elect's culpable failure to come to Christ. This is commendable.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Acts 13:48** (for section 1): Appropriate — "as many as were appointed to eternal life believed." Directly supports the elect-only nature of effectual calling.
- **Matthew 22:14; Acts 8:13-21** (for section 2): Matthew 22:14 ("many are called, but few are chosen") is the classic text for the external/effectual calling distinction. Acts 8:13-21 (Simon Magus) illustrates one externally called but not effectually called. Both appropriate.
- **Matthew 7:22; 13:20-21; Hebrews 6:4-6** (for section 3): All three texts illustrate the Spirit's common operations in the non-elect — false professors (Matt. 7:22), temporary faith (Matt. 13:20-21), and those who have "tasted" heavenly gifts (Heb. 6:4-6). Well chosen.
- **John 12:38-40; Acts 28:25-27; John 6:64-65; Psalm 81:11-12; Hebrews 10:29; 1 John 2:19** (for section 4): This is a strong set. John 6:64-65 ("no one can come to me unless it is granted him by the Father") actually supports the *inability* teaching that the BLC's text itself softens — the proof texts are stronger than the catechism language. Psalm 81:11-12 and John 12:38-40 support judicial hardening. 1 John 2:19 supports the perseverance/apostasy distinction. Notably, John 6:64-65 would more naturally support the 1689's "not being effectually drawn by the Father, they neither will, nor can truly come to Christ" than the BLC's "from their own willful neglect... they are justly allowed to remain in their unbelief."
