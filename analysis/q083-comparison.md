# Q83: What shall be done to the wicked at the Day of Judgment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q83 closely follows WLC Q89 in structure and content, faithfully preserving the key theological elements of condemnation, exclusion from God's presence, and eternal punishment. However, it omits one significant element from the Shorter Catechism: the explicit mention that "the bodies of the wicked, being raised out of their graves," are sentenced "together with their souls." The BLC references "both of body and soul" at the end but drops the Shorter's emphasis on bodily resurrection preceding the sentencing, which is a distinctive element of the Shorter's answer. Additionally, the BLC replaces WLC's precise phrase "fearful but just sentence of condemnation pronounced against them" with a paraphrase that weakens the judicial precision.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- Q: "What shall be done to the wicked, at the day of judgment?"
- A: "At the day of judgment the bodies of the wicked, being raised out of their graves, shall be sentenced, together with their souls, to unspeakable torments with the devil and his angels for ever."

- **Omissions:**
  - The Shorter explicitly states "the bodies of the wicked, being raised out of their graves" — the BLC drops the entire resurrection element from this answer. The Shorter makes bodily resurrection the grammatical subject and starting point, emphasizing that the raised bodies are sentenced together with their souls. The BLC instead begins with placement on Christ's left hand (following WLC) and only mentions "both of body and soul" as a qualifier of the torments at the end.
  - The Shorter's phrase "shall be sentenced" (a formal judicial act) is replaced by the BLC's "they will be justly and terribly condemned" — while not a contradiction, "sentenced" carries a more precise courtroom connotation that mirrors the legal/forensic theology.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter's structure is: resurrection → sentencing → punishment. The BLC's structure is: placement at left hand → evidence presented → condemnation → exclusion → punishment. The BLC follows the WLC's expanded structure rather than the Shorter's more compressed form, which is appropriate for a Larger Catechism but means the Shorter's distinctive emphasis on bodily resurrection as the lead element is lost.
- **Agreement:** Both affirm unspeakable torments, punishment of body and soul, with the devil and his angels, forever.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**
- Chapter 32 (Of the Last Judgment), Paragraph 1: "God hath appointed a Day wherein he will judge the world in Righteousness, by Jesus Christ; to whom all power and judgement is given of the Father; in which Day not only the Apostate Angels shall be judged; but likewise all persons that have lived upon the Earth, shall appear before the Tribunal of Christ; to give an account of their Thoughts, Words, and Deeds, and to receive according to what they have done in the body, whether good or evil."
- Chapter 32, Paragraph 2: "The end of Gods appointing this Day, is for the manifestation of the glory of his Mercy, in the Eternal Salvation of the Elect; and of his Justice in the Eternal damnation of the Reprobate, who are wicked and disobedient; for then shall the Righteous go into Everlasting Life, and receive that fulness of Joy, and Glory, with everlasting reward, in the presence of the Lord: but the wicked who know not God, and obey not the Gospel of Jesus Christ, shall be cast into Eternal torments, and punished with everlasting destruction, from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power."

- **Omissions:**
  - The 1689's description of the wicked as those "who know not God, and obey not the Gospel of Jesus Christ" (drawn from 2 Thess. 1:8) is not reflected in the BLC's answer. This is a theologically significant characterization — it identifies the wicked not merely generically but specifically as those who refuse the gospel.
  - The 1689's emphasis on the *purpose* of the judgment ("manifestation of the glory of his...Justice in the Eternal damnation of the Reprobate") is not captured. However, this is an overarching statement about the Day rather than specific to the wicked's experience.
  - The 1689's language "punished with everlasting destruction" is not directly preserved, though the BLC's "punished forever with unspeakable torments" conveys the same substance.
- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC exposits the wicked's experience at judgment in more detail than the Confession does, which is appropriate for a catechism.
- **Agreement:** Strong agreement on eternal torments, separation from God's presence, and punishment of the wicked. The BLC's phrase "cast out from the favorable presence of God" closely tracks the 1689's "from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- WLC Q89: "What shall be done to the wicked at the day of judgment?"
- A: "At the day of judgment, the wicked shall be set on Christ's left hand, and, upon clear evidence, and full conviction of their own consciences, shall have the fearful but just sentence of condemnation pronounced against them; and thereupon shall be cast out from the favourable presence of God, and the glorious fellowship with Christ, his saints, and all his holy angels, into hell, to be punished with unspeakable torments, both of body and soul, with the devil and his angels forever."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None — this question involves no baptismal, ecclesiological, or covenant theology issues.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC says "upon clear evidence, and full conviction of their own consciences" — the BLC renders this as "with the evidence against them clearly presented and fully recognized by them." This is a reasonable modernization, but "fully recognized by them" is weaker than "full conviction of their own consciences." The WLC's language points to the conscience as the internal mechanism of conviction (connecting to Rom. 2:15-16), while the BLC's phrasing could suggest mere intellectual acknowledgment rather than deep conscientious conviction.
  - The WLC's "fearful but just sentence of condemnation pronounced against them" becomes the BLC's "they will be justly and terribly condemned." The WLC's phrase specifies that a *sentence* is *pronounced* — formal judicial language — while the BLC uses a more general verb. The WLC's pairing "fearful but just" is theologically precise, acknowledging the terror while immediately grounding it in justice. The BLC's "justly and terribly" captures this but reverses the emphasis (justice first, then terror) and loses the "but" conjunction that in the WLC concedes the fearfulness while insisting on the justice.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "all his holy angels" — the WLC includes "all his holy angels" in the fellowship from which the wicked are excluded. The BLC has "His angels" but drops "all" and "holy," a minor reduction.
  - "pronounced against them" — the formal judicial act of sentencing is reduced to the more generic "condemned."

## Detailed Notes
1. **Bodily resurrection omission:** The most notable gap relative to the Shorter Catechism is the complete absence of the resurrection motif. The Shorter makes the resurrection of the wicked's bodies the lead element — "the bodies of the wicked, being raised out of their graves, shall be sentenced." This is theologically significant because it underscores that judgment is *bodily*, not merely spiritual. The BLC does say "both of body and soul" at the end, preserving the substance, but the Shorter's emphasis on the *event* of resurrection as the prerequisite and prelude to judgment is lost. This is mitigated by the fact that the BLC likely has a separate question on the resurrection (as the WLC does in Q87), so the omission here may be a matter of scope division rather than theology.

2. **Conscience vs. recognition:** The shift from "conviction of their own consciences" to "fully recognized by them" subtly flattens the anthropological specificity of the WLC. The conscience (syneidēsis) is a theologically loaded term in the Reformed tradition, tied to Romans 2:15. The BLC's proof text (Rom. 2:15-16) still points there, but the catechetical language itself doesn't name the conscience as the faculty involved.

3. **"Fearful but just" → "justly and terribly":** The WLC's "fearful but just" has a particular rhetorical and theological function — it concedes what might seem problematic (the fearfulness) while asserting its grounding (justice). The BLC's inversion and change of conjunction removes this nuance.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Matthew 25:33** — Correctly cited for placement on Christ's left hand. Matches WLC footnote 385.
- **Romans 2:15-16** — Correctly cited for conscience-bearing witness at judgment. Matches WLC footnote 386. However, note that the BLC text doesn't actually name the "conscience" that this verse speaks about.
- **Matthew 25:41-43** — Correctly cited for the sentence of condemnation. Matches WLC footnote 387.
- **Luke 16:26; 2 Thessalonians 1:8-9** — Correctly cited for exclusion from God's presence and eternal punishment. Matches WLC footnote 388. The BLC's proof texts are identical to those of the WLC for Q89.
- The Shorter Catechism cites John 5:28-29 (resurrection of the wicked), Matthew 25:41,46, and 2 Thessalonians 1:8-9. The BLC drops John 5:28-29, consistent with its omission of the resurrection element.
