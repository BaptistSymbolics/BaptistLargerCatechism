# Q14: What are the decrees of God?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q14 closely follows the WLC Q12 structure and preserves the core content of both the Shorter Catechism and 1689 Confession. However, it replaces the phrase "counsel of his will" (used in the WLC, the Shorter, and the 1689) with "purposes of His will," weakening a precise theological term. It also omits the 1689's important qualifications about God not being the author of sin and the preservation of second causes. Additionally, the BLC changes the WLC's "especially" to "particularly," a minor but notable shift in emphasis.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What are the decrees of God? A. The decrees of God are his eternal purpose according to the counsel of his will, whereby, for his own glory, he hath foreordained whatsoever comes to pass (Eph. 1:4, 11; Rom. 9:22-23; Is. 46:10; Lam. 3:37)."

- **Omissions:**
  - "according to the counsel of his will" — The Shorter uses "counsel of his will," echoing Ephesians 1:11 (τὴν βουλὴν τοῦ θελήματος αὐτοῦ). The BLC replaces this with "from the purposes of His will." While "purpose" appears in Ephesians 1:11 (πρόθεσιν), "counsel" (βουλή) is the specific term Paul uses to describe the nature of God's decretive will. The Shorter, the 1689, and the WLC all use "counsel." The BLC's substitution drops this precise biblical and confessional term.
  - "eternal purpose" — The Shorter's phrase "eternal purpose" is replaced by "from all eternity" modifying the verb "foreordained." The substance is preserved but expressed differently.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC expands the Shorter by adding "wise, free, and holy acts" (from the WLC) and "in Himself" (from the 1689), and adds the qualifier about angels and men. These are legitimate expansions from the standards.
- **Additions:** "in Himself" is drawn from 1689 III.1 ("Decreed in himself") — a valid expansion. "In time" is drawn from the WLC — also valid.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter III, Paragraph 1 — "God hath Decreed in himself from all Eternity, by the most wise and holy Councel of his own will, freely and unchangeably, all things whatsoever comes to passe; yet so as thereby is God neither the author of sin, nor hath fellowship with any therein, nor is violence offered to the will of the Creature, nor yet is the liberty, or contingency of second causes taken away, but rather established, in which appears his wisdom in disposing all things, and power, and faithfulness in accomplishing his Decree."

- **Omissions:**
  - "counsel of his own will" — Again, "counsel" is replaced with "purposes." The 1689 explicitly uses "Councel of his own will."
  - The entire second half of 1689 III.1 is absent: God not being the author of sin, no fellowship with sin, no violence to the creature's will, and the preservation of second causes. These are significant theological guardrails. While the BLC is a catechism answer (not a confessional paragraph) and cannot include everything, these qualifications are among the most pastorally important elements of the doctrine of decrees. The Shorter Catechism also omits these (being a shorter form), so the BLC follows the Shorter's pattern here. However, as a *Larger* Catechism, one might expect these protections to appear, as the WLC addresses them in subsequent questions (Q13-14).
- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC draws "in Himself" directly from the 1689's "Decreed in himself," which is a positive connection.
- **Agreement:** The core theological content — eternal, free, unchangeable foreordination of all things for God's glory — is faithfully preserved.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 12. What are the decrees of God? A. God's decrees are the wise, free, and holy acts of the counsel of his will, whereby, from all eternity, he hath, for his own glory, unchangeably foreordained whatsoever comes to pass in time, especially concerning angels and men."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC Q14 is clearly modeled on WLC Q12 and follows its structure almost verbatim.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected here; this is a doctrine of common agreement.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - "of the counsel of his will" → "from the purposes of His will" — This substitution is unexpected. "Counsel" (βουλή) is the biblical term from Ephesians 1:11 and carries the connotation of deliberation, wisdom, and settled determination. "Purposes" is vaguer and loses the precision of the confessional tradition. All three standards (Shorter, 1689, WLC) use "counsel"; the BLC alone departs.
  - "especially" → "particularly" — A subtle change. "Especially" in the WLC signals that while the decrees encompass all things, they have a special/heightened concern regarding angels and men (the rational, moral creatures). "Particularly" could be read as merely specifying or singling out, without the connotation of heightened importance. The difference is minor but shifts the nuance slightly.
- **Key phrases dropped:** "counsel of his will" — dropped without clear theological reason.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "counsel" question is the primary concern.** The phrase "counsel of his will" is not merely stylistic; it is a precise translation of Ephesians 1:11 (κατὰ τὴν βουλὴν τοῦ θελήματος αὐτοῦ) and has been the standard Reformed expression since the Westminster Assembly. Its replacement with "purposes" obscures this direct scriptural connection and departs from all three standards simultaneously. If the BLC is meant to modernize, "counsel" is not an archaic term requiring updating.

2. **The "in Himself" addition is commendable.** Drawing from the 1689's "Decreed in himself," this emphasizes the aseity and self-sufficiency of God's decretive activity — that the decrees are not responses to anything external. This is a faithful Baptist-confessional expansion beyond the WLC.

3. **"Especially" vs. "particularly."** While these words overlap in meaning, "especially" more clearly conveys the theological point that the decree of God has a particular weightiness and concern regarding moral agents. "Particularly" can function similarly but is slightly less emphatic.

4. **Absence of authorship-of-sin guardrails.** The 1689's qualifications about God not being the author of sin are absent. The WLC also does not include them in Q12 itself but addresses them in subsequent questions. Whether the BLC addresses these elsewhere would determine whether this is a real gap.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts are generally well-chosen and overlap significantly with the WLC and 1689.
- **Isaiah 45:6-7** is a strong addition not in the WLC or Shorter, emphasizing God's sovereignty over both light and darkness.
- **Romans 9:14-15, 18** supports the "free" aspect of the decrees.
- **Romans 11:33** supports the "wise" aspect.
- **Psalm 33:11** and **Isaiah 14:24** support unchangeability.
- **Acts 2:23; 4:27-28** support foreordination of specific events — strong choices.
- **Ephesians 1:4, 11** — notably, Eph 1:11 is the very verse that uses βουλή ("counsel"), making the BLC's replacement of "counsel" with "purposes" in the answer text somewhat ironic given its own proof text.
- The third section ("particularly concerning angels and men") has no proof texts listed, which is a gap. The WLC and 1689 both cite texts for this claim (e.g., 1 Tim 5:21; Matt 25:41).
