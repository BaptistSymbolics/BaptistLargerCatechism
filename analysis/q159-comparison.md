# Q159: What is the duty of those who are rightly baptized?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q159 is substantially faithful to the Baptist Shorter Catechism, preserving the core duty of joining a particular church and walking in obedience. However, it omits the Shorter's word "blameless" (from Luke 1:6), which adds an important qualitative standard to the obedience described. It also replaces the Shorter's "give up themselves to some particular and orderly church" with "join themselves to a visible and orderly church," which slightly weakens the covenantal self-surrender language. The WLC parallel (Q167) takes an entirely different approach to post-baptismal duty, making direct comparison less instructive.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- "Q. What is the duty of such who are rightly baptized? A. It is the duty of such who are rightly baptized to give up themselves to some particular and orderly church of Jesus Christ, that they may walk in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless (Acts 2:41, 42; 5:13, 14; 9:26; 1 Pet. 2:5; Lk. 1:6)."

- **Omissions:**
  1. **"blameless"** — The Shorter concludes with "walk in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless," drawing on Luke 1:6 (Zechariah and Elizabeth). The BLC drops "blameless" entirely. This word sets the standard of sincerity and diligence in obedience — not perfection, but a blameless manner of walking. Its omission weakens the standard from earnest, wholehearted obedience to mere compliance with commandments and ordinances.
  2. **"give up themselves"** — The Shorter's phrase "give up themselves to some particular and orderly church" carries strong covenantal language of self-dedication and surrender to the church body. The BLC replaces this with "join themselves to a visible and orderly church," which is adequate but loses the depth of self-giving commitment implied by the Shorter's language.

- **Structural changes:**
  1. **"particular" → "visible"** — The Shorter says "some particular and orderly church"; the BLC says "a visible and orderly church." The word "particular" emphasizes the local, specific congregation (consistent with 1689 XXVI.2's "particular Congregations"), while "visible" introduces a different ecclesiological category (visible vs. invisible church). While "visible" is not incorrect, it shifts the emphasis from the concrete act of joining *a specific local church* to the more abstract quality of the church being *visible*. The Shorter's "particular" is more precise for the duty being described.

- **Agreement:** The core structure is preserved — the duty of the baptized is to join themselves to a church for the purpose of walking in obedience to Christ's commandments and ordinances. The question wording is identical. The overall shape of the answer is faithful.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter XXVI (Of the Church), Paragraph 5: "In the execution of this power wherewith he is so intrusted, the Lord Jesus calleth out of the World unto himself, through the Ministry of his word, by his Spirit, those that are given unto him by his Father; that they may walk before him in all the ways of obedience, which he prescribeth to them in his Word. Those thus called he commandeth to walk together in particular societies, or Churches, for their mutual edification; and the due performance of that publick worship, which he requireth of them in the World."

Chapter XXVI, Paragraph 6: "The Members of these Churches are Saints by calling, visibly manifesting and evidencing (in and by their profession and walking) their obedience unto that call of Christ; and do willingly consent to walk together according to the appointment of Christ, giving up themselves, to the Lord & one to another by the will of God, in professed subjection to the Ordinances of the Gospel."

- **Omissions:**
  1. The 1689 specifies "particular societies, or Churches" (XXVI.5) — the BLC uses "visible" instead of "particular," which loses the confessional language.
  2. The 1689's "giving up themselves, to the Lord & one to another by the will of God, in professed subjection to the Ordinances of the Gospel" (XXVI.6) contains the rich covenantal language of self-surrender that the Shorter's "give up themselves" echoes. The BLC's "join themselves" partially captures this but loses the dual dimension of giving oneself to the Lord *and* to one another.
  3. The 1689 specifies the purpose includes "mutual edification" and "due performance of that publick worship" (XXVI.5) — the BLC's "walk in all the commandments and ordinances" is a broader summary that covers this but is less specific about the corporate purposes.

- **Structural changes:** The theological structure is preserved — baptism leads to church membership, which is the context for obedience. The BLC faithfully maintains the 1689's order: baptism → church membership → obedient walking.

- **Agreement:** The BLC correctly links baptism to the duty of church membership, consistent with the 1689's teaching that Christ "commandeth to walk together in particular societies, or Churches." The phrase "commandments and ordinances of the Lord" aligns with the 1689's "ways of obedience" and "professed subjection to the Ordinances of the Gospel."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- WLC Q167: "How is our baptism to be improved by us? A. The needful but much neglected duty of improving our baptism, is to be performed by us all our life long, especially in the time of temptation, and when we are present at the administration of it to others; by serious and thankful consideration of the nature of it, and of the ends for which Christ instituted it, the privileges and benefits conferred and sealed thereby, and our solemn vow made therein; by being humbled for our sinful defilement, our falling short of, and walking contrary to, the grace of baptism, and our engagements; by growing up to assurance of pardon of sin, and of all other blessings sealed to us in that sacrament; by drawing strength from the death and resurrection of Christ, into whom we are baptized, for the mortifying of sin, and quickening of grace; and by endeavoring to live by faith, to have our conversation in holiness and righteousness, as those that have therein given up their names to Christ; and to walk in brotherly love, as being baptized by the same Spirit into one body."

- **Solidarity:** WEAK — This is expected. The WLC and BLC take fundamentally different approaches to the post-baptismal duty question. The WLC asks about "improving" baptism (a lifelong, devotional, and sanctification-oriented answer), while the BLC follows the Shorter Catechism in asking about the immediate institutional duty of joining a church. These are complementary rather than competing concerns, but they reflect different catechetical priorities.

- **Intentional Baptist divergences:**
  1. The WLC's language of "sacrament" and "sealed" reflects a sacramental theology that Baptists deliberately avoid. The BLC's focus on the duty of church membership rather than baptismal improvement avoids implying ongoing sacramental efficacy.
  2. The WLC's "solemn vow made therein" reflects paedobaptist assumptions about the covenantal significance of baptism as a seal. The BLC's approach bypasses this entirely.
  3. The WLC speaks of "the grace of baptism" and blessings "sealed to us in that sacrament" — language that implies baptismal efficacy beyond what Baptist theology affirms.

- **Unexpected divergences:** None. The differences are entirely in expected areas of sacramental theology and ecclesiology. The BLC's decision to follow the Shorter Catechism's approach (duty of church membership) rather than the WLC's approach (baptismal improvement) is a reasonable Baptist choice.

- **Key phrases dropped:** The WLC's final phrase "to walk in brotherly love, as being baptized by the same Spirit into one body" (1 Cor. 12:13) is a genuinely valuable phrase that could have been incorporated without sacramental baggage. However, its omission is not theologically problematic since the BLC follows the Shorter's framework rather than the WLC's, and the Shorter does not include this phrase either.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"blameless" is the most significant omission.** The word comes from Luke 1:6 — "And they were both righteous before God, walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless." The Shorter deliberately echoes this verse. The BLC preserves the phrase "walk in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord" but drops "blameless," severing the biblical allusion and removing the qualitative standard. Since this is one of the Shorter's proof texts (Lk. 1:6), the omission appears to be an oversight rather than a theological choice.

2. **"particular" vs. "visible" is a meaningful lexical shift.** The 1689 consistently uses "particular" for local churches (XXVI.2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9) — it is the confessional term for a local congregation. The BLC's substitution of "visible" introduces a different category. Both the 1689 and the Shorter use "particular" in this context. A baptized believer's duty is not to join "a visible church" in the abstract but to commit to *a particular local congregation*. The BLC's "visible" is theologically true but imprecise for the duty being described.

3. **Proof text changes are minor but notable.** The BLC uses Acts 2:46-47 where the Shorter has Acts 2:41-42. Both reference the early church's corporate life, but Acts 2:41-42 ("they that gladly received his word were baptized: and the same day there were added unto them about three thousand souls. And they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers") more directly connects baptism to being "added" to the church and then persevering in ordinances. Acts 2:46-47 describes the ongoing life of the church. The BLC drops Acts 5:13-14 and Luke 1:6 (the source of "blameless") and adds Romans 16:5 and Hebrews 10:25.

4. **The addition of Hebrews 10:25 and Romans 16:5 is appropriate.** Hebrews 10:25 ("not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together") strengthens the duty of church attendance, and Romans 16:5 ("the church that is in their house") illustrates the particular, local nature of the church. These are good additions.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Acts 2:46-47** — Describes the corporate worship life of the early church. Appropriate but less directly tied to the baptism→church membership connection than the Shorter's Acts 2:41-42.
- **Acts 9:26** — Saul's attempt to join the disciples at Jerusalem. Excellent proof text for the duty of joining a particular church after conversion/baptism. Shared with the Shorter.
- **1 Peter 2:5** — "Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house." Supports church membership as incorporation into a spiritual body. Shared with the Shorter.
- **Hebrews 10:25** — "Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together." Strong support for the duty of church attendance and membership. Good addition by the BLC.
- **Romans 16:5** — "The church that is in their house." Illustrates the particular/local nature of the church. Good addition, though it somewhat ironically supports the word "particular" (which the BLC replaced with "visible").
- **Dropped: Luke 1:6** — The loss of this proof text corresponds to the loss of "blameless." This is the most significant proof text omission.
- **Dropped: Acts 2:41-42** — The shift from 2:41-42 to 2:46-47 loses the explicit baptism→addition→ordinances sequence that perfectly maps the duty described in the answer.
- **Dropped: Acts 5:13-14** — "And of the rest durst no man join himself to them: but the people magnified them. And believers were the more added to the Lord." This text supports the seriousness of church membership and the pattern of believers being added. Its omission is minor.
