# Q65: What is justifying faith?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
The BLC calls justifying faith "an act of the free grace of God" rather than "a saving grace," which fundamentally misidentifies faith's nature—confusing an attribute (it is a grace) with an action (it is an act). The BLC also replaces the WLC's critical "not only...but" construction with a weaker two-sentence structure that separates assent from reception rather than subordinating them, and it drops the key Shorter Catechism phrase "him alone" from "receive and rest upon him alone."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What is faith in Jesus Christ? A. Faith in Jesus Christ is a saving grace (Heb. 10:39), whereby we receive and rest upon him alone for salvation, as he is offered to us in the gospel (Jn. 1:12; Is. 26:3, 4; Ph. 3:9; Gal. 2:16)."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"a saving grace"** — The Shorter says faith IS "a saving grace." The BLC says faith is "an act of the free grace of God." This is a category confusion: the Shorter identifies what faith IS (a grace, i.e., a gift/disposition); the BLC identifies what faith IS (an act). "Grace" in the Shorter describes faith's nature; "grace" in the BLC describes God's action in producing faith. These are different statements.
  - **"him alone"** — The Shorter says we "receive and rest upon him alone for salvation." The BLC says "They receive and rest on Christ and His righteousness for pardon of sin." The exclusive particle "alone" is dropped. While the BLC does not explicitly deny exclusivity, omitting "alone" weakens the solus Christus emphasis that the Shorter intentionally includes.
  - **"as he is offered to us in the gospel"** — The Shorter's formulation connects faith directly to Christ as offered in the gospel. The BLC separates this into a distinct section: "as held forth in the gospel." This is a minor structural shift but preserves the basic idea.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter gives a compact definition with a single "whereby" clause. The BLC expands into multiple sections, which is appropriate for a Larger Catechism but introduces the structural issues noted below.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 14 (Of Saving Faith), Paragraph 1: "The Grace of Faith, whereby the Elect are enabled to beleive to the saving of their souls, is the work of the Spirit of Christ in their hearts; and is ordinarily wrought by the Ministry of the Word."

Chapter 14, Paragraph 2: "By this Faith, a Christian believeth to be true, whatsoever is revealed in the Word, for the Authority of God himself... But the principal acts of Saving Faith, have immediate relation to Christ, accepting, receiving, and resting upon him alone, for Justification, Sanctification, and Eternal Life, by vertue of the Covenant of Grace."

Chapter 11 (Of Justification), Paragraph 1: "Those whom God Effectually calleth, he also freely justifieth, not by infusing Righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting, and accepting their Persons as Righteous... they receiving, and resting on him, and his Righteousness, by Faith; which faith they have not of themselves, it is the gift of God."

Chapter 11, Paragraph 2: "Faith thus receiving and resting on Christ, and his Righteousness, is the alone instrument of Justification."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"him alone"** — 1689 14.2 explicitly says the principal acts of saving faith are "accepting, receiving, and resting upon him alone." The BLC drops "alone."
  - **"accepting"** — The 1689 uses a triad: "accepting, receiving, and resting upon." The BLC uses only "receive and rest on," following the WLC pattern. The term "accepting" is part of the Confession's own vocabulary and its omission is notable though less critical.
  - **"the alone instrument of Justification"** — The 1689 11.2 identifies faith as "the alone instrument of Justification." While Q65 is about defining faith rather than explaining how it justifies, the BLC's opening redefinition of faith as "an act" rather than "a grace" moves in the wrong direction. The Confession carefully calls faith "the Grace of Faith" (14.1) and identifies it as an "instrument" (11.2). Calling it "an act" risks conflating the instrument with its exercise.
  - **"by vertue of the Covenant of Grace"** — The 1689 14.2 grounds saving faith's relation to Christ in the Covenant of Grace. The BLC makes no reference to the covenant framework.
  - **"for Justification, Sanctification, and Eternal Life"** — The 1689 14.2 says faith has immediate relation to Christ for justification, sanctification, AND eternal life. The BLC narrows the scope to pardon and acceptance as righteous, which is appropriate for a question specifically about "justifying faith" but drops the broader scope of saving faith that the 1689 maintains even while discussing the same topic.
- **Structural changes:** The 1689 carefully distinguishes faith as a grace/gift (14.1) from its acts/exercises (14.2). The BLC collapses this distinction by defining justifying faith as "an act."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 72. What is justifying faith? A. Justifying faith is a saving grace, wrought in the heart of a sinner by the Spirit and Word of God, whereby he, being convinced of his sin and misery, and of the disability in himself and all other creatures to recover him out of his lost condition, not only assenteth to the truth of the promise of the gospel, but receiveth and resteth upon Christ and his righteousness, therein held forth, for pardon of sin, and for the accepting and accounting of his person righteous in the sight of God for salvation."

- **Solidarity:** MODERATE — The BLC closely follows the WLC's overall structure and covers the same ground, but introduces key departures in its opening definition and internal logic.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent. This question does not touch on baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology in ways that would require Baptist modification.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - **"a saving grace" → "an act of the free grace of God"** — The WLC says faith IS "a saving grace." The BLC says faith IS "an act of the free grace of God." This is a significant and unexpected departure. In Reformed theology, "saving grace" identifies faith as a gracious habit/disposition given by God; "act of the free grace of God" redescribes faith as a divine action rather than a human disposition wrought by God. The WLC's formula is the standard Reformed expression shared across Presbyterian and Baptist confessional traditions.
  - **"not only assenteth...but receiveth and resteth"** — The WLC uses a critical "not only...but" construction that subordinates assent to reception. Assent is real but insufficient; faith's chief act is receiving and resting on Christ. The BLC breaks this into two separate sentences: "they give full assent to the truth of the gospel promise" and then separately "They receive and rest on Christ." This separating of assent from reception into distinct statements loses the WLC's intentional subordination and makes them appear as coordinate, sequential acts rather than a single movement of faith where reception includes and surpasses assent.
  - **"the truth of the promise of the gospel" → "the truth of the gospel promise"** — A minor word-order change but the WLC's phrasing emphasizes that it is the promise contained in the gospel, while the BLC's phrasing is vaguer.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - **"a saving grace"** — The foundational Reformed identification of faith's nature.
  - **"not only...but"** — The crucial subordinating construction.
  - **"therein held forth"** — The WLC says Christ and his righteousness are "therein held forth" (in the promise of the gospel). The BLC separates this into a distinct section: "as held forth in the gospel." The connection between the gospel promise and Christ being held forth in it is loosened.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"An act of the free grace of God" — Category confusion:** The most significant issue is the opening definition. In the Reformed confessional tradition, faith is consistently identified as a "grace" (a divinely wrought disposition/habit) that exercises itself in various "acts" (assent, reception, trust). The 1689 14.1 calls it "the Grace of Faith." The WLC calls it "a saving grace." The Shorter calls it "a saving grace." The BLC calls it "an act of the free grace of God." This conflates the act with the grace, and risks suggesting that justifying faith is something God does rather than something God gives. The proof text cited (Heb. 10:39) is the same one the Shorter uses to prove faith is "a saving grace," which makes the BLC's departure from the standard language even more puzzling — the same text is being used to support a different formulation.

2. **Separation of assent and reception:** The WLC's "not only...but" is a masterful theological construction. It affirms that assent to gospel truth is genuine and necessary, while simultaneously insisting that faith's principal act goes beyond assent to personal reception and rest on Christ. The BLC's two-sentence structure ("they give full assent... They receive and rest...") presents these as two parallel or sequential acts rather than one movement with an inferior and a superior component. This could inadvertently support a view of faith as mere intellectual assent followed by a separate act of trust, rather than one unified grace whose essence is trust.

3. **"Full assent" vs. "not only assenteth":** The BLC adds the modifier "full" to assent ("give full assent to the truth of the gospel promise"), which is not in the WLC. While not heretical, this risks over-emphasizing the assent component that the WLC deliberately subordinates. The WLC's whole point is that assent, while real, is "not only" what faith does.

4. **The word "alone" omitted:** Both the Shorter ("him alone") and the 1689 14.2 ("him alone") include the exclusive particle when describing the object of faith. The BLC's omission of "alone" weakens the exclusivity of Christ as the object of justifying faith. In a question about justification — the article by which the church stands or falls — this omission is not trivial.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Hebrews 10:39** — Used by BLC to support "an act of the free grace of God." However, both the Shorter Catechism and WLC use this same text to prove faith is "a saving grace." The verse speaks of "believing to the saving of the soul," which more naturally supports faith as a saving grace than as an act.
- **2 Corinthians 4:13; Ephesians 1:17-19; 1 Corinthians 12:3; 1 Peter 1:2** — Support the Spirit's role in faith. These are solid and appropriate.
- **Romans 10:14-17; 1 Corinthians 1:21** — Support the Word's instrumentality. Appropriate.
- **Acts 2:37; Acts 16:30; John 16:8-9; Romans 6:6; Ephesians 2:1; Acts 4:12** — Support conviction of sin and misery. Appropriate, though the BLC's expansion ("and of their inability in themselves (and all other creatures) to recover out of their lost condition") is well-supported and faithful to the WLC.
- **Ephesians 1:13; Hebrews 11:13** — Support assent to gospel truth. Appropriate.
- **John 1:12; Acts 16:31; Acts 10:43; Zechariah 3:8-9** — Support receiving and resting on Christ. The inclusion of Zechariah 3:8-9 is a helpful addition pointing to the Messianic Branch and priestly cleansing.
- **Philippians 3:9; Acts 15:11** — Support acceptance as righteous. Appropriate.
- Overall, the proof texts are well-chosen and largely track the WLC's Scripture references. The issue is not with the texts cited but with the doctrinal formulations they are cited to support.
