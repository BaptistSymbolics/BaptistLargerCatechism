# Q99: What is the second commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q99 faithfully renders the text of the second commandment (Exodus 20:4-6) in modern English. Since this question simply states the commandment text rather than expositing it, the theological comparison is straightforward: the BLC preserves all key elements of the commandment — the prohibition of idols, the prohibition of bowing/worshipping, God's jealousy, intergenerational punishment for those who hate him, and mercy to thousands of generations for those who love him and keep his commandments. The modernized language choices are faithful to the original.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Which is the second commandment?
> A. The second commandment is, Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of anything that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth; thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them: for I the Lord thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me; and shewing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments (Ex. 20:4, 5, 6).

- Omissions: None of substance. All key elements are preserved.
- Structural changes: None. Both follow the same structure of stating the commandment text.
- The BLC renders "graven image" as "idol" — a reasonable modernization that captures the essential meaning, though "graven image" is slightly more specific (a carved/sculpted representation). The BLC adds "in the form of anything" which corresponds to "or any likeness of anything" — a simplification but theologically equivalent.
- "Visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children" becomes "punishing the children for the sin of the fathers" — a faithful dynamic equivalent rendering.
- "Shewing mercy unto thousands" becomes "showing love to a thousand [generations]" — the brackets around "generations" helpfully clarify what "thousands" refers to, matching the traditional interpretation. However, "love" (Hebrew: *chesed*) where the Shorter has "mercy" is a legitimate translation choice, though "mercy" or "steadfast love" may better capture the covenantal connotation.
- "Nor serve them" becomes "or worship them" — this slightly collapses two distinct concepts (bowing down and serving) into bowing down and worshipping, though worship encompasses both ideas.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraph 1 — Of Religious Worship and the Sabbath Day:
> "The light of Nature shews that there is a God, who hath Lordship, and Soveraigntye over all; is just, good, and doth good unto all; and is therefore to be feared, loved, praised, called upon, trusted in, and served, with all the Heart, and all the Soul, and with all the Might. But the acceptable way of Worshipping the true God, is instituted by himself; and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be Worshipped according to the imaginations, and devices of Men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representations, or any other way, not prescribed in the Holy Scriptures."

The Confession cites Exodus 20:4-6 (note "c" reference) as the scriptural basis for the regulative principle of worship — that God "may not be Worshipped according to the imaginations, and devices of Men... under any visible representations." The BLC's rendering of the commandment text is fully consistent with the Confession's use of this passage.

- Omissions: None. The BLC is stating the commandment text, not expositing it; the Confession's regulative principle exposition would be expected in subsequent BLC questions about what is required/forbidden by the second commandment.
- Structural changes: None.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 107. Which is the second commandment?

The second commandment is, Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of anything that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath, or that is in the water under the earth. Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them: for I the Lord thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me; and showing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC mirrors the WLC's structure exactly (stating the commandment text), differing only in using modern English rather than KJV language.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None applicable — this question states biblical text, not confessional exposition.
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None of theological significance. The differences are purely translational (KJV to modern English).

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC uses the NIV-influenced rendering "idol" rather than the more literal "graven image." While "idol" is broader than "graven image" (which specifically denotes a carved representation), this is a defensible modernization since the commandment's scope extends beyond carved images to all forms of idolatrous representation, as the subsequent phrase "in the form of anything" makes clear.
- The phrase "nor serve them" (Shorter/WLC) becoming "or worship them" (BLC) is a minor translational choice. The Hebrew *abad* can mean "serve" or "worship," but collapsing the two clauses from "bow down... nor serve" to "bow down... or worship" slightly reduces the distinction between the physical act of prostration and the broader concept of religious service. This is a negligible concern since both concepts are adequately captured under "worship."
- The citation of Exodus 20:4-6 is accurate and matches all three standards.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Exodus 20:4-6**: The sole proof text cited, which is the text of the commandment itself. This matches the Shorter Catechism and WLC citations exactly.
- The BLC's modern rendering is consistent with major modern translations (NIV, ESV, CSB) in its handling of the Hebrew text, particularly the rendering of *pesel* (graven image/idol) and the intergenerational language.
