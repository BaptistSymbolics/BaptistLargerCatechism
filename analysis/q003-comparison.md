# Q3: How may we know there is a God?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
The Larger Catechism Q3 is virtually identical to the Shorter Catechism Q3, with only minor structural differences (the Larger breaks the answer into three proof-texted sections rather than one continuous sentence). The theological content faithfully reflects the 1689 Confession's teaching on natural revelation and the necessity of Word and Spirit for saving knowledge, found across multiple chapters.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** Q3 — "How may we know there is a God? A. The light of nature in man and the works of God plainly declare there is a God; but his word and Spirit only do it fully and effectually for the salvation of sinners (Rom. 1:19,20; Ps. 19:1,2,3; Acts 17:24; 1 Cor. 2:10; 2 Tim. 3:15,16)."

The Larger Catechism answer is substantively identical to the Shorter. The only differences are:

1. **Structure:** The Larger breaks the answer into three proof-texted sections: (a) the light of nature in man, (b) the works of God, (c) the insufficiency of both for salvation and the necessity of Word and Spirit. The Shorter combines these into a single flowing sentence.
2. **Wording:** The Larger says "reveal Him unto men for the salvation of sinners" where the Shorter says "do it fully and effectually for the salvation of sinners." The Larger's phrasing is slightly more explicit about what Word and Spirit accomplish — they "reveal Him," not merely confirm His existence.
3. **Proof texts:** The Larger significantly expands the Scripture citations. Where the Shorter cites five references, the Larger provides approximately sixteen. Notable additions include Proverbs 20:27 (the spirit of man as God's lamp — supporting "light of nature in man"), Psalm 139:14, Job 12:7-9, Acts 14:17, and Isaiah 46:9-10 and 41:21-29 (God's challenge to false gods through predictive prophecy). The Larger also adds 1 Corinthians 1:20-21 (the world's wisdom failing to know God) and Isaiah 59:21 (the Spirit's role in the covenant).

These are all legitimate expansions appropriate to a Larger Catechism. No theological divergence is present.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Sections:** Chapter 1, Paragraph 1; Chapter 1, Paragraphs 5-6; Chapter 10, Paragraph 4; Chapter 20, Paragraph 2; Chapter 22, Paragraph 1

The Catechism answer maps precisely onto the Confession's theology:

1. **Natural revelation is real but insufficient (1689 1.1):** The Confession states that "the light of Nature, and the works of Creation and Providence do so far manifest the goodness, wisdom and power of God, as to leave men unexcusable; yet are they not sufficient to give that knowledge of God and His will, which is necessary unto Salvation." The Catechism captures this in two parts: natural revelation "plainly declares there is a God," while only Word and Spirit reveal God "for the salvation of sinners."

2. **The Spirit is necessary for saving understanding (1689 1.5-6):** The Confession teaches that "our full perswasion, and assurance of the infallible truth, and divine authority thereof, is from the inward work of the Holy Spirit" and that "the inward illumination of the Spirit of God" is "necessary for the saving understanding of such things as are revealed in the Word." The Catechism's "His Word and Spirit only do fully and effectually reveal Him" faithfully summarizes this.

3. **Light of nature cannot save (1689 10.4, 20.2):** The Confession is emphatic that men cannot be saved by framing their lives "according to the light of nature" (10.4) and that the works of creation and providence with the light of nature do not "make discovery of Christ, or of Grace by him; so much as in a general, or obscure way" (20.2). The Catechism's "but...only" construction preserves this essential limitation.

4. **Light of nature shows there is a God (1689 22.1):** The Confession states directly that "The light of Nature shews that there is a God, who hath Lordship, and Soveraigntye over all." This is the most direct confessional parallel to the Catechism's affirmative claim.

The Catechism faithfully distills the Confession's multi-chapter teaching on natural and special revelation into a single catechetical answer without loss or distortion.

## Detailed Notes
- The Larger Catechism's addition of "in man" (i.e., "the light of nature *in man*") is present in both Shorter and Larger, and corresponds to the Confession's use of Romans 1:19 (that which may be known of God is manifest "in them"). This is an internal, subjective sense — not merely external natural theology. The proof text Proverbs 20:27 ("the spirit of man is the lamp of the LORD") helpfully supports this.
- The Larger's inclusion of Isaiah 46:9-10 and 41:21-29 as proof texts for "works of God" is a notable addition that goes beyond the typical natural theology proof texts. These passages present God's predictive prophecy and challenge to idols as evidence of His deity. This is creative but defensible — fulfilled prophecy is indeed a "work of God" that declares His existence.
- The Larger Catechism's pairing of "Word and Spirit" closely follows the Confession's consistent pattern of coupling the written Word with the Spirit's illumination (1689 1.5-6).

## Scripture Analysis
The Larger Catechism's proof texts are a superset of the Shorter's with several thoughtful additions:

- **Romans 1:18-19** (Larger) vs. **Romans 1:19,20** (Shorter): The Larger begins one verse earlier, capturing God's wrath against those who suppress truth — strengthening the inexcusability theme.
- **Proverbs 20:27** (Larger only): Supports the specific claim about "light of nature *in man*" — the human spirit as God's searchlight.
- **Psalm 139:14; Job 12:7,9** (Larger only): Additional witnesses from creation.
- **Psalm 9:16; Acts 14:17** (Larger only): God known through providential acts.
- **Isaiah 46:9-10; 41:21-29** (Larger only): God known through fulfilled prophecy — a less common but valid proof text choice.
- **1 Corinthians 2:9-10** (Larger) vs. **1 Corinthians 2:10** (Shorter): The Larger begins one verse earlier, capturing the "eye hath not seen" context.
- **1 Corinthians 1:20-21** (Larger only): The world by wisdom did not know God — supporting the insufficiency of natural knowledge.
- **2 Timothy 3:14-15** (Larger) vs. **2 Timothy 3:15,16** (Shorter): Nearly identical; the Larger starts one verse earlier.
- **Isaiah 59:21** (Larger only): The Spirit's permanent covenant presence with God's people — supporting the Word-and-Spirit theme.
- **Acts 17:24** (Shorter only): Present in the Shorter but not explicitly in the Larger, though Acts 17:28 covers similar Pauline natural-theology ground.

All proof text additions are appropriate and well-chosen. No misuse of Scripture is evident.
