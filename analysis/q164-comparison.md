# Q164: What are the special privileges of the invisible church?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q164 compresses the WLC's extended treatment of union and communion with Christ (WLC Q65-90) into a single answer. The core theology is sound -- union with Christ, present firstfruits, and future glorification. However, the BLC omits the Shorter Catechism's explicit mention that believers' bodies remain "united to Christ" and "rest in their graves till the resurrection," and does not clearly distinguish the intermediate state from the final state. The phrase "full redemption of their bodies" is faithful language but the compressed format risks conflating the intermediate state (souls in glory, bodies in the grave) with the resurrection state (body and soul reunited).

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

"Q. What benefits do believers receive from Christ at their death? A. The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness (Heb. 12:23), and do immediately pass into glory (2 Cor. 5:1, 6, 8; Phil. 1:23; Luke 23:43); and their bodies being still united to Christ (1 Thess. 4:14), do rest in their graves (Is. 57:2) till the resurrection (Job 19:26, 27)."

"Q. What benefits do believers receive from Christ at the resurrection? A. At the resurrection believers, being raised up in glory (1 Cor. 15:43), shall be openly acknowledged, and acquitted in the day of judgment (Mt. 25:23; Mt. 10:32), and made perfectly blessed, both in soul and body, in the full enjoyment of God (1 John 3:2; 1 Cor. 13:12) to all eternity (1 Thess. 4:17, 18)."

- Omissions:
  - The Shorter's explicit statement that believers' **bodies remain united to Christ** and **rest in their graves till the resurrection** is not clearly present in the BLC. The BLC says they "receive full redemption of their bodies" but does not articulate the intermediate state where bodies rest in the grave while souls are in glory.
  - The Shorter's teaching that believers shall be **"openly acknowledged, and acquitted in the day of judgment"** is absent from the BLC.
  - The Shorter's culminating phrase **"made perfectly blessed, both in soul and body, in the full enjoyment of God to all eternity"** is compressed to "complete holiness of their souls" without the explicit "full enjoyment of God" language.
- Structural changes: The Shorter carefully distinguishes two stages -- benefits at death (intermediate state) and benefits at resurrection (final state). The BLC compresses these into "firstfruits" vs. "full fruits following death" without the same clarity of temporal distinction.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XXVI (Of the Church), Paragraph 1; Chapter XXVII (Communion of Saints), Paragraph 1; Chapter XXXI (State of Man after Death), Paragraphs 1-3

Chapter XXVII.1: "All Saints that are united to Jesus Christ their Head, by his Spirit, and Faith; although they are not made thereby one person with him, have fellowship in his Graces, sufferings, death, resurrection, and glory."

Chapter XXXI.1: "The Bodies of Men after Death return to dust, and see corruption; but their Souls (which neither die nor sleep) having an immortal subsistence, immediately return to God who gave them: the Souls of the Righteous being then made perfect in holyness, are received into paradise where they are with Christ, and behold the face of God, in light and glory; waiting for the full Redemption of their Bodies."

- Omissions:
  - The Confession's key qualification that saints "are not made thereby one person with him" (XXVII.1) is absent. This is an important anti-mystical guard.
  - The Confession's phrase that saints have fellowship "in his Graces, sufferings, death, resurrection, and glory" includes **sufferings** and **death** -- the BLC focuses only on the positive aspects (glory, holiness) without mentioning fellowship in Christ's sufferings.
  - The Confession's description of the intermediate state -- souls "received into paradise where they are with Christ, and behold the face of God, in light and glory; **waiting for** the full Redemption of their Bodies" (XXXI.1) -- emphasizes the waiting/anticipatory character. The BLC's "enjoy the full fruits of their communion in Christ following death, whereby they receive full redemption of their bodies" could be read as collapsing the waiting into immediate reception.
- Structural changes: The Confession keeps the intermediate state (XXXI.1) and the resurrection (XXXI.2-3) as distinct temporal realities. The BLC's compressed treatment risks losing this distinction.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q65: "What special benefits do the members of the invisible church enjoy by Christ? A. The members of the invisible church by Christ enjoy union and communion with him in grace and glory."

WLC Q66: "What is that union which the elect have with Christ? A. The union which the elect have with Christ is the work of God's grace, whereby they are spiritually and mystically, yet really and inseparably, joined to Christ as their head and husband; which is done in their effectual calling."

The BLC compresses WLC Q65-Q90 (26 questions covering union, communion in grace through justification/adoption/sanctification, communion in glory in this life, at death, and at resurrection) into a single answer.

- Solidarity: **MODERATE**. The BLC captures the main themes (union, firstfruits in this life, full glory after death) but loses the WLC's careful, extended treatment that distinguishes multiple dimensions of communion.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is an area of complete Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - WLC Q66's description of union as "spiritually and mystically, yet really and inseparably" joined to Christ as "head and husband" is reduced in the BLC to "spiritually joined to Christ as their head." The "mystically, yet really and inseparably" language and the "husband" title are dropped without theological reason.
  - The WLC's extensive treatment of communion in grace (justification, adoption, sanctification -- Q69-Q78) is entirely absent. The BLC only addresses communion in glory.
- Key phrases dropped: "mystically, yet really and inseparably" (WLC Q66); "head and husband" reduced to "head"; the entire communion-in-grace dimension.

## Detailed Notes
The most significant structural issue is that BLC Q164 attempts to cover in one answer what the WLC devotes 26 questions to. The result is a compressed treatment that captures the bookends (union with Christ, final glorification) but loses the middle -- particularly the communion in grace that believers enjoy in this life through justification, adoption, and sanctification. The Shorter Catechism covers this middle ground in Q71-Q80, and the BLC may address it elsewhere, but Q164 as a standalone answer is significantly thinner than either standard.

The phrase "enjoy the firstfruits of communion in the glory that Christ fully possesses" is good theological language. The concern is with the second half -- "enjoy the full fruits of their communion in Christ following death, whereby they receive full redemption of their bodies and complete holiness of their souls" -- which could be read as conflating the intermediate and final states. The 1689 and Shorter both carefully distinguish these.

## Scripture Analysis
BLC proof texts are well-chosen: Ephesians 5:23,30 (union with Christ), Romans 5:1-2,5 and 2 Corinthians 1:22 (firstfruits), Romans 8:23 (redemption of bodies), Hebrews 12:23 (spirits of the righteous made perfect). The use of Romans 8:23 ("waiting for... the redemption of our bodies") actually supports the temporal distinction that the answer itself could express more clearly.
