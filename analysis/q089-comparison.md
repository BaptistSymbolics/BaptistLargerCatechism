# Q89: Of what particular use is the moral law to unregenerate men?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q89 is a close and faithful modernization of WLC Q96, preserving all four key elements: awakening conscience, fleeing wrath, driving to Christ, and leaving the impenitent inexcusable and under the curse. There is no direct Shorter Catechism parallel for this question. The 1689 Confession addresses the law's use primarily in the context of believers (Ch. 19.6), with only an implicit reference to others, making the WLC the primary structural source. The BLC faithfully reproduces the WLC's content with only minor stylistic changes.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not subdivide the uses of the moral law into categories (all men, unregenerate, regenerate) as the WLC and BLC do. The closest related content is the general treatment of the law's rule and the question about escaping God's wrath and curse (Q. "What doth God require of us that we may escape his wrath and curse, due to us for sin?"), but this addresses a different topic.
- Omissions: N/A — no parallel to compare against
- Structural changes: N/A

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraph 6 — "Although true Believers be not under the Law, as a Covenant of Works, to be thereby Justified or condemned; yet it is of great use to them as well as to others: in that, as a Rule of Life, informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty, it directs and binds them, to walk accordingly; discovering also the sinfull pollutions of their Natures, Hearts and Lives; so as Examining themselves thereby, they may come to further Conviction of, Humiliation for, and Hatred against Sin; together with a clearer sight of the need they have of Christ and the perfection of his Obedience..."
- Omissions: The 1689 does not have a distinct treatment of the law's particular use to unregenerate men. The phrase "as well as to others" implicitly covers the unregenerate but without elaboration. The BLC draws its content primarily from the WLC rather than the 1689 here.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's content is compatible with the 1689's theology. The 1689's emphasis on the law showing "the need they have of Christ" and the reference to Galatians 3:10 (curse of the law, cited in 19.1) align with the BLC's teaching.
- The BLC appropriately expands on the 1689's implicit reference to the law's use for the unregenerate, filling a gap where the Confession is terse.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q96: "What particular use is there of the moral law to unregenerate men? A. The moral law is of use to unregenerate men, to awaken their consciences to flee from wrath to come, and to drive them to Christ; or, upon their continuance in the estate and way of sin, to leave them inexcusable, and under the curse thereof."
- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this question involves no baptismal, ecclesiological, or covenant theology issues where Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement would be expected.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance. The only structural difference is the replacement of "or" (presenting alternative outcomes) with "However, if they continue" (a conditional clause). Both formulations convey the same dual outcome: the law either drives to Christ or leaves the impenitent under condemnation. The BLC's conditional phrasing arguably makes the logical relationship slightly more explicit for modern readers.
- Key phrases dropped: None. All four elements are preserved: (1) awakening conscience, (2) fleeing from wrath to come, (3) driving to Christ, (4) leaving inexcusable and under the curse.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC question wording ("Of what particular use is the moral law to unregenerate men?") closely mirrors the WLC ("What particular use is there of the moral law to unregenerate men?") — a straightforward modernization.
- The BLC's "coming wrath" is a modernization of the WLC's "wrath to come" — theologically identical.
- The BLC's phrasing "the moral law has left them without excuse" modernizes the WLC's "to leave them inexcusable" — equivalent.
- The BLC adds "unregenerate and sinful" where the WLC has "estate and way of sin." The addition of "unregenerate" is slightly redundant (since the question already specifies unregenerate men) but not theologically problematic; "sinful condition" is an adequate modernization of "estate and way of sin."
- The BLC's "under its curse" closely tracks the WLC's "under the curse thereof."

## Scripture Analysis
- **Psalm 51:13** — BLC addition (not in WLC). This verse ("Then will I teach transgressors thy ways; and sinners shall be converted unto thee") speaks of sinners being converted through the teaching of God's ways. While appropriate to the general theme, it more naturally fits conversion/regeneration rather than the law's pre-conversion work on the unregenerate conscience. A minor proof-text choice that could be debated but is not theologically problematic.
- **1 Timothy 1:9-11** — Matches WLC footnote 410 (WLC cites 1 Tim 1:9-10; the BLC extends to v.11). This is the classic text about the law being given for the lawless and disobedient.
- **Galatians 3:24** — Matches WLC footnote 411. The law as schoolmaster/tutor leading to Christ.
- **Romans 1:20** — Matches WLC footnote 412. The inexcusability of those who suppress the truth.
- **Romans 2:15** — Matches WLC footnote 412. The law written on the heart, conscience bearing witness.
- **Galatians 3:10** — Matches WLC footnote 413. The curse upon those under the works of the law.
- Overall, the proof texts closely follow the WLC's citations with one addition (Psalm 51:13) and a minor extension (1 Tim 1:9-11 vs 1:9-10).
