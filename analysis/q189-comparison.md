# Q189: For what do we pray in the fifth petition?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q189 closely follows WLC Q194 with strong structural fidelity and preserves the core theological content of both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession. However, it introduces a subtle shift in the final clause regarding the forgiving-others condition and slightly alters the WLC's formulation of assurance. Additionally, the BLC's rendering of the Shorter's "for Christ's sake, would freely pardon all our sins" is expanded but subtly restructured, replacing a single direct petition with a multi-part sequence that may dilute the Shorter's concise force.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What do we pray for in the fifth petition?
> A. In the fifth petition, which is, And forgive us our debts as we forgive our debtors, we pray that God, for Christ's sake, would freely pardon all our sins; which we are rather encouraged to ask because of his grace we are enabled from the heart to forgive others.

- **Omissions:** The Shorter's phrase "for Christ's sake" — a compact Christological ground — is not reproduced verbatim. The BLC replaces it with the more elaborate "of his free grace and through the obedience and satisfaction of Christ, which is grasped and applied by faith." This is a legitimate expansion (mirroring the WLC) rather than an omission, but the Shorter's phrase "freely pardon all our sins" is a single comprehensive petition that the BLC breaks into multiple sub-petitions (acquit from guilt and punishment, accept in the Beloved, continue favor, forgive daily sins, fill with assurance). This is appropriate for a Larger Catechism's role.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter's "for Christ's sake, would freely pardon all our sins" becomes a multi-element exposition. This is fitting for a Larger Catechism and mirrors the WLC approach.
- The Shorter's critical phrase "by his grace we are enabled from the heart to forgive others" is altered in the BLC. The Shorter attributes the ability to forgive others to God's grace ("by his grace we are enabled"), whereas the BLC says "when and if we are assured in ourselves that we have genuinely, from the heart, forgiven others who have wronged us." The BLC's "when and if" + "assured in ourselves" shifts from a grace-grounded enablement to a conditional self-assessment. This weakens the Shorter's explicit attribution of forgiving others to divine grace. The WLC also lacks the Shorter's explicit "by his grace" attribution here, so the BLC follows the WLC pattern — but the Shorter's formulation is the baseline the BLC should mirror.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter XI (Of Justification), Paragraph 1: "Those whom God Effectually calleth, he also freely justifieth, not by infusing Righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting, and accepting their Persons as Righteous; not for any thing wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ's sake alone, not by imputing faith it self, the act of beleiving, or any other evangelical obedience to them, as their Righteousness; but by imputing Christs active obedience unto the whole Law, and passive obedience in his death, for their whole and sole Righteousness, they receiving, and resting on him, and his Righteousness, by Faith."

Chapter XI, Paragraph 5: "God doth continue to Forgive the sins of those that are justified, and although they can never fall from the state of justication; yet they may by their sins fall under Gods Fatherly displeasure; and in that condition, they have not usually the light of his Countenance restored unto them, untill they humble themselves, confess their sins, beg pardon, and renew their faith, and repentance."

Chapter XVI (Of Good Works), Paragraph 5: "We cannot by our best works merit pardon of Sin or Eternal Life at the hand of God... whom by them we can neither profit, nor satisfie for the debt of our former sins."

Chapter XVIII (Of Assurance), Paragraphs 1-4 (on the nature, grounds, and variability of assurance).

- **Omissions:** None significant. The BLC's language about acknowledging "neither we nor any other created being can make the least satisfaction for that debt" directly echoes 1689 XVI.5. The mention of "obedience and satisfaction of Christ" reflects XI.1 and XI.3.
- **Structural changes:** None problematic. The BLC appropriately connects justification language (acquittal from guilt and punishment) with ongoing forgiveness (daily sins) and assurance, which maps well to 1689 XI.1, XI.5, and XVIII.
- The BLC's phrase "grasped and applied by faith" corresponds to the Confession's "receiving, and resting on him, and his Righteousness, by Faith" (XI.1). The word "grasped" is a reasonable modernization of "apprehended" (from WLC) which itself parallels the Confession's "receiving."
- The 1689 XI.5 teaches that continued forgiveness flows to the justified, but restoration of assurance after sin requires humbling, confession, begging pardon, and renewing faith and repentance. The BLC's mention of "daily gift of growing assurance of being forgiven" is consistent with the progressive nature of assurance in Ch. XVIII, though it lacks the 1689's nuance about assurance being shaken, diminished, and intermitted (XVIII.4). This is acceptable for a catechism question focused on prayer rather than the doctrine of assurance per se.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 194. What do we pray for in the fifth petition?
> A. In the fifth petition, (which is, Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors,) acknowledging, that we and all others are guilty both of original and actual sin, and thereby become debtors to the justice of God; and that neither we, nor any other creature, can make the least satisfaction for that debt: we pray for ourselves and others, that God of his free grace would, through the obedience and satisfaction of Christ, apprehended and applied by faith, acquit us both from the guilt and punishment of sin, accept us in his Beloved; continue his favour and grace to us, pardon our daily failings, and fill us with peace and joy, in giving us daily more and more assurance of forgiveness; which we are the rather emboldened to ask, and encouraged to expect, when we have this testimony in ourselves, that we from the heart forgive others their offenses.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC follows the WLC extremely closely in structure and content. Nearly every clause has a direct WLC counterpart.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None. This question touches no Baptist-Presbyterian distinctives (baptism, ecclesiology, covenant theology).
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **Assurance formulation:** WLC: "fill us with peace and joy, in giving us daily more and more assurance of forgiveness." BLC: "fill us with the peace and joy that come from the daily gift of growing assurance of being forgiven." The BLC's rewording is substantively equivalent but slightly restructures the relationship. The WLC presents peace and joy as resulting from God's act of *giving* assurance progressively. The BLC's "daily gift of growing assurance" is similar but turns the WLC's verbal phrase into a nominal phrase, slightly abstracting the dynamic.
  2. **Forgiving-others condition:** WLC: "which we are the rather emboldened to ask, and encouraged to expect, when we have this testimony in ourselves, that we from the heart forgive others their offenses." BLC: "We may more boldly make this request and be encouraged to expect to be forgiven, when and if we are assured in ourselves that we have genuinely, from the heart, forgiven others who have wronged us." The BLC adds "and if" to the WLC's "when," introducing a conditional element not present in the WLC. The WLC's "when we have this testimony in ourselves" treats the forgiving spirit as a testimony/evidence; the BLC's "when and if we are assured in ourselves" makes it sound more like a prerequisite condition. Additionally, the WLC's "testimony in ourselves" becomes "assured in ourselves" — a subtle shift from evidence to self-assurance.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "this testimony in ourselves" (WLC) → replaced with "assured in ourselves" (BLC). The WLC's "testimony" language connects to the Reformed doctrine of the internal testimony/witness, whereas "assured" is a different concept.
  - "their offenses" (WLC) → "who have wronged us" (BLC). Minor modernization, theologically neutral.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "when and if" addition:** This is the most notable change from the WLC. The WLC says "when we have this testimony in ourselves" — treating the forgiving disposition as something believers *do* have as evidence of grace. The BLC's "when and if" introduces doubt about whether this condition obtains, which may unintentionally weaken the pastoral encouragement the petition is meant to provide. In the WLC, the forgiving spirit is presented as an assuring mark of grace; in the BLC, it becomes more of a conditional self-examination.

2. **Loss of the Shorter's grace attribution:** The Shorter Catechism says "by his grace we are enabled from the heart to forgive others." This explicitly grounds the believer's forgiving spirit in divine grace — it is something God enables. Neither the WLC nor the BLC preserve this explicit grace-attribution. The BLC follows the WLC in omitting it, but since the Shorter is the BLC's primary standard, this is worth noting. The forgiving spirit in the BLC appears as a human act to be verified ("assured in ourselves that we have genuinely... forgiven") rather than a grace-enabled fruit.

3. **"Created being" vs. "creature":** The BLC's "created being" is a thoughtful modernization of the WLC's "creature" that avoids the modern connotation of "creature" as animal. Theologically precise.

4. **"Accepted in the One he loves":** The BLC renders the WLC's "accept us in his Beloved" as "accept us in the One he loves." This is a dynamic translation of Ephesians 1:6 language. While not wrong, "the Beloved" is a recognized theological title for Christ, and the shift to "the One he loves" slightly dilutes this titulary force. Minor point.

5. **Overall structure is faithful:** The BLC preserves the WLC's acknowledgment-petition-encouragement structure and maintains all major theological elements: universal guilt (original and actual sin), human inability to satisfy, free grace, Christ's obedience and satisfaction, faith as instrument, acquittal, acceptance in Christ, continued favor, daily forgiveness, and assurance.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts closely parallel the WLC's references:
- Romans 3:9-22 and 5:19 for universal guilt — matches WLC ref 1266
- Matthew 18:24-25 for the debt imagery — matches WLC ref 1266
- Psalm 130:3-4 and Micah 6:6-7 for inability to satisfy — matches WLC ref 1266
- Romans 3:24-26, 5:19, Hebrews 9:22, Acts 13:39 for acquittal through Christ — matches WLC ref 1267
- Ephesians 1:6-7 for acceptance in the Beloved — matches WLC ref 1268
- 2 Peter 1:2 for continued favor — matches WLC ref 1269
- The daily forgiveness verses (Hosea 14:2, Jeremiah 14:7, Psalm 143:2, Psalm 130:3) are a fresh selection not directly from the WLC, which cites Psalm 51:7,12 and Hosea 14:2. The BLC adds Jeremiah 14:7 and Psalm 143:2 — appropriate supplementation.
- Romans 15:13 and 5:1-2 for peace and joy — matches WLC ref 1271
- Luke 11:4, Matthew 6:14-15, Matthew 18:35 for the forgiving-others clause — matches WLC ref 1272

The scripture support is robust and well-chosen, with no significant gaps or questionable references.
