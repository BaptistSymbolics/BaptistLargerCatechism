# Q183: What rule has God given for our direction in prayer?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q183 combines the content of both the Shorter's Q99 (the rule of direction in prayer) and WLC Q187 (how the Lord's Prayer is to be used) into a single answer. The first two sections faithfully mirror the Shorter Catechism. The third section, drawn from WLC Q187, introduces a subtle structural change: it replaces the WLC's list of graces "necessary to the right performance of the duty of prayer" with a phrase about graces "necessary for us to pray properly," while also adding the concept that the Lord's Prayer will "promote" these graces in us—a claim not found in any of the standards.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** Q99: "Q. What rule hath God given for our direction in prayer? A. The whole word of God is of use to direct us in prayer (1 Jn. 5:14); but the special rule of direction is that prayer which Christ taught his disciples, commonly called the Lord's prayer (Mt. 6:9-13; with Lk. 11:2-4)."

- Omissions: None from the Shorter's Q99. The BLC's first two sections faithfully reproduce this content in modernized form.
- Structural changes: None for the Q99 parallel material. The BLC adds a third section drawn from WLC Q187, which goes beyond the Shorter's scope.
- The BLC's first two sections are a close and faithful modernization of the Shorter.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 22, Paragraph 3: "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance; and when with others, in a known tongue."

- The 1689 does not specifically address the Lord's Prayer as a rule of direction in prayer. This topic is handled by the catechisms rather than the Confession.
- The 1689's list of graces for acceptable prayer (understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, perseverance) is relevant to the BLC's third section. The BLC mentions "understanding, faith, reverence, and the other gifts of God in us that are necessary for us to pray properly," which partially mirrors the 1689's list but omits explicit mention of humility, fervency, love, and perseverance, replacing them with "the other gifts of God."
- Omissions: No doctrinal omission from the 1689, since the Confession does not address this specific topic.
- Structural changes: None relative to the 1689.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Questions:**
- WLC Q186: "What rule hath God given for our direction in the duty of prayer? A. The whole Word of God is of use to direct us in the duty of prayer; but the special rule of direction is that form of prayer which our Savior Christ taught his disciples, commonly called The Lord's Prayer."
- WLC Q187: "How is the Lord's Prayer to be used? A. The Lord's Prayer is not only for direction, as a pattern, according to which we are to make other prayers; but may also be used as a prayer, so that it be done with understanding, faith, reverence, and other graces necessary to the right performance of the duty of prayer."

- Solidarity: STRONG — The BLC clearly follows both WLC Q186 and Q187, combining them into a single answer.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues here).
- Unexpected divergences:
  1. The WLC calls the Lord's Prayer "that form of prayer" — the BLC calls it "that prayer." This is a minor flattening; "form of prayer" subtly emphasizes the Lord's Prayer as a structured model, while "that prayer" is more neutral. Not doctrinally significant.
  2. The WLC says the Lord's Prayer is "for direction, as a pattern, according to which we are to make other prayers." The BLC renders this as "not only direct us as a model for our other prayers." The shift from "pattern" to "model" is functionally synonymous, but "according to which we are to make other prayers" is slightly stronger directive language than "for our other prayers."
  3. The BLC adds that using the Lord's Prayer "will promote our understanding, faith, reverence, and the other gifts of God in us." The WLC says it may be used as a prayer "so that it be done with" these graces. This is a meaningful structural change: the WLC presents the graces as conditions/prerequisites for proper use of the Lord's Prayer, while the BLC presents the Lord's Prayer as an instrument that promotes these graces in us. The WLC says "do it WITH these graces"; the BLC says "it will PROMOTE these graces."
- Key phrases dropped: "necessary to the right performance of the duty of prayer" — the WLC's full phrase connecting the graces to the duty of prayer is simplified to "necessary for us to pray properly."

## Detailed Notes
1. **The third section's causal reversal**: The most notable theological shift is in the third section. The WLC's Q187 presents graces (understanding, faith, reverence, etc.) as conditions that must accompany the use of the Lord's Prayer ("so that it be done with..."). The BLC reverses the direction: the Lord's Prayer itself "will promote" these graces. While not contradictory (the Lord's Prayer may indeed cultivate such graces through regular use), this introduces a claim not found in the standards and shifts the theological emphasis from the believer's responsibility to approach prayer with proper dispositions toward the prayer's instrumental power to produce those dispositions.

2. **Combining two WLC questions**: The BLC combines WLC Q186 and Q187 into a single answer. This is a legitimate structural choice for a catechism that aims to be more concise, but it does compress two distinct theological points (the rule of direction, and how that rule is to be used) into one.

3. **Grammar issue**: The third section reads "The Lord's Prayer not only direct us" — this should be "directs" (subject-verb agreement).

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 John 5:14; 2 Timothy 3:16-17**: The BLC adds 2 Timothy 3:16-17 to support the claim that the whole Word of God directs us in prayer. The Shorter cites only 1 John 5:14. The addition of 2 Timothy 3:16-17 is appropriate and strengthens the point about Scripture's sufficiency for direction.
- **Matthew 6:9-13; Luke 11:2-4**: These are the standard proof texts for the Lord's Prayer, matching the Shorter exactly.
- **Matthew 6:9; Luke 11:2**: These are cited for the third section about using the Lord's Prayer. The WLC cites these same passages (with footnote 1200) for Q187. However, these verses simply introduce the Lord's Prayer; they do not specifically support the claim that the Lord's Prayer "will promote" graces in the one who prays it.
