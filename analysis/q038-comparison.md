# Q38: Why was it necessary that the Mediator should be God?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q38 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q38, preserving every theological element in the same order. The only textual changes are updates to archaic language: "requisite" becomes "necessary," "sinking under" becomes "collapsing under," "peculiar people" becomes "His very own people," and "His" is capitalized for divine pronouns. The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel to this question. The 1689 Confession's teaching on the necessity of Christ's divine nature for the mediatorial work is fully preserved.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel.

The Baptist Shorter Catechism (Charleston, 1813) does not contain a question specifically addressing why the Mediator must be God. The closest relevant content is:

> "Q. Who is the Redeemer of God's elect? A. The only Redeemer of God's elect is the Lord Jesus Christ (1 Tim. 2:5, 6); who, being the eternal Son of God, became man (John 1:14; Gal. 4:4), and so was and continueth to be God and man in two distinct natures, and one person for ever (Rom. 9:5; Lk. 1:35; Col. 2:9; Heb. 7:24, 25)."

This establishes Christ's deity and humanity but does not elaborate on *why* His deity was necessary — precisely the kind of doctrinal expansion a Larger Catechism is expected to provide. The BLC appropriately fills this gap by following the WLC's structure.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8 (Of Christ the Mediator), Paragraphs 1-5, 8

Key texts:

> **8.2:** "The Son of God, the second Person in the Holy Trinity, being very and eternal God, the brightness of the Fathers glory, of one substance and equal with him..."

> **8.4:** "...he was made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it, and underwent the punishment due to us, which we should have born and suffered, being made Sin and a Curse for us: enduring most grievous sorrows in his Soul; and most painful sufferings in his body..."

> **8.5:** "The Lord Jesus by his perfect obedience and sacrifice of himself, which he through the Eternal Spirit once offered up unto God, hath fully satisfied the Justice of God, procured reconciliation, and purchased an Everlasting inheritance in the Kingdom of Heaven, for all those whom the Father hath given unto him."

> **8.8:** "To all those for whom Christ hath obtained eternal redemption, he doth certainly, and effectually apply, and communicate the same; making intercession for them, uniting them to himself by his spirit... governing their hearts by his word and spirit, and overcoming all their enemies by his Almighty power, and wisdom..."

The BLC's eight reasons for the Mediator's deity map onto the Confession as follows:

| BLC Element | 1689 Reference |
|---|---|
| Sustain human nature under infinite wrath and death | 8.2 (eternal God), 8.4 (enduring wrath and death) |
| Give worth and efficacy to sufferings, obedience, intercession | 8.5 ("through the Eternal Spirit once offered up"), 8.4 (intercession) |
| Satisfy God's justice | 8.5 ("fully satisfied the Justice of God") |
| Procure God's favor | 8.5 ("procured reconciliation") |
| Purchase His very own people | 8.5 ("purchased an Everlasting inheritance... for all those whom the Father hath given") |
| Give His Spirit to them | 8.8 ("uniting them to himself by his spirit") |
| Conquer all their enemies | 8.8 ("overcoming all their enemies by his Almighty power") |
| Bring them to everlasting salvation | 8.5 ("Everlasting inheritance in the Kingdom of Heaven") |

- Omissions: None. All key doctrinal elements from the Confession are represented.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC follows the logical order of the WLC, which itself aligns with the Confession's progression from atonement (8.4-5) to application (8.8).

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q38:

> "Q. 38. Why was it requisite that the Mediator should be God? A. It was requisite that the Mediator should be God, that he might sustain and keep the human nature from sinking under the infinite wrath of God, and the power of death, give worth and efficacy to his sufferings, obedience, and intercession; and to satisfy God's justice, procure his favour, purchase a peculiar people, give his Spirit to them, conquer all their enemies, and bring them to everlasting salvation."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question addresses Christological doctrine where Baptists and Presbyterians are in full agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Structure preservation:** The BLC preserves the exact order and number of the WLC's eight reasons for the Mediator's deity. No reasons are added, removed, or rearranged.

2. **"Peculiar people" modernization:** The update from "purchase a peculiar people" to "purchase His very own people" is theologically sound. The WLC's proof text is Titus 2:13-14, where the Greek *laon periousion* (a people for His own possession) is captured well by "His very own people." The BLC retains the same proof text.

3. **Divine pronoun capitalization:** The BLC consistently capitalizes pronouns referring to God ("He," "His"), which is a stylistic convention not present in the WLC. This is a common modern evangelical convention and does not alter theological content.

4. **"Collapsing" vs. "sinking":** Both metaphors communicate the same theological reality — that without divine nature, the human nature of the Mediator could not bear the full weight of God's infinite wrath. "Collapsing" may even strengthen the image slightly, conveying sudden structural failure rather than gradual descent.

The BLC generally expands the WLC's proof texts with additional supporting references (e.g., adding Rom. 1:4 and Rom. 4:25 to the first section; adding Matt. 3:17 to the favor section; expanding John references for the Spirit section). These additions are all theologically appropriate and strengthen the scriptural case. The BLC also specifies Luke 1:68-74 as a range where the WLC cited individual verses (68-69, 71, 74), which is a minor presentation choice covering the same passage. The addition of Heb. 9:11-15 to the final section enriches the connection between Christ's high priestly work and eternal salvation.
