# Q121: What are the particular sins of equals?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q121 faithfully modernizes WLC Q132 with near-identical structure and content. The Shorter Catechism treats the fifth commandment only generally (duties and sins for all relations combined), so Q121 functions as an expansion rather than a parallel. The BLC preserves every element of the WLC's answer, rephrases "usurping pre-eminence" as "trying to lord it over them," and adds supplementary proof texts — all without theological loss or distortion.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is forbidden in the fifth commandment?
> A. The fifth commandment forbiddeth the neglect of, or doing any thing against the honour and duty which belongeth to every one in their several places and relations (Mt. 15:4-6; Ez. 34:24; Rom. 13:8).

The Shorter Catechism does not break the fifth commandment into separate questions for superiors, inferiors, and equals. It treats all relational sins under a single question. The BLC's Q121 is therefore an expansion of the Shorter's general principle as it applies specifically to equals.

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's general principle — forbidding "the neglect of, or doing any thing against the honour and duty which belongeth to every one in their several places and relations" — is preserved in the BLC's opening clause ("In addition to failing to do what is required of them"), which mirrors the WLC's "besides the neglect of the duties required." The BLC then specifies the sins of equals in particular, which the Shorter leaves implicit.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC expands the Shorter's general treatment rather than rearranging it.
- The Shorter cites Romans 13:8 for the general fifth-commandment sins; the BLC retains this same verse for its opening clause, maintaining continuity.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God)

The 1689 Confession does not treat the individual commandments in detail. Chapter 19 establishes the moral law's perpetual binding authority:

> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation." (19.5)

The Confession establishes the framework (the moral law binds all believers) but leaves the detailed exposition of individual commandments to the catechisms. The BLC's treatment of the sins of equals is consistent with the Confession's understanding that the moral law, including the fifth commandment, continues to govern Christian conduct.

- Omissions: Not applicable — the Confession does not treat specific fifth-commandment applications.
- Structural changes: None.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 132. What are the sins of equals?
> A. The sins of equals are, besides the neglect of the duties required, the undervaluing of the worth, envying the gifts, grieving at the advancement of prosperity one of another; and usurping pre-eminence one over another.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question concerns the moral law's application to interpersonal relationships, an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every element of the WLC answer is preserved in the BLC:

| WLC Phrase | BLC Phrase | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| "besides the neglect of the duties required" | "In addition to failing to do what is required of them" | Faithful modernization |
| "the undervaluing of the worth" | "undervaluing the worth of each other" | Faithful modernization with clarifying addition |
| "envying the gifts" | "envying their gifts" | Faithful modernization |
| "grieving at the advancement of prosperity one of another" | "grieving over their success or prosperity" | Faithful modernization; "success" added as synonym for "advancement" |
| "usurping pre-eminence one over another" | "trying to lord it over them" | Faithful modernization using biblical language (cf. 1 Peter 5:3; Matt. 20:25) |

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's rephrasing of "usurping pre-eminence" as "trying to lord it over them" is a particularly effective modernization. The WLC's "usurping pre-eminence" may be opaque to modern readers, while "lord it over" is immediately understood and echoes Christ's own language in Matthew 20:25-27 and Luke 22:24-26 (both cited as proof texts).
- The BLC adds "of each other" to "undervaluing the worth," making the relational dimension explicit. This is a helpful clarification, not a change in meaning.
- The BLC's substitution of "success or prosperity" for "advancement of prosperity" is a minor stylistic choice. The WLC's "advancement" could mean either increase in prosperity or promotion in station; the BLC's "success or prosperity" covers both senses.
- The opening clause structure is identical: both the WLC and BLC begin by referencing the corresponding duties question (WLC Q131 / BLC Q120) before listing specific sins. This preserves the WLC's pedagogical pattern of pairing duties with sins.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts largely overlap with the WLC's, with some additions:

**Opening clause — "In addition to failing to do what is required of them":**
- Romans 13:8 — Shared with WLC (footnote 713) and with the Shorter Catechism. Continuity across all three standards.

**"undervaluing the worth of each other":**
- 2 Timothy 3:3 — Shared with WLC (footnote 714). "Despisers of those that are good."
- Proverbs 14:21 — BLC addition. "He that despiseth his neighbour sinneth." An apt and appropriate addition that strengthens the biblical support.
- Isaiah 65:5 — BLC addition. "Stand by thyself, come not near to me; for I am holier than thou." Illustrates the sin of considering oneself above equals. A reasonable addition.

**"envying their gifts":**
- Acts 7:9 — Shared with WLC (footnote 715). The patriarchs' envy of Joseph.
- Galatians 5:26 — Shared with WLC (footnote 715). "Envying one another."

**"grieving over their success or prosperity":**
- Numbers 12:2 — Shared with WLC (footnote 716). Miriam and Aaron grieving over Moses' prominence.
- Esther 6:12-13 — Shared with WLC (footnote 716). Haman grieving at Mordecai's advancement.
- 1 John 3:12 — BLC addition. Cain's hostility toward Abel. Appropriate — illustrates grieving at a brother's acceptance by God.
- Matthew 20:15 — BLC addition. "Is thine eye evil, because I am good?" The parable of the laborers — directly addresses envy at another's prosperity.
- Luke 15:28-29 — BLC addition. The elder brother's anger at the prodigal's reception. An excellent illustration of grieving at a peer's restored prosperity.

**"trying to lord it over them":**
- 3 John 9 — Shared with WLC (footnote 717). Diotrephes "who loveth to have the preeminence."
- Luke 22:24-26 — Shared with WLC (footnote 717, which cites Luke 22:24). The BLC extends the citation to include Christ's instruction, not just the sin.
- Matthew 20:25-27 — BLC addition. Christ's teaching that rulers "lord it over" others, but "it shall not be so among you." This is the direct biblical source for the BLC's rephrasing and an excellent addition.

All proof texts are appropriate and well-chosen. The BLC's additions (Proverbs 14:21, Isaiah 65:5, 1 John 3:12, Matthew 20:15, Luke 15:28-29, Matthew 20:25-27) enrich the biblical support without introducing any strained or problematic citations.
