# Q163: What is the invisible church?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q163 teaches the core doctrine of the invisible church correctly but makes two significant changes from the standards. First, it replaces the temporal phrase "that have been, are, or shall be" (found in both the 1689 Confession and WLC) with "in all ages and places." This substitution loses the eschatological precision of the originals, which emphasize the church as spanning past, present, and future elect — a formulation that underscores divine sovereignty over the not-yet-gathered. Second, and more seriously, the BLC omits the Confession's identification of the church as "the spouse, the body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all" and the qualifying clause "with respect to the internal work of the Spirit, and truth of grace" — key doctrinal content that explains *why* this church may be called invisible and identifies its intimate, organic relationship to Christ.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not contain a question defining the invisible church.

The closest related material is the Shorter Catechism's teaching on the duty of the baptized:

> Q. What is the duty of such who are rightly baptized?
> A. It is the duty of such who are rightly baptized to give up themselves to some particular and orderly church of Jesus Christ, that they may walk in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless (Acts 2:41, 42; 5:13, 14; 9:26; 1 Pet. 2:5; Lk. 1:6).

And the implicit teaching that the means of grace are "made effectual to the elect for salvation" (in the question on outward means of grace). The Shorter focuses on the visible/local church and does not explicitly define the invisible church, so the BLC is here expanding beyond the Shorter's scope.

- **Omissions:** No direct parallel to evaluate omissions. The Shorter implicitly teaches the visible/invisible distinction through its references to "the elect" in the context of the means of grace.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC follows the WLC's structure in adding an explicit question on the invisible church that the Shorter does not contain.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

**Chapter 26, Paragraph 1:** "The Catholick or universal Church, which (with respect to the internal work of the Spirit, and truth of grace) may be called invisible, consists of the whole number of the Elect, that have been, are, or shall be gathered into one, under Christ the head thereof; and is the spouse, the body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all."

- **Omissions:**
  1. **"The Catholick or universal Church"** — The BLC drops the term "catholic" or "universal." While "invisible church" is an acceptable shorthand, the Confession deliberately uses "catholic or universal" as the primary designation and treats "invisible" as a qualifying descriptor. This is not trivial: the Confession's phrasing emphasizes the church's universality across all times and places, with invisibility being an aspect of how it is perceived, not its primary identity.
  2. **"With respect to the internal work of the Spirit, and truth of grace"** — This critical qualifying clause explains *why* this church may be called invisible: because its membership is determined by the Spirit's internal work and the truth of grace, which are not visible to human observation. The BLC omits this entirely. This is a significant loss of theological precision. Without it, "invisible" becomes a bare label rather than a theologically grounded description.
  3. **"And is the spouse, the body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all"** — The Confession identifies the invisible church not merely as a collection of the elect but as Christ's *spouse*, his *body*, and *the fulness of him that filleth all in all* (drawing on Ephesians 1:22-23 and 5:23-32). These are rich ecclesiological titles that express the church's intimate, organic, covenantal union with Christ. The BLC reduces the church to its membership ("consists of the whole number of the elect... gathered into one under Christ, the head") without these relational descriptors.
  4. **"That have been, are, or shall be"** — Replaced with "in all ages and places." See structural changes below.

- **Structural changes:** The Confession's temporal phrase "that have been, are, or shall be gathered into one" is replaced by the BLC's spatial-temporal phrase "in all ages and places, gathered into one." The original formulation is more precise theologically: it specifies that the invisible church includes the *already gathered* (past saints in glory), the *presently being gathered* (living believers), and the *yet to be gathered* (the future elect not yet born or converted). The BLC's "in all ages and places" captures breadth but loses the eschatological dimension — particularly the forward-looking "shall be" which affirms that Christ has elect who are not yet gathered but certainly will be.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q64: What is the invisible church?
> A. The invisible church is the whole number of the elect, that have been, are, or shall be gathered into one under Christ the head.

- **Solidarity:** MODERATE
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected here. The doctrine of the invisible church is an area of complete Baptist-Presbyterian agreement. The 1689 Confession's wording at 26.1 is virtually identical to the Westminster Confession 25.1 (with minor wording differences but the same doctrine).
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"That have been, are, or shall be" changed to "in all ages and places":** This is an unnecessary departure from both the WLC and the 1689 in an area of full agreement. The WLC's temporal tripartite formula ("have been, are, or shall be") is theologically richer — it emphasizes the certainty of future gathering (God's decree ensuring future elect *shall be* gathered). "In all ages and places" is a spatial-geographical phrase that, while true, lacks the same eschatological force.
  2. **"The invisible church is" changed to "The invisible church consists of":** A minor wording change. The WLC's "is" identifies the invisible church with the elect, while "consists of" describes its composition. The theological difference is negligible.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  1. "That have been, are, or shall be" — replaced with "in all ages and places," losing the explicit affirmation that future elect *shall be* gathered
  2. The WLC's concise precision is largely preserved, but the unnecessary rewording weakens the resonance with both standards

## Detailed Notes

### The "In All Ages and Places" Substitution
The BLC's substitution of "in all ages and places" for "that have been, are, or shall be" is the most distinctive change from the WLC. Both phrases aim to express the universality and transtemporality of the invisible church, but they do so differently:

- **"Have been, are, or shall be gathered"** — emphasizes the *action* of God in gathering his elect across time, with the progressive tenses underscoring that this is an ongoing divine work. "Shall be" expresses confident certainty about the future.
- **"In all ages and places"** — emphasizes the *scope* of the elect's existence across geography and history. It is descriptive rather than active. It does not express the same confidence about future gathering.

The WLC/1689 formulation is standard Reformed language found across confessional documents. Departing from it without theological reason raises the question of why the change was made. If modernization was the goal, "who have been, are being, or will be gathered" would preserve the theology while updating the English.

### Missing Ecclesiological Depth from the 1689
The most concerning omission is the Confession's description of the church as "the spouse, the body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all." This is not decorative language — it expresses the church's Christological identity. The invisible church is not merely a collection of individually elected persons; it is the bride of Christ (Ephesians 5:23-32), his body (Ephesians 1:22-23), and his fulness (Ephesians 1:23). These titles distinguish a Reformed doctrine of the church from mere individualism. The BLC retains "Christ, the head" but drops the corresponding body/bride/fulness language that gives the headship its ecclesiological meaning.

### Missing Explanatory Clause
The Confession's "with respect to the internal work of the Spirit, and truth of grace" explains the theological basis for the visible/invisible distinction. The church is invisible not because it is hidden or ethereal, but because its true membership is determined by realities invisible to human eyes — the Spirit's regenerating work and the truth of grace. Without this clause, students of the catechism may misunderstand "invisible" as meaning the church is imperceptible or merely spiritual, rather than understanding it as a description of how membership is determined.

## Scripture Analysis
**BLC proof texts:** Ephesians 1:10,22-23; John 10:16; John 11:52; Ephesians 5:23-32; Revelation 7:9; Isaiah 59:21

**1689 Confession proof texts for 26.1:** Heb. 12:23; Col. 1:18; Eph. 1:10,22-23; Eph. 5:23,27,32

- **Ephesians 1:10,22-23** — Excellent. Christ as head over all things to the church, which is his body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all. Notably, this text directly supports the Confession's language about the church being "the body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all" — language that the BLC *cites as a proof text* but *omits from the answer*. This is a significant internal inconsistency: the BLC appeals to Ephesians 1:22-23 as scriptural support but does not include the very doctrine that text teaches.
- **John 10:16** — "Other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring." Excellent proof text for the "shall be gathered" aspect of the invisible church, supporting the very temporal formulation the BLC omitted.
- **John 11:52** — "And not for that nation only, but that also he should gather together in one the children of God that were scattered abroad." Supports universal gathering.
- **Ephesians 5:23-32** — Christ and the church as bridegroom and bride. Again, the BLC cites this text but omits the bride/spouse language from its answer.
- **Revelation 7:9** — The great multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language. Supports the "all ages and places" language. This is a good addition not found in the 1689's proof texts.
- **Isaiah 59:21** — God's covenant promise to his people and their offspring. Supports the perpetuity of the church across generations.

The proof text selection is strong and well-chosen. However, it creates a notable tension: the BLC cites texts (Ephesians 1:22-23; Ephesians 5:23-32) that directly support doctrinal content present in the 1689 Confession but absent from the BLC's own answer.
