# Q12: What are the personal properties of the three persons of the Godhead?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q12 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q10, restating the personal properties of the three Trinitarian persons with identical theological content. The repositioning of "from all eternity" from the end to the beginning of the answer is a stylistic improvement that makes the temporal qualifier explicitly modify all three properties rather than appearing to attach primarily to the procession. The 1689 Confession's additional statement about the Father's aseity ("the Father is of none neither begotten nor proceeding") is not included, but neither catechism (WLC or Shorter) includes this negative definition, so no omission from the standards occurs.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The nearest Shorter question is:
> Q. How many persons are there in the Godhead?
> A. There are three persons in the godhead, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; and these three are one God, the same in essence, equal in power and glory (1 John 5:7; Mt. 28:19).

The Shorter Catechism does not have a separate question on personal properties. BLC Q12 expands on the Shorter's teaching by specifying what distinguishes the three persons — the eternal relations of origin — drawing this material from the WLC and 1689 Confession.

- Omissions: None (no Shorter baseline to omit from)
- Structural changes: None (this is supplementary material)

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 2, Paragraph 3:
> "In this divine and infinite Being there are three subsistences, the Father the Word (or Son) and Holy Spirit, of one substance, power, and Eternity, each having the whole Divine Essence, yet the Essence undivided, the Father is of none neither begotten nor proceeding, the Son is Eternally begotten of the Father, the holy Spirit proceeding from the Father and the Son, all infinite, without beginning, therefore but one God, who is not to be divided in nature and Being; but distinguished by several peculiar, relative properties, and personal relations; which doctrine of the Trinity is the foundation of all our Communion with God, and comfortable dependance on him."

- Omissions: The 1689 states "the Father is of none neither begotten nor proceeding" — a negative definition of the Father's personal property (aseity/innascibility). The BLC states only the positive property ("it is proper to the Father to beget the Son"). However, the WLC likewise omits this negative definition, so the BLC follows established catechetical practice. The positive statement ("to beget the Son") implicitly distinguishes the Father as the unbegotten source.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC accurately reflects the 1689's teaching on eternal generation and eternal procession.
- The 1689's broader context — unity of essence, the doctrine of the Trinity as the foundation of communion with God — is addressed by surrounding BLC questions rather than Q12 itself.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q10:
> Q. 10. What are the personal properties of the three persons in the Godhead?
> A. It is proper to the Father to beget the Son, and to the Son to be begotten of the Father, and to the Holy Ghost to proceed from the Father and the Son from all eternity.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is core Trinitarian doctrine with no Baptist-Presbyterian difference)
- Unexpected divergences: None
- Key phrases dropped: None. All substantive content is preserved.
- Structural note: The BLC moves "from all eternity" from the end of the WLC answer to the beginning. In the WLC, the phrase grammatically attaches to the procession clause but is understood to modify all three properties. The BLC's repositioning makes this universal application explicit — a clarifying improvement rather than a theological alteration.

## Detailed Notes
- The question wording is nearly identical: BLC uses "of the Godhead" vs. WLC's "in the Godhead" — a negligible variation.
- The BLC divides the answer into three sections with individual proof texts, whereas the WLC presents the answer as a single flowing statement. This structural division aids catechetical instruction without altering content.
- The filioque ("from the Father and the Son") regarding the Spirit's procession is faithfully maintained, consistent with Western Trinitarian orthodoxy and the 1689's explicit teaching.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Hebrews 1:5-8** (Father begets Son): The WLC cites Heb 1:5-6, 8. The BLC's citation of 1:5-8 is slightly broader but covers the same material — the Father's declaration of the Son's divine sonship.
- **John 1:14-18** (Son begotten): Matches the WLC's citation of John 1:14, 18. The BLC's range captures the intervening verses as well. These verses teach the Son as "the only begotten of the Father."
- **John 17:3** (Son begotten): Added by the BLC beyond the WLC's citations. This verse ("the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent") speaks to the Son's distinct personal relation to the Father. A reasonable supplementary text, though its primary emphasis is on the mission of the Son rather than eternal generation per se.
- **John 15:26** (Spirit proceeds): Identical to WLC. The classic Western proof text for the procession: "the Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father."
- **Galatians 4:6** (Spirit proceeds): Identical to WLC. "God has sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts" — supporting the double procession (from the Father and the Son).
