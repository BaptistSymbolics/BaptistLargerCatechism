# Q130: What is required in the eighth commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q130 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q141, closely tracking its structure, order, and content point by point. It preserves all the Shorter Catechism's core elements. However, the BLC's final clause — "to acquire, preserve, and increase our own and others' money and possessions" — narrows the WLC's broader "wealth and outward estate" to the more specific "money and possessions," and reverses the WLC's other-first ordering ("others, as well as our own") to a self-first ordering ("our own and others'"). Additionally, the BLC omits the WLC's word "further" (replacing with "increase"), which subtly shifts from actively promoting others' prosperity to merely growing assets. These are minor concerns that slightly weaken the other-regarding emphasis of the original.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is required in the eighth commandment?
> A. The eighth commandment requireth the lawful procuring and furthering the wealth and outward estate of ourselves and others (Gen. 30:30; 1 Tim. 5:8; Lev. 25:35; Deut. 22:1, 2, 3, 4, 5; Ex. 23:4, 5; Gen. 47:14, 20).

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter's compact formula — "the lawful procuring and furthering the wealth and outward estate of ourselves and others" — is fully expanded in the BLC's detailed enumeration. Every key concept from the Shorter is present in the BLC: lawfulness ("just and lawful means"), procuring ("acquire"), furthering ("preserve, and increase"), wealth and outward estate ("money and possessions"), and the dual obligation to self and others ("our own and others'").
- Structural changes: The Shorter presents a single comprehensive principle; the BLC unpacks it into twelve specific duties. This is appropriate expansion for a Larger Catechism. However, the BLC's final summary clause uses "money and possessions" where the Shorter has "wealth and outward estate." The Shorter's language is broader — "outward estate" encompasses one's entire material condition (livelihood, reputation as it affects economic standing, property, income, social station) — while "money and possessions" is more narrowly focused on tangible assets. This is a slight narrowing of scope.
- The Shorter says "ourselves and others" (self-first); the BLC's final clause says "our own and others'" (also self-first). This matches the Shorter's ordering, though both differ from the WLC's other-first phrasing.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God), Paragraphs 5-6; Chapter 27 (Of the Communion of Saints), Paragraph 1:

Chapter 19.5-6:
> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation."

Chapter 27.1:
> "...nevertheless their communion one with another as Saints, doth not take away or infringe, the title or propriety, which each man hath in his goods and possessions."

- Omissions: The 1689 does not provide a detailed exposition of eighth commandment duties (that is the catechism's role), so there is no specific confessional content to omit. The BLC's content is consistent with the 1689's affirmation of the perpetual binding force of the moral law (Ch. 19.5) and its explicit affirmation of private property rights (Ch. 27.1).
- Structural changes: None. The BLC appropriately expands on the confessional framework by enumerating specific duties under the eighth commandment.
- The 1689's Ch. 27.1 language about property — "title or propriety, which each man hath in his goods and possessions" — is reflected in the BLC's emphasis on lawful acquisition, maintenance, and disposition of goods, as well as the duty of restitution to "rightful owners."
- The BLC's teaching on giving and lending freely (section 3) and relieving others' needs coheres with the 1689 Ch. 27.1's teaching that saints exercise communion "in outward things according to their several abilities, and necessities."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q141: What are the duties required in the eighth commandment?
> A. The duties required in the eighth commandment are, truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and commerce between man and man; rendering to everyone his due; restitution of goods unlawfully detained from the right owners thereof; giving and lending freely, according to our abilities, and the necessities of others; moderation of our judgments, wills, and affections concerning worldly goods; a provident care and study to get, keep, use, and dispose these things which are necessary and convenient for the sustentation of our nature, and suitable to our condition; a lawful calling, and diligence in it; frugality; avoiding unnecessary lawsuits, and suretiship, or other like engagements; and an endeavor, by all just and lawful means, to procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others, as well as our own.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC's structure almost exactly, preserving the same order of duties, the same logical progression, and nearly identical content at each point. This is one of the closest BLC-to-WLC correspondences in the catechism.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. The eighth commandment is not an area of Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement.
- Unexpected divergences: Three minor points:
  1. **"wealth and outward estate" → "money and possessions"**: The WLC's "wealth and outward estate" is a broader, more encompassing phrase. "Outward estate" in Reformed usage includes one's entire material and social condition, not merely monetary assets. The BLC's "money and possessions" is more concrete but narrower. This may simply be a modernization choice, but it risks losing the breadth of the original concept.
  2. **"others, as well as our own" → "our own and others'"**: The WLC deliberately places others first in the closing clause, emphasizing the other-regarding dimension of eighth commandment obedience. The BLC reverses this to self-first ordering. While both the Shorter and the WLC include both self and others, the WLC's rhetorical emphasis on others is theologically intentional — it counters the natural tendency to prioritize self-enrichment, which is precisely what the eighth commandment guards against. The BLC loses this rhetorical force.
  3. **"further" → "increase"**: The WLC says "procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others." "Further" means to actively promote and advance another's prosperity, which is relationally richer than "increase," which focuses on quantitative growth.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"commerce"**: The WLC says "contracts and commerce between man and man"; the BLC says "contractual and business relationships." "Business relationships" covers the concept, though "commerce" is more precise in referring to trade and exchange.
  - **"provident care and study"**: The WLC speaks of "a provident care and study to get..." The BLC says "we must exercise prudence in the acquisition." The WLC's "provident care and study" is richer — it implies both foresight (providence) and diligent mental engagement (study), while "exercise prudence" is a more generic virtue term.
  - **"convenient for the sustentation of our nature"**: The WLC says things "necessary and convenient for the sustentation of our nature." The BLC says "the things that we need and are appropriate to sustain us humanly." "Convenient" in 17th-century usage means "fitting" or "suitable" — the BLC's "appropriate" captures this well. "Sustain us humanly" is a reasonable modernization of "sustentation of our nature."

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's section-by-section breakdown maps almost perfectly onto the WLC's single flowing sentence:
  - BLC §1 ↔ WLC "truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and commerce...rendering to everyone his due"
  - BLC §2 ↔ WLC "restitution of goods unlawfully detained from the right owners thereof"
  - BLC §3 ↔ WLC "giving and lending freely, according to our abilities, and the necessities of others"
  - BLC §4 ↔ WLC "moderation of our judgments, wills, and affections concerning worldly goods"
  - BLC §5-6 ↔ WLC "a provident care and study to get, keep, use, and dispose these things..."
  - BLC §7 ↔ WLC "a lawful calling"
  - BLC §8 ↔ WLC "and diligence in it"
  - BLC §9 ↔ WLC "frugality"
  - BLC §10 ↔ WLC "avoiding unnecessary lawsuits"
  - BLC §11 ↔ WLC "and suretiship, or other like engagements"
  - BLC §12 ↔ WLC "and an endeavor, by all just and lawful means, to procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others, as well as our own"
- The BLC adds "Micah 6:8" and "Romans 13:7" to the proof texts for the first section, beyond the WLC's citations. These are excellent additions — Micah 6:8 ("do justly, love mercy, walk humbly") and Romans 13:7 ("render to all their dues") directly support the stated duties.
- The BLC adds "Romans 12:5-8" to the lawful calling section, emphasizing the diversity of gifts and callings within the body of Christ — a fitting expansion.
- The BLC's final section adds "Philippians 2:4" ("look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others") and "Matthew 22:39" ("thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself"), which helpfully ground the duty in the second great commandment and the Christological ethic of Philippians 2. However, the addition of these other-regarding texts makes the reversal of the WLC's other-first ordering all the more noticeable.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts substantially overlap with the WLC's, sharing the core references at nearly every point: Psalm 15:2-4, Zechariah 7:4,10, Zechariah 8:16-17, Leviticus 6:2-5, Luke 19:8, Luke 6:30,38, Ephesians 4:28, Galatians 6:10, 1 Timothy 6:6-9, Galatians 6:14, 1 Timothy 5:8, Proverbs 27:23-27, Ecclesiastes 2:24, Ecclesiastes 3:12-13, 1 Timothy 6:17-18, Isaiah 38:1, Matthew 11:8, 1 Corinthians 7:20, Genesis 2:15, Genesis 3:19, John 6:12, Proverbs 21:20, 1 Corinthians 6:1-9, Proverbs 6:1-6, Proverbs 11:15, Leviticus 25:35, Deuteronomy 22:1-4, Exodus 23:4-5, Genesis 47:14,20.
- Notable BLC additions: Micah 6:8, Romans 13:7, Deuteronomy 15:7-10, Proverbs 10:4, Romans 12:11, Proverbs 12:27, Philippians 2:4, Matthew 22:39, Romans 12:5-8. All are theologically appropriate and strengthen the scriptural foundation.
- The WLC's footnote 803 includes 1 John 3:17 ("whoso hath this world's good, and seeth his brother have need, and shutteth up his bowels of compassion from him, how dwelleth the love of God in him?") which the BLC does not cite. This is a notable omission from the proof texts, as it powerfully grounds the duty of generosity in the indwelling love of God — though the duty itself is still clearly taught.
- The BLC's proof texts for the final section are particularly strong, combining OT case law (Leviticus 25:35, Deuteronomy 22:1-4, Exodus 23:4-5) with NT ethical teaching (Philippians 2:4, Matthew 22:39), effectively demonstrating the continuity of the moral law across the Testaments.
