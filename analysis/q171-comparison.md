# Q171: How must we receive the Lord's Supper when it's offered?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q171 closely follows the structure and content of WLC Q174 (the "during the time of administration" question) and preserves most of its substance faithfully. However, it critically omits two doctrinal elements present in the Shorter Catechism's parallel: **knowledge to discern the Lord's body** (as an object of self-examination) and **new obedience** as a required qualification. It also drops the Shorter's solemn warning that coming unworthily results in eating and drinking judgment. Since the BLC collapses the Shorter's single question on worthy receiving into this one answer (with no separate preparation question), these omissions represent lost doctrinal content, not merely content deferred to another question. The phrase "stir up in us a lively effect of God's spiritual gifts" is also an unclear departure from the WLC's "vigorous exercise of their graces."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> Q. What is required to the worthy receiving of the Lord's supper?
> A. It is required of them that would worthily partake of the Lord's supper, that they examine themselves of their knowledge to discern the Lord's body (1 Cor. 11:28, 29), of their faith to feed upon him (2 Cor. 13:5), of their repentance (1 Cor. 11:31), love (1 Cor. 10:16, 17), and new obedience (1 Cor. 5:7, 8), lest coming unworthily they eat and drink judgment to themselves (1 Cor. 11:28, 29).

The Shorter Catechism identifies **five things** that must be examined for worthy receiving: (1) knowledge to discern the Lord's body, (2) faith to feed upon him, (3) repentance, (4) love, and (5) new obedience. It concludes with a warning about the consequence of unworthy participation.

**BLC Q171 coverage of the Shorter's five elements:**
1. **Knowledge to discern the Lord's body** — The BLC says "take specific notice of the Lord's body" (citing 1 Cor 11:29), which captures the *discerning* element but not the self-examination of one's *knowledge* to do so. The Shorter's point is that one must examine whether one has sufficient knowledge; the BLC's point is merely to do the discerning itself.
2. **Faith to feed upon him** — Present: "feeding on him by faith" (John 6:35; Gal 2:20). Faithfully preserved.
3. **Repentance** — Present in two parts: "critically examine ourselves" (1 Cor 11:31) and "be sorry for our sins" (Zech 12:10). Faithfully expanded.
4. **Love** — Present: "our love for fellow believers" (Acts 2:42; 1 Cor 10:17). Faithfully preserved.
5. **New obedience** — **OMITTED.** The Shorter Catechism explicitly requires self-examination regarding new obedience (citing 1 Cor 5:7-8: "let us keep the feast... with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth"). This is entirely absent from BLC Q171. This is a significant omission because new obedience is the practical fruit of repentance — the Shorter includes both the sorrow for sin and the turn toward obedience, while the BLC includes only the sorrow.

- **Omissions:**
  1. **"New obedience"** — A complete omission of one of the Shorter's five required qualifications for worthy receiving.
  2. **The warning clause** — "Lest coming unworthily they eat and drink judgment to themselves" is entirely absent. The Shorter includes this as a solemn motivating warning; the BLC drops it.
  3. **Self-examination of knowledge** — The Shorter says one must examine oneself *of their knowledge* to discern the Lord's body. The BLC addresses discerning the body but not the examination of whether one's knowledge is sufficient for this.

- **Structural changes:** The Shorter structures the answer around self-examination ("examine themselves of..."), making self-examination the organizing framework for the entire answer. The BLC restructures: self-examination appears as one item among many ("we should critically examine ourselves"), rather than as the framework encompassing all the others. In the Shorter, faith, repentance, love, and new obedience are all *objects of self-examination*; in the BLC, they are separate activities performed during communion alongside self-examination.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 30, Paragraphs 7-8

> (30.7) "Worthy receivers, outwardly partaking of the visible Elements in this Ordinance, do then also inwardly by faith, really and indeed, yet not carnally, and corporally, but spiritually receive, and feed upon Christ crucified & all the benefits of his death: the Body and Blood of Christ, being then not corporally, or carnally, but spiritually present to the faith of Believers, in that Ordinance, as the Elements themselves are to their outward senses."

> (30.8) "All ignorant and ungodly persons, as they are unfit to enjoy communion with Christ; so are they unworthy of the Lords Table; and cannot without great sin against him, while they remain such, partake of these holy mysteries, or be admitted thereunto: yea whosoever shall receive unworthily are guilty of the Body and Blood of the Lord, eating and drinking judgement to themselves."

Also relevant, Chapter 30, Paragraph 1:

> "The Supper of the Lord Jesus, was instituted by him... for the perpetual remembrance, and shewing forth the sacrifice of himself in his death, confirmation of the faith of believers in all the benefits thereof, their spiritual nourishment, and growth in him, their further ingagement in, and to, all duties which they owe unto him; and to be a bond and pledge of their communion with him, and with each other."

- **Agreement:** The BLC faithfully captures 30.7's theology of spiritual feeding by faith ("feeding on him by faith," "drawing from his fullness"). The BLC's language about meditating on Christ's death and suffering aligns with 30.1's "perpetual remembrance, and shewing forth the sacrifice of himself in his death." The BLC's final section on renewing covenant and love for believers aligns with 30.1's "bond and pledge of their communion with him, and with each other."

- **Omissions:**
  1. **"Engagement in, and to, all duties which they owe unto him"** — The 1689 (30.1) explicitly identifies one purpose of the Supper as furthering the believer's engagement in all duties owed to Christ. This parallels the Shorter's "new obedience" and is absent from the BLC.
  2. **The warning of 30.8** — That unworthy receivers are "guilty of the Body and Blood of the Lord, eating and drinking judgement to themselves." While BLC Q170 may address the exclusion of the unworthy, the absence of any warning in Q171 about the consequences of unworthy participation means the individual communicant receives no admonition about the gravity of what they are doing.
  3. **"Spiritually present to the faith of Believers"** — The 1689's careful formulation that Christ's body and blood are "spiritually present to the faith of Believers, in that Ordinance, as the Elements themselves are to their outward senses" establishes a real spiritual presence. The BLC's language is weaker, speaking of "feeding on him by faith" without the confession's emphasis on real spiritual presence.

- **Structural changes:** The 1689 structures the doctrine around the concept of *worthy receivers* — the category of person determines the nature of their participation. The BLC structures around a series of activities to perform, losing the 1689's emphasis on the *kind of person* who rightly receives.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q174

> Q. 174. What is required of them that receive the sacrament of the Lord's supper in the time of the administration of it?
> A. It is required of them that receive the sacrament of the Lord's supper, that, during the time of the administration of it, with all holy reverence and attention they wait upon God in that ordinance, diligently observe the sacramental elements and actions, heedfully discern the Lord's body, and affectionately meditate on his death and sufferings, and thereby stir up themselves to a vigorous exercise of their graces; in judging themselves, and sorrowing for sin; in earnest hungering and thirsting after Christ, feeding on him by faith, receiving of his fullness, trusting in his merits, rejoicing in his love, giving thanks for his grace; in renewing of their covenant with God, and love to all the saints.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC follows WLC Q174 almost clause-by-clause. The order of topics, the specific activities enumerated, and the flow from observation to meditation to active graces to covenant renewal are all closely preserved. This is one of the closest BLC-to-WLC parallels in the catechism.

- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None. This question does not touch baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology in ways requiring Baptist modification. Both traditions agree on what the communicant should do during the Lord's Supper.

- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"Stir up themselves to a vigorous exercise of their graces" → "stir up in us a lively effect of God's spiritual gifts"** — This is the most problematic divergence. The WLC speaks of the communicant actively stirring themselves to exercise *their graces* (faith, repentance, love, etc. — the subjective graces already implanted by the Spirit). The BLC speaks of stirring up "a lively effect of God's spiritual gifts," which shifts from active self-exertion of graces to a more passive expectation of effects from spiritual gifts. The WLC's language is about the believer's active duty; the BLC's language is more ambiguous and could suggest passivity. "Spiritual gifts" is also a different concept from "graces" — gifts (charismata) and graces (internal spiritual dispositions) are theologically distinct.
  2. **"Heedfully discern" → "take specific notice of"** — The WLC's "heedfully discern" carries a stronger sense of spiritual perception and understanding. "Take specific notice of" is weaker, suggesting mere attention rather than spiritual discernment.

- **Key phrases dropped:**
  1. **"During the time of the administration of it"** — The WLC is precise that this is about what happens *during* the administration. The BLC's "when it's offered" is vaguer and could encompass preparation as well as reception.
  2. **"Heedfully"** — Dropped in favor of "specific notice," losing the sense of careful spiritual attention.
  3. **"Vigorous exercise of their graces"** — Replaced with the unclear "lively effect of God's spiritual gifts" (see above).
  4. **"Judging themselves"** — The WLC says "judging themselves," which carries the weight of 1 Corinthians 11:31 ("if we would judge ourselves, we should not be judged"). The BLC says "critically examine ourselves," which is adequate but slightly weaker than "judge."

## Detailed Notes
1. **The BLC collapses two WLC questions into one.** The WLC separates preparation before communion (Q171) from duties during communion (Q174). The BLC has no separate preparation question — Q171 is titled "How must we receive the Lord's Supper when it's offered?" and covers both preparation and reception duties. This means the BLC must carry the full weight of both questions in one answer. This makes the omissions of "new obedience" and the judgment warning more significant: they cannot be found in a neighboring BLC question on preparation.

2. **"Lively effect of God's spiritual gifts"** is not only theologically imprecise but introduces potential confusion. In Reformed theology, "graces" (faith, hope, love, repentance, etc.) are distinguished from "spiritual gifts" (prophecy, teaching, healing, etc.). The WLC is speaking of graces; the BLC's substitution of "spiritual gifts" could be read as referring to charismatic gifts, which is not the WLC's intent at all.

3. **Philippians 1:16 as a proof text for "trusting in his merit"** appears to be an error. Philippians 1:16 (KJV: "The one preach Christ of contention, not sincerely, supposing to add affliction to my bonds") has no relevance to trusting in Christ's merit. The intended reference is almost certainly **Philippians 3:9** ("and be found in him, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith"), which is also cited. The BLC actually cites both Philippians 1:16 and Philippians 3:9 together, but Philippians 1:16 appears erroneous and should likely be removed or corrected.

4. **The BLC's "meditate feelingly on his death and suffering"** is a good modernization of the WLC's "affectionately meditate on his death and sufferings." The word "feelingly" captures the WLC's emphasis that this is not cold intellectual reflection but emotionally engaged meditation.

5. **The covenant renewal language** is well preserved: "We thereby renew our covenant with God and our love for fellow believers" faithfully modernizes "in renewing of their covenant with God, and love to all the saints."

6. **Psalm 36:4-5 for "rejoicing in his love"** — The BLC cites Psalm 36:4-5, but Psalm 36:4 (KJV: "He deviseth mischief upon his bed; he setteth himself in a way that is not good; he abhorreth not evil") is about the wicked, not about rejoicing in God's love. Psalm 36:7-8 ("How excellent is thy lovingkindness, O God!") would be the appropriate reference. This appears to be a verse reference error.

## Scripture Analysis
**Proof text alignment with WLC Q174 footnotes:**
The BLC's proof texts largely correspond to the WLC's footnoted references. Notable observations:

- **Leviticus 10:3; Hebrews 12:28; Psalm 5:7; 1 Corinthians 11:17, 26-27** (for reverent waiting) — These match WLC footnote 1112 exactly.
- **Exodus 24:8; Matthew 26:28** (for observing elements) — Match WLC footnote 1113. The BLC adds Galatians 3:1 ("before whose eyes Jesus Christ hath been evidently set forth, crucified among you"), which is an interesting and appropriate addition.
- **1 Corinthians 11:29** (for discerning the Lord's body) — Matches WLC footnote 1114.
- **Luke 22:19** (for meditating on his death) — Matches WLC footnote 1115.
- **1 Corinthians 11:26; 10:3-5, 11, 14** (for stirring up graces) — Partially matches WLC footnote 1116. The BLC includes 1 Corinthians 10:3-5, 11, 14, which are about Israel's spiritual food and drink and the warning against idolatry — an unusual choice for a proof text about stirring up graces/gifts.
- **Philippians 1:16** (for trusting in his merit) — Almost certainly erroneous (see Detailed Notes #3). WLC footnote 1122 cites Philippians 3:9, which the BLC also includes.
- **Psalm 36:4-5** (for rejoicing in his love) — Likely erroneous (see Detailed Notes #6). The WLC footnotes for this clause cite 1 Peter 1:8 and other texts.
- **Psalm 22:26** (for giving thanks for his grace) — The BLC cites only this one text. WLC footnote 1124 cites Psalm 103:1-4, which is more directly about thanksgiving for grace. Psalm 22:26 ("The meek shall eat and be satisfied") is relevant to communion but less directly about thanksgiving.
- **Jeremiah 50:5; Psalm 50:5** (for covenant renewal) — Match WLC footnote 1125.
- **Acts 2:42; 1 Corinthians 10:17** (for love to fellow believers) — Match WLC footnote 1126.

**Erroneous references:** Two proof text citations appear to be errors: Philippians 1:16 and Psalm 36:4-5. These should be verified and corrected.
