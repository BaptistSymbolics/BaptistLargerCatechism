# Q57: What benefits has Christ procured by His mediation?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q57 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q57, preserving both its structure and theological content essentially unchanged. The question identifies Christ's mediation as the ground of redemption and all covenant of grace benefits. While the Shorter Catechism has no standalone parallel question, the BLC's theology aligns with the Shorter's implicit treatment and the 1689 Confession's teaching on Christ the Mediator.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter moves from Christ's threefold office (Q on prophet/priest/king) and humiliation/exaltation directly to application:

> "Q. How are we made partakers of the redemption purchased by Christ?
> A. We are made partakers of the redemption purchased by Christ, by the effectual application of it to us by his Holy Spirit."

The Shorter presupposes that Christ has "purchased" redemption but does not dedicate a separate question to enumerating what He procured. The BLC follows the WLC in making this an explicit transitional question before moving to application.

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel to omit from)
- Structural changes: The BLC, following the WLC structure, inserts an explicit question about the content of Christ's procurement before addressing its application. This is an expansion beyond the Shorter, not a contradiction.

The Shorter's phrase "redemption purchased by Christ" is theologically consonant with the BLC's "redemption... procured by His mediation." Both locate the ground of redemption in Christ's mediatorial work.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8 (Of Christ the Mediator), especially paragraphs 5 and 8:

> **8.5:** "The Lord Jesus by his perfect obedience and sacrifice of himself, which he through the Eternal Spirit once offered up unto God, hath fully satisfied the Justice of God, procured reconciliation, and purchased an Everlasting inheritance in the Kingdom of Heaven, for all those whom the Father hath given unto him."

> **8.8:** "To all those for whom Christ hath obtained eternal redemption, he doth certainly, and effectually apply, and communicate the same; making intercession for them, uniting them to himself by his spirit, revealing unto them, in and by the word, the mystery of salvation; perswading them to believe, and obey; governing their hearts by his word and spirit, and overcoming all their enemies by his Almighty power, and wisdom; in such manner, and wayes as are most consonant to his wonderful, and unsearchable dispensation; and all of free, and absolute Grace, without any condition foreseen in them, to procure it."

Also relevant, Chapter 7, Paragraph 2 (Of Gods Covenant):

> "Moreover Man having brought himself under the curse of the Law by his fall, it pleased the Lord to make a Covenant of Grace wherein he freely offereth unto Sinners, Life and Salvation by Jesus Christ, requiring of them Faith in him, that they may be saved; and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal Life, his holy Spirit, to make them willing, and able to believe."

- Omissions: None of concern. The BLC is a summary question; the 1689's detailed enumeration of what Christ procured (satisfaction of justice, reconciliation, everlasting inheritance) and how He applies it are properly developed in surrounding BLC questions on Christ's offices, humiliation, exaltation, and application.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's structure of identifying redemption and "all other benefits of the covenant of grace" as procured by Christ's mediation faithfully summarizes 8.5's teaching that Christ's mediatorial work procured reconciliation and inheritance, and 8.8's teaching that eternal redemption is obtained and then applied.

The 1689's emphasis that these benefits flow to "all those whom the Father hath given unto him" (the particular redemption emphasis) is not explicit in Q57 but is properly the subject of application questions that follow.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q57:

> "Q. 57. What benefits hath Christ procured by his mediation?
> A. Christ, by his mediation, hath procured redemption, with all other benefits of the covenant of grace."

WLC Proof Texts: Hebrews 9:12; 2 Corinthians 1:20.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology distinctives that would require Baptist departure.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The BLC answer is a direct modernization of the WLC answer.
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every substantive element is preserved:
  - "Christ, by his mediation" -> "Christ, by His mediation"
  - "hath procured redemption" -> "has procured redemption"
  - "with all other benefits of the covenant of grace" -> "with all the other benefits of the covenant of grace"

The only difference is the addition of the article "the" before "other," which is stylistic and does not alter meaning. The BLC proof texts overlap significantly with the WLC's (Hebrews 9:12; 2 Corinthians 1:20) while adding additional supporting references.

## Detailed Notes
This is one of the simplest questions to evaluate because the BLC reproduces the WLC answer almost verbatim in modernized English. The question serves as a theological hinge between Christ's work (offices, humiliation, exaltation) and the application of redemption that follows. Its brevity is intentional: it summarizes the comprehensive scope of Christ's procurement ("redemption... with all the other benefits") before subsequent questions unpack how those benefits are applied.

The term "covenant of grace" anchors the answer in Reformed covenant theology, consistent with both the 1689 and WLC frameworks. The 1689's chapter 7 establishes this covenant, and chapter 8 details Christ as its Mediator who procures its benefits. The BLC faithfully reflects this theological architecture.

## Scripture Analysis
**BLC Proof Texts:**
- **1 Timothy 2:5-6** — Christ as the one mediator; supports the mediatorial framing
- **Hebrews 9:12** — Christ obtaining eternal redemption by His own blood (also WLC proof text 247)
- **Ephesians 1:7** — Redemption through His blood, forgiveness of trespasses
- **2 Corinthians 1:20** — All promises of God are Yes in Christ (also WLC proof text 248)
- **Ephesians 1:3-6** — Every spiritual blessing in Christ; election and adoption
- **2 Peter 1:3-4** — Divine power granting all things pertaining to life and godliness; precious promises

The BLC expands the proof text base beyond the WLC's two references while preserving both original WLC texts. The additions are well-chosen: 1 Timothy 2:5-6 grounds the mediatorial language, Ephesians 1:7 specifies redemption, and the remaining texts support the comprehensive scope of covenant benefits. All proof texts are contextually appropriate.
