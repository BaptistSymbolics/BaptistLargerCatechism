# Q39: Why was it necessary that the Mediator should be man?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q39 is a near-verbatim reproduction of WLC Q39, with only the minimal modernization of "requisite" to "necessary." All six reasons for the necessity of the Mediator's humanity are preserved in identical order and substance. The theology aligns with the 1689 Confession's teaching on the incarnation and mediatorial work of Christ, and while the Shorter Catechism lacks a direct parallel question, the BLC is consistent with the Shorter's broader Christological teaching.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism does not ask a separate question about *why* the Mediator should be man. The closest questions address the person of Christ more broadly:

Q. "Who is the Redeemer of God's elect?"
A. "The only Redeemer of God's elect is the Lord Jesus Christ (1 Tim. 2:5, 6); who, being the eternal Son of God, became man (John 1:14; Gal. 4:4), and so was and continueth to be God and man in two distinct natures, and one person for ever (Rom. 9:5; Lk. 1:35; Col. 2:9; Heb. 7:24, 25)."

Q. "How did Christ, being the Son of God become man?"
A. "Christ the Son of God became man by taking to himself a true body (Heb. 2:14, 17; 10:5), and a reasonable soul (Mt. 26:38); being conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit in the womb of the Virgin Mary, and born of her (Luke 1:27, 31, 34, 35, 42; Gal. 4:4), yet without sin (Heb. 4:15; 7:26)."

- Omissions: N/A — no direct parallel exists, so the BLC is expanding beyond the Shorter's scope, which is the expected function of a larger catechism.
- Structural changes: N/A

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8 (Of Christ the Mediator), Paragraphs 1–4

Paragraph 2: "The Son of God, the second Person in the Holy Trinity, being very and eternal God... did when the fullness of time was come take unto him mans nature, with all the Essential properties, and common infirmities thereof, yet without sin... So that two whole, perfect, and distinct natures, were inseparably joined together in one Person... which Person is very God, and very Man; yet one Christ, the only Mediator between God and Man."

Paragraph 4: "This office the Lord Jesus did most willingly undertake, which that he might discharge he was made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it, and underwent the punishment due to us... was crucified, and died... on the third day he arose from the dead... ascended into heaven: and there sitteth at the right hand of his Father, making intercession; and shall return to judge Men and Angels."

- Omissions: None. The BLC's six reasons map to the Confession's teaching:
  1. "advance our nature" — aligns with taking "mans nature, with all the Essential properties, and common infirmities thereof" (8.2)
  2. "perform obedience to the Law" — aligns with "was made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it" (8.4)
  3. "suffer and make intercession for us in our nature" — aligns with suffering in body/soul and "making intercession" (8.4)
  4. "fellow feeling of our infirmities" — aligns with taking man's nature "with all the... common infirmities thereof" (8.2)
  5. "adoption of sons" — consistent with the purpose of redemption (8.1, 8.5)
  6. "comfort and access with boldness unto the throne of grace" — consistent with Christ's intercession and mediatorial office (8.4, 8.8)
- Structural changes: None. The BLC organizes the Confession's diffuse Christological teaching into a focused catechetical answer about the necessity of the Mediator's humanity.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q39: "Why was it requisite that the Mediator should be man?"
A. "It was requisite that the Mediator should be man, that he might advance our nature, perform obedience to the law, suffer and make intercession for us in our nature, have a fellow-feeling of our infirmities; that we might receive the adoption of sons, and have comfort and access with boldness unto the throne of grace."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC answer is virtually identical to the WLC. The only change is modernizing "requisite" to "necessary" in both the question and answer. Every clause, every reason, and the ordering of reasons is preserved exactly.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this is a question of Christology, not ecclesiology, baptism, or covenant theology.
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None. All WLC phrases are retained verbatim.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's proof texts are drawn from the same pool as the WLC's. The WLC references (footnotes 152–157) correspond to: Heb. 2:16 (advance our nature), Gal. 4:4 (obedience to the law), Heb. 2:14 and 7:24–25 (suffer and intercede in our nature), Heb. 4:15 (fellow-feeling of infirmities), Gal. 4:5 (adoption of sons), and Heb. 4:16 (comfort and access). The BLC adds 2 Pet. 1:4 to the first section ("advance our nature"), adds Matt. 5:17, Rom. 5:19, and Phil. 2:8 to the obedience section, enriching the proof text base.
- This question forms part of the Q38–Q40 triad (why God? why man? why God-and-man-in-one-person?) that systematically exposits the necessity of the theanthropic person of the Mediator. The BLC faithfully preserves this WLC structure.
- The six reasons divide into two groups: the first four concern what Christ does *for us* in his humanity (advances nature, obeys law, suffers/intercedes, sympathizes), and the last two concern what *we receive* through his humanity (adoption, access). This logical structure is identical to the WLC.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and appropriate:
- **Heb. 2:16; 2 Pet. 1:4** — "advance our nature": Heb. 2:16 ("he took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham") directly supports Christ's assumption of human nature. 2 Pet. 1:4 ("partakers of the divine nature") is a fitting addition, pointing to the eschatological advancement of human nature through the incarnation.
- **Gal. 4:4; Matt. 5:17; Rom. 5:19; Phil. 2:8** — "perform obedience to the Law": This is a stronger proof text set than a minimalist approach. Gal. 4:4 (born under the law), Matt. 5:17 (fulfill the law), Rom. 5:19 (obedience of the one), and Phil. 2:8 (obedient unto death) together cover both active and passive obedience.
- **Heb. 2:14; Heb. 7:24–25** — "suffer and make intercession for us in our nature": Heb. 2:14 (partaker of flesh and blood to destroy death) and Heb. 7:24–25 (ever living to make intercession) are standard and appropriate.
- **Heb. 4:15** — "fellow feeling of our infirmities": The locus classicus for Christ's sympathetic high priesthood.
- **Gal. 4:5** — "adoption of sons": Directly states the purpose of Christ being born under the law — "that we might receive the adoption of sons."
- **Heb. 4:16** — "comfort and access with boldness unto the throne of grace": Flows naturally from Heb. 4:15, completing the exhortation grounded in Christ's sympathetic humanity.
