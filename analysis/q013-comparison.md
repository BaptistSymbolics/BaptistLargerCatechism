# Q13: How may we know that the Son and the Holy Ghost are God, equal with the Father?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q13 is a near-verbatim reproduction of WLC Q11, preserving the same four-fold argument structure (names, attributes, works, worship) for the deity and equality of the Son and Holy Spirit. The Baptist Shorter Catechism has no separate question on this topic, making the WLC the primary comparison point. The BLC faithfully mirrors the WLC's answer while expanding the proof texts with additional relevant Scripture references, including a correction of what appears to be a long-recognized oddity in the WLC proof texts (2 Cor 8:14 replaced by the more fitting 2 Cor 13:14 for worship).

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question asking *how* we may know the Son and Spirit are God equal with the Father. The closest is:

> Q. How many persons are there in the Godhead?
> A. There are three persons in the godhead, the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit; and these three are one God, the same in essence, equal in power and glory (1 John 5:7; Mt. 28:19).

- Omissions: None. The Shorter simply affirms the Trinity without elaborating the *evidence* for the deity and equality of Son and Spirit. The BLC properly expands on this by providing the four-fold scriptural argument.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC Q13 functions as the natural Larger Catechism expansion of the Shorter's compact trinitarian affirmation, providing the apologetic basis for what the Shorter states as fact.
- The Shorter's phrase "the same in essence, equal in power and glory" is the doctrinal claim; BLC Q13 provides the scriptural evidence supporting that claim. This is a proper Larger-to-Shorter relationship.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 2, Paragraph 3:

> "In this divine and infinite Being there are three subsistences, the Father the Word (or Son) and Holy Spirit, of one substance, power, and Eternity, each having the whole Divine Essence, yet the Essence undivided, the Father is of none neither begotten nor proceeding, the Son is Eternally begotten of the Father, the holy Spirit proceeding from the Father and the Son, all infinite, without beginning, therefore but one God, who is not to be divided in nature and Being; but distinguished by several peculiar, relative properties, and personal relations; which doctrine of the Trinity is the foundation of all our Communion with God, and comfortable dependance on him."

- Omissions: None of concern. The 1689 does not itself use the four-fold argument structure (names, attributes, works, worship), so there is nothing of that form to omit. The Confession makes the doctrinal affirmation; the BLC provides the catechetical proof.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's fourfold proof (names, attributes, works, worship ascribed to Son and Spirit that are proper to God only) is fully consistent with and supportive of the 1689's affirmation that Father, Son, and Spirit are "of one substance, power, and Eternity, each having the whole Divine Essence."
- The 1689's language of "three subsistences" with "the whole Divine Essence" and being "of one substance, power, and Eternity" is the doctrinal framework that the BLC's scriptural argument supports.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q11:

> Q. 11. How doth it appear that the Son and the Holy Ghost are God equal with the Father?
> A. The Scriptures manifest that the Son and the Holy Ghost are God equal with the Father, ascribing unto them such names, attributes, works, and worship, as are proper to God only.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**. The BLC answer is virtually identical to the WLC answer, with only minor modernizing changes in the question wording ("How may we know" vs. "How doth it appear"). The answer's theological content is word-for-word the same.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is pure Trinitarian theology with no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology implications.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The BLC preserves the WLC answer in full.
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every key phrase is retained: "The Scriptures manifest," "God equal with the Father," "ascribing unto them such names, attributes, works, and worship, as are proper to God only."

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC question changes "How doth it appear" to "How may we know," which is a natural modernization that preserves the epistemological intent. Both ask about the basis for knowing the deity and equality of Son and Spirit.
- The BLC structures the answer into discrete sections, each with its own proof texts, which is a helpful pedagogical improvement over the WLC's single paragraph with footnoted proof texts.
- The final section ("as are proper to God only") has no proof texts, which is appropriate since it is a concluding clause rather than a fifth category of evidence.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts show both continuity with and expansion beyond the WLC:

**Names (WLC note 39):**
- Shared: Isaiah 6:3, 5, 8; John 12:41; Acts 28:25; 1 John 5:20; Acts 5:3-4
- BLC additions: Jeremiah 23:5-6 (the LORD Our Righteousness — a divine name for the Messiah); Psalm 45:6-7 (the Son addressed as God). Both are excellent additions strengthening the argument from divine names.

**Attributes (WLC note 40):**
- Shared: John 1:1; Isaiah 9:6; John 2:24-25; 1 Corinthians 2:10-11
- The proof texts are identical. No additions or omissions.

**Works (WLC note 41):**
- Shared: Colossians 1:16; Genesis 1:2
- BLC addition: Mark 2:1-10 (Jesus forgives sins — a divine prerogative). This is a strong addition demonstrating the Son's exercise of divine works.

**Worship (WLC note 42):**
- Shared: Matthew 28:19
- BLC replaces 2 Corinthians 8:14 with 2 Corinthians 13:14 (the Trinitarian benediction). This is a clear improvement: the WLC's citation of 2 Cor 8:14 (about financial equality) is widely regarded as a puzzling or erroneous proof text for Trinitarian worship, while 2 Cor 13:14 ("The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Ghost, be with you all") is a standard and fitting proof for Trinitarian worship.
- BLC addition: Revelation 22:3 (the throne of God and the Lamb, worship directed to both). Another strong addition.
