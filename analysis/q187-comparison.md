# Q187: For what do we pray in the third petition?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q187 closely follows WLC Q192 in structure, content, and proof texts. It faithfully preserves the Shorter Catechism's core teaching on the third petition. However, one notable divergence from the WLC is the replacement of "diligence" with "steadfastness" in the list of virtues characterizing angelic obedience, which alters the nuance slightly. The BLC also adds "all humans" and "completely" to strengthen the depravity language, and adds several proof texts beyond the WLC, which are appropriate expansions. The addition of "and the directions of Satan" is a welcome expansion of the WLC's briefer "the will of the flesh, and of the devil."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What do we pray for in the third petition?
> A. In the third petition, which is, Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven (Mt. 6:10), we pray that God by his grace would make us able and willing to know, obey, and submit to his will in all things (Ps. 67: throughout; Ps. 119:36; 2 Sam. 15:25; Job 1:21), as the angels do in heaven (Ps. 103:20, 21).

- **Omissions:** None. The BLC fully preserves the Shorter's core petition ("that God would by his grace make us able and willing to know, obey, and submit to his will in all things... as the angels do in heaven"). The BLC modernizes "obey" to "do" but preserves the essential meaning.
- **Structural changes:** None of significance. The BLC expands the Shorter's concise answer with extensive treatment of human depravity (the "acknowledging" portion from the WLC) and the list of angelic virtues, all of which are legitimate expansions rather than restructurings.
- **Key finding:** The Shorter says "able and willing to know, obey, and submit to his will in all things." The BLC says "willing to know, do, and submit to his will in every circumstance." The BLC changes "able and willing" to just "willing" in that specific clause, though "able" is implied by the earlier prayer to "remove from us... all spiritual blindness, weakness, indisposition... and perverseness of heart." Still, the Shorter explicitly pairs ability and willingness as twin gifts of grace in the positive petition. The BLC partially separates these: the removal of blindness/weakness/indisposition addresses the ability side, while "make us willing" addresses the willingness side. This is a structural redistribution rather than an omission, since both concepts are present.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter VI, Paragraph 4:
> "From this original corruption, whereby we are utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all good, and wholly inclined to all evil, do proceed all actual transgressions."

Chapter IX, Paragraph 3 (Of Free Will):
> "Man by his fall into a state of sin hath wholly lost all ability of Will, to any spiritual good accompanying salvation; so as a natural man, being altogether averse from that good, and dead in Sin, is not able, by his own strength, to convert himself; or to prepare himself thereunto."

Chapter IX, Paragraph 4:
> "When God converts a sinner, and translates him into the state of Grace he freeth him from his natural bondage under sin, and by his grace alone, enables him freely to will, and to do that which is spiritually good."

Chapter XIX, Paragraph 7:
> "Neither are the forementioned uses of the Law contrary to the Grace of the Gospel; but do sweetly comply with it; the Spirit of Christ subduing and inabling the Will of man, to do that freely and chearfully, which the will of God revealed in the Law, requireth to be done."

Chapter XXII, Paragraph 3 (Of Prayer):
> "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance."

- **Omissions:** None of substance. The BLC's depravity language ("completely incapable of and unwilling to know and to do the will of God... actively prone to rebel... naturally inclined to follow our own selfish desires") faithfully reflects 1689 VI.4 ("utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all good, and wholly inclined to all evil") and IX.3 ("wholly lost all ability of Will, to any spiritual good").
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC's movement from human inability to the prayer for divine enablement mirrors the 1689's movement from natural bondage (IX.3) to gracious enablement (IX.4).
- **Key finding:** The 1689 XIX.7 specifically says the Spirit enables the will of man "to do that freely and chearfully" which God's will requires — the BLC includes "cheerfulness" in its list of virtues, showing alignment with this confessional emphasis. The BLC's prayer to remove "spiritual blindness" aligns well with 1689 X.1's language about God "inlightning their minds, spiritually."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q192:
> In the third petition, (which is, Thy will be done in earth as it is in heaven,) acknowledging, that by nature we and all men are not only utterly unable and unwilling to know and do the will of God, but prone to rebel against his Word, to repine and murmur against his providence, and wholly inclined to do the will of the flesh, and of the devil: we pray, that God would by his Spirit take away from ourselves and others all blindness, weakness, indisposedness, and perverseness of heart; and by his grace make us able and willing to know, do, and submit to his will in all things, with the like humility, cheerfulness, faithfulness, diligence, zeal, sincerity, and constancy, as the angels do in heaven.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology content.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"diligence" replaced with "steadfastness"**: The WLC lists "diligence" (footnote 1248, citing Psalm 119:4-5) while the BLC replaces this with "steadfastness" (citing Psalm 119:4-5 with the same proof text). The proof text ("Thou hast commanded us to keep thy precepts diligently") arguably supports "diligence" more naturally than "steadfastness," though "steadfastness" is not theologically problematic. This is a curious departure from the WLC without obvious theological motivation.
  2. **"sincerity" proof text expanded**: The BLC adds 2 Corinthians 1:12 alongside Psalm 119:80, strengthening the scriptural support.
  3. **"constancy" proof text expanded**: The BLC adds Romans 2:7 alongside Psalm 119:112.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  1. **"utterly unable"** → BLC has "completely incapable of" — this is a fair modernization, not a weakening.
  2. **"repine and murmur against his providence"** → BLC has "to be unhappy with and complain about his providence" — fair modernization.
  3. **"wholly inclined to do the will of the flesh, and of the devil"** → BLC has "naturally inclined to follow our own selfish desires and the directions of Satan" — the BLC's "selfish desires" is slightly less precise than "the will of the flesh" (which carries the Pauline technical sense of sarx), but this is a reasonable modernization.
  4. **"diligence"** → replaced with "steadfastness" as noted above.
  5. **"take away from ourselves and others"** → BLC has "remove from us and others" — equivalent.
  6. **"able and willing"** in the positive petition → BLC has only "willing" (though ability is addressed in the removal prayer). See Shorter Catechism comparison above for analysis.

## Detailed Notes
1. **BLC adds "all humans"**: Where the WLC says "we and all men," the BLC says "we and all humans." This is a modernization of "men" to "humans" — appropriate and standard.
2. **BLC adds explicit Scripture references**: The BLC provides additional proof texts not in the WLC, particularly Romans 8:5, 8 for inability, and Matthew 20:11-12 and Psalm 73:3 for murmuring. These are appropriate and strengthen the scriptural foundation.
3. **"indisposedness" → "indisposition to spiritual activities"**: The BLC helpfully clarifies the archaic WLC term by specifying that the indisposition is toward spiritual activities, making the meaning more accessible while preserving the concept.
4. **Structural addition of Ezekiel 11:19**: Added alongside Jeremiah 31:18-19 for "perverseness of heart." This is a strong addition — Ezekiel 11:19 (the heart of stone/flesh passage) is directly relevant to the prayer for God to change the heart.
5. **"in every circumstance" vs. "in all things"**: A modernization that preserves the universal scope.
6. **The "diligence" → "steadfastness" swap**: This is the most notable divergence. The WLC's "diligence" (Gk. concept of earnest effort and careful attention) emphasizes the active pursuit of obedience, while "steadfastness" emphasizes enduring perseverance. Both are biblical virtues, but they carry different emphases. Since the list already includes "constancy" (which overlaps with "steadfastness"), the replacement creates slight redundancy while losing the distinct emphasis on diligent effort.
7. **1 Samuel 3:18 added**: Added as a proof text for submission to God's will — an excellent addition showing Samuel's submission to God's revealed word.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and largely mirror the WLC's citations with helpful additions:
- **Romans 7:18; Romans 8:5, 8**: Expand the WLC's Romans 7:18 with additional Pauline texts on the flesh's inability.
- **Job 21:14; 1 Corinthians 2:14**: Retained from WLC.
- **Romans 8:7**: Retained from WLC for rebellion against God's Word.
- **Exodus 17:7; Numbers 14:2**: Retained from WLC. BLC adds **Matthew 20:11-12** (laborers in the vineyard complaining) and **Psalm 73:3** (envying the wicked) — both appropriate additions for murmuring/complaining.
- **Ephesians 2:2-3; Titus 3:3**: The BLC adds Titus 3:3 alongside Ephesians 2:2-3, strengthening the inclination toward the flesh/Satan.
- **Psalm 119:4-5**: Retained from WLC but now assigned to "steadfastness" rather than "diligence" — the text ("Thou hast commanded us to keep thy precepts diligently") arguably better supports "diligence."
- **Daniel 7:10**: Added to the angelic proof texts alongside Isaiah 6:2-3, Psalm 103:20-22, and Matthew 18:10. Daniel 7:10 ("thousand thousands ministered unto him") is an apt addition.
- **Psalm 123:2; Psalm 131:2**: Added for "humility" — excellent additions showing the posture of servants looking to their master and childlike trust.

Overall, the proof text work is strong and largely enhances the WLC's foundation. The only questionable assignment is Psalm 119:4-5 for "steadfastness" rather than "diligence."
