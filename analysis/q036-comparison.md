# Q36: Who is the Redeemer of God's elect?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q36 successfully combines the Shorter Catechism's Redeemer language with the WLC's Mediator language, creating a comprehensive answer that substantially preserves the theology of all three standards. The Christological content—eternal Sonship, incarnation, hypostatic union, and perpetuity of the person—is faithfully rendered. However, the BLC uses "complete" where both the WLC and 1689 use "entire," and substitutes "within" for the traditional "in" when describing the relationship of two natures to one person, which slightly alters the Chalcedonian precision. The phrase "two entire distinct natures" is a precise theological formula; changing "entire" to "complete" is defensible but unnecessary.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Who is the Redeemer of God's elect?
> A. The only Redeemer of God's elect is the Lord Jesus Christ (1 Tim. 2:5, 6); who, being the eternal Son of God, became man (John 1:14; Gal. 4:4), and so was and continueth to be God and man in two distinct natures, and one person for ever (Rom. 9:5; Lk. 1:35; Col. 2:9; Heb. 7:24, 25).

- **Omissions:** None of substance. The Shorter does not include "of one substance and equal with the Father" or "in the fullness of time"—these are expansions from the WLC/1689, not omissions from the Shorter.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC restructures the Shorter's single flowing answer into four sectioned clauses. This is a presentational change that does not alter theological relationships.
- **Agreement:** The BLC preserves the Shorter's key elements: (1) "only Redeemer of God's elect," (2) "the Lord Jesus Christ," (3) "eternal Son of God," (4) "became man," (5) "God and man in two distinct natures, and one person for ever."
- **Additions:** The BLC adds "(and the only Mediator of the Covenant of Grace)" from the WLC tradition, and "of one substance and equal with the Father" and "in the fullness of time" from the WLC/1689. These are legitimate expansions for a Larger Catechism.
- **Wording change:** The Shorter says "in two distinct natures" while the BLC says "two complete and distinct natures." The Shorter omits "entire/complete" which the WLC and 1689 both include as "entire"—so the BLC is right to expand here, though "complete" rather than "entire" is a vocabulary substitution.
- **Wording change:** The BLC says "within one person" where the Shorter says "one person" (no preposition needed as the Shorter integrates it grammatically). The BLC's "within" is a subtle shift from the traditional "in" used by the WLC.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8, Paragraph 2:
> "The Son of God, the second Person in the Holy Trinity, being very and eternal God, the brightness of the Fathers glory, of one substance and equal with him: who made the World, who upholdeth and governeth all things he hath made: did when the fullness of time was come take unto him mans nature, with all the Essential properties, and common infirmities thereof, yet without sin: being conceived by the Holy Spirit in the Womb of the Virgin Mary... So that two whole, perfect, and distinct natures, were inseparably joined together in one Person: without conversion, composition, or confusion: which Person is very God, and very Man; yet one Christ, the only Mediator between God and Man."

- **Omissions:**
  - "the brightness of the Fathers glory" — omitted, but acceptable for catechetical compression.
  - "who made the World, who upholdeth and governeth all things he hath made" — omitted; these describe the Son's divine prerogatives. Acceptable for a catechism vs. confession.
  - "with all the Essential properties, and common infirmities thereof, yet without sin" — the "yet without sin" is a significant phrase, though it may be addressed in a subsequent BLC question (as the WLC handles it in Q37).
  - "being conceived by the Holy Spirit in the Womb of the Virgin Mary" — virgin conception omitted here, likely addressed in a subsequent question (parallel to WLC Q37).
  - "without conversion, composition, or confusion" — the Chalcedonian negative qualifications are omitted. This is a notable loss of precision, though the positive affirmation of "two complete and distinct natures within one person" implicitly excludes these errors.
  - "inseparably joined together" — omitted; the 1689 explicitly states the natures are "inseparably" joined. The BLC's "within one person, forever" partly covers this with "forever" implying permanence, but "inseparable" is a specific Chalcedonian term addressing Nestorian separation.
  - "two whole, perfect, and distinct" — the 1689 uses three adjectives; the BLC reduces to "two complete and distinct," combining "whole" and "perfect" into "complete."
- **Structural changes:** None of theological significance. The BLC follows the same logical order: identity → divine nature → incarnation → hypostatic union → perpetuity.
- **Agreement:** The core Christological affirmation is preserved: the Son is eternal God, of one substance and equal with the Father, became man in the fullness of time, two natures in one person forever.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 36. Who is the Mediator of the covenant of grace?
> A. The only Mediator of the covenant of grace is the Lord Jesus Christ, who, being the eternal Son of God, of one substance and equal with the Father, in the fulness of time became man, and so was and continues to be God and man, in two entire distinct natures, and one person, forever.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** The BLC changes the question from "Who is the Mediator of the covenant of grace?" to "Who is the Redeemer of God's elect?"—incorporating the Shorter Catechism's question while adding "(and the only Mediator of the Covenant of Grace)" parenthetically in the answer. This follows the Baptist Shorter Catechism's framing while preserving Mediatorial language. This is an appropriate adaptation.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - "two entire distinct natures" (WLC) → "two complete and distinct natures" (BLC). The word "entire" is the historic English rendering from the Westminster tradition. "Complete" is a reasonable modernization but represents a vocabulary departure from all three standards (WLC uses "entire," 1689 uses "whole, perfect," Shorter uses just "distinct").
  - "in... one person" (WLC) → "within one person" (BLC). The preposition "within" could subtly suggest the natures are contained inside the person as a vessel, rather than the classical formulation where the person subsists in two natures. This is likely innocent modernization but represents an imprecision.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - None of substance. The BLC preserves essentially the entire WLC answer, with only the vocabulary changes noted above.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Redeemer + Mediator synthesis:** The BLC's combining of the Shorter's "Redeemer" framing with the WLC's "Mediator" content is well-executed. The parenthetical "(and the only Mediator of the Covenant of Grace)" preserves the WLC's covenant-theological emphasis without abandoning the Shorter's familiar question.

2. **"Complete" vs. "entire":** The WLC, 1689 (via "whole, perfect"), and the broader Reformed tradition consistently use "entire" to describe the natures. "Complete" is semantically close but represents an unnecessary departure from established theological vocabulary. In Christological definition, precision of terminology matters because these words were chosen to exclude specific heresies.

3. **"Within" vs. "in":** The Chalcedonian Definition and all Reformed confessions speak of two natures "in" one person. The BLC's "within" is a subtle shift. While likely intended as modernization, "within" has a spatial connotation that "in" does not carry in the classical usage. The traditional "in" expresses a metaphysical relationship, not a spatial one.

4. **Sectioned format:** The BLC breaks the answer into four proof-texted sections, which aids teaching but slightly disrupts the flowing unity of the Christological affirmation. This is a pedagogical choice, not a theological concern.

5. **Proof texts:** The BLC significantly expands the proof texts beyond both the Shorter and WLC. Notable additions include John 14:6, Acts 4:12 (exclusivity of Christ), Psalm 2:7, Matthew 3:17, Matthew 17:5 (Sonship declarations), Matthew 1:23 (virgin birth/Emmanuel), Acts 1:11 (continuing God-man), Hebrews 13:8 (immutability), and Philippians 2:5-11 (kenosis hymn). These are well-chosen and strengthen the scriptural support.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Section 1 proof texts** (1 Tim 2:5; John 14:6; Acts 4:12): 1 Timothy 2:5 is shared with both the Shorter and WLC as the primary Mediator text. John 14:6 and Acts 4:12 are appropriate additions emphasizing exclusivity, drawing from 1689 Chapter 8.9's emphasis that the office of Mediator is "proper onely to Christ."
- **Section 2 proof texts** (John 1:1, 14, 18; John 10:30; Phil 2:6; Ps 2:7; Matt 3:17; Matt 17:5): John 1:1, 14 and John 10:30 align with WLC footnote 138. Philippians 2:6 is also in WLC 138. Psalm 2:7 and the baptism/transfiguration texts are additions that strengthen eternal Sonship.
- **Section 3 proof texts** (Gal 4:4; Matt 1:23; John 1:14): Galatians 4:4 is the WLC's footnote 139. Matthew 1:23 adds the Emmanuel prophecy. John 1:14 appears in both sections 2 and 3.
- **Section 4 proof texts** (Luke 1:35; Acts 1:11; Rom 9:5; Col 2:9; Heb 7:24-25; Heb 13:8; Phil 2:5-11): These substantially overlap with the Shorter's proof texts (Rom 9:5, Luke 1:35, Col 2:9, Heb 7:24-25) and WLC footnote 140. The additions (Acts 1:11, Heb 13:8, Phil 2:5-11) reinforce the perpetuity of the incarnation.
