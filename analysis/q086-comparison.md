# Q86: What is the moral law?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q86 closely follows WLC Q93 in structure and content, with appropriate modernization and a theologically sound addition ("founded upon the perfections of God"). However, the BLC drops "obedience" as a distinct term alongside "conformity" (present in both the WLC and 1689), substitutes "complete, exact" for the WLC's "perfect" (drawing from the 1689's "entire exact"), and omits the WLC's phrase "in the frame and disposition of the whole man." These are largely stylistic shifts, but the loss of "obedience" as a paired term with "conformity" is a minor weakening of precision.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter does not define the moral law. The closest questions are:

> "Q. What did God at first reveal to man for the rule of his obedience? A. The rule which God at first revealed to man for his obedience, was the moral law (Rom. 2:14, 15, and 10:5)."

> "Q. Where is the moral law summarily comprehended? A. The moral law is summarily comprehended in the ten commandments (Deut. 10:4; Mt. 19:17)."

- Omissions: N/A — no direct parallel to compare.
- Structural changes: N/A.
- The Shorter identifies the moral law as "the rule...for his obedience" but does not provide a full definition. The BLC appropriately expands on this in its Larger Catechism role.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraph 1:

> "God gave to Adam a Law of universal obedience, written in his Heart, and a particular precept of not eating the Fruit of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; by which he bound him, and all his posterity to personal entire exact and perpetual obedience; promised life upon the fulfilling, and threatned death upon the breach of it; and indued him with power and ability to keep it."

- Omissions: The 1689's phrase "indued him with power and ability to keep it" is not present, but this is appropriate since the 1689 is speaking specifically of the prelapsarian state and Adam's original endowment, not defining the moral law abstractly. The BLC's "complete, exact" appears to draw from the 1689's "entire exact," which is a good alignment choice.
- Structural changes: The 1689 speaks of "obedience" (not "conformity and obedience" as in the WLC). The BLC uses only "conformity," departing from both standards' emphasis on "obedience" as the key term.
- The BLC adds "founded upon the perfections of God," which, while theologically sound and implicit in the 1689's broader theology, is not found in this chapter. This is a modest theological addition that strengthens the grounding of the law in God's nature — consistent with Reformed theology but novel as an explicit inclusion in the catechetical definition.
- Agreement: The BLC faithfully preserves the promise-of-life/threat-of-death structure and the scope encompassing duties to both God and man.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q93:

> "Q. 93. What is the moral law? A. The moral law is the declaration of the will of God to mankind, directing and binding every one to personal, perfect, and perpetual conformity and obedience thereunto, in the frame and disposition of the whole man, soul and body, and in performance of all those duties of holiness and righteousness which he oweth to God and man: promising life upon the fulfilling, and threatening death upon the breach of it."

- Solidarity: STRONG
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected on this topic; none found.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - "obedience" dropped: The WLC pairs "conformity and obedience" — both the inward alignment and the outward performance. The BLC retains only "conformity," weakening the explicit outward dimension.
  - "perfect" replaced: The WLC's "personal, perfect, and perpetual" becomes the BLC's "personal, complete, exact, and perpetual." The BLC appears to draw "complete, exact" from the 1689's "entire exact" rather than the WLC's "perfect." While "complete" and "exact" together may cover the semantic range of "perfect," this is an unusual divergence from WLC wording in a definition that otherwise tracks it closely.
  - "in the frame and disposition of the whole man" dropped: The WLC's careful phrase about the law governing the inner frame and disposition is replaced by the simpler "applies to the whole man, body and soul." The WLC's language better captures the law's reach into the internal orientation and attitudes, not merely the faculties of body and soul.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "obedience thereunto" — the explicit pairing of obedience with conformity
  - "in the frame and disposition of" — the interiority language
  - "upon the fulfilling...upon the breach" — replaced with simpler "for keeping it...for disobeying it" (stylistic modernization, minor)

## Detailed Notes
1. **Addition of "founded upon the perfections of God"**: This is theologically sound and reflects the Reformed understanding that the moral law is a transcript of God's nature. However, it is not found in the WLC, 1689, or Shorter Catechism at this point. While defensible as an expansion appropriate to a Larger Catechism, it introduces a phrase not rooted in any of the three standards.

2. **Loss of "obedience"**: Both the WLC ("conformity and obedience") and the 1689 ("obedience") explicitly name obedience. "Conformity" alone suggests alignment or likeness, while "obedience" carries the sense of submission to command. The moral law is not merely something to conform to inwardly but something to obey in practice. The BLC does include "performances of all those duties," which partially compensates, but the explicit term "obedience" as a defining characteristic of what the law demands is lost from the opening definition.

3. **"Complete, exact" vs "perfect"**: The BLC's use of "complete, exact" draws from the 1689's "entire exact" rather than the WLC's "perfect." This is a reasonable choice that aligns the BLC more closely with its primary confessional standard. "Perfect" is arguably more comprehensive (encompassing both completeness and exactness in a single term), but the two-word formulation from the 1689 is theologically equivalent.

4. **Sentence restructuring**: The WLC presents the definition as one flowing sentence with participial clauses. The BLC breaks it into three sections/sentences. This restructuring aids readability but slightly fragments the unity of the definition — the WLC presents the law's scope and sanctions as a single integrated reality, while the BLC's structure could be read as presenting them as separable components.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Deuteronomy 5:1-3, 31-33**: Appropriate for the law's binding authority and the promise/threat structure. The WLC does not cite this passage for Q93, making this a BLC addition that fits the content.
- **Luke 10:26-27**: Supports the summary of duties to God and man. Sound choice.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:23**: Supports the "whole man, body and soul" language. Appropriate.
- **Ephesians 4:24**: Supports holiness and righteousness. Relevant.
- **Luke 1:75; Acts 24:16; 1 Peter 1:15-16**: Support the duties of holiness and righteousness. Appropriate.
- **Romans 10:5**: Directly supports the promise of life for obedience. This is shared with the 1689 (19.1 proof text).
- **Galatians 3:10-12**: Supports the threat of death for disobedience. Shared with the 1689.
- **Romans 5:12**: Supports the death-for-breach concept. Appropriate addition.
- Overall, the proof texts are well-chosen and show good alignment with the 1689's own citations for Chapter 19.1.
