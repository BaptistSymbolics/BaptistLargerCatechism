# Q62: What benefits do the elect experience through Christ in this life?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q62 combines the content of WLC Q65 (union and communion in grace and glory) with WLC Q69 (communion in grace as partaking of Christ's mediation), but replaces the WLC's precise phrase "partaking of the virtue of his mediation" with "partake of the power of His mediation." More critically, it drops the Shorter Catechism's foundational framing that these benefits flow from effectual calling, substituting a vaguer structure that grounds benefits directly in union and communion rather than in the ordo salutis sequence the Shorter and 1689 both teach.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What benefits do they that are effectually called partake of in this life?
> A. They that are effectually called do in this life partake of justification (Rom. 8:30), adoption (Eph. 1:5), sanctification, and the several benefits which in this life do either accompany or flow from them (1 Cor. 1:30).

- **Omissions:**
  - The Shorter's subject is "they that are effectually called" — the BLC changes this to "the elect," dropping the explicit connection to effectual calling as the means by which these benefits are received. This is theologically significant: the Shorter (and 1689 Ch. X-XIII) teaches that effectual calling is the gateway through which the elect actually receive justification, adoption, and sanctification. The BLC's phrasing could suggest these benefits belong to the elect apart from the applied work of the Spirit in calling.
  - The Shorter's phrase "the several benefits which in this life do either accompany or flow from them" is replaced with "whatever else, in this life, manifests their union with Him." While the latter comes from WLC Q69, the Shorter's language emphasizes the causal relationship (benefits that "accompany or flow from" justification, adoption, and sanctification), whereas the BLC's phrasing restructures these as manifestations of union rather than as flowing from the three great benefits.
- **Structural changes:**
  - The Shorter presents a straightforward sequence: effectual calling → justification, adoption, sanctification → accompanying benefits. The BLC introduces union/communion as the overarching framework and nests the three benefits under "the power of His mediation," which is a different structural arrangement. While not heretical, it departs from the Shorter's simpler ordo salutis framing.
  - The BLC adds "union and communion with Him in grace and glory" (from WLC Q65) as a lead concept before listing the specific benefits. The Shorter does not do this — it goes directly to the enumerated benefits.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter X (Of Effectual Calling), paragraph 1:
> "Those whom God hath predestinated unto Life, he is pleased in his appointed, and accepted time, effectually to call by his word, and Spirit, out of that state of sin, and death, in which they are by nature, to grace and Salvation by Jesus Christ..."

Chapter XI (Of Justification), paragraph 1:
> "Those whom God Effectually calleth, he also freely justifieth..."

Chapter XII (Of Adoption), paragraph 1:
> "All those that are justified, God vouchsafed, in, and for the sake of his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the Grace of Adoption..."

Chapter XIII (Of Sanctification), paragraph 1:
> "They who are united to Christ, Effectually called, and regenerated, having a new heart, and a new Spirit created in them, through the vertue of Christ's death, and Resurrection; are also farther sanctified..."

Chapter XXVII (Of the Communion of Saints), paragraph 1:
> "All Saints that are united to Jesus Christ their Head, by his Spirit, and Faith; although they are not made thereby one person with him, have fellowship in his Graces, sufferings, death, resurrection, and glory..."

- **Omissions:**
  - The 1689 consistently grounds justification, adoption, and sanctification in effectual calling (Ch. X-XIII form an explicit chain: effectual calling → justification → adoption → sanctification). The BLC drops this grounding entirely.
  - The 1689 Ch. XXVII.1 specifies union is "by his Spirit, and Faith" — the BLC does not mention either the Spirit or faith as the means of union, leaving the instrument of union unspecified.
- **Structural changes:**
  - The 1689 treats union with Christ, effectual calling, justification, adoption, and sanctification in separate sequential chapters, each building on the prior. The BLC compresses this into a single question that uses union/communion as the umbrella concept. While not contradictory, the 1689's careful ordering (effectual calling as the gateway) is flattened.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q65:
> Q. What special benefits do the members of the invisible church enjoy by Christ?
> A. The members of the invisible church by Christ enjoy union and communion with him in grace and glory.

WLC Q69:
> Q. What is the communion in grace which the members of the invisible church have with Christ?
> A. The communion in grace which the members of the invisible church have with Christ, is their partaking of the virtue of his mediation, in their justification, adoption, sanctification, and whatever else, in this life, manifests their union with him.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — the BLC clearly combines WLC Q65 and Q69 into a single question and closely follows the WLC language.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:**
  - The WLC uses "members of the invisible church" while BLC uses "the elect" — this is an expected ecclesiological difference, though the WLC's language is arguably more precise since it identifies the corporate context of these benefits.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - "Partaking of the **power** of His mediation" replaces the WLC's "partaking of the **virtue** of his mediation." The word "virtue" (Latin *virtus*, meaning efficacy, moral power, inherent quality) carries a richer theological meaning than "power." "Virtue" encompasses not just the force but the meritorious quality of Christ's mediatorial work. "Power" is a narrower modernization that loses some theological precision.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - The WLC's structural separation of Q65 (union and communion in grace and glory as the overarching benefit) from Q69 (communion in grace specifically as partaking of Christ's mediation) is collapsed. The WLC's architecture asks Q65 as a summary, then Q66-68 explain the union and calling, then Q69 explains communion in grace. This pedagogical structure is lost.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The effectual calling gap is the most significant issue.** Both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession explicitly and consistently teach that the benefits of justification, adoption, and sanctification come to the elect *through effectual calling*. The Shorter's parallel question is titled precisely: "What benefits do they that are *effectually called* partake of in this life?" The BLC's shift to "the elect" without reference to effectual calling obscures the applied, experiential dimension of salvation. Election is the decree; effectual calling is the application. The Shorter insists on the latter.

2. **"Virtue" vs. "Power."** While this may seem like a minor modernization, "virtue of his mediation" is a phrase with deep theological resonance in Reformed theology. The WLC deliberately chose "virtue" to capture the meritorious efficacy of Christ's mediatorial work — not just that it is powerful, but that it is intrinsically efficacious and morally perfect. "Power" reduces this to a single dimension.

3. **The BLC's combination of Q65 and Q69** is a reasonable editorial choice for a catechism that aims to be more concise than the WLC, but the compression loses the WLC's careful pedagogy. The WLC asks what union is (Q66), what effectual calling is (Q67), and who is effectually called (Q68) *between* the summary (Q65) and the explanation of communion in grace (Q69). By collapsing this, the BLC has no intervening treatment of effectual calling before listing the benefits.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts for section 1 (union and communion) largely match those cited in 1689 Ch. XXVII.1: John 17:21, 1 John 1:3, John 1:16, Philippians 3:10 all appear in the Confession's proof texts for that chapter.
- Romans 8:30 for justification is appropriate — it is the classic "golden chain" text. However, notably, Rom. 8:30 itself says "whom he called, them he also justified," reinforcing the effectual calling → justification sequence that the BLC's question text omits.
- Ephesians 1:5 for adoption and 1 Corinthians 1:30 for sanctification match the Shorter Catechism's proof texts exactly.
- The addition of "glory" references (John 17:24) alongside "grace" is drawn from WLC Q65's "grace and glory" language, which the Shorter does not include. This is a legitimate expansion.
- Ephesians 3:16-19 is a strong text for communion with Christ but is more naturally associated with sanctification/spiritual strengthening than with the general concept of union and communion.
