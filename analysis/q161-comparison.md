# Q161: What are the special privileges of the visible church?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q161 closely follows WLC Q63 in structure and content, with appropriate modernization. However, it omits the WLC's phrase "the ordinary means of salvation" — a significant Reformed concept — and replaces the WLC's "communion of saints" with the weaker phrase "fellowship for God's people." The 1689 Confession does not have a direct parallel paragraph on church privileges per se, but its ecclesiology chapters support the general content. The omission of "ordinary means of salvation" is the primary concern, as this is standard Reformed/Baptist language present in the Baptist Shorter Catechism's treatment of the means of grace.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not include a question specifically addressing the special privileges of the visible church.

The closest related questions are:
> Q. What are the outward means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption?
> A. The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption are his ordinances, especially the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer; all which means are made effectual to the elect for salvation (Mt. 28:19, 20; Acts 2:42, 46, 47).

While this is not a direct parallel, it establishes that the Shorter Catechism preserves the language of "ordinary means" for salvation. The BLC's Q161 describes the church as "the ordinary means by which people are saved" — this captures the concept but reframes it. In the Shorter (and WLC), the "ordinary means of salvation" are the Word, sacraments, and prayer enjoyed *within* the church. The BLC makes the church itself the ordinary means, which is a subtle but meaningful structural shift. The WLC and Shorter present the church as the context where the ordinary means are enjoyed, not as itself being the means.

- **Omissions:** No direct parallel to evaluate omissions against.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC restructures the relationship between the church and the means of grace. Rather than the church providing access to the ordinary means (Word, sacraments, prayer), the BLC identifies the church itself as "the ordinary means by which people are saved." This conflates the institution with its instruments.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

**Chapter 26, Paragraph 3:** "The purest Churches under heaven are subject to mixture, and error; and som have so degenerated as to become no Churches of Christ, but Synagogues of Satan; nevertheless Christ always hath had, and ever shall have a Kingdome in this world, to the end thereof, of such as believe in him, and make profession of his Name."

This supports BLC's teaching on the church being "protected and preserved throughout the ages, in spite of the opposition of all her enemies." The Confession uses Matthew 16:18 (shared with BLC) and adds Psalm 72:17, 102:28, and Revelation 12:17.

**Chapter 26, Paragraph 5:** "In the execution of this power wherewith he is so intrusted, the Lord Jesus calleth out of the World unto himself, through the Ministry of his word, by his Spirit, those that are given unto him by his Father; that they may walk before him in all the ways of obedience, which he prescribeth to them in his Word. Those thus called he commandeth to walk together in particular societies, or Churches, for their mutual edification; and the due performance of that publick worship, which he requireth of them in the World."

This supports the concept of churches providing fellowship and mutual edification, but note: the Confession is careful to say Christ calls people "through the Ministry of his word, by his Spirit" — the ministry of the Word is the instrument, not the church itself.

**Chapter 26, Paragraph 6:** "The Members of these Churches are Saints by calling, visibly manifesting and evidencing (in and by their profession and walking) their obedience unto that call of Christ; and do willingly consent to walk together according to the appointment of Christ, giving up themselves, to the Lord & one to another by the will of God, in professed subjection to the Ordinances of the Gospel."

**Chapter 20, Paragraph 4:** "Although the Gospel be the only outward means, of revealing Christ, and saving Grace; and is, as such, abundantly sufficient thereunto; yet that men who are dead in Trespasses, may be born again, Quickned or Regenerated; there is moreover necessary, an effectual, insuperable work of the Holy Spirit, upon the whole Soul..."

This is critical: the Confession identifies *the Gospel* as "the only outward means of revealing Christ, and saving Grace" — not the church. The church is the context in which the Gospel is preached, but the Confession carefully distinguishes between the institution and its means.

- **Omissions:** The BLC does not reflect the Confession's careful distinction between the church as institution and the Gospel/Word/ordinances as means of grace.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC's phrase "functions as the ordinary means by which people are saved" identifies the church itself as the means of salvation, while the Confession identifies the Gospel as the outward means and the church as the community where the Gospel is preached and ordinances administered.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q63: What are the special privileges of the visible church?
> A. The visible church hath the privilege of being under God's special care and government; of being protected and preserved in all ages, notwithstanding the opposition of all enemies; and of enjoying the communion of saints, the ordinary means of salvation, and offers of grace by Christ to all the members of it in the ministry of the gospel, testifying, that whosoever believes in him shall be saved, and excluding none that will come unto him.

- **Solidarity:** MODERATE
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected here — this question concerns general ecclesiology where Baptists and Presbyterians substantially agree on the privileges of the visible church. There are no baptism or covenant theology issues to navigate.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"Communion of saints" replaced with "fellowship for God's people":** The WLC uses the historic creedal phrase "communion of saints" (communio sanctorum), which carries deep theological weight — it connects to the Apostles' Creed, the 1689 Confession Chapter 27 (Of the Communion of Saints), and the historic Reformed tradition. The BLC's "fellowship for God's people" is a significant weakening. "Fellowship" is a more general and less theologically precise term than "communion of saints."
  2. **"The ordinary means of salvation" restructured:** The WLC lists three privileges the church *enjoys*: (a) communion of saints, (b) the ordinary means of salvation, and (c) offers of grace by Christ. These are three distinct things the church provides access to. The BLC restructures this by making the church itself "the ordinary means by which people are saved." This is a different theological claim. The WLC says the church enjoys the ordinary means (Word, sacraments, prayer); the BLC says the church *is* the ordinary means.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  1. "Communion of saints" — replaced with weaker "fellowship for God's people"
  2. "The ordinary means of salvation" as a distinct privilege — restructured so the church itself becomes the means
  3. "In all ages" — the BLC changes this to "throughout the ages," which is a minor modernization with no theological consequence
  4. "Offers of grace by Christ" — the BLC changes this to "extends God's grace through Christ," which shifts from Christ offering grace to the church extending it. The WLC emphasizes Christ's agency in offering grace through the church's ministry; the BLC makes the church the active agent extending grace.

## Detailed Notes

### The "Ordinary Means" Problem
The most significant concern is the BLC's treatment of the "ordinary means of salvation." In the WLC, the three-part structure is clear:
1. The communion of saints (fellowship and mutual edification)
2. The ordinary means of salvation (Word, sacraments, prayer — per the Shorter Catechism)
3. Offers of grace by Christ in the ministry of the gospel

The BLC collapses items 1 and 2 into: "provides fellowship for God's people, functions as the ordinary means by which people are saved." This makes the church itself the means of salvation rather than the context in which the means of salvation (Word, sacraments, prayer) are enjoyed. While it is true that salvation ordinarily occurs within the church context, the Reformed tradition has been careful to identify the specific ordinances — not the institution — as the means of grace.

### "Fellowship" vs. "Communion of Saints"
The replacement of "communion of saints" with "fellowship for God's people" is a notable weakening. The communion of saints in Reformed theology encompasses not merely social fellowship but the sharing in spiritual gifts, graces, and mutual edification as described in 1689 Confession 27.1-2. "Fellowship" is a subset of what "communion of saints" entails.

### Agency Shift in Grace
The WLC says Christ offers grace "to all the members of it in the ministry of the gospel." The BLC says the church "extends God's grace through Christ to all her members in the ministry of the gospel." This shifts agency from Christ (who offers through the church's ministry) to the church (which extends grace). While subtle, this could suggest the church mediates grace rather than Christ offering grace through the church's ministry.

### Positive Elements
- The BLC preserves "God's special care and government" almost verbatim from the WLC.
- The preservation language ("protected and preserved... in spite of the opposition of all her enemies") closely tracks the WLC.
- The final clause about "excluding no one from fellowship who desires to come to Christ" appropriately modernizes the WLC's "excluding none that will come unto him."
- The gospel proclamation element ("testifying that whosoever believes in him will be saved") is faithfully preserved.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Isaiah 4:5-6; Isaiah 49:14-16** — Appropriate for God's special care. Not in the WLC proof texts but suitable additions.
- **1 Timothy 4:10** — "The living God, who is the Saviour of all men, specially of those that believe." Supports God's special care for the church, though this text is typically used in discussions of common vs. special grace, not specifically church privileges.
- **1 Corinthians 12:28; Ephesians 4:11-12; Acts 13:1-2** — Support church government and ministry gifts. Good additions for the "government" clause.
- **Psalm 115; Isaiah 31:4-5; Zechariah 12:2-4, 8-9; Matthew 16:18** — Strong texts for preservation. Matthew 16:18 is shared with both the WLC and the 1689 Confession (26.3).
- **Acts 2:39-42** — Used to support "fellowship" and "ordinary means." This is a strong text for church community and devotion to apostolic teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, and prayer — which actually supports the WLC's enumeration of the specific means of grace rather than the BLC's identification of the church itself as the means.
- **Psalm 147:19-20; Romans 9:4** — These texts about God's special revelation to Israel are appropriate for privileges of the visible church.
- **John 6:37** — "Him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out." Excellent proof text for the final clause, shared conceptually with the WLC.
- **Mark 16:15-16; Acts 16:31; Revelation 22:17** — Support the gospel proclamation and free offer. Good additions.
