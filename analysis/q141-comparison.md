# Q141: What does every sin deserve from God?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q141 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q152, preserving every theological element in the same structural order. It fully encompasses the Shorter Catechism's teaching while appropriately expanding it with the WLC's explanatory framework (the four-fold basis for sin's desert and the Christological conclusion). The 1689 Confession's relevant teachings on sin's demerit are accurately reflected.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What doth every sin deserve?
> A. Every sin deserveth God's wrath and curse, both in this life and that which is to come (Eph. 5:6; Gal. 3:10; Lam. 3:39; Mt. 25:41; Rom. 6:23).

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's core content — "God's wrath and curse, both in this life and that which is to come" — is preserved verbatim in modernized form in the BLC.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC follows the WLC's fuller form, which expands upon the Shorter rather than departing from it. The Shorter's answer appears as the central declaration within the BLC's more developed answer.
- The BLC adds explanatory material drawn from the WLC (the basis of sin's desert in God's sovereignty, goodness, holiness, and righteous law; and the Christological conclusion about expiation by Christ's blood). These are legitimate expansions that the Shorter already presupposes but does not spell out.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter VI.3: "the guilt of the Sin was imputed, and corrupted nature conveyed, to all their posterity descending from them by ordinary generation, being now conceived in Sin, and by nature children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal, unless the Lord Jesus set them free."

Chapter XV.5: "although there is no sin so small, but it deserves damnation; yet there is no sin so great, that it shall bring damnation on them that repent; which makes the constant preaching of Repentance necessary."

Chapter XIX.6: "the Threatnings of it serve to shew what even their Sins deserve; and what afflictions in this Life they may expect for them, although free'd from the Curse and unallayed Rigor thereof."

- Omissions: None. The BLC faithfully reflects the Confession's teaching that (a) even the smallest sin deserves damnation (Ch. XV.5, captured by "even the least sin"), (b) sin brings miseries both temporal and eternal (Ch. VI.3, captured by "both in this life and the life to come"), and (c) deliverance is only through Christ (Ch. VI.3 "unless the Lord Jesus set them free," captured by "cannot be expiated except by the blood of Christ").
- Structural changes: None. The Confession's teaching is more distributed across multiple chapters, while the BLC consolidates these elements into a single catechetical answer — an appropriate function of a catechism.
- The BLC's phrase "every sin deserves God's wrath and curse" aligns with the Confession's language about sin deserving "damnation" (XV.5) and sinners being "children of wrath" (VI.3). The BLC's specification that sin goes against God's "sovereignty, goodness, and holiness" and "righteous law" is consistent with the Confession's teaching on God's character and the moral law (Ch. XIX).

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 152. What doth every sin deserve at the hands of God?
> A. Every sin, even the least, being against the sovereignty, goodness, and holiness of God, and against his righteous law, deserveth his wrath and curse, both in this life, and that which is to come; and cannot be expiated but by the blood of Christ.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question involves no baptismal, ecclesiological, or covenant theology distinctives where Baptist-Presbyterian divergence would be expected.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The BLC follows the WLC virtually word-for-word in modernized English.
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every element of the WLC answer is preserved:
  - "Every sin, even the least" → "Since even the least sin"
  - "being against the sovereignty" → "goes against the sovereignty"
  - "goodness" → "goodness"
  - "and holiness of God" → "and holiness of God"
  - "and against his righteous law" → "and against His righteous law"
  - "deserveth his wrath and curse" → "every sin deserves God's wrath and curse"
  - "both in this life" → "both in this life"
  - "and that which is to come" → "and the life to come"
  - "and cannot be expiated but by the blood of Christ" → "and cannot be expiated except by the blood of Christ"

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC restructures the WLC's single flowing sentence into a series of sectioned clauses, each with its own proof texts. This is a formatting choice that actually enhances catechetical usability without altering theological content.
- The BLC's question wording ("What does every sin deserve from God?") simplifies the WLC's "What doth every sin deserve at the hands of God?" by dropping "at the hands of." This is a minor modernization that does not change the meaning — the answer still makes clear the desert comes from God.
- The BLC's use of "Since even the least sin goes against..." restructures the WLC's participial phrase ("being against") into a causal clause. This actually makes the logical relationship clearer: the reason every sin deserves wrath is because it offends God's attributes and law.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC significantly expands the proof texts compared to both the Shorter Catechism and the WLC, which is appropriate for a larger catechism format.
- Sovereignty: James 2:10-11 (WLC uses this); Malachi 1:14 — both appropriate for God's sovereign majesty.
- Goodness: Exodus 20:1-2 (the preface establishing God's gracious redemption); Deuteronomy 32:6 (God as Father/Creator) — well-chosen texts emphasizing God's goodness that sin offends.
- Holiness: Habakkuk 1:13; Leviticus 10:3; 11:44-45; 1 Peter 1:15-16 — strong holiness texts.
- Righteous law: 1 John 3:4; Romans 7:12 — directly relevant to sin as transgression of God's righteous law.
- Wrath and curse: Ephesians 5:6; Galatians 3:10 — both shared with the Shorter/WLC tradition.
- This life: Lamentations 3:39; Deuteronomy 28:15-29; Proverbs 13:21 — Lamentations 3:39 is shared with the Shorter; Deuteronomy 28 and Proverbs are solid additions.
- Life to come: Matthew 25:41; Romans 6:21-23 — Matthew 25:41 is shared with the Shorter; Romans 6:23 is shared with the Shorter (the BLC extends the citation to include vv. 21-22 for fuller context).
- Blood of Christ: Hebrews 9:22; 1 Peter 1:18-19; 1 John 1:7 — excellent proof texts for the necessity of Christ's blood for expiation, expanding beyond the WLC's citations.
