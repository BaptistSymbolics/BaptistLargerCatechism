# Q107: What is the fourth commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q107 simply quotes the text of the fourth commandment (Exodus 20:8-11) in modernized English. The Shorter Catechism and WLC both do the same in traditional English. All differences are purely matters of language modernization (e.g., "shall you" for "shalt thou," "animals" for "cattle," "made it holy" for "hallowed it") with no theological alteration. The BLC faithfully preserves the full commandment text.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
Q. "What is the fourth commandment?"
A. "The fourth commandment is, Remember the Sabbath day to keep it holy: six days shalt thou labour and do all thy work; but the seventh day is the Sabbath of the Lord thy God, in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, nor thy man-servant, nor thy maid-servant, nor thy cattle, nor the stranger that is within thy gates: for in six days the Lord made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day; wherefore the Lord blessed the Sabbath day and hallowed it (Ex. 20:8-11)."

- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves the complete commandment text.
- Structural changes: None. The order and structure are identical.
- The BLC modernizes language throughout ("shall you" for "shalt thou," "your" for "thy," etc.) while preserving complete fidelity to the content. Minor vocabulary shifts ("animals" for "cattle," "alien" for "stranger," "made it holy" for "hallowed it") reflect standard modern Bible translations (NIV) and do not alter meaning.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraph 7 — "As it is of the Law of nature, that in general a proportion of time by Gods appointment, be set a part for the Worship of God; so by his Word in a positive-moral, and perpetual Commandement, binding all men, in all Ages, he hath particularly appointed one day in seven for a Sabbath to be kept holy unto him, which from the beginning of the World to the Resurrection of Christ, was the last day of the week; and from the resurrection of Christ, was changed into the first day of the week which is called the Lords day; and is to be continued to the end of the World, as the Christian Sabbath; the observation of the last day of the week being abolished."

- Omissions: N/A — Q107 is a Scripture quotation question, not a theological exposition question. The doctrinal content of 1689 22.7 (the change from seventh to first day, the designation as "Lord's day," the Christian Sabbath concept, and the perpetual moral obligation) would properly be addressed in subsequent BLC questions about what the fourth commandment requires and how the Sabbath is observed.
- Structural changes: None.
- The BLC correctly quotes the commandment text that the 1689 Confession itself cites (Exo. 20:8) as the foundation for its Sabbath theology.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q115: "Which is the fourth commandment? A. The fourth commandment is, Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy. Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work; but the seventh day is the sabbath of the Lord thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy man-servant, nor thy maid-servant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates. For in six days the Lord made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested in the seventh day: wherefore the Lord blessed the sabbath-day and hallowed it."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — Both BLC Q107 and WLC Q115 ask the identical question and give the identical answer (the full text of Exodus 20:8-11), differing only in language modernization.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is a Scripture quotation with no denominational distinctives).
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None. All elements of the WLC's quotation are preserved in the BLC's modernized form.

## Detailed Notes
- The question form differs slightly: BLC asks "What is the fourth commandment?" while WLC asks "Which is the fourth commandment?" This is a trivial modernization matching the Shorter Catechism's "What is" phrasing.
- BLC uses "by keeping it holy" where the traditional text has "to keep it holy." The preposition shift is stylistic and follows modern translation conventions; the meaning is identical.
- BLC uses "nor your manservant or maidservant" where the traditional text has "nor thy man-servant, nor thy maid-servant." Using "or" instead of a second "nor" is slightly less formal but does not change the prohibition's scope.
- BLC uses "animals" rather than "cattle." This is a legitimate modernization reflecting that the Hebrew *behemah* includes livestock generally, not just bovines. Modern translations (NIV, ESV) also use broader terms.

## Scripture Analysis
- Exodus 20:8-11 is the sole proof text, correctly cited by both BLC and all three standards. The BLC's modernized rendering follows standard modern translation conventions and preserves the full scope of the commandment without abbreviation or alteration.
