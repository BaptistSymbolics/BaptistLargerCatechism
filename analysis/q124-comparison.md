# Q124: What is required in the sixth commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q124 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q135, preserving the full structure and nearly all content of the Westminster answer. The Shorter Catechism's core formula ("all lawful endeavours to preserve our own life and the life of others") is well preserved. However, the BLC drops the WLC's opening phrase "all careful studies" (alongside "lawful endeavors"), and its rendering of "resisting all thoughts and purposes" as "not thinking about or planning" is an incomplete and potentially confusing modernization that loses the WLC's emphasis on active resistance against murderous inclinations.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- Q. "What is required in the sixth commandment?"
- A. "The sixth commandment requireth all lawful endeavours to preserve our own life (Eph. 5:28,29) and the life of others (1 Kings 18:4)."

- **Omissions:** None of substance. The Shorter's answer is a compact summary; the BLC expands it as expected for a Larger Catechism treatment.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC opens with the same core principle — making "every lawful effort to preserve our own life and the lives of others" — directly mirroring the Shorter's "all lawful endeavours to preserve our own life and the life of others." The phrase "every lawful effort" is a reasonable modernization of "all lawful endeavours."
- **Agreement:** Strong. The BLC preserves the Shorter's core content and expands it with the detailed WLC-style enumeration, as a Larger Catechism should.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God)

The 1689 does not expound individual commandments in detail. The relevant theological framework is:

> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation." (19.5)

> "Although true Believers be not under the Law, as a Covenant of Works, to be thereby Justified or condemned; yet it is of great use to them as well as to others: in that, as a Rule of Life, informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty, it directs and binds them, to walk accordingly..." (19.6)

- **Omissions:** None. The 1689 does not treat the sixth commandment specifically, so the BLC's detailed exposition is supplementary material consistent with the Confession's framework of the moral law as a binding rule of life.
- **Structural changes:** None.
- **Agreement:** The BLC's treatment of the sixth commandment as requiring positive duties of life-preservation, charitable disposition, and active care for others is fully consistent with the 1689's teaching that the moral law binds believers as a rule of life directing them in the will of God and their duty.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q135: "What are the duties required in the sixth commandment?"
> "The duties required in the sixth commandment are all careful studies, and lawful endeavors, to preserve the life of ourselves and others by resisting all thoughts and purposes, subduing all passions, and avoiding all occasions, temptations, and practices, which tend to the unjust taking away the life of any; by just defence thereof against violence, patient bearing of the hand of God, quietness of mind, cheerfulness of spirit; a sober use of meat, drink, physic, sleep, labour, and recreations; by charitable thoughts, love, compassion, meekness, gentleness, kindness; peaceable, mild and courteous speeches and behaviour; forbearance, readiness to be reconciled, patient bearing and forgiving of injuries, and requiting good for evil; comforting and succouring the distressed and protecting and defending the innocent."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None (no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues in this question).
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"All careful studies" dropped.** The WLC opens with "all careful studies, and lawful endeavors." The BLC renders only "every lawful effort," dropping "careful studies." The WLC's phrase emphasizes the intellectual/deliberative dimension — actively studying and considering how to preserve life, not merely making efforts when the occasion arises. This is a minor loss of precision.
  2. **"Resisting all thoughts and purposes" rendered as "not thinking about or planning."** The WLC's phrase "resisting all thoughts and purposes" describes the active resistance against murderous thoughts and intentions that arise internally. The BLC's "not thinking about or planning" could be read as merely a prohibition on deliberate plotting, losing the WLC's emphasis on actively resisting sinful thoughts that come unbidden. The WLC's "resisting" is stronger and more theologically precise than the BLC's "not thinking about."
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "all careful studies" — the deliberative/intellectual dimension of life-preservation
  - "resisting" (replaced with weaker "not thinking about") — active combat against sinful inclinations
  - "forbearance" — listed explicitly in WLC but only implied in BLC's "be tolerant of others"
  - "succouring" — the WLC's "comforting and succouring the distressed" becomes "provide aid and comfort to those in distress," which preserves the substance adequately

## Detailed Notes

### Structural Fidelity
The BLC follows the WLC's structure with remarkable precision. The order of topics is preserved almost exactly:
1. Core principle (preserve life) ✓
2. Resisting thoughts/purposes ✓ (though weakened)
3. Subduing passions → "controlling emotions" ✓
4. Avoiding occasions, temptations, practices ✓
5. Defence against violence ✓
6. Patient bearing of God's hand ✓
7. Quietness of mind ✓
8. Cheerfulness of spirit ✓
9. Sober use of meat, drink, physic, sleep, labour, recreations ✓
10. Charitable thoughts, love, compassion, meekness, gentleness, kindness ✓
11. Peaceable, mild, courteous speech and behaviour ✓
12. Forbearance, reconciliation, forgiving injuries, good for evil ✓
13. Comforting distressed, protecting innocent ✓

This is near-perfect structural solidarity with WLC Q135.

### The "Not Thinking About or Planning" Issue
The BLC's section "We do this by not thinking about or planning" (with prooftexts Jeremiah 26:15-16; Acts 23:12, 16-17, 21, 27; Matthew 5:22) is the weakest modernization in this question. The WLC says we preserve life "by resisting all thoughts and purposes" — this is about internal spiritual warfare against murderous inclinations. The BLC's phrasing sounds more like an external prohibition against premeditation, which is a narrower concept. The Reformed tradition's emphasis on the heart-level application of the commandments (per Christ's teaching in the Sermon on the Mount, which the proof text Matthew 5:22 itself supports) calls for the stronger "resisting" language.

### Modernization Quality
Most modernizations are excellent:
- "subduing all passions" → "controlling our emotions" — faithful
- "physic" → "medications" — necessary update
- "meat" → "eat" — appropriate
- "sober use" → "practice temperance" — good
- "comforting and succouring" → "provide aid and comfort" — adequate

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts closely mirror the WLC's footnoted references:
- Ephesians 5:28-29 (self-preservation) — matches WLC fn 721
- 1 Kings 18:4 (preserving others) — matches WLC fn 722
- Jeremiah 26:15-16; Acts 23:12,16-17,21,27 (resisting purposes) — matches WLC fn 723
- Ephesians 4:26-27 (subduing passions) — matches WLC fn 724
- 2 Samuel 2:22; Deuteronomy 22:8 (avoiding occasions) — matches WLC fn 725
- Matthew 4:6-7 (temptations) — matches WLC fn 726
- 1 Samuel 24:12; 26:9-11; Genesis 37:21-22 (practices against life) — matches WLC fn 727
- Psalm 82:4; Proverbs 24:11-12 (defence against violence) — matches WLC fn 728
- James 5:7-11; Hebrews 12:5,9 (patient bearing) — matches WLC fn 729
- 1 Thessalonians 4:11; 1 Peter 3:3-4 (quietness) — matches WLC fn 730
- Proverbs 17:22 (cheerfulness) — matches WLC fn 731
- Proof texts for meat, drink, physic, sleep, labour, recreations all match WLC fns 732-737
- Charitable thoughts, love, compassion references match WLC fns 738-741
- Peaceable speech references match WLC fns 742-743
- Forbearance and reconciliation references match WLC fn 744
- Comforting distressed and protecting innocent match WLC fn 745

The BLC adds Matthew 10:23 to the self-preservation section (fleeing persecution as a lawful means of preserving life), which is a reasonable addition beyond the WLC's proof texts. It also adds Proverbs 22:24-25 and 1 Samuel 25:32-33 to the "avoiding occasions" section, and several other supplementary texts throughout. These additions are appropriate and well-chosen.

The BLC adds 1 Kings 21:9-10,19 to the "practices tending to unjust taking of life" section, referencing Jezebel's orchestrated murder of Naboth — an apt illustration. It also adds 1 Samuel 14:45 (the people rescuing Jonathan from Saul's oath) to the defence-against-violence section, which is a fitting example.

Overall, the proof text selection demonstrates careful attention to the WLC's references while adding well-chosen supplementary passages.
