# Q168: How do we prepare to receive the Lord's Supper?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q168 is a close modernization of WLC Q171, faithfully expanding the Shorter Catechism's list of self-examination topics into the fuller WLC structure. The proof texts align closely with the WLC's, with one apparent correction (2 Cor 13:5 for "faith" instead of the WLC's likely erroneous 1 Cor 13:5). The primary concern is the omission of the Shorter Catechism's concluding warning — "lest coming unworthily they eat and drink judgment to themselves" (1 Cor 11:28-29) — which functions as motivational grounding for the entire preparatory exercise. Additionally, the Shorter's phrase "knowledge to discern the Lord's body" is weakened to the more generic "whether we truly and to what degree know God."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What is required to the worthy receiving of the Lord's supper? A. It is required of them that would worthily partake of the Lord's supper, that they examine themselves of their knowledge to discern the Lord's body (1 Cor. 11:28, 29), of their faith to feed upon him (2 Cor. 13:5), of their repentance (1 Cor. 11:31), love (1 Cor. 10:16, 17), and new obedience (1 Cor. 5:7, 8), lest coming unworthily they eat and drink judgment to themselves (1 Cor. 11:28, 29)."

- **Omissions:**
  - The Shorter's concluding warning clause — "lest coming unworthily they eat and drink judgment to themselves" — is entirely absent from the BLC. This is not a minor stylistic element; it is the Shorter's own summary of why preparation matters, drawn directly from 1 Corinthians 11:29. The BLC gives no equivalent warning about unworthy reception.
  - The Shorter's "knowledge to discern the Lord's body" is a theologically precise phrase connecting preparation to the specific sacramental act (discerning the body and blood of Christ in the elements). The BLC replaces this with "whether we truly and to what degree know God," which is more generic and loses the sacramental specificity. The WLC's fuller phrasing — "the truth and measure of their knowledge" — is also more precise than the BLC's rendition, though neither the WLC nor the Shorter use the exact same phrase.
  - The Shorter's "faith to feed upon him" connects faith directly to the sacramental action of spiritual feeding. The BLC's "believe in Him" is a generic expression of faith that loses this sacramental connection.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter treats the five topics (knowledge, faith, repentance, love, new obedience) as a single coordinated list of self-examination points. The BLC expands this list (following the WLC) but fragments the relationship between faith and its sacramental object.
- **Agreement:** The five core examination topics from the Shorter (knowledge, faith, repentance, love, new obedience) are all present in the BLC.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter XXX (Of the Lord's Supper), Paragraphs 7-8.

Paragraph 7: "Worthy receivers, outwardly partaking of the visible Elements in this Ordinance, do then also inwardly by faith, really and indeed, yet not carnally, and corporally, but spiritually receive, and feed upon Christ crucified & all the benefits of his death: the Body and Blood of Christ, being then not corporally, or carnally, but spiritually present to the faith of Believers, in that Ordinance, as the Elements themselves are to their outward senses."

Paragraph 8: "All ignorant and ungodly persons, as they are unfit to enjoy communion with Christ; so are they unworthy of the Lords Table; and cannot without great sin against him, while they remain such, partake of these holy mysteries, or be admitted thereunto: yea whosoever shall receive unworthily are guilty of the Body and Blood of the Lord, eating and drinking judgement to themselves."

- **Omissions:**
  - The Confession's paragraph 8 explicitly warns that unworthy reception makes one "guilty of the Body and Blood of the Lord, eating and drinking judgement to themselves." The BLC's preparation question includes no such warning, which the Shorter Catechism does include. This warning provides the theological rationale for the entire preparation process.
  - The Confession's "knowledge" of Christ in the Lord's Supper is tied to "discerning" the Lord's body (via 1 Cor 11:29), not merely knowing God generically.
- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC's question is about preparation specifically, while the Confession's paragraphs 7-8 address worthy reception and its consequences. These are complementary rather than parallel structures.
- **Agreement:** The BLC's emphasis on self-examination, faith, repentance, love, and new obedience all align with the Confession's theology. The Confession's identification of "ignorant and ungodly persons" as unworthy provides the negative case that the BLC's preparation is designed to address positively.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 171. How are they that receive the sacrament of the Lord's supper to prepare themselves before they come unto it? A. They that receive the sacrament of the Lord's supper are, before they come, to prepare themselves thereunto, by examining themselves of their being in Christ, of their sins and wants; of the truth and measure of their knowledge, faith, repentance; love to God and the brethren, charity to all men, forgiving those that have done them wrong; of their desires after Christ, and of their new obedience; and by renewing the exercise of these graces, by serious meditation, and fervent prayer."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** The WLC uses "sacrament" while the BLC avoids this term entirely, consistent with Baptist preference for "ordinance" language (though the BLC does not use "ordinance" either, simply referring to "the Lord's Supper").
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC's "the truth and measure of their knowledge" is rendered as "whether we truly and to what degree know God" in the BLC. The WLC's "knowledge" (footnoted to 1 Cor 11:29 — "not discerning the Lord's body") is sacramentally specific knowledge about discerning Christ's body. The BLC's "know God" is more generic.
  - The WLC's "faith" (originally footnoted to 1 Cor 13:5 in the WLC, likely a printer's error for 2 Cor 13:5) is rendered simply as "believe in Him" in the BLC. The BLC uses "2 Corinthians 13:5; Matthew 26:28" — the 2 Cor reference appears to be a correction of the WLC's likely error, and the addition of Matt 26:28 (the blood of the new covenant) adds a sacramental connection that partially compensates for the generic wording.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "the truth and measure of" — This precise qualifier about examining not just whether one has knowledge/faith/repentance but the truth and degree of it is a significant pastoral qualification. The BLC partially captures this with "whether we truly and to what degree" for knowledge but does not apply it to faith and repentance.
  - "their being in Christ" — The WLC begins self-examination with "their being in Christ." The BLC renders this as "the condition of our life in Christ," which is a reasonable modernization but subtly shifts from examining whether one is in Christ (union with Christ as a binary question) to examining the condition/quality of one's life in Christ (which presupposes union).

## Detailed Notes
1. **The missing warning:** The most notable omission across all three standards is the absence of any warning about unworthy reception. The Shorter Catechism closes with "lest coming unworthily they eat and drink judgment to themselves"; the 1689 Confession paragraph 8 warns extensively about unworthy reception; the WLC addresses this in a separate question (Q172-173) rather than in Q171 itself. The BLC follows the WLC's structure by separating preparation from warning — but unlike the WLC, the BLC apparently has no separate question addressing the danger of unworthy participation. If such a question exists elsewhere in the BLC, this concern is mitigated. If not, a key element from both the Shorter and the 1689 is simply absent.

2. **Proof text correction:** The BLC's use of "2 Corinthians 13:5" for "believe in Him" appears to be a deliberate correction of a longstanding error in WLC proof texts, where footnote 1092 cites "1 Corinthians 13:5" (a chapter about love, not faith) rather than "2 Corinthians 13:5" ("Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith"). This is a commendable correction.

3. **"Condition of our life in Christ" vs. "being in Christ":** The WLC's "being in Christ" points to examining whether one truly has saving union with Christ. The BLC's "condition of our life in Christ" is slightly softer — it could be read as examining how one is living as a Christian rather than whether one truly is a Christian. However, combined with the subsequent "whether we truly... know God, believe in Him, and have repented," the BLC does cover the existential question of genuine saving faith.

4. **Luke 1:53 addition:** The BLC adds Luke 1:53 ("He hath filled the hungry with good things; and the rich he hath sent empty away") to the proof texts for "how much we desire Christ," which is not in the WLC but is a fitting addition emphasizing spiritual hunger.

The proof text alignment is remarkably close, with the primary difference being the apparent correction of the WLC's 1 Cor 13:5 to 2 Cor 13:5 for "faith."
