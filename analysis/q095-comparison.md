# Q95: What is the first commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q95 faithfully states the first commandment in modernized English, matching the substance of the Shorter Catechism and WLC identically. This is a simple declarative question that quotes Scripture (Exodus 20:3), and the BLC preserves the content precisely. The only difference is the expected modernization of language ("You shall" for "Thou shalt"), which is the BLC's stated purpose.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Which is the first commandment?
> A. The first commandment is, Thou shalt have no other gods before me (Ex. 20:3).

- Omissions: None. The full content of the Shorter's answer is preserved.
- Structural changes: None. The structure is identical: question identifying the commandment, answer quoting the commandment text with Scripture reference.

The BLC modernizes the question from "Which is" to "What is" and the answer from "Thou shalt" to "You shall." Both are standard modernizations that preserve the meaning exactly. The proof text (Exodus 20:3) is identical. This is a direct, verbatim-equivalent rendering of the Shorter Catechism's answer.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraph 2

> "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables; the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man."

The 1689 Confession does not quote individual commandments but affirms the Ten Commandments as the summary of the moral law delivered at Sinai. BLC Q95 operates within this framework by identifying and quoting the first of these commandments. The Confession's classification of "the four first containing our duty towards God" is consistent with the first commandment's exclusive demand for worship of the one true God.

- Omissions: None applicable. The Confession does not quote individual commandments for comparison.
- Structural changes: None.

The Confession's Chapter 2.1 ("The Lord our God is but one only living, and true God") provides the theological foundation for why the first commandment demands exclusive worship. BLC Q95's quotation of the commandment is consistent with this doctrine.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q103. Which is the first commandment?
> A. The first commandment is, Thou shall have no other gods before me.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is a direct Scripture quotation with no ecclesiological or sacramental content).
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None. The BLC is a direct modernized equivalent of WLC Q103.

The BLC Q95 corresponds precisely to WLC Q103. Both ask and answer the same question with the same content. The WLC uses "Which is" while the BLC uses "What is" — a trivial stylistic modernization. The commandment text itself is rendered identically in substance ("You shall" for "Thou shalt").

Note that the WLC numbers this as Q103 because it has additional preliminary questions (Q98-102) on the rules for understanding the commandments, the preface to the Decalogue, and the sum of the four commandments containing duty to God. The BLC's different numbering reflects its own catechetical structure but the content is the same.

## Detailed Notes
This is one of the most straightforward questions in the catechism to evaluate, as it is a direct quotation of Scripture (Exodus 20:3). There is essentially no room for theological divergence — all three standards (Shorter, WLC, and BLC) quote the same text. The BLC's modernization to "You shall have no other gods before me" is a faithful contemporary rendering of the Hebrew/KJV "Thou shalt have no other gods before me."

The real theological substance regarding the first commandment will appear in the subsequent questions about what the commandment requires (duties) and forbids (sins). The Shorter handles this in two questions (Q103-104 on requirements, Q105-106 on what is forbidden), while the WLC expands to three questions (Q104 duties, Q105 sins, Q106 on "before me"). The BLC's treatment of those subsequent questions will be where substantive theological comparison becomes meaningful.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC cites Exodus 20:3, which is the universal proof text for the first commandment across all Reformed catechisms. The Shorter Catechism cites the same verse. The WLC cites it as well (footnote 445 references "Exodus 20:3. Thou shalt have no other gods before me."). There is complete agreement on the scriptural basis.
