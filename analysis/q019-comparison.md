# Q19: How did God create man?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q19 closely follows WLC Q17 in structure and content, faithfully expanding upon the Shorter Catechism's compressed answer. It preserves all key doctrinal elements from the 1689 Confession Chapter IV.2. The one concern is structural: the BLC separates the final clause about being subject to fall into its own section with added language ("being left to the liberty of their own will, which was subject to change") drawn from the 1689, but adds "Yet" as a conjunction and frames the section independently rather than as a qualification directly modifying the preceding content about the image of God. This is a minor stylistic observation. A more notable concern is that the 1689's phrase "rendering them fit unto that life to God, for which they were created" — which grounds the soul's purpose theologically — is omitted, following the WLC pattern which also lacks this phrase. Additionally, the 1689 uses "true holiness" while the BLC follows the WLC/Shorter in saying simply "holiness."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. How did God create man?
> A. God created man, male and female, after his own image, in knowledge, righteousness, and holiness, with dominion over the creatures. (Gen. 1:26, 27, 28; Col. 3:10, Eph. 4:24)

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's compressed answer is fully preserved and expanded in the BLC.
- Structural changes: None that alter meaning. The BLC expands the Shorter's answer with additional detail about the body's formation, the soul, the law written on the heart, power to fulfill it, and susceptibility to fall — all legitimate expansions consistent with the 1689 and WLC pattern.
- The Shorter's core theological structure — creation after God's image in knowledge, righteousness, and holiness, with dominion — is preserved intact in the BLC.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter IV, Paragraph 2:
> "After God had made all other Creatures, he Created man, male and female, with reasonable and immortal souls, rendring them fit unto that life to God; for which they were Created; being made after the image of God, in knowledge, righteousness, and true holyness; having the Law of God written in their hearts, and power to fulfill it; and yet under a possibility of transgressing, being left to the liberty of their own will, which was subject to change."

Also relevant, Chapter IV, Paragraph 3:
> "Besides the Law written in their hearts, they received a command not to eat of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; which whilst they kept, they were happy in their Communion with God, and had dominion over the Creatures."

- Omissions:
  1. **"rendering them fit unto that life to God, for which they were created"** — This important phrase grounding the soul's purpose in the divine telos is absent from the BLC. The BLC follows the WLC here, which also omits this clause. This is a distinctively 1689 phrase not found in the Westminster Standards.
  2. **"true holiness"** — The 1689 says "true holyness" (following Ephesians 4:24's "true holiness"), while the BLC says simply "holiness." This follows the Shorter and WLC pattern. The adjective "true" distinguishes original holiness from mere ceremonial or external holiness, though the theological point is still conveyed.
  3. **"under a possibility of transgressing"** — The 1689 phrases the mutability clause as "under a possibility of transgressing, being left to the liberty of their own will, which was subject to change." The BLC restructures this as "they were subject to fall, being left to the liberty of their own will, which was subject to change." The phrase "subject to fall" comes from the WLC; the phrase "being left to the liberty of their own will, which was subject to change" comes from the 1689. The BLC effectively blends both sources.
  4. The 1689 places **dominion over creatures in a separate paragraph (IV.3)** linked to the positive command not to eat of the tree, while the BLC (following the WLC) places dominion within the creation answer itself. This is a structural difference but not a theological loss.
- Structural changes: The 1689 presents the creation of man as a continuous theological statement, while the BLC breaks it into discrete sections. This is inherent to the catechetical format and not problematic.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q17:
> Q. 17. How did God create man?
> A. After God had made all other creatures, he created man male and female; formed the body of the man of the dust of the ground, and the woman of the rib of the man, endued them with living, reasonable, and immortal souls; made them after his own image, in knowledge, righteousness, and holiness; having the law of God written in their hearts, and power to fulfill it, and dominion over the creatures; yet subject to fall.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC Q17 very closely in both structure and wording. The BLC is essentially a modernized version of the WLC answer broken into proof-texted sections.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None apparent. This is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences:
  1. The WLC's final clause reads "yet subject to fall" as a terse, integrated modifier. The BLC expands this into a full section: "Yet, they were subject to fall, being left to the liberty of their own will, which was subject to change." This expansion draws from the 1689 Confession's language and actually adds precision, explaining *why* they were subject to fall. This is a positive expansion.
  2. The BLC adds "Moreover" before the dominion clause, separating it more distinctly from the image-of-God content. The WLC integrates dominion into the flow of the single answer. This is a minor stylistic difference.
  3. The BLC adds the section on the body's formation from dust and the woman from the rib as a separate section, while in the WLC this flows continuously. Again, this is a formatting difference inherent to the BLC's section-based structure.
- Key phrases dropped: No WLC phrases are dropped. The BLC preserves all elements of the WLC answer.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Excellent WLC solidarity**: This is one of the strongest parallels between BLC and WLC. The BLC essentially reproduces WLC Q17 in sectioned, modernized form with scripture proofs added to each clause.
2. **1689 enrichment**: The BLC's final section draws on 1689 IV.2's language about "the liberty of their own will, which was subject to change," enriching the WLC's terse "yet subject to fall" with confessional specificity. This is a commendable integration of the Baptist confessional tradition.
3. **Omitted 1689 teleological phrase**: The phrase "rendering them fit unto that life to God, for which they were created" is a theologically rich statement about the purpose of the soul that is unique to the 1689 (not found in the WCF). Its omission means the BLC misses an opportunity to express this distinctively Baptist confessional emphasis on the telos of man's creation. However, since the BLC's primary structural model is the WLC, this is understandable.
4. **"True holiness" vs. "holiness"**: The 1689 follows Ephesians 4:24 more precisely with "true holiness." The Shorter and WLC both say simply "holiness." The BLC follows the WLC/Shorter here. The theological substance is preserved since the context makes clear this is original, genuine holiness.
5. **Section structure**: The BLC's breaking of the answer into nine proof-texted sections is pedagogically helpful and does not alter the theological content. Each section receives targeted scripture support.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and closely follow the WLC's footnotes and the 1689's marginal references.
- **Genesis 1:27; Matthew 19:4** for male and female creation — Matthew 19:4 is an addition not found in the WLC footnotes (which cite only Genesis 1:27), providing a New Testament attestation of the creation order. Good addition.
- **Genesis 2:7, 22** for body formation — matches WLC footnotes 59-60.
- **Genesis 2:7; Ecclesiastes 12:7; Matthew 10:28; Job 35:11; Luke 23:43** for the soul — Genesis 2:7 matches WLC. The additional references (Ecclesiastes 12:7 on the spirit returning to God, Matthew 10:28 on the soul's indestructibility, Job 35:11 on rational capacity, Luke 23:43 on the soul's conscious immortality) expand the scriptural support helpfully.
- **Genesis 1:26-27** for the image of God — matches standard references.
- **Colossians 3:10; Ephesians 4:24** for knowledge, righteousness, and holiness — matches both the Shorter and WLC.
- **Romans 2:14-15** for the law written on hearts — matches WLC footnote 65 and 1689 reference.
- **Ecclesiastes 7:29** for power to fulfill the law — matches WLC footnote 66 and 1689 reference.
- **Genesis 1:28; Psalm 8:6-8** for dominion — Genesis 1:28 matches WLC footnote 67. Psalm 8:6-8 is a valuable addition connecting creation dominion to the Psalter's theology of human dignity.
- **Genesis 2:16-17; Genesis 3:6; Ecclesiastes 7:29** for mutability/subject to fall — Genesis 3:6 matches WLC footnote 68. Genesis 2:16-17 and Ecclesiastes 7:29 add the context of the positive command and man's original uprightness. Well-chosen.
