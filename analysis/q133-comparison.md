# Q133: What is required in the ninth commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q133 closely follows WLC Q144 in structure and content, providing a faithful modernized exposition of the ninth commandment's positive duties. It preserves nearly all the WLC's content with strong structural solidarity. However, it omits the Shorter Catechism's key phrase "especially in witnessbearing," which specifically emphasizes the judicial/testimonial context as the primary application of the commandment. Additionally, the WLC's word "fully" is replaced with "without equivocation," which shifts from a positive duty (complete disclosure) to a negative prohibition (avoiding ambiguity), and the WLC's "honest" (from Philippians 4:8) is omitted in the final clause.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What is required in the ninth commandment? A. The ninth commandment requireth the maintaining and promoting of truth between man and man, and of our own neighbour's good name, especially in witnessbearing." (Proof texts: Zech. 8:16; Jn. 5:12; Pr. 14:5, 25)

- **Omissions:** The Shorter's phrase **"especially in witnessbearing"** is absent from the BLC. This is a significant qualifier that identifies the judicial/testimonial context as the *primary* and most weighty application of the ninth commandment ("Thou shalt not bear false witness"). The BLC does address "all matters relating to the law and justice" as one application but does not single out witnessbearing as the *especially* important context the way the Shorter does. The Shorter's "especially" creates a hierarchy of application — witnessbearing is the paramount duty — which the BLC flattens.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter's compact formula ("maintaining and promoting of truth between man and man, and of our own neighbour's good name") is preserved in the BLC's opening section, though "our own" good name is listed alongside "others" rather than treating the neighbor's name as the primary concern. The Shorter's proof text Pr. 14:5, 25 (which specifically concerns faithful witnesses) underscores the witnessbearing emphasis that the BLC lacks.
- The BLC's expansion from the Shorter's concise answer into detailed duties is appropriate for a Larger Catechism and follows the WLC pattern faithfully.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God) and Chapter 23 (Of Lawful Oaths and Vows)

The 1689 Confession does not provide a commandment-by-commandment exposition of the Decalogue. Chapter 19 treats the moral law generally, affirming its perpetual binding authority:

> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation." (19.5)

Chapter 23 on Lawful Oaths and Vows is the most directly relevant confession chapter, touching on truthfulness:

> "A lawful Oath is a part of religious worship, wherein the person swearing in Truth, Righteousness, and Judgement, solemnly calleth God to witness what he sweareth..." (23.1)
> "An Oath is to be taken in the plain, and common sense of the words; without equivocation, or mental reservation." (23.4)

- **Omissions:** None of doctrinal significance. The 1689 does not provide a detailed ninth commandment exposition, so the BLC appropriately draws from the catechetical tradition rather than the confession text.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC's language about speaking "without equivocation" (section 4) directly echoes 1689 23.4's prohibition of equivocation and mental reservation in oaths, appropriately extending this principle to all truthful speech.
- The BLC's treatment is consistent with the 1689's general teaching on the perpetual moral law and the duty of truthfulness in oaths and vows.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q144: "What are the duties required in the ninth commandment? A. The duties required in the ninth commandment are, the preserving and promoting of truth between man and man, and the good name of our neighbour, as well as our own; appearing and standing for the truth; and from the heart, sincerely, freely, clearly, and fully, speaking the truth, and only the truth, in matters of judgment and justice, and in all other things whatsoever; a charitable esteem of our neighbours; loving, desiring, and rejoicing in their good name; sorrowing for, and covering of their infirmities; freely acknowledging of their gifts and graces, defending their innocency; a ready receiving of a good report, and unwillingness to admit of an evil report, concerning them; discouraging tale-bearers, flatterers, and slanderers; love and care of our own good name, and defending it when need requireth; keeping of lawful promises; studying and practicing of whatsoever things are true, honest, lovely, and of good report."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None. The ninth commandment involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. The WLC's adverb **"fully"** is replaced with **"without equivocation"** in the BLC. The WLC lists five positive adverbs for how truth should be spoken: "sincerely, freely, clearly, and fully." The BLC replaces "fully" with "without equivocation," which shifts from a positive duty of completeness to a negative prohibition against ambiguity. While "without equivocation" has good confessional warrant (1689 23.4), it is not synonymous with "fully." One can speak without equivocation yet still withhold relevant truth; speaking "fully" requires more comprehensive disclosure. This is a subtle weakening.
  2. The WLC's final clause references "whatsoever things are true, **honest**, lovely, and of good report" (drawing from Phil. 4:8 KJV). The BLC renders this as "things that are true, **noble**, lovely, and admirable." The substitution of "noble" for "honest" and "admirable" for "of good report" reflects a modernization of the KJV language (the Greek *semna* can mean "noble/honorable" and *euphema* can mean "admirable/well-spoken-of"). This is a reasonable translation update, though "honest" carries a direct connection to the truthfulness theme of the ninth commandment that "noble" does not.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  1. **"and only the truth"** — The WLC says "speaking the truth, and only the truth." The BLC renders this as "speaking the truth and nothing but the truth." This is a modernization that preserves the meaning adequately (echoing courtroom oath language), though "nothing but the truth" is more colloquial.
  2. **"fully"** — replaced with "without equivocation" as noted above.
  3. **"honest"** — replaced with "noble" in the Phil. 4:8 allusion.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Structure:** The BLC follows the WLC's ordering of duties almost exactly: (1) general principle of truth and good name, (2) standing for truth, (3) manner of speaking truth, (4) scope of truthful speech, (5) charitable regard for neighbors, (6) their good name, (7) sorrowing for/covering failings, (8) acknowledging gifts, (9) defending innocence, (10) receiving good/bad reports, (11) discouraging gossips, (12) own good name, (13) keeping promises, (14) Phil. 4:8 virtues. This is excellent structural solidarity.

2. **"Charitable esteem" vs. "charitable regard":** The WLC's "charitable esteem of our neighbours" becomes the BLC's "charitable regard for others." "Esteem" implies a higher positive valuation than "regard," which is more neutral. This is a minor flattening of the WLC's stronger language.

3. **"Sorrowing for" vs. "regretting":** The WLC's "sorrowing for... their infirmities" becomes "regretting... their failings." "Sorrowing" conveys deeper emotional engagement than "regretting," which can be more detached. "Infirmities" (weaknesses) is softer than "failings" (faults). These shifts somewhat change the pastoral tone — the WLC portrays a more sympathetic grief over others' weaknesses, while the BLC is more matter-of-fact about others' faults.

4. **"Studying and practicing" vs. "focus our lives and thoughts on":** The WLC's concluding duty is "studying and practicing of whatsoever things are true, honest, lovely, and of good report." The BLC renders this as "do the best we can to focus our lives and thoughts on things that are true, noble, lovely, and admirable." The WLC's "studying and practicing" implies both intellectual engagement and active behavioral pursuit. The BLC's "focus our lives and thoughts" is comparable, though "do the best we can" introduces a qualifier not present in the WLC that slightly softens the imperative.

5. **"Keeping of lawful promises" handling:** The WLC says "keeping of lawful promises." The BLC expands this to "keep every lawful promise we make no matter what," adding the emphatic "no matter what" and the proof text Ps. 15:4 ("who keeps an oath even when it hurts"). This is a faithful and effective strengthening of the WLC's point.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts largely correspond to the WLC's traditional citations, with some updates and additions.
- The opening section (Zech. 8:16; Eph. 4:25; 3 John 12) aligns with WLC footnote 840 (Zech. 8:16) and adds appropriate NT support.
- Proverbs 31:8-9 for "come forward and stand up for the truth" is an apt choice, though the WLC cites Proverbs 31:8-9 more broadly.
- The proof text for "especially in witnessbearing" (Pr. 14:5, 25) from the Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel in the BLC, consistent with the omission of that emphasis.
- 2 Samuel 14:18-20 is cited for "without equivocation," which is an interesting choice — the passage concerns Joab's plot using the woman of Tekoa. The WLC's corresponding footnotes for "fully" cite Acts 20:20, 27, which the BLC also includes in the same section.
- The Phil. 4:8 citation is shared between BLC and WLC for the concluding duty, though the BLC's modernized rendering of the virtues reflects ESV/NIV language rather than KJV.
- Ps. 15:4 for promise-keeping is shared between BLC and WLC.
