# Q122: What reason is added to the fifth commandment emphasizing how important it is to obey it?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q122 faithfully preserves the theological content of both the Shorter Catechism and WLC Q133 on the reason annexed to the fifth commandment. The answer includes the scriptural quotation, identifies it as an express promise of long life and prosperity, and retains the critical qualification that this promise holds "if these glorify God and are for their good." The modernized language is clear and the theological substance is intact.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What is the reason annexed to the fifth commandment? A. The reason annexed to the fifth commandment is a promise of long life and prosperity (as far as it shall serve for God's glory, and their own good) to all such as keep this commandment (Deut. 5:16; Eph. 6:2, 3)."
- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter says "a promise" where BLC says "expressly promise" — the BLC actually strengthens the language here, matching the WLC's "express promise."
- Structural changes: The BLC splits the answer into two sections: first quoting the Scripture, then explaining its meaning. The Shorter combines both into a single statement. This structural change is pedagogically sound and does not alter the theology.
- The critical qualification "as far as it shall serve for God's glory, and their own good" is preserved in the BLC as "if these glorify God and are for their good." The meaning is identical in modernized form.
- Proof texts: The Shorter cites Deut. 5:16 and Eph. 6:2-3. The BLC cites Exodus 20:12 for the quotation itself (appropriate since it's the Exodus version of the Decalogue), then Deut. 5:16 and Eph. 6:2-3 for the explanation — matching the Shorter's citations and adding 1 Kings 8:25.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 19, Paragraph 6 — "The Promises of it likewise shew them Gods approbation of Obedience, and what blessings they may expect upon the performance thereof, though not as due to them by the Law as a Covenant of Works; so as mans doing Good and refraining from Evil, because the Law incourageth to the one and deterreth from the other, is no Evidence of his being under the Law and not under Grace."
- The 1689 does not expound individual commandments or their annexed reasons, so there is no direct parallel text to compare.
- The BLC's teaching that the promise of long life and prosperity is qualified ("if these glorify God and are for their good") is consistent with the 1689's framework that blessings from obedience are not "due to them by the Law as a Covenant of Works" but show God's approbation of obedience. The conditional language prevents a works-righteousness reading.
- Omissions: N/A — the Confession does not address this specific topic.
- Structural changes: N/A.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 133. What is the reason annexed to the fifth commandment, the more to enforce it? A. The reason annexed to the fifth commandment, in these words, That thy days may be long upon the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee, is an express promise of long life and prosperity, as far as it shall serve for God's glory and their own good, to all such as keep this commandment."
- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC tracks the WLC very closely in both structure and substance.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this is an area where no Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement exists.
- Unexpected divergences: None of theological significance. The BLC's question wording adds "emphasizing how important it is to obey it" where the WLC has "the more to enforce it" — a stylistic modernization that preserves the sense.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC phrase "the more to enforce it" is replaced but not dropped — the BLC's question wording "emphasizing how important it is to obey it" captures the same idea. All substantive phrases from the WLC answer are preserved in the BLC.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's phrasing "if these glorify God and are for their good" uses "these" to refer back to "long life and prosperity," which is grammatically clear in context. The antecedent is the promised blessings, and the qualification is that God grants them insofar as they serve his glory and the believer's good. This matches the WLC/Shorter's "as far as it shall serve for God's glory and their own good."
- The BLC appropriately splits the answer into two sections: (1) identifying the words of the reason, and (2) explaining what the words mean. This mirrors the WLC's structure of quoting then explaining.
- The addition of 1 Kings 8:25 as a proof text is a reasonable expansion, as it connects the conditional promise of blessing to obedience in the Davidic covenant context.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Exodus 20:12** — Cited for the quotation of the commandment with its reason. Appropriate as the primary Decalogue text.
- **Deuteronomy 5:16** — The parallel Decalogue text, which adds "and that it may go well with you" (the prosperity element). Present in both the Shorter and BLC.
- **1 Kings 8:25** — Solomon's prayer referencing God's conditional promise to David. An addition beyond the WLC/Shorter, reinforcing the pattern of conditional divine blessing upon obedience. A reasonable expansion.
- **Ephesians 6:2-3** — Paul's quotation of the fifth commandment as "the first commandment with a promise." Present in both the Shorter and BLC. Key NT text confirming the continuing relevance of this promise.
