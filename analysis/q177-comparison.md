# Q177: What is the significance of praying in Christ's name?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q177 closely tracks WLC Q180 in modernized form and preserves the core theological content including Christ's mediation. However, it subtly shifts the WLC's "hope of acceptance in prayer" to "hope that our prayers will be answered," moving from the concept of being received favorably at the throne of grace to the expectation of receiving what is asked. Both the WLC and the 1689 Confession use "acceptance/accepted" language, making this an unexplained divergence from both standards.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism defines prayer but does not unpack the meaning of praying "in the name of Christ":

> Q. What is prayer?
> A. Prayer is an offering up our desires to God (Ps. 62:8), by the assistance of the Holy Spirit (Rom. 8:26), for things agreeable to his will (1 Jn. 5:14; Rom. 8:27), in the name of Christ (Jn. 16:23), believing (Mt. 21:22; James 1:6), with confession of our sins (Ps. 32:5, 6; Dan. 9:4), and thankful acknowledgments of his mercies (Ph. 4:6).

- Omissions: N/A — the Shorter does not elaborate on what praying in Christ's name means, so the BLC is expanding rather than paraphrasing.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC appropriately unpacks a concept the Shorter merely names.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraphs 2-3

Paragraph 2: "Religious Worship is to be given to God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and to him alone; not to Angels, Saints, or any other Creatures; and since the fall, not without a Mediator, nor in the Mediation of any other but Christ alone."

Paragraph 3: "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance; and when with others, in a known tongue."

- Omissions: The 1689 states prayer must be "according to his Will" and lists qualifications of acceptable prayer: "understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance." These are not expected in BLC Q177 (which addresses the narrower question of what it means to pray *in Christ's name*, not what constitutes acceptable prayer generally). No material omission.
- Structural changes: The 1689 uses the language "that it may be **accepted**" — prayer is made in the Name of the Son so that it may be *accepted*. The BLC shifts this to "hope that our prayers will be **answered**." The 1689's concern is with the acceptance of the worshipper's prayer before God through the Mediator, not with the outcome of receiving what is requested. This is a subtle but notable shift.
- The BLC rightly preserves the mediation theme from 22.2-3.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q180:

> Q. 180. What is it to pray in the name of Christ?
> A. To pray in the name of Christ is, in obedience to his command, and in confidence on his promises, to ask mercy for his sake; not by bare mentioning of his name, but by drawing our encouragement to pray, and our boldness, strength, and hope of acceptance in prayer, from Christ and his mediation.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC follows WLC Q180 nearly word-for-word in modernized English, preserving the three-part structure: (1) obedience and reliance on promises to ask mercy, (2) not merely mentioning the name, (3) drawing encouragement, boldness, strength, and hope from Christ and His mediation.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected or found; this doctrine is common ground.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"hope of acceptance in prayer"** (WLC) becomes **"hope that our prayers will be answered"** (BLC). "Acceptance" refers to being favorably received at the throne of grace through Christ's mediation — a concept rooted in Hebrews 4:14-16 (the proof text). "Answered" shifts the focus from the mediatorial access to the outcome. This is a weakening of precision. The WLC's language better reflects the proof text (Heb 4:16: "come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need" — emphasis on access and mercy, not on specific answers to requests).
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"acceptance in prayer"** — replaced with "prayers will be answered," losing the mediatorial-access nuance.
  - **"bare"** (in "bare mentioning") — replaced with "just" in "not just mentioning." Minor; "just" captures the meaning adequately though with slightly less precision.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The acceptance/answered shift** is the most theologically significant finding. The WLC and 1689 both emphasize that praying in Christ's name is about *acceptance before God* — our prayers being received through the Mediator. The BLC shifts this to *answers from God* — our prayers being granted. While these concepts are related (accepted prayers may be answered), they are not identical. A prayer can be accepted (favorably received by God through Christ) without being answered in the way requested (God may say no in wisdom). The WLC/1689 language preserves the gracious mediatorial access without implying that praying in Christ's name guarantees the outcome desired.

2. **Structural fidelity is otherwise excellent.** The three-section breakdown of the BLC maps precisely to the three clauses of WLC Q180. The proof texts are identical. The modernization is generally well done.

3. **The BLC appropriately combines WLC Q180 and Q181 territory.** WLC Q181 asks *why* we pray in Christ's name (because of human sinfulness and Christ being the only Mediator). The BLC's Q177 focuses on the *what* (Q180), which is the right scope for this question.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC uses identical proof texts to WLC Q180:
- **John 14:13-14; John 16:24; Daniel 9:17** (section 1) = WLC note 1161 — supports asking in Christ's name / for His sake
- **Matthew 7:21; Luke 6:46** (section 2) = WLC note 1162 (Matt 7:21) — supports "not merely mentioning the name." The BLC adds Luke 6:46 ("Why do you call me 'Lord, Lord'..."), which is an appropriate supplementary text.
- **Hebrews 4:14-16; 1 John 5:13-15** (section 3) = WLC note 1163 — supports drawing boldness from Christ's mediation. Notably, Hebrews 4:14-16 speaks of coming "boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy" — the language of *access and acceptance*, not of having prayers *answered*. This makes the BLC's shift from "acceptance" to "answered" somewhat at odds with its own proof text. 1 John 5:13-15 does speak of confidence that God "hears" us and we "have the petitions that we desired of him," which supports the "answered" language — but this text was not in the WLC's proof texts for this phrase. The BLC may have added it to support its rewording.
