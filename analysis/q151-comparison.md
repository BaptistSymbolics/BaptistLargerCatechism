# Q151: How many sacraments has Christ established in His Church under the New Testament?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q151 is a near-verbatim modernization of WLC Q164, teaching that Christ instituted only two sacraments (baptism and the Lord's Supper) under the New Testament. The Baptist Shorter Catechism has no direct "how many sacraments" parallel, so the BLC rightly draws from the WLC structure here. The doctrine aligns with 1689 Confession Chapter XXVIII, which names baptism and the Lord's Supper as the two ordinances of Christ's institution.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest is the outward means question:

> Q. What are the outward means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption?
> A. The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption are his ordinances, especially the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer; all which means are made effectual to the elect for salvation (Mt. 28:19, 20; Acts 2:42, 46, 47).

- Omissions: None relevant. The Shorter does not have a question enumerating sacraments, so there is nothing to omit.
- Structural changes: The BLC appropriately introduces a question (following WLC structure) that the Shorter does not include. This is legitimate expansion in a "Larger" catechism.
- The Shorter's outward means question lists four means (word, baptism, Lord's Supper, prayer); the BLC handles this scope at Q147 and narrows to the two sacraments here. This is appropriate catechetical development.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XXVIII, Paragraph 1:

> "Baptism and the Lords Supper are ordinances of positive, and soveraign institution; appointed by the Lord Jesus the only Law-giver, to be continued in his Church to the end of the world." (Matt. 28:19-20; 1 Cor. 11:26)

- Omissions: The 1689's language of "positive, and soveraign institution" and "the only Law-giver" and "to be continued...to the end of the world" are not present in Q151. However, these elements appear in BLC Q152 (baptism definition), which describes baptism as "a sacrament of positive and sovereign institution...to be continued in His church to the end of the world." The BLC distributes the 1689's content across multiple questions appropriately.
- Structural changes: None. The 1689 names only baptism and the Lord's Supper in this chapter, implicitly teaching the "only two" that the BLC makes explicit.
- Terminology note: The 1689 never uses "sacrament," only "ordinance." The BLC uses "sacrament" following the WLC. This is an intentional terminological choice already established in BLC Q149, which defines "sacrament" as "a holy ordinance established by Christ in His church." The terms are treated as synonymous, which is theologically defensible and follows the broader Reformed tradition's usage.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q164:

> Q. 164. How many sacraments hath Christ instituted in his church under the New Testament?
> A. Under the New Testament Christ hath instituted in his church only two sacraments, baptism and the Lord's supper.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC answer is a near-verbatim modernization of the WLC answer.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None in this question. The question concerns the number of sacraments, not mode, subjects, or ecclesiology.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The only change is "established" for "instituted" — a simple vocabulary modernization with no theological significance.
- Key phrases dropped: None. All key elements are preserved: "Under the New Testament," "in his church," "only two sacraments," "baptism and the Lord's supper."

## Detailed Notes
- The word "only" is theologically important here, excluding Roman Catholic claims of seven sacraments and any other additions. Both the WLC and BLC preserve this restrictive term.
- The phrase "under the New Testament" is significant, distinguishing the New Covenant sacraments from Old Testament signs (circumcision, Passover). Both texts preserve this qualifier.
- The BLC's use of "His Church" (capitalized, with possessive pronoun) slightly modernizes "his church" but preserves the same theological meaning — these are ordinances given to the visible church, not to individuals privately.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Matthew 28:19** — The Great Commission, establishing baptism. Cited by both BLC and WLC (proof text 1057). Also cited by 1689 XXVIII.1.
- **1 Corinthians 11:20, 23-26** — Institution of the Lord's Supper. The WLC cites 11:20, 23; the BLC extends to 11:23-26, capturing more of the institution narrative. The 1689 cites 11:26.
- **Matthew 26:26-28** — The BLC adds this dominical institution text not cited by the WLC for Q164 but used by the 1689 in Chapter XXX. This is a valuable addition connecting to Christ's own words at the Last Supper.
- The proof texts are well-chosen and appropriately cover both sacraments with institution texts.
