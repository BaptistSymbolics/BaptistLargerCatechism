# Q180: For whom should we pray?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q180 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q183, preserving all categories of persons for whom prayer is and is not to be made. It accurately expands the 1689 Confession's more compressed statement (22.4) into the enumerated form of the WLC, and the minor wording variations ("fellow believers" for "brethren," "civil authorities" for "magistrates") are legitimate modernizations that preserve meaning. No omissions, contradictions, or structural changes of concern.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest reference is the Lord's Prayer preface question: "The preface of the Lord's prayer, which is Our Father which art in heaven (Mt. 6:9), teacheth us to draw near to God with all holy reverence and confidence, as children to a father, able and ready to help us (Rom. 8:15; Lk. 11:13; Is. 24:8); and that we should pray with and for others (Acts 12:5; 1 Tim. 2:1, 2)."
- Omissions: N/A — the Shorter does not enumerate prayer subjects; this is properly a Larger Catechism expansion.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC appropriately develops the Shorter's general principle ("pray with and for others") into specific categories.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraph 4 — "Prayer is to be made for things lawful, and for all sorts of men living, or that shall live hereafter; but not for the dead, nor for those of whom it may be known that they have sinned the sin unto death." (1 Tim. 2:1,2; 2 Sam. 7:29; 2 Sam. 12:21,22,23; 1 John 5:16)
- Omissions: None. The 1689 states the general principle ("all sorts of men living, or that shall live hereafter") and the two exclusions (the dead; those who have sinned the sin unto death). The BLC preserves all of this content.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC expands the 1689's compressed statement by enumerating the specific categories (church, authorities, ministers, ourselves, fellow believers, enemies) before restating the general scope and exclusions — the same approach the WLC takes. This expansion is consistent with the Confession's intent.
- The BLC's phrase "for things lawful" from 1689 22.4 is not addressed in Q180, but this concerns *what* to pray for rather than *for whom*, so it belongs to a different question.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q183: "For whom are we to pray? A. We are to pray for the whole church of Christ upon earth; for magistrates, and ministers; for ourselves, our brethren, yea, our enemies; and for all sorts of men living, or that shall live hereafter; but not for the dead, nor for those that are known to have sinned the sin unto death."
- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues where Baptist-Presbyterian differences would appear.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's "yea" before "our enemies" is dropped, but this is merely an intensifier — the BLC retains "our enemies" as a prayer subject. The WLC's "all sorts of men living" is rendered "all people who are alive" — a modernization that preserves meaning. The WLC's "our brethren" becomes "fellow believers" — an accurate modernization.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC breaks the WLC's single flowing answer into sectioned proof-text units, which is its standard format. This allows individual Scripture citations for each category, which is pedagogically helpful.
- The BLC's "entire church of Christ on earth" faithfully renders WLC's "whole church of Christ upon earth."
- "Civil authorities" for "magistrates" is a natural modernization; the proof text (1 Tim. 2:1-2) is identical.
- "Fellow believers" for "brethren" removes potential gender confusion in modern English while preserving the theological meaning of praying for those in the community of faith.
- The BLC adds "those who are going to live in the future" as a separate section from "all people who are alive," mirroring the WLC's structure and the 1689's "or that shall live hereafter."
- The negative exclusions ("not for the dead," "not for those who have sinned the sin that leads to death") are fully preserved with identical proof texts.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts are essentially identical to the WLC's traditional citations. Ephesians 6:18 and Psalm 28:9 are added for the church; Genesis 32:11 for praying for ourselves; James 5:16 for fellow believers — these are appropriate additions that strengthen the scriptural grounding.
- The two exclusion proof texts (2 Sam. 12:21-23 for not praying for the dead; 1 John 5:16 for the sin unto death) match both the WLC and 1689 exactly.
- 1 Timothy 2:1-2 appears twice: once for civil authorities and once for all people generally, which reflects the text's own dual application.
- John 17:20 ("I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word") is a strong proof for praying for future generations, paired with 2 Samuel 7:29 which also appears in the 1689.
