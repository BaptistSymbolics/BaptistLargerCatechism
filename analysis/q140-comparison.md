# Q140: What sins are more heinous because of the harm that results from them?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q140 closely follows the WLC Q151 structure and covers virtually all the same aggravating factors with appropriate modernization. However, the question's framing—"because of the harm that results from them"—narrows the WLC/Shorter's broader concept of "aggravations" to a consequentialist category ("harm"), when many of the listed factors are about the sinner's culpability or the nature of the offense rather than downstream harm. Two WLC terms ("gifts" and "men of eminency" as distinct categories, plus "wilfully" and "impudently" as separate manner-adverbs) are dropped without clear theological reason.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- Q. "Are all transgressions of the law equally heinous?"
- A. "Some sins in themselves, and by reason of several aggravations, are more heinous in the sight of God than others." (Ez. 8:6, 13, 15; 1 Jn. 5:16; Ps. 78:17, 32, 56)

The Shorter Catechism does not expand on what those "several aggravations" are—that is the work of the Larger Catechism. BLC Q139 parallels this Shorter question, and BLC Q140 provides the detailed expansion (paralleling WLC Q151).

- **Omissions:** The Shorter's phrase "several aggravations" is replaced in BLC Q139/Q140 with "the harm that results from them." This reframes the concept. "Aggravations" is a broader theological term covering factors that increase the heinousness of sin intrinsically (the sinner's knowledge, office, the nature of the act), not merely the consequential harm. Many items listed in the BLC's own answer (e.g., sinning deliberately, presumptuously, against conscience) are about culpability rather than "harm that results." The Shorter's language is more precise.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter establishes that sins vary "in themselves, and by reason of several aggravations." The BLC Q139 restructures this as "some sins in themselves and others by reason of the harm that results from them," which creates an artificial dichotomy between inherently heinous sins and those heinous by consequence, whereas the Shorter treats these as overlapping categories.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 6 (Of the Fall of Man, of Sin, and of the Punishment thereof) and Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God)

The 1689 does not contain a specific article addressing the degrees or aggravations of sin comparable to WLC Q151. The relevant doctrinal framework is:

> Chapter 6.4: "From this original corruption, whereby we are utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all good, and wholly inclined to all evil, do proceed all actual transgressions."

> Chapter 19.5-6: The moral law binds all persons; it serves as a "Rule of Life" discovering "the sinfull pollutions of their Natures, Hearts and Lives" and showing "what even their Sins deserve."

- **Omissions:** No direct parallel to analyze for omissions; the 1689 does not itemize aggravating factors.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC is working in territory the 1689 leaves to catechetical exposition. The BLC's treatment is consistent with the 1689's framework of the moral law binding all persons and sins having varying severity.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q151:
> "What are those aggravations that make some sins more heinous than others?"
> "Sins receive their aggravations,
> 1. From the persons offending: if they be of riper age, greater experience or grace, eminent for profession, gifts, place, office, guides to others, and whose example is likely to be followed by others.
> 2. From the parties offended: if immediately against God, his attributes, and worship; against Christ, and his grace; the Holy Spirit, his witness, and workings; against superiors, men of eminency, and such as we stand especially related and engaged unto; against any of the saints, particularly weak brethren, the souls of them, or any other, and the common good of all or many.
> 3. From the nature and quality of the offense: if it be against the express letter of the law, break many commandments, contain in it many sins; if not only conceived in the heart, but breaks forth in words and actions, scandalize others, and admit of no reparation; if against means, mercies, judgments, light of nature, conviction of conscience, public or private admonition, censures of the church, civil punishments; and our prayers, purposes, promises, vows, covenants, and engagements to God or men; if done deliberately, wilfully, presumptuously, impudently, boastingly, maliciously, frequently, obstinately, with delight, continuance, or relapsing after repentance.
> 4. From circumstances of time and place: if on the Lord's day, or other times of divine worship; or immediately before or after these, or other helps to prevent or remedy such miscarriages; if in public, or in the presence of others, who are thereby likely to be provoked or defiled."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC follows the WLC's four-part structure exactly (persons offending, parties offended, nature/quality, circumstances of time/place) and covers nearly all the same ground with modernized language.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None identified. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology differences.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"Gifts" dropped** (Section 1): The WLC lists "gifts" as a distinct aggravating factor alongside "profession," "place," and "office." The BLC omits this. In the WLC, "gifts" refers to spiritual gifts or natural talents that increase responsibility; its omission removes a meaningful category.
  2. **"Men of eminency" dropped** (Section 2): The WLC distinguishes between "superiors" (those in authority over us) and "men of eminency" (those of notable standing). The BLC collapses this to just "those above us," losing the WLC's distinction between hierarchical authority and social/spiritual prominence.
  3. **"Wilfully" dropped** (Section 3): The WLC lists "deliberately, wilfully, presumptuously" as three distinct categories. The BLC has only "deliberately" and "presumptuously," dropping "wilfully." While "deliberately" and "wilfully" overlap, the WLC distinguishes between premeditated sin (deliberate) and sin committed with a hardened, resolved will (wilful). "Wilfully" carries overtones of Hebrews 10:26 ("if we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth").
  4. **"Impudently" dropped** (Section 3): The WLC lists "impudently" alongside "boastingly" as separate manners. The BLC drops "impudently" (shameless, brazen sinning). "Boastfully" does not cover the same semantic ground as "impudently."
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "gifts" (as distinct aggravating factor for the person offending)
  - "men of eminency" (as distinct category of parties offended)
  - "wilfully" (as distinct manner of sinning)
  - "impudently" (as distinct manner of sinning)

## Detailed Notes

### Framing: "Aggravations" vs. "Harm"
The most notable theological issue is the question's framing. The WLC asks about "aggravations that make some sins more heinous"—a broad category encompassing anything that increases a sin's heinousness. The BLC asks about sins "more heinous because of the harm that results from them." This consequentialist framing does not fit the content of the answer itself:
- Section 1 (persons offending): An older person sinning is more heinous not because of greater "harm resulting" but because of greater culpability.
- Section 3 (nature/quality): Sinning "deliberately" or "presumptuously" describes the manner of commission, not the resulting harm.
- Section 3 (against conscience): Sinning against one's own conscience is an intrinsic aggravation, not a harm-based one.

The answer actually matches the WLC's broader "aggravations" framework, while the question imposes a narrower "harm" framework. This creates a mild tension between question and answer.

### BLC Addition: "Clearly know better"
The BLC adds "clearly know better" (James 4:17; Luke 12:47-48) as a distinct category in Section 1 that the WLC does not separately list among the persons offending. The WLC addresses knowledge later under "light of nature" and "conviction of conscience" in Section 3. The BLC's placement here is defensible but reorganizes the WLC's theological structure—moving a quality of the offense (sinning against knowledge) into a quality of the offender.

### Modernization
The vast majority of language changes are appropriate modernizations: "riper age" → "older," "saints" → "fellow believer," "weak brethren" → "weaker in the faith," "admonition" → "warnings," "reparation" → "cannot be made right," "miscarriages" → "failures," "provoked" → "aroused."

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are generally well-chosen and often overlap with the WLC's traditional citations:
- Jeremiah 2:8 for those who commit sins is appropriate for leaders/teachers sinning.
- The addition of Matthew 21:38-39 and 1 John 5:10 as a header for "those sinned against" is a helpful addition.
- 1 Corinthians 8:11-12 and Romans 14:13,15,21 for weaker brethren are standard Reformed proof texts.
- The Revelation 17:6 citation for sinning against fellow believers is an unusual choice; it refers to Babylon being "drunk with the blood of the saints," which speaks to persecution of believers rather than the interpersonal aggravation context.
- Zephaniah 2:8-11 for sinning against "any fellow believer" is an odd choice—this passage concerns Moab and Ammon's taunts against Israel as a nation, not individual believers. The WLC's citations for this category are more targeted.
- The BLC's proof texts for "repeatedly" (Numbers 14:22) and "obstinately" (Zechariah 7:11-12) are well-chosen and match the WLC tradition.
