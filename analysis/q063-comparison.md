# Q63: What is justification?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q63 is substantially faithful to the 1689 Confession and Shorter Catechism on justification. It commendably preserves the 1689's distinctive language on Christ's "active obedience unto the whole law and passive obedience in His death" which the WLC generalizes. However, it omits the Shorter's key phrase "only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us" and the 1689's crucial language about faith as "receiving and resting on him and his righteousness," reducing faith's role from actively receiving and resting upon Christ to merely "appropriating" righteousness. The BLC also drops the 1689's explicit rejection of faith itself being imputed as righteousness.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is justification?
> A. Justification is an act of God's free grace, wherein he pardoneth all our sins (Rom. 3:24, 25; and 4:6, 7, 8), and accepteth us as righteous in his sight (2 Cor. 5:19, 21), only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us (Rom. 5:17-19), and received by faith alone (Gal. 2:16; Phil. 3:9).

- **Omissions:**
  - The Shorter's phrase **"only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us"** is the hinge of the answer — it states the sole ground of justification in a single, clear clause. The BLC replaces this with a more detailed explanation about active/passive obedience, which is theologically richer but structurally shifts from the Shorter's elegant ground-then-instrument pattern.
  - The Shorter says **"received by faith alone"** — the BLC says **"which can only be appropriated by faith."** The word "received" is the standard Reformed term for faith's instrumental role (cf. 1689 11.1: "receiving, and resting on him"). "Appropriated" is less precise and less confessionally grounded.
- **Structural changes:**
  - The Shorter follows a tight four-part structure: (1) act of free grace, (2) pardons sins, (3) accepts as righteous — only for Christ's imputed righteousness, (4) received by faith alone. The BLC expands part (3) into a three-part explanation (not by works, imputation of active obedience, imputation of passive obedience) which breaks apart the Shorter's unified "only for" clause. This is an expansion rather than a distortion, but it moves away from mirroring the Shorter's compact formulation.
  - The BLC says "unto sinners" where the Shorter says "wherein he pardoneth all our sins" — a minor modernization from the WLC that is appropriate for a Larger Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 11, Paragraph 1:
> "Those whom God Effectually calleth, he also freely justifieth, not by infusing Righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting, and accepting their Persons as Righteous; not for any thing wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ's sake alone, not by imputing faith it self, the act of beleiving, or any other evangelical obedience to them, as their Righteousness; but by imputing Christs active obedience unto the whole Law, and passive obedience in his death, for their whole and sole Righteousnnss, they receiving, and resting on him, and his Righteousness, by Faith; which faith they have not of themselves, it is the gift of God."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"not by infusing Righteousness into them"** — The 1689 explicitly rejects the Roman Catholic doctrine of infused righteousness. The BLC drops this important anti-Roman polemic. While "not because of anything worked in them" partially covers this ground, it lacks the precision of rejecting infusion specifically.
  - **"not by imputing faith it self, the act of beleiving, or any other evangelical obedience to them, as their Righteousness"** — This crucial clause rejects the Arminian/neonomianism position that faith itself is counted as the believer's righteousness. The BLC entirely omits this. This is a notable doctrinal omission.
  - **"receiving, and resting on him, and his Righteousness, by Faith"** — The 1689's description of faith's role is rich and active: believers *receive and rest on Christ and his righteousness*. The BLC's "which can only be appropriated by faith" is thinner and less relational. The 1689 emphasizes union with the Person of Christ ("on him"), not merely the acquisition of a benefit.
  - **"which faith they have not of themselves, it is the gift of God"** — The 1689 explicitly affirms that justifying faith is God's gift. The BLC omits this, though it may be covered elsewhere in the catechism.
  - **"for Christ's sake alone"** — The 1689's "alone" is an important qualifier that the BLC does not directly replicate.
  - **Effectual calling connection** — The 1689 begins with "Those whom God Effectually calleth, he also freely justifieth," linking justification to effectual calling. The BLC drops this ordo salutis connection in its justification definition.
- **Structural changes:**
  - The 1689 uses a dialectical structure: not X, but Y. It explicitly rejects wrong views (infusion, faith-as-righteousness) before stating the positive doctrine. The BLC preserves the "not by works" denial but drops the more specific rejections. This simplifies but weakens the polemical precision.
- **Agreement:**
  - The BLC commendably preserves the 1689's distinctive language on "active obedience unto the whole law" and "passive obedience in his death" as "their whole and sole righteousness." This is a key Baptist confessional distinctive over against the WLC's more general language.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q70:
> "Justification is an act of God's free grace unto sinners, in which he pardoneth all their sins, accepteth and accounteth their persons righteous in his sight; not for any thing wrought in them, or done by them, but only for the perfect obedience and full satisfaction of Christ, by God imputed to them, and received by faith alone."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected on justification (this is not a baptism/ecclesiology/covenant theology locus), and none found.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC says **"accepteth and accounteth their persons righteous in his sight"** — the BLC says **"accepts them as righteous in His sight."** The BLC drops "accounteth," which is the forensic/declarative term. "Accepts as righteous" could be read as merely treating someone as righteous, while "accounteth" makes the legal reckoning explicit. This is a minor precision loss.
  - The WLC says **"received by faith alone"** — the BLC says **"which can only be appropriated by faith."** The WLC's "received by faith alone" is the classic Reformation formula. "Appropriated" is an unusual substitution that lacks confessional pedigree. The word "alone" (sola) is also weakened to "only... by faith" which, while logically equivalent, loses the emphatic Reformation cadence.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - **"accounteth"** — the forensic/declarative dimension of justification
  - **"received by faith alone"** — replaced with the less standard "appropriated by faith"
  - The WLC's **"perfect obedience and full satisfaction of Christ"** is replaced by the 1689's more specific "active obedience unto the whole law and passive obedience in His death" — this is a deliberate and appropriate preference for the 1689's formulation over the WLC's, showing proper Baptist confessional priority.

## Detailed Notes

1. **"Appropriated" vs. "received"**: This is the most notable word choice in Q63. The Reformed tradition consistently uses "receive" (Latin: *recipere*) for faith's instrumental role in justification. "Appropriate" (Latin: *appropriare*, to make one's own) has a different semantic range — it suggests taking possession of something rather than receiving a gift. While not heretical, it subtly shifts the emphasis from God's giving to the believer's taking. Neither the 1689, the Shorter, nor the WLC uses this term.

2. **Active and passive obedience distinction**: The BLC's preservation of the 1689's explicit active/passive obedience distinction is excellent. The WLC generalizes this as "perfect obedience and full satisfaction," while the 1689 (and now the BLC) makes the distinction explicit. This is a strength of the BLC.

3. **Missing anti-Arminian polemic**: The 1689's rejection of faith-as-imputed-righteousness was directed against the view (associated with Arminianism and later neonomianism) that God counts the act of believing as the believer's righteousness under a "new law." By omitting this, the BLC loses an important confessional boundary marker. In a catechetical context this may be addressed in a separate question, but its absence from the definition of justification itself is notable.

4. **"Unto sinners"**: The BLC follows the WLC in adding "unto sinners" to qualify the recipients of justification. This is a good addition that clarifies the gracious nature of the act — God justifies sinners, not the already-righteous.

5. **Structure as expansion**: The BLC effectively takes the Shorter's compact answer and expands it in the manner of a Larger Catechism, adding the active/passive obedience detail from the 1689. This is appropriate for the genre. The concern is not the expansion itself but the elements lost in the restructuring.

## Scripture Analysis

The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and largely overlap with the standards:
- **Romans 3:22-25** (free grace) — aligns with Shorter (Rom. 3:24-25) and WLC (Rom. 3:22, 24-25)
- **Romans 4:6-8** (pardon of sins) — aligns with Shorter (Rom. 4:6-8) and 1689 (Rom. 4:5-8)
- **2 Corinthians 5:19-21** (accepted as righteous) — aligns with Shorter and WLC
- **Romans 5:10, 19** (imputation of obedience) — close to WLC (Rom. 5:17-19) and 1689 (Rom. 5:17-19)
- **Titus 3:5-7** (not by works) — aligns with WLC (Titus 3:5, 7)
- **Hebrews 10:11-14** (passive obedience/satisfaction) — not in the parallel standards but appropriate
- **Matthew 3:15** (active obedience) — a distinctive addition supporting Christ's fulfillment of all righteousness; not in the standards but theologically apt
- **Acts 10:43; Galatians 2:16; Philippians 3:9** (faith alone) — aligns with WLC and Shorter
- **Jeremiah 23:6** (The LORD our Righteousness) — a strong addition not in the parallels
- **Romans 10:4** (Christ the end of the law) — supports active obedience; a good addition

Notable absence: **Romans 5:17** is cited by both the Shorter and WLC for imputation but is not directly cited in the BLC's imputation section (though Rom. 5:19 is cited). The BLC also does not cite **Romans 3:28** which the 1689 uses for faith as the alone instrument of justification — consistent with the BLC's shift from "faith alone" language to "appropriated by faith."
