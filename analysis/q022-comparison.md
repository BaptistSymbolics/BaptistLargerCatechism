# Q22: What was the providence of God toward man in the estate in which he was created?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q22 follows the WLC Q20 structure very closely, enumerating God's providential acts toward man in his created estate. The content is substantially faithful to all three standards. However, the BLC describes the covenant condition as "perfect obedience" where both the WLC ("personal, perfect, and perpetual") and the 1689 Confession ("personal entire exact and perpetual") use a fuller enumeration of qualifiers. The BLC follows the Shorter Catechism's simpler phrasing here, which is defensible for a catechism, but as a *Larger* Catechism meant to exposit the 1689, the reduced precision is notable.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What special act of providence did God exercise towards man in the estate wherein he was created? A. When God had created man, he entered into a covenant of life with him upon condition of perfect obedience: forbidding him to eat of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, upon pain of death (Gal. 3:12; Gen. 2:17)."

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter focuses narrowly on the covenant of life, while the BLC expands to cover the full range of providential acts (paradise, dominion, marriage, communion, Sabbath). This expansion is appropriate for a Larger Catechism.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC preserves the Shorter's covenant-of-life framework and its core formula: covenant of life → condition of perfect obedience → prohibition of the tree → pain of death.
- The BLC matches the Shorter's "perfect obedience" rather than the WLC's fuller "personal, perfect, and perpetual obedience." This is consistent with the Shorter as baseline but represents a missed opportunity for a Larger Catechism to provide fuller exposition.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Sections:**

*Chapter 4, Paragraph 3:* "Besides the Law written in their hearts, they received a command not to eat of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; which whilst they kept, they were happy in their Communion with God, and had dominion over the Creatures."

*Chapter 6, Paragraph 1:* "Although God created Man upright, and perfect, and gave him a righteous law, which had been unto life had he kept it, and threatned death upon the breach thereof..."

*Chapter 7, Paragraph 1:* "The distance between God and the Creature is so great, that although reasonable Creatures do owe obedience unto him as their Creator, yet they could never have attained the reward of Life, but by some voluntary condescension on Gods part, which he hath been pleased to express, by way of Covenant."

*Chapter 19, Paragraph 1:* "God gave to Adam a Law of universal obedience, written in his Heart, and a particular precept of not eating the Fruit of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; by which he bound him, and all his posterity to personal entire exact and perpetual obedience; promised life upon the fulfilling, and threatned death upon the breach of it; and indued him with power and ability to keep it."

- Omissions: The 1689 Ch. 19:1 describes the obedience required as "personal entire exact and perpetual." The BLC reduces this to "perfect." While "perfect" captures the essence, the 1689's fourfold enumeration carries specific theological weight: *personal* (not by proxy), *entire* (whole-souled, not partial), *exact* (precise, not approximate), and *perpetual* (without cessation). A Larger Catechism meant to exposit the 1689 would ideally preserve more of this specificity.
- The 1689 Ch. 7:1 emphasizes that the covenant was an act of "voluntary condescension" by God — man could never have attained life without covenantal arrangement. The BLC does not explicitly teach this principle, though it is implied by the covenantal framing.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's enumeration of providential acts (paradise, dominion, communion, marriage, Sabbath, covenant) tracks the 1689's teaching across Chapters 4, 6, and 7.
- The BLC's proof texts for the covenant section (Gen. 2:16-17; Hosea 6:7; Rom. 5:12-14) are theologically appropriate. Hosea 6:7 is a significant addition supporting the covenant of works/life framework.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 20. What was the providence of God toward man in the estate in which he was created? A. The providence of God toward man in the estate in which he was created, was the placing him in paradise, appointing him to dress it, giving him liberty to eat of the fruit of the earth; putting the creatures under his dominion, and ordaining marriage for his help; affording him communion with himself; instituting the sabbath; entering into a covenant of life with him, upon condition of personal, perfect, and perpetual obedience, of which the tree of life was a pledge; and forbidding to eat of the tree of knowledge of good and evil, upon the pain of death."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**. The BLC follows the WLC's structure, sequence, and content with remarkable fidelity. The question wording is identical. The answer covers the same eight providential acts in the same order.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question deals with creation and pre-fall covenant theology where Baptist and Presbyterian theology fully agree.
- Unexpected divergences: The WLC's "personal, perfect, and perpetual obedience" is reduced to "perfect obedience" in the BLC. This is the only substantive divergence and falls in an area of complete Baptist-Presbyterian agreement on the nature of the covenant of works/life.
- Key phrases dropped: "personal" and "perpetual" as qualifiers of the obedience required. These are not merely rhetorical flourishes but carry doctrinal weight: "personal" excludes vicarious obedience (relevant to the Adam-Christ typology), and "perpetual" establishes that the obligation was not temporary or probationary in a time-limited sense.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "personal, perfect, and perpetual" question:** This is the central issue. The WLC's threefold qualification and the 1689's fourfold qualification ("personal entire exact and perpetual") both emphasize that the Adamic covenant demanded more than generic "perfect obedience." The BLC, as a *Larger* Catechism, would be expected to provide at least the level of specificity found in the WLC rather than the Shorter's abbreviation. However, this is a matter of precision rather than error — "perfect obedience" is not *wrong*, merely less precise.

2. **Structural fidelity:** The BLC's decision to break the answer into discrete sections with individual proof texts is pedagogically helpful and preserves the theological relationships faithfully. The parenthetical treatment of the tree of life as a pledge maintains the WLC's subordinate grammatical relationship.

3. **"Keep it" addition:** The BLC says "appointing him to dress it and keep it" where the WLC has "appointing him to dress it." The addition of "keep it" follows Genesis 2:15 more completely and is a legitimate expansion.

4. **"By His providence" addition:** The BLC adds "God, by His providence" as an introductory framing that makes explicit what the WLC treats implicitly. This is a helpful clarification.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Genesis 2:8, 15** (paradise/vocation): Appropriate and complete.
- **Genesis 2:16** (liberty to eat): Appropriate.
- **Genesis 1:28** (dominion): Appropriate.
- **Genesis 2:18; Matthew 19:3-6; Ephesians 5:31** (marriage): The NT references strengthen the proof beyond the WLC's typical OT-only citations for this point.
- **Genesis 1:26-28; Genesis 3:8** (communion): Genesis 3:8 is an interesting choice — it describes God walking in the garden *after* the fall, but is commonly used to imply the pre-fall pattern of communion. Appropriate traditional usage.
- **Genesis 2:3; Exodus 20:11** (Sabbath): Appropriate.
- **Genesis 2:16-17; Hosea 6:7; Romans 5:12-14** (covenant of life): Strong selection. Hosea 6:7 ("like Adam, they transgressed the covenant") is a significant exegetical commitment supporting the covenant of works framework. Romans 5:12-14 grounds the federal headship of Adam.
- **Genesis 2:9; Genesis 3:22-24** (tree of life as pledge): Appropriate. Genesis 3:22-24 (barring access to the tree of life after the fall) strengthens the argument that the tree functioned as a sacramental pledge.
- **Genesis 2:17; James 2:10** (prohibition/death): James 2:10 ("whoever keeps the whole law but fails in one point has become guilty of all of it") is a strong addition supporting the all-or-nothing nature of the covenant of works.
