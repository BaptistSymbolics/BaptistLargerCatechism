# Q137: What is forbidden in the tenth commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q137 faithfully modernizes the Baptist Shorter Catechism's answer on the sins forbidden in the tenth commandment, preserving all three key elements: discontentment with one's own condition, envy/grief at the good of others, and inordinate desires for what belongs to others. The answer maintains strong structural solidarity with WLC Q148 and is fully consistent with the 1689 Confession's teaching on the moral law. The proof texts are virtually identical to those used by both the Shorter and WLC.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is forbidden in the tenth commandment?
> A. The tenth commandment forbiddeth all discontentment with our own estate (1 Kings 21:4; Esther 5:13; 1 Cor. 10:10), envying or grieving at the good of our neighbour (Gal. 5:26; James 3:14, 16), and all inordinate motions and affections to anything that is his (Rom. 7:7, 8, 13:9; Deut. 5:21).

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter's "all discontentment with our own estate" becomes "any dissatisfaction with what belongs to us" — a legitimate modernization that preserves the meaning. The Shorter's "inordinate motions and affections" becomes "all improper desire" — again a faithful modernization, though "motions and affections" is slightly richer than "desire" alone (see Detailed Notes).
- Structural changes: The BLC breaks the Shorter's three-part answer into four sections by separating "envying" from "grieving at the good of our neighbour" into distinct clauses ("envy" and "grief at the success of others"). This is a presentational change that does not alter theology. The three theological categories remain intact: (1) discontentment with one's own condition, (2) envy/grief at the good of one's neighbor, and (3) inordinate desires for what belongs to another.
- The BLC's proof texts match the Shorter's exactly, with the addition of Colossians 3:5 to the final section, which is an appropriate supplementary reference.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraphs 2 and 5

> "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables; the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man." (19.2)

> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation." (19.5)

- Omissions: None. The 1689 does not offer a specific exposition of the tenth commandment, so the BLC fills a catechetical role that the Confession leaves to catechisms.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's treatment of the tenth commandment is fully consistent with the 1689's teaching that the moral law (the Ten Commandments) remains binding on all persons, including believers.
- The BLC's emphasis on internal sins (dissatisfaction, envy, grief, improper desire) aligns well with the 1689's teaching in 19.6 that the law discovers "the sinful pollutions of their Natures, Hearts and Lives" — the tenth commandment being the quintessential commandment addressing the heart.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 148. What are the sins forbidden in the tenth commandment?
> A. The sins forbidden in the tenth commandment are, discontentment with our own estate; envying and grieving at the good of our neighbour, together with all inordinate motions and affections to anything that is his.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. There are no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues in this question.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The BLC tracks the WLC's content precisely, merely modernizing the language.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's "inordinate motions and affections" is simplified to "all improper desire." The word "motions" (referring to internal movements of the soul toward sin) is a theologically precise term that captures something slightly different from "desire" — motions can be involuntary inclinations, while desire suggests a more conscious state. However, the BLC's "improper desire" is a reasonable modernization that captures the essential point, and the proof texts (especially Rom. 7:7-8) preserve the connection to involuntary concupiscence. This is not a theological divergence but a stylistic simplification.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's substitution of "dissatisfaction with what belongs to us" for the Shorter/WLC's "discontentment with our own estate" is a straightforward modernization. "Estate" in the older texts means one's lot, condition, or possessions — "what belongs to us" captures this.
- The BLC says "grief at the success of others" where the Shorter says "grieving at the good of our neighbour" and the WLC says "grieving at the good of our neighbour." The BLC's "success" is slightly narrower than "good" — one could grieve at a neighbor's good character, good health, or good relationships without those being "success" per se. However, "success" is commonly understood broadly enough to cover general prosperity and well-being, making this an acceptable modernization.
- The separation of "envy" into its own section is a presentational choice that actually gives it slightly more emphasis, which is appropriate given the prominence of envy in Scripture's treatment of the tenth commandment.
- The most notable modernization is "inordinate motions and affections" → "improper desire." The older language captures both involuntary inclinations ("motions") and voluntary yearnings ("affections"), while "desire" tends to collapse these into a single category. This is a minor loss of precision but not a theological alteration, as the proof texts (Rom. 7:7-8) make clear that even involuntary concupiscence is in view.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are virtually identical to those used by both the Shorter Catechism and the WLC:
- **1 Kings 21:4; Esther 5:13; 1 Cor. 10:10** — for discontentment (identical to Shorter/WLC)
- **Gal. 5:26; James 3:14-16** — for envy (identical to Shorter/WLC)
- **Psalm 112:9-10; Nehemiah 2:10** — for grief at others' good (Ps. 112:9-10 matches WLC; Neh. 2:10 is a fitting addition showing Sanballat's grief at Israel's welfare)
- **Rom. 7:7-8; Rom. 13:9; Col. 3:5; Deut. 5:21** — for inordinate desire (Rom. 7:7-8, Rom. 13:9, and Deut. 5:21 match Shorter/WLC; Col. 3:5 is a sound addition connecting covetousness to idolatry)

The proof text additions (Neh. 2:10 and Col. 3:5) are appropriate and strengthen the scriptural support without introducing any novel theological claims.
