# Q26: Wherein consists the sinfulness of that estate whereinto men fell?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q26 faithfully preserves the core content of both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession on original sin, and expands the Shorter's compressed answer with the WLC's fuller description of corruption. One minor concern: the BLC uses "men" (plural) in the question where both the Shorter and WLC have "man" (singular), which may subtly shift the emphasis from the collective/federal headship concept to a more distributive sense. The answer itself also changes the Shorter's singular "man" to plural "men" in the question only, though the answer text uses singular "man" and "his" properly. Additionally, the BLC slightly weakens the WLC's "wholly inclined to all evil" to "continually inclined to all evil," and restructures the WLC's concluding clause.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Wherein consists the sinfulness of that estate whereinto man fell?
> A. The sinfulness of that estate whereinto man fell, consists in the guilt of Adam's first sin, the want of original righteousness, and the corruption of his whole nature, which is commonly called original sin; together with all actual transgressions which proceed from it. (Rom. 5:12, to the end; Eph. 2:1, 2, 3; James 1:14, 15; Mt. 15:19)

- **Omissions:** None of substance. The Shorter's compressed formulation is fully preserved and expanded.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC inserts the WLC's descriptive clause ("whereby he is utterly indisposed, disabled, and opposed to all that is spiritually good as well as being continually inclined to all evil") between "corruption of his whole nature" and "which is commonly called original sin." This is a legitimate expansion, not a structural change to theological relationships.
- **Question wording:** The BLC uses "men" (plural) where the Shorter has "man" (singular). This is a minor shift that weakens the federal/representative headship language slightly.
- **Agreement:** The three-fold structure of original sin (guilt, want of righteousness, corruption of nature) plus actual transgressions is perfectly preserved.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 6, Paragraphs 1-4

> Par. 2: "Our first Parents by this Sin, fell from their original righteousness and communion with God, and we in them, whereby death came upon all; all becoming dead in Sin, and wholly defiled, in all the faculties, and parts, of soul, and body."
>
> Par. 3: "They being the root, and by Gods appointment, standing in the room, and stead of all mankind; the guilt of the Sin was imputed, and corrupted nature conveyed, to all their posterity descending from them by ordinary generation, being now conceived in Sin, and by nature children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal, unless the Lord Jesus set them free."
>
> Par. 4: "From this original corruption, whereby we are utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all good, and wholly inclined to all evil, do proceed all actual transgressions."

- **Omissions:** The 1689's language about being "dead in Sin" and "wholly defiled, in all the faculties, and parts, of soul, and body" (6.2) is not explicitly replicated, though "corruption of his whole nature" captures the substance. The 1689's language about being "conceived in Sin, and by nature children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death" (6.3) is not included, though this is arguably the province of a different question (on the misery of the fallen estate rather than the sinfulness).
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC follows the same logical order: guilt imputed → want of original righteousness → corruption of nature → actual transgressions proceeding from it.
- **Agreement:** The BLC's description of corruption ("utterly indisposed, disabled, and opposed to all that is spiritually good") closely mirrors the 1689's "utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all good" (6.4). The BLC's "continually inclined to all evil" corresponds to the 1689's "wholly inclined to all evil."
- **Note:** The BLC says "opposed to" where the 1689 says "made opposite to" — a minor modernization. More significantly, the 1689 says "wholly inclined to all evil" while the BLC says "continually inclined to all evil." "Wholly" speaks to the comprehensive extent of the inclination; "continually" speaks to its temporal persistence. These are related but not identical concepts. The 1689/WLC language "wholly...and that continually" affirms both extent and duration, while the BLC's "continually" captures only duration.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q25:
> Q. 25. Wherein consisteth the sinfulness of that estate whereinto man fell?
> A. The sinfulness of that estate whereinto man fell, consisteth in the guilt of Adam's first sin, the want of that righteousness wherein he was created, and the corruption of his nature, whereby he is utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite unto all that is spiritually good, and wholly inclined to all evil, and that continually; which is commonly called original sin, and from which do proceed all actual transgressions.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None — this is common Reformed soteriology with no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology differences in view.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. The WLC says "the want of that righteousness wherein he was created" — the BLC (following the Shorter) simplifies to "the want of original righteousness." Both are theologically adequate, but the WLC's phrasing more explicitly ties the lost righteousness to the creation estate.
  2. The WLC says "wholly inclined to all evil, and that continually" — the BLC collapses this to "continually inclined to all evil," losing the WLC's emphasis on the comprehensive scope ("wholly") while retaining the temporal aspect ("continually"). The WLC/1689 affirm both that the inclination is total in extent and unceasing in duration.
  3. The WLC says "from which do proceed all actual transgressions" — the BLC restructures to "together with all actual transgressions which proceed from it." The WLC's phrasing more clearly establishes a causal relationship (original sin is the source from which actual sins proceed). The BLC's "together with" phrasing (following the Shorter) could be read as merely listing actual transgressions alongside original sin rather than deriving them from it, though "which proceed from it" recovers the causal connection.
- **Key phrases dropped:** "wholly" (replaced with "continually"), "and that continually" (collapsed into the adverb modifying "inclined"), "made opposite unto" (simplified to "opposed to").

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Men" vs. "Man" in the question:** Both the Shorter and WLC use the singular "man" in the question. The BLC's shift to "men" is a minor modernization but subtly moves from the federal/representative concept (mankind considered as one in Adam) toward a distributive sense (individual men). The answer itself correctly uses singular "man" and "his," so the theological content is preserved.

2. **"Wholly" vs. "Continually":** This is the most substantive concern. The WLC/1689 formulation "wholly inclined to all evil, and that continually" makes two distinct affirmations: (a) the inclination to evil is comprehensive/total in scope ("wholly"), and (b) it is unceasing in duration ("continually"). The BLC's "continually inclined to all evil" retains the temporal dimension but loses the explicit affirmation of the comprehensive scope. While "all evil" partially compensates (the inclination covers all evil), the loss of "wholly" weakens the affirmation of total depravity's extent. This is a precision loss rather than a theological error.

3. **"As well as being" construction:** The BLC's "opposed to all that is spiritually good as well as being continually inclined to all evil" is a stylistic modernization of the WLC's "made opposite unto all that is spiritually good, and wholly inclined to all evil, and that continually." The modern phrasing is somewhat less forceful but not theologically different in its opposition/inclination structure.

4. **The BLC adds "spiritually good"** where the 1689 has simply "all good" (6.4). The WLC also has "all that is spiritually good." The BLC follows the WLC here rather than the 1689, which is the appropriate choice for a larger catechism.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC provides more extensive proof texts than either the Shorter or WLC, dividing them by section:
- **Section 1** (guilt, want of righteousness): Genesis 2:16-17; Romans 5:12-20; 1 Corinthians 15:21-22 — strong and appropriate, covering both the covenant breach and imputation of guilt.
- **Section 2** (corruption of nature): Romans 3:10-19; Romans 8:7-8; Genesis 6:5; 1 Corinthians 2:14 — excellent selection. Genesis 6:5 is particularly apt for the "continually" language. 1 Corinthians 2:14 is a good addition supporting spiritual inability (not in the Shorter's proof texts).
- **Section 3** (actual transgressions): James 1:14-15; Psalm 53:1-3; Matthew 15:19; John 3:6 — James and Matthew overlap with the Shorter/WLC. Psalm 53 and John 3:6 are valuable additions.

The Shorter's proof text Ephesians 2:1-3 is absent from the BLC. This is a notable omission since Ephesians 2:1-3 is one of the most important texts on original sin, speaking to being "dead in trespasses and sins" and "by nature children of wrath." However, it may appear in adjacent BLC questions.
