# Q46: How does Christ execute the office of a king?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q46 closely follows WLC Q45 in structure and content, faithfully expanding the Shorter Catechism's compressed treatment. However, the BLC changes "their good" (WLC) to "their well-being," which subtly weakens a robust theological term, and the final clause about retribution against the wicked introduces "just retribution" language not present in any standard. The Shorter Catechism's emphasis on Christ "subduing us to himself" and "defending us" — two distinct kingly acts — are absorbed into the BLC's broader WLC-derived structure but lose their pointed directness in the process.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "How doth Christ execute the office of king?"
> "Christ executeth the office of a king, in subduing us to himself, in ruling, and defending us, and in restraining and conquering all his and our enemies." (Acts 15:14-16; Isa. 33:22; Isa. 32:1-2; 1 Cor. 15:25; Ps. 110)

- **Omissions:** The Shorter's phrase "subduing us to himself" — a powerful expression of Christ's sovereign, effectual work on the hearts of His people — is replaced in the BLC by "calling a people for Himself out of the world." While "calling" is legitimate, "subduing" carries a stronger connotation of overcoming resistance and spiritual enmity that "calling" alone does not fully convey. The Shorter's "defending us" (supported by Isa. 32:1-2 in the Shorter's proof texts) has no distinct parallel in the BLC; it is arguably subsumed under "preserves and supports them" but "defense" and "preservation/support" are not identical concepts — defense implies active protection against external threats, while preservation/support may be more passive.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter keeps the kingly office tightly focused on four acts: subduing, ruling, defending, conquering. The BLC, following the WLC, expands this into a much more elaborate enumeration. This expansion is appropriate for a "larger" catechism but the Shorter's tight structure is somewhat lost.
- **Agreement:** The core elements of ruling, restraining enemies, and conquering are all preserved. The BLC's expansion faithfully develops each of these themes.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 8, Paragraph 1:
> "It pleased God in his eternal purpose, to chuse and ordain the Lord Jesus his only begotten Son, according to the Covenant made between them both, to be the Mediator between God and Man; the Prophet, Priest and King; Head and Saviour of his Church, the heir of all things, and judge of the world: Unto whom he did from all Eternity give a people to be his seed, and to be by him in time redeemed, called, justified, sanctified, and glorified."

Chapter 8, Paragraph 8:
> "To all those for whom Christ hath obtained eternal redemption, he doth certainly, and effectually apply, and communicate the same; making intercession for them, uniting them to himself by his spirit, revealing unto them, in and by the word, the mystery of salvation; perswading them to believe, and obey; governing their hearts by his word and spirit, and overcoming all their enemies by his Almighty power, and wisdom; in such manner, and wayes as are most consonant to his wonderful, and unsearchable dispensation; and all of free, and absolute Grace, without any condition foreseen in them, to procure it."

Chapter 8, Paragraph 10:
> "...in respect of our averseness, and utter inability to return to God, and for our rescue, and security from our spiritual adversaries, we need his Kingly office, to convince, subdue, draw, uphold, deliver, and preserve us to his Heavenly Kingdome."

- **Omissions:** The 1689's emphasis on Christ's kingly work being done "of free, and absolute Grace, without any condition foreseen in them" (8.8) has no echo in the BLC. While this is more about the nature of effectual calling broadly, it is theologically significant that the kingly exercise of saving grace is grounded in unconditional grace. The 1689's list in 8.10 — "convince, subdue, draw, uphold, deliver, and preserve" — is a rich enumeration of kingly acts that the BLC only partially captures. "Convince" and "draw" are absent; "subdue" is replaced by "calling."
- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC's structure (visible government, saving grace, preservation, judgment) is compatible with the 1689's treatment.
- **Agreement:** The BLC's content on governing by word and spirit, overcoming enemies, and effectual application of redemption aligns well with 1689 Chapter 8, Paragraphs 1 and 8. The BLC's "bestows saving grace upon His elect" faithfully reflects the 1689's particularism.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q45: "How doth Christ execute the office of a king?"
> "Christ executeth the office of a king, in calling out of the world a people to himself, and giving them officers, laws, and censures, by which he visibly governs them; in bestowing saving grace upon his elect, rewarding their obedience, and correcting them for their sins, preserving and supporting them under all their temptations and sufferings, restraining and overcoming all their enemies, and powerfully ordering all things for his own glory, and their good; and also in taking vengeance on the rest, who know not God, and obey not the gospel."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC follows the WLC almost verbatim, with only minor modernization.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent. This question does not touch on baptism, ecclesiology in a distinctive way, or covenant theology. The reference to "officers, laws, and censures" is generic enough to encompass both Presbyterian and Baptist polity.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. WLC: "their good" → BLC: "their well-being." "Good" is the traditional Reformed theological term (echoing Rom. 8:28 — "all things work together for good") and carries richer theological weight than "well-being," which has modern connotations of comfort or welfare. This is a weakening modernization.
  2. WLC: "taking vengeance on the rest, who know not God, and obey not the gospel" → BLC: "executes just retribution against all others who neither know God nor obey the gospel." The BLC adds "just" (not in the WLC) and replaces "taking vengeance" with "executes just retribution" and "the rest" with "all others." Adding "just" is a defensible clarification but introduces language not in any standard. "Executes just retribution" is more formal but loses the biblical directness of "taking vengeance" (cf. 2 Thess. 1:8). The shift from "the rest" to "all others" is essentially synonymous. The BLC also adds "Finally" as a transitional word, slightly altering the structural flow.
  3. WLC: "calling out of the world a people to himself" → BLC: "calling a people for Himself out of the world." The BLC's reordering changes the emphasis slightly — WLC emphasizes the "out of the world" separation first, while BLC emphasizes the "for Himself" purpose first. Both are valid but the WLC's word order more naturally captures the extraction/separation aspect of effectual calling.
- **Key phrases dropped:** None of theological significance. The WLC's content is substantially preserved.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Subduing" vs. "calling":** The most theologically significant difference between the BLC and its Shorter Catechism basis is the replacement of "subduing us to himself" with "calling a people for Himself out of the world." The Shorter's language reflects the Reformed emphasis on the resistance of the natural heart and Christ's sovereign power to overcome it. "Calling" is a legitimate biblical and confessional term, but it lacks the force of "subduing." The WLC also uses "calling" rather than "subduing," so the BLC follows the WLC here rather than the Shorter — but since the Shorter is the primary Baptist standard, this is worth noting.

2. **"Defending":** The Shorter explicitly names "defending us" as a distinct kingly act (with Isa. 32:1-2 as proof text). Neither the WLC nor the BLC has a distinct "defending" clause. The WLC/BLC's "preserving and supporting" is related but not identical. Defense implies active shielding from enemies; preservation implies keeping safe through trials. The BLC's omission of this specific Shorter language is mitigated by its broader treatment of the same theme.

3. **"Good" vs. "well-being":** This is a small but unnecessary weakening. Romans 8:28 says "good" (ἀγαθόν), and the theological tradition consistently uses "good" to describe God's comprehensive purpose for His people. "Well-being" narrows and softens this.

4. **"Just retribution":** Adding "just" to describe Christ's vengeance is theologically accurate but introduces a qualifier not in the WLC, Shorter, or 1689. It could be seen as unnecessarily defensive, as if divine vengeance needed qualification. The Reformed tradition assumes the justice of divine vengeance without needing to add "just."

## Scripture Analysis
- **Acts 15:14-16; Gen. 49:10; Ps. 110:3; John 17:2** — For "calling a people": These are strong proof texts. Ps. 110:3 and John 17:2 particularly support the sovereign, effectual nature of this calling. The BLC adds Gen. 49:10 and John 17:2 beyond the WLC's proof texts, which strengthens the scriptural grounding.
- **Eph. 4:11-12; 1 Cor. 12:28** — For "officers": Appropriate and standard.
- **Isa. 33:22** — For "laws": Matches the Shorter's proof text exactly.
- **Matt. 18:17-18; 1 Cor. 5:4-5** — For "censures": Appropriate ecclesiological texts.
- **Acts 5:31** — For "saving grace": A strong text ("Him God has exalted... to give repentance to Israel and forgiveness of sins").
- **Rev. 22:12; Rev. 2:10** — For "rewards obedience": Appropriate.
- **Rev. 3:19** — For "corrects for sins": Appropriate ("those whom I love I rebuke and discipline").
- **Isa. 63:9** — For "preserves and supports": Good text ("in all their affliction he was afflicted").
- **1 Cor. 15:25; Ps. 110:1-2** — For "restrains and overcomes enemies": Standard and strong.
- **Rom. 14:10-11; Rom. 8:28** — For "orders everything for His own glory and their well-being": Rom. 8:28 is the classic text. Rom. 14:10-11 supports the glory aspect.
- **2 Thess. 1:8-9; Ps. 2:8-9** — For "retribution against the wicked": Direct and appropriate. 2 Thess. 1:8-9 is the same text the WLC cites.
