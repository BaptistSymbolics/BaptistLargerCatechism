# Baptist Larger Catechism: Theological Consistency Analysis

## Overview

This analysis compares all 191 questions of the Baptist Larger Catechism (BLC) against three reference documents:

1. **Baptist Shorter Catechism** (1813 Charleston edition) -- Primary standard. The BLC must align with and exposit the Shorter.
2. **1689 Second London Baptist Confession** -- Primary standard. The BLC must align with and exposit the Confession.
3. **Westminster Larger Catechism** (WLC) -- Solidarity reference. The BLC should show modernized solidarity with the WLC, diverging only for Baptist distinctives (baptism, ecclesiology, covenant theology).

### Detection Priorities
1. **Omissions** -- Key doctrinal content present in the standards but absent from the BLC. Omissions are as serious as contradictions.
2. **Structural changes** -- Rearrangements that alter how theological concepts relate to each other.
3. **Contradictions** -- Direct theological conflicts with the standards.

## SIGNIFICANT CONCERN (30 questions)

### Q10: What is God?
BLC Q10 closely follows the WLC Q7 structure and achieves strong solidarity with the Westminster tradition. However, it significantly departs from the Shorter Catechism's distinctive structural formulation and omits several important doctrinal elements from the 1689 Confession, including God's simplicity ("without body, parts, or passions"), His aseity in knowledge, His sovereignty ("working all things according to the counsel of his own immutable and most righteous will"), His freedom and absoluteness, and His judicial severity ("terrible in his judgements, hating all sin, and who will by no means clear the guilty").

### Q34: How does the Covenant of Grace differ from the Old Covenant?
BLC Q34 correctly presents the 1689 Baptist distinctive that the Old Covenant differs from the Covenant of Grace "not merely in administration, but also in substance." However, the BLC omits crucial 1689 teachings about the progressive revelation of the Covenant of Grace (Ch. 7.3), the foundational Eternal Covenant transaction between Father and Son (Ch. 7.3), and the fact that all the saved from fallen Adam onward were saved by the same Covenant of Grace (Ch. 7.3). The question also structurally risks implying that Old Testament saints were not saved by the Covenant of Grace.

### Q35: How is the Old Covenant related to the Covenant of Grace?
BLC Q35 correctly teaches progressive revelation through types and shadows and that salvation has always been by the Covenant of Grace alone. However, the BLC's framing introduces a terminological tension: the 1689 does not use the term "Old Covenant" in Chapter 7 at all. Additionally, the BLC omits the 1689's crucial statement that the Covenant of Grace is "founded in that Eternal Covenant transaction, that was between the Father and the Son, about the Redemption of the Elect" and the statement that man is "utterly uncapable of acceptance with God upon those terms, on which Adam stood in his state of innocency."

### Q41: Why was our Redeemer called Jesus Christ?
BLC Q41 combines WLC Q41-42 into a single question but omits three critical elements: (1) the explicit naming of the three offices -- prophet, priest, and king; (2) the phrase "in the estate both of his humiliation and exaltation"; and (3) the WLC's "ability" alongside "authority." The BLC replaces the specific three offices with the vague phrase "His offices within the Church," which fails to exposit what those offices are.

### Q47: What was the estate of Christ's humiliation?
BLC Q47 omits critical content from the Shorter Catechism: that Christ was "made under the law," endured "the wrath of God," and suffered "the cursed death of the cross." These are core soteriological elements that the 1689 Confession also affirms. The BLC reduces the humiliation to a bare chronological outline without the theological substance that makes Christ's humiliation salvifically significant.

### Q52: What was the estate of Christ's exaltation?
BLC Q52 introduces the word "imminent" to describe Christ's return, a theological addition absent from all three standards. This injects a contested eschatological position. Additionally, the BLC omits "on the third day" from the resurrection, omits "making intercession" from the session at God's right hand, omits the judgment of "Angels," and omits "at the last day." The addition of "imminent" combined with the omission of "at the last day" produces a structural shift in eschatological framing that is novel relative to all three standards.

### Q56: How is Christ to be exalted in His imminent return to judge the world?
The BLC answer is virtually identical to WLC Q56, faithfully preserving all key doctrinal content. However, the **question** introduces the word "imminent" ("His imminent return"), replacing the standards' neutral phrasing "coming again to judge the world." The 1689 explicitly teaches that the day is "unknown to Men" (Ch. 32.3), which is broader and more cautious than "imminent."

### Q58: Who are made partakers of redemption through Christ?
The BLC drops the Shorter's key phrase "thereby uniting us to Christ" -- replacing faith as the instrument of union with a looser construction where union follows faith but is described as a parallel effect rather than faith's own uniting function. The WLC's phrase "really" (in "spiritually and mystically, yet really and inseparably") is changed to "truly," which weakens an important theological term.

### Q59: What is effectual calling?
The BLC omits a critical element from the Shorter Catechism: "convincing us of our sin and misery." This is a significant doctrinal omission because conviction of sin is the necessary precondition for the sinner's reception of Christ. Additionally, the BLC drops the WLC's "powerfully determining their wills" and the 1689's teaching that "the Creature being wholly passive therein" and that the effectual call operates by "no less power than that which raised up Christ from the dead."

### Q61: Can those who have never heard the gospel be saved by living according to their own natural understanding?
BLC Q61 drops the WLC's critical concluding clause -- "who is the Savior only of his body the church" (Eph. 5:23) -- which ties the exclusivity of salvation to Christ's particular redemption. The BLC also omits the 1689's important "much less" (a fortiori) argument and replaces "light of nature" with "natural understanding," weakening a technical Reformed phrase.

### Q62: What benefits do the elect experience through Christ in this life?
BLC Q62 replaces the WLC's precise phrase "partaking of the virtue of his mediation" with "partake of the power of His mediation." More critically, it drops the Shorter Catechism's foundational framing that these benefits flow from effectual calling, substituting a vaguer structure that grounds benefits directly in union and communion rather than in the ordo salutis sequence.

### Q65: What is justifying faith?
The BLC calls justifying faith "an act of the free grace of God" rather than "a saving grace," fundamentally misidentifying faith's nature -- confusing an attribute (it is a grace) with an action (it is an act). The BLC also drops the key Shorter Catechism phrase "him alone" from "receive and rest upon him alone."

### Q69: What is repentance unto life?
BLC Q69 introduces two significant theological shifts: (1) it replaces "saving grace" with "act of God's free grace," which changes repentance from an ongoing disposition to a one-time divine action; and (2) it omits the critical phrase "by Faith in Christ" from the 1689 Confession's definition. The BLC also drops "endeavouring" from the Shorter Catechism's "full purpose of and endeavour after new obedience."

### Q72: What are the benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification?
BLC Q72 omits **"perseverance therein to the end"** -- a critical benefit explicitly named by the Shorter Catechism and grounded in an entire chapter of the 1689 Confession (Chapter 17). This is a serious omission of a core Particular Baptist distinctive. Additionally, the BLC subtly restructures the WLC's "communion in glory with Christ" into "communion with Christ," weakening the eschatological dimensions.

### Q74: Can true believers be infallibly assured that they will remain in a state of grace?
BLC Q74 replaces the 1689's specific Christological ground of assurance -- "the Blood and Righteousness of Christ revealed in the Gospel" -- with the vaguer phrase "the truth of God's promises," weakening the objective, Christ-centered foundation. It also omits the 1689's qualification "in the right use of means," which guards against passive presumption.

### Q75: Are all true believers at all times assured of their salvation?
BLC Q75 replaces the 1689's carefully enumerated causes of diminished assurance with the psychologized phrase "all kinds of psychological problems," omits the 1689's rich description of what believers retain even when assurance is interrupted (the seed of God, life of faith, love of Christ and the brethren, sincerity of heart, conscience of duty), and omits the 1689's teaching that assurance "may in due time be revived." The final section introduces language ("inner sense of His presence and support") that diverges from both the 1689 and WLC.

### Q90: Of what particular use is the moral law to regenerate men?
The BLC reduces the 1689's richly detailed account of the law's use for regenerate believers to three narrow functions. The omissions are extensive: the law's role as a rule of life, its sin-discovering function, its restraining function, and its promissory function -- all present in the Confession and most present in the WLC.

### Q108: What is required in the fourth commandment?
BLC Q108 drops the 1689's foundational law-of-nature argument for Sabbath observance, omits the concept of "keeping holy" the day, loses the characterization of the Sabbath command as "positive-moral, and perpetual," and fails to describe the day as "a holy sabbath to Himself" (i.e., belonging to God).

### Q125: What is forbidden in the sixth commandment?
BLC Q125 follows the WLC Q136 structure closely but has significant proof text misalignment throughout -- nearly every section has verses that do not correspond to the content they are cited to support. The theological content is largely sound when read as prose, but the verse-to-text mapping is severely disordered.

### Q136: What is required in the tenth commandment?
BLC Q136 drops three critical elements: (1) the word "full" modifying contentment; (2) the WLC's crucial phrase "the whole soul"; and (3) the WLC's key phrase "all our inward motions and affections." The BLC replaces this rich internal theology with "naturally inclined to wish the best," which is both weaker and potentially misleading, since fallen humanity is not "naturally" inclined toward good.

### Q139: Are all transgressions of the law equally heinous?
The BLC replaces the traditional phrase "several aggravations" (found in both the Shorter Catechism and WLC) with "the harm that results from them," fundamentally narrowing the grounds on which sins are differentiated from the broad Reformed taxonomy of aggravations to consequentialist reasoning about harm.

### Q143: What are the outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicates the benefits of redemption?
The BLC replaces "especially the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer" with the generic phrase "particularly the Word, sacraments, and prayer." This collapses the Shorter Catechism's deliberate enumeration of the two specific Baptist ordinances into Presbyterian-sounding generic language.

### Q152: What is baptism?
BLC Q152 significantly restructures the doctrine of baptism: it upgrades "ordinance" to "sacrament," adds theological content not found in either Baptist standard (regeneration, adoption, resurrection unto everlasting life), omits the Shorter's crucial language about baptism being a sign to "the party baptized," drops "death, burial, and resurrection" and "ingrafted into him," and introduces the novel qualifier "for believers."

### Q160: What is the visible church?
The BLC introduces a marks-of-the-church definition ("wherein the gospel is truly preached and the sacraments...are rightly administered") absent from all three standards. Meanwhile, it omits the 1689's key qualifications about visible sainthood and replaces the WLC's universal ecclesiology with the more restrictive "organized gathering."

### Q163: What is the invisible church?
BLC Q163 replaces "that have been, are, or shall be" with "in all ages and places," losing eschatological precision. It also omits the Confession's identification of the church as "the spouse, the body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all" and the qualifying clause "with respect to the internal work of the Spirit, and truth of grace."

### Q165: What is the Lord's Supper?
BLC Q165 uses "sacrament" instead of the Shorter's "ordinance" and omits the Shorter's key qualification that receivers partake "not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith" -- a phrase the Shorter deliberately includes to guard against Roman Catholic and Lutheran errors. It also drops "with all his benefits."

### Q171: How must we receive the Lord's Supper when it's offered?
BLC Q171 omits two doctrinal elements from the Shorter: **knowledge to discern the Lord's body** and **new obedience** as a required qualification. It also drops the solemn warning that coming unworthily results in eating and drinking judgment.

### Q173: In what ways do the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's Supper coincide?
The BLC retains the WLC's assertion that baptism and the Lord's Supper are "seals of the same covenant," a phrase which the 1689 Confession's own appendix explicitly rejects. The appendix states that "the seal of that Covenant is the indwelling of the Spirit of Christ...and nothing else," directly contradicting the claim that baptism and the Lord's Supper function as covenant seals.

### Q175: What is prayer?
BLC Q175 omits two key elements present in the Shorter Catechism's definition: the qualification "for things agreeable to his will" and the condition "believing." Both are also present in the 1689 and WLC. The omission of "for things agreeable to his will" removes the crucial limitation on the content of prayer; the omission of "believing" removes the subjective condition of faith.

### Q186: What do we pray for in the second petition?
BLC Q186 omits the Shorter Catechism's critical theological framework of the three kingdoms (Satan's kingdom, kingdom of grace, kingdom of glory). Additionally, the informal phrasing "hurry up and come again" trivializes the solemn eschatological petition found in both the Shorter and the WLC.

## MINOR CONCERN (93 questions)

### Q6: May all men make use of the Holy Scriptures?
Conflates two distinct theological questions -- the right to use Scripture and the manner of using Scripture -- into a single answer, drawing material that the Shorter Catechism addresses separately.

### Q7: How may we know that the Scriptures are the Word of God?
Omits the 1689's and WLC's reference to the Scriptures' "light" (alongside "power") and the 1689's soteriological argument for Scripture's self-authentication.

### Q11: Is there more than one God?
Introduces "Yet" to connect monotheism and Trinity, subtly positioning the Trinity as a concession rather than presenting both as harmonious. Omits several key elements from the 1689's Trinitarian paragraph.

### Q14: What are the decrees of God?
Replaces "counsel of his will" (used in the WLC, Shorter, and 1689) with "purposes of His will," weakening a precise theological term. Omits the 1689's qualifications about God not being the author of sin.

### Q18: How did God create angels?
Replaces WLC's careful "yet subject to change" with "Yet they were subject to fall into sin and damnation," collapsing the WLC's two-question structure and importing providential outcomes into the creation question.

### Q19: How did God create man?
Omits the 1689's phrase "rendering them fit unto that life to God, for which they were created." Uses "holiness" rather than the 1689's "true holiness."

### Q21: What is God's providence towards the angels?
Rewording of "in the administrations of his power, mercy, and justice" to "as administrative agents" subtly shifts theological emphasis from the sphere of divine activity to the role identity of the angels.

### Q22: What was the providence of God toward man in the estate in which he was created?
Describes the covenant condition as "perfect obedience" where the WLC and 1689 use a fuller enumeration of qualifiers ("personal, perfect, and perpetual" / "personal entire exact and perpetual").

### Q25: Did all mankind fall in Adam's transgression?
Substitutes "original generation" for "ordinary generation" -- a term used consistently across all three standards -- introducing potential ambiguity.

### Q26: Wherein consists the sinfulness of that estate whereinto men fell?
Slightly weakens the WLC's "wholly inclined to all evil" to "continually inclined to all evil."

### Q28: What is the misery of that estate whereinto man fell?
Loses the Shorter's explicit naming of "death itself" and "the pains of hell for ever" as distinct categories.

### Q30: What are the punishments for sin in the world to come?
Uses "include" rather than the WLC's definitive "are," suggesting a non-exhaustive list. Omits the Shorter's "with the devil and his angels."

### Q32: How is the grace of God manifested in the Covenant of Grace?
Adds "only" to "the only condition" (emphasis absent from all three standards). Compresses the 1689's "to make them willing, and able to believe" into the less specific "to work in them that faith."

### Q33: How was the Covenant of Grace revealed through the Scriptures?
Introduces a parenthetical gloss on Old Covenant structure that goes beyond the Confession's simpler language. Omits the 1689's important concluding affirmation about man's incapability of acceptance on Adamic terms.

### Q36: Who is the Redeemer of God's elect?
Uses "complete" where both the WLC and 1689 use "entire," and substitutes "within" for the traditional "in" when describing the two natures in one person.

### Q42: What offices does Christ execute as our Redeemer?
Drops the WLC's content about Christ being "anointed with the Holy Ghost above measure" and "set apart, and fully furnished with all authority and ability."

### Q46: How does Christ execute the office of a king?
Changes "their good" to "their well-being," weakening a robust theological term. Introduces "just retribution" language not present in any standard.

### Q48: How did Christ humble Himself in His conception and birth?
Weakens WLC's "divers circumstances of more than ordinary abasement" to "conditions that were even worse than ordinary," losing emphasis on multiplicity and variety.

### Q49: How did Christ humble Himself in His life?
Substitutes "disgraces" for WLC's "indignities" and "frailty" for "infirmities," narrowing the semantic range. Replaces "conflicting with" with "struggling with."

### Q50: How did Christ humble Himself in His death?
Inverts the WLC's climactic structure by placing the cross death before the offering-for-sin statement. The 1689's penal substitution language is not explicitly reflected.

### Q60: Are the elect only effectually called?
Omits the 1689's teaching that the non-elect "neither will, nor can truly come to Christ," replacing the language of inability with language focused solely on culpability.

### Q63: What is justification?
Omits the Shorter's "only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us" and reduces faith's role from "receiving and resting on him and his righteousness" to merely "appropriating" righteousness.

### Q64: How is justification an act of God's free grace?
Omits the phrase that Christ's satisfaction was accepted "in their stead" (vicarious substitution language) and drops the 1689's doxological clause about justice and grace being co-glorified.

### Q67: What is adoption?
Omits several significant privileges from the 1689: perseverance language ("yet never cast off; but sealed to the day of Redemption"), access to the throne of grace with boldness, and the ability to cry "Abba, Father."

### Q68: What is sanctification?
Omits the 1689's emphasis on sanctification occurring "by his word and Spirit dwelling in them" and its affirmation that sanctification occurs "really, and personally."

### Q70: What is the difference between justification and sanctification?
Omits the WLC's clause that sanctification is "neither equal in all" believers. Introduces "Spirit-empowered effort of the believer" where the WLC simply says sin is "subdued."

### Q71: Why are believers not completely sanctified?
Omits the 1689's critical paragraph teaching that despite corruption's temporary prevalence, the regenerate part ultimately overcomes through the Spirit.

### Q77: Since death is the wages of sin, why are the righteous not delivered from death?
Drops the qualifier "seeing all their sins are forgiven in Christ" from the question. Substitutes "deeper communion" for the WLC's "further communion."

### Q78: What benefits do believers receive from Christ at death?
Omits the Shorter's "immediately pass into glory." Imports the WLC metaphor "rest in the grave as in a bed" which the Baptist standards do not use.

### Q80: What must we believe about the resurrection?
Omits the 1689's "and none other" regarding the selfsame bodies. Adds "who offend Him" which shifts the reason for the wicked's dishonor.

### Q81: What shall immediately follow after the resurrection?
Substitutes "anticipate" for "watch," weakening the vigilance motif. Entirely omits the 1689's purpose language about deterring men from sin and consoling the godly.

### Q82: What benefits do believers receive from Christ at the Day of Judgment?
Replaces the WLC's "immediate vision and fruition" with "visual presence and enjoyment," shifting from the beatific vision as a theological concept to something more sensory. Changes "innumerable saints" to "the vast company of each other."

### Q83: What shall be done to the wicked at the Day of Judgment?
Drops the Shorter's emphasis on bodily resurrection preceding the sentencing. Weakens the judicial precision of the WLC's "fearful but just sentence of condemnation."

### Q84: What is the duty which God requires of man?
Adds "plainly and fully" to qualify "revealed will," introducing a qualification not present in any standard that may inadvertently narrow the scope of duty.

### Q85: In the beginning what did God reveal unto man as the rule of His obedience?
Drops "the fruit of" from the tree reference -- a minor precision loss. Adds Genesis 1:26-27 proof text drawing a connection to the 1689's teaching on the law written on man's heart.

### Q86: What is the moral law?
Drops "obedience" as a distinct term alongside "conformity." Omits the WLC's "in the frame and disposition of the whole man."

### Q87: Is there any use of the moral law to man since the Fall?
Flattens the WLC's careful threefold structure into a simpler twofold statement, treating these merely as an exhaustive division of humanity rather than identifying distinct categories of use.

### Q88: Of what use is the moral law to all men?
Replaces the WLC's language of the law "binding" men with "informs them of their duty to live by it," weakening the obligatory force.

### Q91: Where is the moral law summarized?
Omits the WLC's reference to the commandments being "recorded in the twentieth chapter of Exodus." Subtly alters the language of divine authorship.

### Q93: What is the preface to the Ten Commandments?
Omits the Shorter Catechism's explicit title "Redeemer" for God, replacing it with a description of deliverance from "spiritual enslavement."

### Q94: What are the guidelines for the proper understanding of the Ten Commandments?
Several precise WLC phrases are weakened: "full conformity in the whole man" becomes less precise, "gestures" is replaced with "self-expressions," and the crucial phrase "provocations thereunto" is dropped.

### Q96: What is required in the first commandment?
The WLC's "thinking" (cogitative duty) is replaced with "valuing" (affective/estimative duty), dropping the WLC's emphasis on intellectual contemplation of God.

### Q98: What are we especially taught by these words before me in the first commandment?
Splits the WLC's single answer into two sections, slightly weakening the rhetorical connection between God's displeasure at idolatry and the positive duty of serving Him.

### Q100: What is required in the second commandment?
Replaces "monuments of idolatry" with "all forms of idolatry." The 1689's distinctive emphasis on singing (Psalms, Hymns, and Spiritual songs) is absent.

### Q101: What is forbidden in the second commandment?
Drops the WLC's distinct phrase "tolerating a false religion" and substitutes "regulations" for "ordinances," losing theological specificity.

### Q102: What are the reasons annexed to the second commandment?
Conflates sovereignty and propriety into a single idea rather than preserving them as the Shorter's distinct reasons.

### Q104: What is required in the third commandment?
Replaces the WLC's "holy profession, and answerable conversation" with a looser paraphrase. Substitutes "qualities" for "attributes" and "regulations" for "ordinances."

### Q105: What is forbidden in the third commandment?
Drops "profane" from the WLC's enumeration, omits "curious prying into" God's decrees, and shifts "offensive walking" from publicly scandalous conduct to personal offense.

### Q109: How is the Sabbath to be sanctified?
Introduces "joyfully" and "carefully plan ahead" not found in any standard. Omits the 1689's specification that rest extends to "words, and thoughts" about worldly matters. Reorders to place the mercy exception before worship duty.

### Q111: What are the reasons annexed to the fourth commandment?
Drops the Shorter's qualifier "lawful" from "lawful employments." Replaces the Shorter's precise term "propriety" with a looser paraphrase.

### Q115: What is the general scope of the fifth commandment?
Omits "mutually" from "mutually owe" and drops the Shorter's "preserving the honour," reducing the commandment's scope to duty-performance alone.

### Q117: What are the particular sins against those in authority by those under them?
Shifts from "counsels" to "advice," losing deliberative weight. Replaces "government" with "authority," losing a nuance present in the WLC and Shorter.

### Q118: What is required of those in authority towards those under them?
Substitutes "blaming" for the WLC's "reproving," weakening the corrective and restorative emphasis.

### Q119: What are the particular sins of those in authority?
Narrows "favouring" evil to "rewarding" evil, and "discountenancing" good to "not rewarding" good. Collapses "inordinate seeking of themselves."

### Q120: What is required of equals?
"As if their own" weakens the WLC's stronger "as their own." "Successes" is narrower than "advancement."

### Q124: What is required in the sixth commandment?
Drops the WLC's "all careful studies" and renders "resisting all thoughts and purposes" as "not thinking about or planning," losing the emphasis on active resistance.

### Q130: What is required in the eighth commandment?
Narrows "wealth and outward estate" to "money and possessions" and reverses the WLC's other-first ordering to a self-first ordering.

### Q131: What is forbidden in the eighth commandment?
Omits the Shorter's distinctive summary formula as a framing principle and drops explicit mention of "covetousness" as a standalone sin.

### Q133: What is required in the ninth commandment?
Omits the Shorter's key phrase "especially in witnessbearing." Replaces "fully" with "without equivocation," shifting from positive duty to negative prohibition.

### Q138: Can anyone perfectly keep the commandments of God?
Moves the "in this life" qualifier to modify "divine gift received in this life" rather than governing the whole statement of inability, weakening the eschatological contrast.

### Q140: What sins are more heinous because of the harm that results from them?
The question's framing narrows the WLC/Shorter's broader concept of "aggravations" to a consequentialist category. Drops "gifts" and "men of eminency" as distinct categories.

### Q144: How is the Word made effectual to salvation?
Says the Spirit "causes" the Word to enlighten rather than "maketh" it "an effectual means," shifting emphasis from Word-as-means. Replaces "corruptions" (indwelling sin) with "corrupting influences" (external pressures).

### Q145: How should the Word of God be read?
Omits the Shorter's "preparation," "faith and love," "lay it up in our hearts," and the soteriological purpose clause "that it may become effectual to salvation."

### Q146: Who should preach the Word of God?
Omits the 1689's explicit mention of the Holy Spirit's role in gifting and fitting preachers. Substitutes "called to do it" for "called to that office."

### Q147: How should those who are called preach the Word of God?
Replaces "diligently" with "accurately," narrowing from broad ministerial diligence to precision of content alone.

### Q148: What is required of those who hear the word preached?
The Shorter's critical phrase "effectual to salvation" is absent. The proof text for "lovingly" (2 Thess. 2:9-10) is problematic, functioning as a warning rather than positive proof.

### Q149: What is a sacrament?
Replaces the WLC's "exhibit" with "outward display," shifting from actively presenting grace to merely showing it externally.

### Q150: How do the sacraments become effectual means of salvation?
Adds the word "rightly" to the Shorter's "by faith receive them," introducing a qualification not present in the primary standard.

### Q153: How should baptism be administered?
Drops the Shorter's explicit anti-sprinkling/pouring clause and the phrase "the whole body." Contains a textual error ("parson" for "person").

### Q154: How do we continue to use our baptism?
Changes "sealed" benefits to "conferred and signified," reducing sacramental precision. Replaces "growing up to assurance" with simpler "assured," losing the progressive dimension.

### Q155: Who should be baptized?
Drops "ordinance" for "sacrament," omits the Shorter's exclusive clause "and to none other," and changes "certain consequence" to "necessary consequence."

### Q158: If Abraham's physical descendants were circumcised, why shouldn't children of believing parents be baptized?
Omits the Shorter's key appeal to the regulative principle. Relies on a two-covenant framework that, while sound, goes beyond what the standards require as the decisive argument.

### Q159: What is the duty of those who are rightly baptized?
Omits the Shorter's "blameless." Replaces "give up themselves" with "join themselves," weakening the covenantal self-surrender language.

### Q161: What are the special privileges of the visible church?
Omits the WLC's "the ordinary means of salvation" and replaces "communion of saints" with the weaker "fellowship for God's people."

### Q162: Is everyone saved who hears the gospel and attends church?
Contains a grammatical ambiguity. Weakens "live in the visible church" to "attends church," reducing membership and life within the covenant community to mere attendance.

### Q164: What are the special privileges of the invisible church?
Compresses the WLC's 26 questions on union and communion into a single answer. Omits the Shorter's explicit mention that believers' bodies remain "united to Christ" and "rest in their graves till the resurrection." Risks conflating the intermediate state with the resurrection state.

### Q166: What are Christ's directions for giving and receiving the bread and wine?
Replaces the WLC/1689's "word of institution" with the vaguer "biblical declaration." Continues to use "sacrament" rather than "ordinance."

### Q167: How do those who receive the Lord's Supper in the right way feed on the body and blood of Christ?
Omits the qualifications "really and indeed" / "truly and really" and the comparative clause that gives the doctrine its Reformed force. Replaces "carnally and corporally" with the weaker "bodily or physical."

### Q168: How do we prepare to receive the Lord's Supper?
Omits the Shorter's concluding warning about eating and drinking judgment. Weakens "knowledge to discern the Lord's body" to "whether we truly and to what degree know God."

### Q170: Should the Lord's Supper be withheld from anyone who professes the faith?
Introduces narrowing language ("spiritual ignorance," "scandalized the church"). Omits the 1689's category of "ungodly persons." Drops the WLC's deontic pairing "may and ought."

### Q172: What should we do after we have received the Lord's Supper?
Introduces the consumeristic tone "whether we got anything out of it" for the WLC's more careful "with what success." One proof text (2 Corinthians 7:11 to 2 Chronicles 7:11) appears to be an error.

### Q174: In what ways do the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's Supper differ?
Describes baptism narrowly as a sign of "our regeneration and engrafting into Christ" where the 1689 is broader. Replaces "exhibit" with "represent and display" and omits "all the benefits thereof."

### Q177: What is the significance of praying in Christ's name?
Shifts "hope of acceptance in prayer" to "hope that our prayers will be answered," moving from being received favorably to expecting results.

### Q179: How does the Spirit help us to pray?
Renders "graces" as "gifts," shifting from internal spiritual dispositions to something that could be read as external endowments.

### Q181: For what should we pray?
Adds "things that promote" before "the glory of God," subtly narrowing the scope. Omits the 1689's explicit exclusion of prayer for the dead and for those who have sinned the sin unto death.

### Q182: How should we pray?
Renders "enlarged hearts" as "confidence," losing the richer sense of expansive spiritual affection. The 1689's "humility" is present only implicitly.

### Q183: What rule has God given for our direction in prayer?
Adds the concept that the Lord's Prayer will "promote" graces in us -- a claim not found in any of the standards.

### Q184: What does the preface of the Lord's Prayer teach us?
Replaces "our interest therein" with "the benefits to us from that goodness," shifting from personal stake to general reception of benefits.

### Q185: What do we pray for in the first petition?
Substitutes "spiritual ignorance" for the WLC's bare "ignorance," narrowing the scope. Uses "invincible providence" where the WLC has "overruling providence."

### Q187: For what do we pray in the third petition?
Replaces "diligence" with "steadfastness" in the list of virtues characterizing angelic obedience.

### Q188: For what do we pray in the fourth petition?
Replaces the WLC's "in the use of lawful means" with a weaker formulation. Changes "holy and comfortable use" to "sanctified by us."

### Q189: For what do we pray in the fifth petition?
Introduces "when and if" where the WLC has only "when," adding a conditional element. Changes the WLC's "testimony in ourselves" to "assured in ourselves," shifting from evidence to self-assurance. Loses the Shorter's explicit grace-attribution for the forgiving spirit.

### Q191: What does the conclusion of the Lord's prayer teach us?
Replaces "enforce our petitions with arguments" with "there must be substance to our requests," losing the concept of argumentative pleading. Shifts "quietly to rely upon him" to "calmly to count on him to answer our prayers."

## Thematic Patterns

### Most Common Issues Across All Questions

1. **Terminological weakening** -- Precise theological terms from the standards replaced with vaguer modern equivalents (e.g., "ordinances" to "regulations," "graces" to "gifts," "counsel" to "purposes").

2. **Omission of qualifiers and conditions** -- The standards' careful qualifications, conditions, and guardrails are frequently dropped, leaving bare assertions where the originals provided nuanced theological formulations.

3. **Ordinance/Sacrament terminology** -- The BLC consistently uses "sacrament" where the Shorter Catechism and 1689 use "ordinance," departing from distinctively Baptist vocabulary.

4. **Loss of Shorter Catechism's compressed force** -- The Shorter's carefully crafted phrases are often expanded into looser formulations that lose the original's precision and theological weight.

5. **Eschatological novelty** -- The introduction of "imminent" for Christ's return (Q52, Q56) is an addition not found in any of the three standards.

6. **Proof text misalignment** -- Several questions show displaced or mismatched proof texts (most severely in Q125).

7. **Covenant theology gaps** -- Key 1689 covenant theology content (Eternal Covenant transaction, progressive revelation framework) is underrepresented.

8. **Assurance doctrine** -- The BLC's treatment of assurance (Q74-75) significantly departs from the 1689's carefully balanced presentation.
