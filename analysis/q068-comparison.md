# Q68: What is sanctification?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q68 is remarkably close to WLC Q75, functioning as a nearly verbatim modernization, which is appropriate since the Shorter Catechism's answer is compressed and the BLC's role is to expand it. The BLC faithfully preserves all key theological elements from both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession. The minor concern relates to the BLC's use of "God's free grace" (following the Shorter) where the WLC has simply "God's grace" — a small addition that is theologically sound but also masks a more substantive observation: the BLC omits the 1689 Confession's emphasis on sanctification occurring "by his word and Spirit dwelling in them" (the instrumental role of the Word alongside the Spirit), and its affirmation that sanctification occurs "really, and personally" in those who are "united to Christ, effectually called, and regenerated."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> "Q. What is sanctification? A. Sanctification is the work of God's free grace (2 Thess. 2:13), whereby we are renewed in the whole man after the image of God (Eph. 4:23, 24), and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness (Rom. 6:4,6; 8:1)."

- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves every element of the Shorter's answer ("work of God's free grace," "renewed in the whole man after the image of God," progressive mortification and vivification) and expands on each.
- Structural changes: None that alter theological meaning. The BLC expands the Shorter's compressed answer by incorporating the WLC's fuller treatment, which is the proper function of a larger catechism.
- The Shorter's phrase "enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness" is faithfully rendered in the BLC as "progressively put sin to death and rise unto newness of life" — a good modernization preserving the same theological content.
- The BLC adds "free" to "God's grace" (following the Shorter against the WLC), which strengthens the gratuitous character of sanctification.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XIII, Paragraphs 1-3

> "1. They who are united to Christ, Effectually called, and regenerated, having a new heart, and a new Spirit created in them, through the vertue of Christ's death, and Resurrection; are also farther sanctified, really, and personally, through the same vertue, by his word and Spirit dwelling in them; the dominion of the whole body of sin is destroyed, and the several lusts thereof, are more and more weakned, and mortified; and they more and more quickened, and strengthned in all saving graces, to the practice of all true holyness, without which no man shall see the Lord."

> "2. This Sanctification is throughout, in the whole man, yet imperfect in this life; there abideth still some remnants of corruption in every part, whence ariseth a continual, and irreconcilable war; the Flesh lusting against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the Flesh."

> "3. In which war, although the remaining corruption for a time may much prevail; yet through the continual supply of strength from the sanctifying Spirit of Christ the regenerate part doth overcome; and so the Saints grow in Grace, perfecting holiness in the fear of God, pressing after an heavenly life, in Evangelical Obedience to all the commands which Christ as Head and King, in his Word hath prescribed to them."

- Omissions:
  - **"by his word and Spirit dwelling in them"**: The 1689 specifies both Word and Spirit as the means of sanctification. The BLC mentions only "the powerful operation of the Spirit." The instrumental role of the Word of God in sanctification is a significant element of Reformed theology. This is worth noting, though it is also absent from the WLC Q75 answer itself (WLC also mentions only the Spirit in Q75).
  - **"really, and personally"**: The 1689's emphasis that sanctification occurs "really, and personally" (guarding against any merely positional or forensic understanding) is not carried over, though the BLC's descriptive content implies real, personal transformation.
  - **"united to Christ, effectually called, and regenerated"**: The 1689's full ordo salutis preamble specifying the subjects of sanctification is compressed in the BLC to "those whom God has chosen to be holy before the foundation of the world" (following the WLC's approach).
  - **"the dominion of the whole body of sin is destroyed"**: This decisive, definitive aspect of sanctification (the breaking of sin's dominion) is absent from the BLC. The BLC moves directly to progressive sanctification without affirming this definitive break.
  - **"without which no man shall see the Lord"**: The 1689's solemn necessity clause (from Heb. 12:14) is absent.
  - **The warfare motif** (paragraphs 2-3): The 1689's treatment of the imperfection of sanctification, remaining corruption, and the flesh/Spirit war is not captured in Q68, though this may be addressed in other BLC questions.
- Structural changes: The BLC follows the WLC's structure (eternal election → Spirit's operation → application of Christ's death/resurrection → renewal → graces → progressive sanctification) rather than the 1689's structure (union with Christ → effectual calling → regeneration → further sanctification by Word and Spirit → dominion of sin destroyed → progressive mortification and vivification). This is acceptable as a catechetical choice but represents a different theological emphasis.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> "Q. 75. What is sanctification? A. Sanctification is a work of God's grace, whereby they whom God hath, before the foundation of the world, chosen to be holy, are in time, through the powerful operation of his Spirit applying the death and resurrection of Christ unto them, renewed in their whole man after the image of God; having the seeds of repentance unto life, and all other saving graces, put into their hearts, and those graces so stirred up, increased, and strengthened, as that they more and more die unto sin, and rise unto newness of life."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- The BLC is a nearly verbatim modernization of WLC Q75. The theological structure, sequence of ideas, and key phrases are all preserved.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None required for this locus — sanctification is not a point of Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"God's free grace"** (BLC) vs. **"God's grace"** (WLC): The BLC adds "free," following the Shorter Catechism. This is theologically unobjectionable and strengthens rather than weakens the teaching.
  - **"aroused"** (BLC) vs. **"stirred up"** (WLC): A modernization of vocabulary; no theological difference.
  - **"progressively put sin to death and rise unto newness of life"** (BLC) vs. **"more and more die unto sin, and rise unto newness of life"** (WLC): Faithful modernization.
  - **"in the process of time"** (BLC) vs. **"are in time"** (WLC): The BLC's "in the process of time" could suggest gradualness where the WLC's "in time" (meaning "in the course of time" as opposed to "before the foundation of the world") is a simple temporal marker. This is a very minor shade of meaning.
- Key phrases dropped: None of significance. The BLC preserves the WLC's full content with only modernization of vocabulary.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Strong WLC alignment**: This is one of the most faithful WLC modernizations in the BLC. Nearly every clause maps directly to a WLC clause.
2. **The "free grace" addition**: By following the Shorter rather than the WLC on "free grace," the BLC actually improves on the WLC by making explicit what the WLC implies — that sanctification, like justification, is entirely of grace and not of human merit.
3. **The Word's role**: The most substantive theological observation is the absence of the Word as an instrument of sanctification. The 1689 Confession specifies "by his word and Spirit dwelling in them." However, it should be noted that the WLC Q75 also does not mention the Word in its answer on sanctification, so the BLC is following the WLC at this point, not departing from it. The Word's role in sanctification may be addressed elsewhere in the BLC.
4. **Definitive sanctification**: The 1689's "the dominion of the whole body of sin is destroyed" captures what theologians call "definitive sanctification" — the decisive break with sin's ruling power at conversion. The BLC's answer speaks only of progressive sanctification. This is a notable omission from the Confession's teaching, though again the WLC Q75 also lacks this emphasis.
5. **The subjects of sanctification**: The 1689's "united to Christ, effectually called, and regenerated" carefully locates sanctification within a full ordo salutis. The BLC (following the WLC) uses "those whom God has chosen to be holy before the foundation of the world," which grounds sanctification in eternal election rather than in the applied ordo. Both are true; the difference is one of emphasis.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts closely follow the WLC's citations, with some additions (e.g., Ezekiel 36:27, Philippians 2:13 in the opening section).
- Ephesians 1:4 is well chosen for the election/holiness connection.
- 1 Corinthians 6:11 appropriately supports the Spirit's work.
- Romans 6:4-6 and related texts support both the application of Christ's death/resurrection and progressive sanctification.
- The addition of 1 John 3:9 ("his seed remains in him") for "seeds of repentance unto life and all the other saving graces" is a thoughtful connection.
- The final section's proof texts (Ezekiel 36:25-27, Romans 6:4-14, 2 Corinthians 7:1, 1 Peter 2:24, Galatians 5:24) are well-chosen and comprehensive for the mortification/vivification theme.
- Notable absence: Romans 8:1 is cited by the Shorter Catechism but not by the BLC, though this text relates more to the justified status of believers than to progressive sanctification per se.
