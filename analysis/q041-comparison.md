# Q41: Why was our Redeemer called Jesus Christ?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q41 combines WLC Q41-42 into a single question but omits three critical elements: (1) the explicit naming of the three offices—prophet, priest, and king—which is the entire purpose of explaining the name "Christ" in the confessional tradition; (2) the phrase "in the estate both of his humiliation and exaltation," which locates Christ's mediatorial work across both states; and (3) the WLC's "ability" alongside "authority," replacing it with "power" and losing the nuance of Christ's fitness for His offices. The BLC replaces the specific three offices with the vague phrase "His offices within the Church," which fails to exposit what those offices are—the very point the catechetical tradition is making at this juncture.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not have a direct parallel question asking "Why was our Redeemer called Jesus Christ?" The closest parallel is:

"Q. What offices doth Christ execute as our Redeemer? A. Christ as our Redeemer executeth the offices of a prophet, of a priest, and of king, both in his estate of humiliation and exaltation." (Acts 3:22; Heb. 12:25; 2 Cor. 13:3; Heb. 5:5, 6, 7; 7:25; Ps. 2:6; Is. 9:6, 7; Mt. 21:5; Ps. 2:8-11)

- Omissions: The BLC drops the explicit naming of the three offices—**prophet, priest, and king**—which the Shorter Catechism states directly. The Shorter also explicitly locates these offices "both in his estate of humiliation and exaltation," which the BLC omits entirely.
- Structural changes: The Shorter Catechism treats the offices as a distinct question focused on identifying them by name. The BLC collapses the naming of "Jesus" and "Christ" into one question and then reduces the explanation of "Christ" to an oblique reference to "His offices" without specifying what they are. This undermines the catechetical structure that names the offices here so subsequent questions can explain each one.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8 ("Of Christ the Mediator"), Paragraphs 1 and 3.

Paragraph 1: "It pleased God in his eternal purpose, to chuse and ordain the Lord Jesus his only begotten Son, according to the Covenant made between them both, to be the Mediator between God and Man; the **Prophet, Priest and King**; Head and Saviour of his Church, the heir of all things, and judge of the world..."

Paragraph 3: "The Lord Jesus in his humane nature thus united to the divine, in the Person of the Son, was sanctified, & anointed with the Holy Spirit, above measure; having in him all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge; in whom it pleased the Father that all fullness should dwell: To the end that being holy, harmless, undefiled, and full of Grace, and Truth, he might be **throughly furnished to execute the office of a Mediator, and Surety**; which office he took not upon himself, but was thereunto **called by his Father**; who also put **all power and judgement** in his hand, and gave him Commandement to execute the same."

- Omissions:
  - The 1689 explicitly names "Prophet, Priest and King" (8.1); the BLC drops these.
  - The 1689 says Christ was "called by his Father" to this office—emphasizing the Father's initiative; the BLC omits this.
  - The 1689 mentions "all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" and "all fullness" dwelling in Christ; the BLC omits these.
  - The 1689 includes "Surety" alongside "Mediator"; the BLC omits this.
  - The 1689 says Christ was "holy, harmless, undefiled, and full of Grace, and Truth"; the BLC omits these qualifications.
- Structural changes: The 1689 connects the anointing to Christ being "throughly furnished to execute the office of a Mediator, and Surety." The BLC restructures this as being "set apart and fully equipped with all authority and power to execute His offices within the Church"—replacing the specific office of "Mediator and Surety" with the generic "His offices." The BLC also adds "within the Church" as a qualifier that narrows Christ's mediatorial work in a way the 1689 does not.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q41: "Why was our Mediator called Jesus? A. Our Mediator was called Jesus, because he saveth his people from their sins."

WLC Q42: "Why was our Mediator called Christ? A. Our Mediator was called Christ, because he was anointed with the Holy Ghost above measure, and so set apart, and fully furnished with all authority and ability, to execute the offices of prophet, priest, and king of his church, in the estate both of his humiliation and exaltation."

- Solidarity: **WEAK**
- Intentional Baptist divergences:
  - "Redeemer" instead of "Mediator" — This is an expected Baptist terminological preference consistent with the Shorter Catechism's usage and is acceptable.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **Omission of "prophet, priest, and king"**: The WLC explicitly names the three offices. The BLC replaces this with the vague "His offices within the Church." This is not a Baptist-Presbyterian difference—both traditions affirm the munus triplex. This omission guts the catechetical purpose of Q42.
  - **Omission of "in the estate both of his humiliation and exaltation"**: This phrase locates Christ's threefold office across both states—a critical theological point that the subsequent catechism questions depend on. The BLC drops it entirely.
  - **"ability" replaced with "power"**: The WLC says "authority and ability"; the BLC says "authority and power." While close, "ability" emphasizes Christ's fitness and competence for the offices, while "power" narrows the meaning. This is a minor point.
  - **Combining Q41 and Q42 into one question**: The WLC separates "Jesus" and "Christ" into two distinct questions to give each name its own theological explanation. The BLC combines them, which compresses the treatment and likely contributed to the omission of key content from Q42.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "the offices of prophet, priest, and king of his church"
  - "in the estate both of his humiliation and exaltation"
  - "ability" (replaced with "power")

## Detailed Notes
1. **The three offices are the central omission.** The entire point of asking "Why was Christ called Christ?" in the catechetical tradition is to introduce the munus triplex (threefold office of prophet, priest, and king). This question sets up the subsequent questions that explain each office individually. By replacing the explicit naming with "His offices within the Church," the BLC fails in its catechetical function—a learner would not know from this answer what those offices are.

2. **"Within the Church" is a problematic addition.** Neither the WLC nor the 1689 limits Christ's mediatorial office to the Church in this way. While Christ's kingly office has a special application to the Church, His prophetic revelation and priestly sacrifice have broader theological significance. The 1689 8.1 calls Him "heir of all things, and judge of the world"—His mediatorial authority extends beyond the Church.

3. **The humiliation/exaltation framework is missing.** The WLC's "in the estate both of his humiliation and exaltation" is theologically important because it teaches that Christ exercises all three offices in both states. Dropping this loses a key doctrinal point that the catechism should be teaching.

4. **The combination of WLC Q41 and Q42 into one question is structurally problematic.** By combining them, the BLC gives adequate treatment to "Jesus" (saves His people from their sins) but truncates the treatment of "Christ" significantly. The WLC's two-question structure allows each name to receive full theological exposition.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Matthew 1:21** — Appropriate proof for the name "Jesus" (saving His people from sins). Matches WLC.
- **Matthew 3:16; Acts 10:37-38; John 3:34; Psalm 45:7** — These are appropriate for the anointing with the Holy Spirit. Psalm 45:7 and Acts 10:38 are standard proof texts for Christ's anointing. These align well with WLC footnote 161 and 1689 8.3 proof texts.
- **John 6:27; Matthew 28:18-20; Romans 1:3-4** — These support Christ being set apart and given authority. Matthew 28:18 parallels the 1689's citation of the same verse for "all power." John 6:27 supports the Father's sealing/setting apart of the Son.
- **Last section has no proof texts** — The final clause "to execute His offices within the Church" has no scripture references. This is notable because the WLC supports the naming of each office with specific proof texts (prophet: Isa. 61:1, Luke 4:18; priest: Heb. 5:5-7, 4:14-15; king: Ps. 2:6, Matt. 21:5). The absence of proof texts for this section may reflect the difficulty of finding a text that supports the vague formulation.
