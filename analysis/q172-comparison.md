# Q172: What should we do after we have received the Lord's Supper?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q172 closely follows the structure and content of WLC Q175, preserving all major elements of post-communion duty in a faithful modernization. However, the opening phrase "whether we got anything out of it" introduces a consumeristic tone that weakens the WLC's more theologically careful "with what success," and the final section's resolution to "be more careful and diligent in the future" softens the WLC's specific call to continue attending the ordinance despite failure. One proof text substitution (2 Corinthians 7:11 → 2 Chronicles 7:11) also appears to be an error rather than an intentional change.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism addresses the Lord's Supper in its questions about the nature of the sacrament and worthy receiving, but does not have a question about duties after receiving:

- "Q. What is the Lord's supper? A. The Lord's supper is an ordinance of the New Testament, instituted by Jesus Christ; wherein by giving and receiving bread and wine, according to his appointment, his death is shown forth, and the worthy receivers are, not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith, made partakers of his body and blood, with all his benefits, to their spiritual nourishment and growth in grace (Mt. 26:26, 27, 28; 1 Cor. 11:23-26; 10:16)."

- "Q. What is required to the worthy receiving of the Lord's supper? A. It is required of them that would worthily partake of the Lord's supper, that they examine themselves of their knowledge to discern the Lord's body (1 Cor. 11:28, 29), of their faith to feed upon him (2 Cor. 13:5), of their repentance (1 Cor. 11:31), love (1 Cor. 10:16, 17), and new obedience (1 Cor. 5:7, 8), lest coming unworthily they eat and drink judgment to themselves (1 Cor. 11:28, 29)."

- Omissions: N/A — no direct parallel exists to measure against.
- Structural changes: N/A.
- The BLC here expands on the Shorter Catechism's sacramental theology by providing post-communion guidance that the Shorter does not address, which is the proper function of a Larger Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 30 (Of the Lord's Supper) — The 1689 does not have a specific paragraph on post-communion duties. The relevant paragraphs focus on the nature of the ordinance (30.1), the memorial character (30.2), administration (30.3), the elements (30.5-6), spiritual feeding (30.7), and fencing the table (30.8).

Key text from 30.7: "Worthy receivers, outwardly partaking of the visible Elements in this Ordinance, do then also inwardly by faith, really and indeed, yet not carnally, and corporally, but spiritually receive, and feed upon Christ crucified & all the benefits of his death: the Body and Blood of Christ, being then not corporally, or carnally, but spiritually present to the faith of Believers, in that Ordinance, as the Elements themselves are to their outward senses."

- Omissions: N/A — the 1689 does not address post-communion duties, so the BLC is supplementing rather than expositing a confessional text.
- Structural changes: N/A.
- The BLC's post-communion theology is consistent with the 1689's emphasis on spiritual reception by faith. The 1689's language about "worthy receivers" being "made partakers...by faith" aligns with the BLC's distinction between those who receive spiritual benefit and those who do not.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q175:

"Q. 175. What is the duty of Christians, after they have received the sacrament of the Lord's supper? A. The duty of Christians, after they have received the sacrament of the Lord's supper, is seriously to consider how they have behaved themselves therein, and with what success; if they find quickening and comfort, to bless God for it, beg the continuance of it, watch against relapses, fulfill their vows, and encourage themselves to a frequent attendance on that ordinance: but if they find no present benefit, more exactly to review their preparation to, and carriage at, the sacrament; in both which, if they can approve themselves to God and their own consciences, they are to wait for the fruit of it in due time: but, if they see they have failed in either, they are to be humbled, and to attend upon it afterwards with more care and diligence."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC reproduces the entire logical structure of WLC Q175 faithfully: (1) self-examination of behavior and success, (2) if benefit found → bless God, pray for continuance, watch for relapses, fulfill vows, attend frequently, (3) if no benefit → review preparation and participation, (4) if no fault found → wait patiently, (5) if fault found → be humbled and resolve to do better.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement on the practice of piety.
- Unexpected divergences:
  1. **"Whether we got anything out of it"** — The WLC's "with what success" frames the inquiry in terms of the sacrament's objective spiritual purpose. The BLC's "whether we got anything out of it" introduces a subjective, consumeristic frame that subtly shifts the standard from "did the sacrament accomplish its spiritual purpose in me?" to "did I feel something?" This is a tonal weakening rather than a doctrinal error, but it could encourage a subjectivist approach to the means of grace.
  2. **"Be encouraged to take communion frequently"** vs. WLC's **"encourage themselves to a frequent attendance on that ordinance"** — The WLC frames frequent attendance as a duty one actively pursues ("encourage themselves"); the BLC's passive "be encouraged" subtly reduces the active character of this duty.
  3. **Final resolution vaguer** — The WLC says those who find fault should "attend upon it afterwards with more care and diligence," specifically directing them to continue coming to the Lord's Supper. The BLC's "resolve to be more careful and diligent in the future" is more generic and does not specifically direct the person back to the Table.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"Seriously"** — The WLC says "seriously to consider"; the BLC says merely "think about." The adverb "seriously" conveys gravity appropriate to sacramental reflection.
  - **"How they have behaved themselves therein"** — The WLC specifically examines one's *conduct/behavior* during the sacrament. The BLC's "our participation" is vaguer and loses the sense of behavioral self-examination.
  - **"Attend upon it afterwards"** — The WLC's specific direction to continue attending the sacrament despite failure is replaced by the BLC's generic "in the future."

## Detailed Notes
1. **Tonal shift in opening section:** The most notable issue is the BLC's first section. "Think about our participation in the sacrament and whether we got anything out of it" reads as casual and consumeristic. Compare the WLC's "seriously to consider how they have behaved themselves therein, and with what success." Even in modernization, something like "seriously reflect on how we conducted ourselves and what spiritual benefit we received" would be more faithful to the gravity and theological precision of the original.

2. **Section splitting:** The BLC breaks the WLC's single flowing answer into nine separate sections, which is an effective pedagogical choice. However, the split between "we must humbly" (section 9) and "resolve to be more careful and diligent in the future" (section 10) creates an awkward break mid-sentence that separates humility from its object.

3. **Structural integrity preserved:** Despite tonal concerns, the BLC preserves the full three-case logical structure (benefit received → no benefit/no fault → no benefit/fault found), which is the theological heart of the WLC's pastoral wisdom. No case is omitted.

## Scripture Analysis
1. **Largely identical proof texts:** The BLC uses nearly identical Scripture references to the WLC for most sections, demonstrating careful alignment.
2. **Notable substitution — Ecclesiastes 5:1-6 for Song of Solomon 5:1-6:** The WLC cites Song of Solomon 5:1-6 (the beloved's failure to open to her lover, followed by seeking him) for the section on reviewing preparation when no benefit is found. The BLC substitutes Ecclesiastes 5:1-6 (being careful with words before God, fulfilling vows). This is a defensible change — Ecclesiastes 5 is more immediately accessible and relevant to careful self-examination — though Song of Solomon 5's covenant-relationship imagery is richer theologically.
3. **Potential error — 2 Chronicles 7:11 for 2 Corinthians 7:11:** The WLC's final section cites 2 Corinthians 7:11 ("For behold this selfsame thing, that ye sorrowed after a godly sort, what carefulness it wrought in you..."). The BLC cites 2 Chronicles 7:11 instead, which reads "Thus Solomon finished the house of the LORD" — a verse about temple construction, not about godly sorrow producing diligence. This appears to be a transcription error (confusing "2 Cor." with "2 Chr.") rather than an intentional substitution, and should be corrected.
4. **Added proof text — 1 Chronicles 15:12-14:** The BLC adds 1 Chronicles 15:12-14 (the Levites consecrating themselves to carry the ark properly after the first failed attempt) to the final section. This is a fitting addition that illustrates the concept of resolving to be more careful after a failure in worship.
