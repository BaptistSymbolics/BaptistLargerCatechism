# Q125: What is forbidden in the sixth commandment?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q125 follows the WLC Q136 structure closely but has significant proof text misalignment throughout—nearly every section has verses that do not correspond to the content they are cited to support. The most notable issues are systematic verse displacement (verses appear shifted by one or more sections) and the inclusion of an empty final section with orphaned proof texts. The theological content is largely sound when read as prose, but the verse-to-text mapping is severely disordered.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is forbidden in the sixth commandment?
> A. The sixth commandment absolutely forbiddeth the taking away of our own life, or the life of our neighbour unjustly, or whatsoever tendeth thereunto (Acts 26:28; Gen. 9:9).

- Omissions: The Shorter uses the word "absolutely" to emphasize the prohibition's force—the BLC drops this intensifier. The Shorter's phrase "unjustly" provides the critical moral qualifier (taking life is forbidden when done *unjustly*), which the BLC replaces with the exception-clause structure ("except in the pursuit of public justice, lawful war, or necessary defense"). This is not a contradiction but a structural change from a single qualified prohibition to a prohibition-with-exceptions format. The Shorter's comprehensive catch-all "whatsoever tendeth thereunto" is preserved in the BLC's final clause "anything else conducive to the destruction of anyone's life."
- Structural changes: The Shorter presents a compact, unified prohibition. The BLC expands this into an enumerated list following the WLC pattern. This expansion is appropriate for a "larger" catechism and does not alter theological meaning.
- The BLC faithfully preserves the Shorter's core content (prohibition of taking life, the "unjust" qualifier via exceptions, and the catch-all "whatsoever tendeth thereunto") while appropriately expanding it.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 19, Paragraph 2 (Of the Law of God):
> "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables; the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man."

Chapter 24, Paragraphs 1-2 (Of the Civil Magistrate):
> "1. God the supream Lord, and King of all the World, hath ordained Civil Magistrates to be under him, over the people for his own glory, and the publick good; and to this end hath armed them with the power of the Sword, for defence and encouragement of them that do good, and for the punishment of evil doers."
> "2. It is lawful for Christians to Accept, and Execute the Office of a Magistrate when called thereunto; in the management whereof, as they ought especially to maintain Justice, and Peace, according to the wholsome Laws of each Kingdome, and Commonwealth: so for that end they may lawfully now under the New Testament wage war upon just and necessary occasions."

- Omissions: None significant. The BLC's exceptions for "public justice" and "lawful war" align with the 1689's teaching on the magistrate's power of the sword (Ch. 24.1) and the lawfulness of war "upon just and necessary occasions" (Ch. 24.2). The "necessary defense" exception is consistent with the confession's granting of the sword for "defence...of them that do good."
- Structural changes: None. The BLC appropriately applies 1689 Chapter 24 principles as exceptions to the sixth commandment prohibition.
- The 1689 does not contain a detailed exposition of the sixth commandment's specific applications (anger, envy, etc.), so the BLC's expanded list is supplementary catechetical teaching consistent with the confession's framework.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q136:
> "The sins forbidden in the sixth commandment are, all taking away the life of ourselves, or of others, except in case of public justice, lawful war, or necessary defence; the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life; sinful anger, hatred, envy, desire of revenge; all excessive passions, distracting cares; immoderate use of meat, drink, labor, and recreations; provoking words, oppression, quarreling, striking, wounding, and whatsoever else tends to the destruction of the life of any."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC's structure and content very closely, preserving the same enumerated list in the same order.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None observed. This is a question on the moral law where Baptist and Presbyterian theology agree.
- Unexpected divergences: None in theological content.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "lawful and necessary" — WLC says "neglecting or withdrawing the *lawful and necessary* means of preservation of life"; BLC says "neglecting or withholding the *necessary* means for the preservation of life." The word "lawful" is dropped. This is a minor precision loss; the WLC specifies that the means must be both lawful *and* necessary.
  - "immoderate use" — WLC says "immoderate use of meat, drink, labor, and recreations"; BLC says "intemperate eating, drinking, working, or playing." The BLC replaces "immoderate use" with "intemperate," which is roughly synonymous, and replaces "meat" with "eating" (modernization), "labor" with "working," and "recreations" with "playing." These are acceptable modernizations, though "playing" is a weaker rendering of "recreations" (which encompasses leisure activities more broadly).
  - "provoking words" — WLC says "provoking words"; BLC says "speaking in a provocative way." Acceptable modernization.

## Detailed Notes

### Severe Proof Text Displacement
The most significant issue with Q125 is systematic misalignment of proof texts to their sections. Comparing against WLC Q136's proof text assignments reveals that the BLC's verses appear shifted or scrambled:

1. **Section 1** (taking life / public justice): Cites Acts 16:28, Proverbs 1:18, Genesis 9:6. The WLC cites Acts 16:28 and Genesis 9:6 for "taking away life of ourselves/others" and separate verses for the exceptions. This is roughly correct.

2. **Section 2** (lawful war): Cites Numbers 35:31-33; Exodus 21:14. But Numbers 35:31-33 concerns not accepting ransom for a murderer and blood polluting the land—this supports public justice/capital punishment (the *previous* section's exception), not lawful war. Exodus 21:14 concerns premeditated murder. The WLC cites Jeremiah 48:10 and Deuteronomy 20:1 for lawful war.

3. **Section 3** (necessary defense): Cites Jeremiah 48:10; Deuteronomy 20:1; Hebrews 11:32-34. But Jeremiah 48:10 ("cursed is he who does the LORD's work negligently, cursed is he who keeps his sword from blood") and Deuteronomy 20:1 (instructions for going to war) support *lawful war*, not necessary defense. The WLC cites Exodus 22:2-3 for necessary defense.

4. **Section 4** (neglecting means of preservation): Cites Exodus 22:2-3. But Exodus 22:2-3 concerns killing a thief who breaks in—this supports *necessary defense* (the previous section). The WLC cites Matthew 25:42-43; James 2:15-16; Ecclesiastes 6:1-2 for neglecting means of preservation.

5. **Section 5** (sinful anger): Cites Matthew 25:42-43; James 2:15-16; Ecclesiastes 6:1-2. But these verses concern failing to feed the hungry and provide for the needy—they support *neglecting means of preservation* (the previous section). The WLC cites Matthew 5:22 for sinful anger.

6. **Section 6** (hatred): Cites Matthew 5:22. But Matthew 5:22 ("whoever is angry with his brother...") more directly supports *sinful anger* (the previous section). The WLC cites 1 John 3:15; Leviticus 19:17; Proverbs 10:12 for hatred.

7. **Section 7** (envy): Cites 1 John 3:15; Leviticus 19:17; Proverbs 10:12. But 1 John 3:15 ("whoever hates his brother is a murderer") supports *hatred* (the previous section). The WLC cites Proverbs 14:30 for envy.

8. **Section 8** (desire for revenge): Cites Proverbs 14:30. But Proverbs 14:30 ("envy rots the bones") supports *envy* (the previous section). The WLC cites Romans 12:19 for desire of revenge.

9. **Section 9** (excessive emotions): Cites Romans 12:19. But Romans 12:19 ("vengeance is mine, I will repay") supports *desire for revenge* (the previous section). The WLC cites Ephesians 4:31 for excessive passions.

10. **Section 10** (distracting anxieties): Cites Ephesians 4:31; James 4:1. Ephesians 4:31 concerns bitterness, wrath, anger—supports *excessive emotions/passions* (the previous section). The WLC cites Matthew 6:31-34 for distracting cares.

11. **Section 11** (intemperate eating, drinking): Cites Matthew 6:31-34. But Matthew 6:31-34 ("do not be anxious about what you shall eat or drink") supports *distracting anxieties* (the previous section). The WLC cites Luke 21:34; Romans 13:13 for immoderate use of meat/drink.

12. **Section 12** (working): Cites Luke 21:34; Romans 13:13. These concern drunkenness and dissipation—they support *intemperate eating/drinking* (the previous section). The WLC cites Ecclesiastes 12:12; Ecclesiastes 2:22-23 for labor.

13. **Section 13** (playing): Cites Ecclesiastes 12:12; Ecclesiastes 2:22-23; Exodus 20:9-10. Ecclesiastes 12:12 and 2:22-23 concern excessive study and toil—supporting *working* (the previous section). The WLC cites Isaiah 5:12 for recreations.

14. **Section 14** (speaking provocatively): Cites Isaiah 5:12; 1 Peter 4:3-4. Isaiah 5:12 concerns feasting with instruments—supporting *playing/recreations* (the previous section). The WLC cites Proverbs 15:1; Proverbs 12:18 for provoking words.

15. **Section 15** (oppressing): Cites Proverbs 15:1; Proverbs 12:18. These concern soft answers and healing tongues—supporting *provoking words* (the previous section). The WLC cites Ezekiel 18:18; Exodus 1:14 for oppression.

16. **Section 16** (quarreling): Cites Ezekiel 18:18; Exodus 1:14; Isaiah 3:15. These concern oppression—supporting *oppression* (the previous section). The WLC cites Galatians 5:15; Proverbs 23:29 for quarreling.

17. **Section 17** (hitting/wounding): Cites Galatians 5:15; Proverbs 23:29. These support *quarreling/contention* (the previous section). The WLC cites Numbers 35:16-21 for striking/wounding.

18. **Section 18** (anything else conducive to destruction): Cites Numbers 35:16-21. These concern instruments used to kill—supporting *striking/wounding* (the previous section). The WLC cites Exodus 21:18-36 for the catch-all.

19. **Section 19** (empty text): Cites Exodus 21:18-36; Proverbs 28:17. These are orphaned verses with no text.

### Pattern: Systematic One-Section Shift
From roughly section 2 onward, nearly every proof text citation belongs to the *previous* section's content. This appears to be a systematic data entry or formatting error where verses were shifted down by one row, leaving each section citing the wrong references and creating an orphaned final section with leftover verses.

### Empty Final Section
Section 19 has empty text (`text = ""`) but contains verses (Exodus 21:18-36; Proverbs 28:17). This appears to be where the displaced final set of proof texts landed after the shift.

## Scripture Analysis
The proof text problem is the dominant concern. If the verses were re-aligned to match their proper sections (each shifted up by one position from approximately section 2 onward), the scriptural support would be appropriate and largely mirror the WLC's proof text scheme. The theological content of the text itself is sound—it is the verse-to-text mapping that is broken.

Key observations:
- The BLC appears to draw from the same pool of proof texts as the WLC Q136, suggesting intentional correspondence
- The displacement pattern is consistent enough to indicate a systematic error rather than individual misjudgments
- The empty final section confirms the shift hypothesis: one row of verses was pushed past the last text section
- Once corrected, the proof text scheme would show strong WLC solidarity
