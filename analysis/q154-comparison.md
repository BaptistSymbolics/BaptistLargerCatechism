# Q154: How do we continue to use our baptism?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q154 is a close modernization of WLC Q167, demonstrating strong structural solidarity with the Westminster parallel. The theological content faithfully reflects the 1689 Confession's doctrine of baptism and the means of grace. However, two points warrant attention: (1) the WLC's language of "sealed" benefits is changed to "conferred and signified," which reduces sacramental precision in a way that may not be intentional; and (2) the WLC phrase "growing up to assurance" is replaced with a simpler "assured," losing the progressive, sanctification-oriented dimension of the original.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest questions are:

> Q. How do baptism and the Lords supper become effectual means of salvation?
> A. Baptism and the Lords supper become effectual means of salvation, not for any virtue in them, or in him that doth administer them, but only by the blessing of Christ (1 Pet. 3:21; Mt. 3:11; 1 Cor. 3:6, 7), and the working of the Spirit in those that by faith receive them (1 Cor. 12:3; Mt. 28:19).

> Q. What is the duty of such who are rightly baptized?
> A. It is the duty of such who are rightly baptized to give up themselves to some particular and orderly church of Jesus Christ, that they may walk in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless (Acts 2:41, 42; 5:13, 14; 9:26; 1 Pet. 2:5; Lk. 1:6).

- **Omissions**: The Shorter's question about sacramental efficacy emphasizes that baptism becomes effectual "not for any virtue in them, or in him that doth administer them, but only by the blessing of Christ, and the working of the Spirit in those that by faith receive them." BLC Q154 does not explicitly repeat these qualifications, but this is appropriate since Q154 addresses the *ongoing use* of baptism, not its initial efficacy — that topic would belong to an earlier BLC question.
- **Structural changes**: The Shorter's question about the duty of the baptized focuses on church membership and walking blamelessly. BLC Q154 does include brotherly love and walking together in one body (section 9), which captures the ecclesiological dimension. However, the Shorter's explicit mention of joining "some particular and orderly church" is not reflected in Q154, though this too belongs to a different question's scope.
- The BLC is not directly expanding a Shorter question here but rather filling a gap — the Shorter has no question on the ongoing improvement of baptism. This is an area where the BLC appropriately follows the WLC's more expansive catechetical structure.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 29, Paragraph 1:
> "Baptism is an Ordinance of the New Testament, ordained by Jesus Christ, to be unto the party Baptized, a sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, and resurrection; of his being engrafted into him; of remission of sins; and of his giving up unto God through Jesus Christ to live and walk in newness of Life."

Chapter 14, Paragraph 1 (Of Saving Faith):
> "The Grace of Faith, whereby the Elect are enabled to beleive to the saving of their souls, is the work of the Spirit of Christ in their hearts; and is ordinarily wrought by the Ministry of the Word; by which also, and by the administration of Baptisme, and the Lords Supper, Prayer and other Means appointed of God, it is increased, and strengthned."

- **Omissions**: None of substance. The BLC's content in Q154 is consistent with the Confession's teaching that baptism signifies fellowship with Christ in his death and resurrection (section 5), remission of sins (section 4), and giving up oneself to God to walk in newness of life (section 8). The Confession's teaching that faith is "increased and strengthened" by baptism (Ch. 14.1) is reflected in the BLC's statement about living by faith (section 6).
- **Structural changes**: None. The BLC follows a logical progression from reflection to humiliation to assurance to mortification to holy living, which is consistent with the Confession's understanding of the Christian life.
- The 1689 does not have a section specifically on "improving" or "continuing to use" baptism. The BLC fills this gap by following the WLC's structure, which is appropriate for a larger catechism that aims to be more comprehensive than the Confession's treatment.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q167:

> Q. 167. How is our baptism to be improved by us?
> A. The needful but much neglected duty of improving our baptism, is to be performed by us all our life long, especially in the time of temptation, and when we are present at the administration of it to others; by serious and thankful consideration of the nature of it, and of the ends for which Christ instituted it, the privileges and benefits conferred and sealed thereby, and our solemn vow made therein; by being humbled for our sinful defilement, our falling short of, and walking contrary to, the grace of baptism, and our engagements; by growing up to assurance of pardon of sin, and of all other blessings sealed to us in that sacrament; by drawing strength from the death and resurrection of Christ, into whom we are baptized, for the mortifying of sin, and quickening of grace; and by endeavoring to live by faith, to have our conversation in holiness and righteousness, as those that have therein given up their names to Christ; and to walk in brotherly love, as being baptized by the same Spirit into one body.

- **Solidarity**: STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences**:
  - The BLC replaces "improved" with "continue to use," modernizing the archaic sense of "improve" (= make profitable use of). This is a legitimate and helpful modernization.
  - The WLC uses "sacrament" language; the BLC uses "sacrament" in section 4, which is consistent with the Baptist tradition (the 1689 does not use "sacrament" for baptism, but the Shorter Catechism does use it in this way, so this is acceptable).
  - No divergences related to infant baptism, covenant theology, or ecclesiology — all expected Baptist-Presbyterian differences are absent from this question since it addresses the *use* of baptism by the already-baptized.

- **Unexpected divergences**:
  1. **"Conferred and sealed" → "conferred and signified"**: The WLC says "privileges and benefits conferred and sealed thereby." The BLC says "privileges and benefits conferred and signified by it." The change from "sealed" to "signified" is notable. While 1689 Baptists do view baptism as a "sign" (Ch. 29.1), the word "sealed" carries a distinct theological meaning — it conveys the confirmatory and assuring function of the sacrament. The 1689 Confession itself does not use "seal" language for baptism (unlike for circumcision in some Reformed usage), so this change may reflect deliberate Baptist caution. However, the WLC's "sealed" language in this context is about personal assurance rather than covenant mechanics, so dropping it may slightly weaken the assurance dimension of baptismal reflection.
  2. **"Growing up to assurance" → "assured"**: The WLC says "by growing up to assurance of pardon of sin, and of all other blessings sealed to us in that sacrament." The BLC says "We are also assured of pardon from sin and of all the other blessings signified in that sacrament." The WLC's "growing up to" conveys a progressive, sanctification-oriented process — assurance increases over time as we improve our baptism. The BLC's "we are assured" is more static and declarative. This loses the WLC's pastoral insight that baptismal reflection is a means of *growing in* assurance, not merely *having* it.
  3. **"Sealed to us in that sacrament" → "signified in that sacrament"**: Same "sealed/signified" shift, reinforcing the pattern noted above.

- **Key phrases dropped**:
  - "growing up to" — the progressive dimension of assurance
  - "sealed" (appears twice in WLC) — replaced with "signified" both times
  - "engagements" — the WLC uses both "solemn vow" and "engagements" as distinct concepts; the BLC has "solemn vows" but drops "engagements" from section 3 (retaining only "other spiritual commitments," which is an acceptable modernization)
  - "endeavoring" — the WLC says "by endeavoring to live by faith"; the BLC drops the qualifying "endeavoring," making the statement more absolute. This is a minor point but "endeavoring" captures the already-not-yet tension.
  - "conversation" — the WLC's "have our conversation in holiness and righteousness" uses the older sense of "conversation" meaning "way of life/conduct." The BLC renders this as "have our human relationships defined by holiness and righteousness." This narrows the scope from the WLC's broader "entire manner of life" to specifically "relationships," which is a restrictive interpretation.

## Detailed Notes

### The "sealed" → "signified" pattern
This is the most theologically significant change. The WLC's "sealed" language for baptism appears in a context about assurance — improving our baptism involves reflecting on what was *sealed* (confirmed, ratified) to us. "Signified" is a weaker word, suggesting representation rather than confirmation. While Baptists historically have been more cautious about "seal" language for baptism than Presbyterians, the concept of baptism as confirmatory of gospel promises (not just representative of them) is well within Baptist orthodoxy and is arguably implied by the 1689's placement of baptism among the means by which faith is "increased and strengthened" (Ch. 14.1). The shift from "sealed" to "signified" in two places may inadvertently weaken the very assurance that the question is supposed to encourage.

### The "conversation" → "human relationships" narrowing
The WLC's "conversation" (from the older English meaning of "manner of life" or "conduct") encompasses the entirety of one's daily walk. The BLC's rendering as "human relationships" restricts this to interpersonal dynamics. While relationships are certainly part of one's conduct, holiness and righteousness should define all of life — work, leisure, thought-life, worship — not merely how one relates to others. This is a narrowing of the WLC's intent.

## Scripture Analysis

The BLC's proof texts closely mirror the WLC's scripture citations:

- **Section 1**: Colossians 2:11-12; Romans 6:4-6, 11; Psalm 22:10-11 — The addition of Psalm 22:10-11 ("From birth I was cast on you; from my mother's womb you have been my God") is interesting for a Baptist catechism, as it could be read as supporting a continuity of divine care from birth that Presbyterians might associate with infant baptism. However, in context it supports the broader point about lifelong reliance on God.
- **Section 2**: Romans 6:3-5 — Appropriate for reflecting on baptism's significance.
- **Section 3**: 1 Corinthians 1:11-13; Romans 6:2-3 — The 1 Corinthians reference (divisions in Corinth) is apt for showing how we fall short of baptismal unity.
- **Section 4**: Romans 4:11-12; 1 Peter 3:21 — Romans 4:11-12 is notable: it speaks of circumcision as a "seal of the righteousness of the faith." Using this text to support baptismal assurance implicitly acknowledges a sign/seal function for the ordinance, which is in tension with the BLC's choice of "signified" over "sealed" in the text itself.
- **Section 5**: Romans 6:2-5 — Core Pauline baptismal theology; well-chosen.
- **Section 6**: Galatians 3:26-27 — Appropriate for faith and baptismal identity.
- **Section 7**: Romans 6:22 — Appropriate for holiness, though the verse speaks more broadly than "human relationships."
- **Section 8**: Acts 2:38 — Good proof text for giving up one's name to Christ in baptism.
- **Section 9**: 1 Corinthians 12:13, 25-27 — Excellent proof text for Spirit-baptism into one body and mutual care.
