# Q84: What is the duty which God requires of man?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
The BLC faithfully preserves the core content of the Shorter Catechism's answer — that man's duty is obedience to God's revealed will — and appropriately expands it by grounding this obligation in God's character as Creator, preserver, Master, and Sovereign (drawing from Romans 12:1). However, the addition of "plainly and fully" to qualify "revealed will" introduces a subtle qualification not present in either the Shorter, the WLC, or the 1689, which may inadvertently narrow the scope of the duty by suggesting it applies only to what is *plainly and fully* revealed rather than to all that God has revealed.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is the duty which God requireth of man?
> A. The duty which God requireth of man is, obedience to his revealed will (Mic 6:8; 1 Sam. 15:22).

- **Omissions:** None of substance. The Shorter's answer is deliberately terse, and the BLC preserves its core assertion.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC adds two elements not in the Shorter: (1) the qualifier "plainly and fully" before "revealed will," and (2) a second clause explaining *why* this obedience is owed — "our reasonable service due to Him as our Creator, preserver, Master, and Sovereign."
- **The qualifier "plainly and fully":** The Shorter simply says "his revealed will" without qualification. Adding "plainly and fully" could be read as limiting the duty to only what is transparently clear, potentially excluding matters that require careful study or inference from Scripture. The Deuteronomy 29:29 proof text ("The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever") supports a distinction between secret and revealed, but the "plainly and fully" language goes beyond what Deuteronomy 29:29 actually says. On the other hand, this may be an attempt to echo the 1689's language about Scripture being "the whole counsel of God" that is "either expressly set down or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture" (1689 1.6), in which case it is a defensible if slightly imprecise choice.
- **The "reasonable service" clause:** This is a legitimate expansion drawn directly from Romans 12:1 and the divine titles (Creator, preserver, Master, Sovereign) are well-grounded in Scripture and the Confession. This kind of expansion is exactly what a Larger Catechism should do.
- **Proof texts:** The Shorter cites Micah 6:8 and 1 Samuel 15:22. The BLC retains both and adds Deuteronomy 29:29, 1 John 5:2-3, and Romans 12:1-2. The additions are appropriate and strengthen the biblical basis.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God), especially paragraphs 1, 5, and 6

> "God gave to Adam a Law of universal obedience, written in his Heart, and a particular precept of not eating the Fruit of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; by which he bound him, and all his posterity to personal entire exact and perpetual obedience" (19.1)

> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it" (19.5)

> "...as a Rule of Life, informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty, it directs and binds them, to walk accordingly" (19.6)

- **Omissions:** The BLC does not reference the moral law specifically, but this is appropriate since Q84 is asking the general question about duty, not yet identifying the specific rule of obedience (which the Shorter handles in the following question about the moral law). The 1689's emphasis on "the authority of God the Creator" (19.5) as a ground of obedience is well reflected in the BLC's list of divine titles.
- **Structural changes:** None of concern. The BLC's structure — stating the duty then grounding it in God's character — is compatible with the Confession's logic.
- **Agreement:** The BLC's mention of God as "Creator" and the concept of obligation based on God's authority echoes 1689 19.5 closely. The language about obedience to God's "will" aligns with 19.6's description of the law as "informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty."
- **The "plainly and fully" question:** The 1689 speaks of "personal entire exact and perpetual obedience" (19.1) — the emphasis is on the *comprehensiveness* of obedience, not on limiting it to what is plain. The Confession's emphasis on obedience to the *authority* of the Creator (not just to what is easily understood) is in mild tension with a reading of "plainly and fully revealed" that would limit the scope.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 91. What is the duty which God requireth of man?
> A. The duty which God requireth of man, is obedience to his revealed will. (Romans 12:1-2; Micah 6:8; 1 Samuel 15:22)

- **Solidarity:** MODERATE — The BLC preserves the WLC's core answer almost verbatim but adds material that the WLC does not include.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None. This is a question of general duty, not touching baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology.
- **Unexpected divergences:** The addition of "plainly and fully" is an unexpected departure from the WLC's simple "revealed will." Neither the WLC nor any of the parallel standards include this qualifier. The WLC's simplicity here is deliberate — the duty is obedience to God's revealed will, full stop.
- **Key phrases dropped:** None. The WLC answer is so terse ("obedience to his revealed will") that the BLC preserves it essentially intact while expanding around it.
- **BLC expansion:** The "reasonable service" clause with divine titles (Creator, preserver, Master, Sovereign) is a substantive expansion not present in the WLC. This is appropriate for a Larger Catechism and draws well from the WLC's own proof text (Romans 12:1-2). The WLC includes Romans 12:1-2 as a proof text but leaves the "reasonable service" theology implicit; the BLC makes it explicit.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Plainly and fully revealed"** — This is the primary concern. While the BLC likely intends this to echo the distinction in Deuteronomy 29:29 between God's secret and revealed will, the addition of "plainly and fully" as qualifiers could be read as: (a) limiting the scope of duty to only what is obvious or explicit, excluding matters requiring careful biblical reasoning; or (b) weakening the force of the obligation by suggesting ambiguous portions of Scripture do not bind the conscience. Neither the Shorter, WLC, nor 1689 includes such a qualifier. The simpler "revealed will" is both more precise and more demanding.

2. **The divine titles** — "Creator, preserver, Master, and Sovereign" is a well-chosen list. "Creator" echoes 1689 19.5 directly. "Preserver" reflects the Confession's doctrine of providence (Chapter 5). "Master" and "Sovereign" reflect the lordship motif throughout Scripture. The proof texts (Romans 12:1; Isaiah 44:21; Deuteronomy 32:6; Malachi 1:6; Psalm 22:27-28) are well-selected and appropriate.

3. **"Reasonable service"** — Drawing from Romans 12:1 to characterize obedience as "reasonable service" is theologically sound and pastorally helpful. It establishes that obedience is not arbitrary but fitting given who God is and what He has done.

## Scripture Analysis
**Section 1 proof texts:**
- **Deuteronomy 29:29:** "The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law." Supports the revealed/secret distinction but notably does not use the language of "plainly and fully" — it simply says "revealed." This text actually supports the Shorter/WLC's simpler formulation more than the BLC's expanded one.
- **Micah 6:8** (shared with Shorter and WLC): "He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?" Excellent support for the core assertion.
- **1 John 5:2-3:** "By this we know that we love the children of God, when we love God, and keep his commandments. For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous." Supports the connection between love and obedience.
- **Romans 12:1-2** (shared with WLC): "Present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service." Primary support for the "reasonable service" expansion.
- **1 Samuel 15:22** (shared with Shorter and WLC): "To obey is better than sacrifice." Classic text on the primacy of obedience.

**Section 2 proof texts:**
- **Romans 12:1:** Repeated from section 1; anchors the "reasonable service" language.
- **Isaiah 44:21:** "Remember these, O Jacob and Israel; for thou art my servant." Supports the Master/servant relationship.
- **Deuteronomy 32:6:** "Do ye thus requite the LORD, O foolish people and unwise? is not he thy father that hath bought thee? hath he not made thee, and established thee?" Supports Creator language.
- **Malachi 1:6:** "A son honoureth his father, and a servant his master: if then I be a father, where is mine honour? and if I be a master, where is my fear?" Supports Master language.
- **Psalm 22:27-28:** "All the ends of the world shall remember and turn unto the LORD... for the kingdom is the LORD's: and he is the governor among the nations." Supports Sovereign language.

All proof texts are well-chosen and appropriate. The selection is richer than the Shorter/WLC and befitting a Larger Catechism format.
