# Q113: What is the fifth commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q113 simply recites the text of the fifth commandment (Exodus 20:12) in modernized English. This is a direct Scripture quotation question with no theological exposition, and the BLC faithfully reproduces the content of both the Shorter Catechism and the Westminster Larger Catechism in contemporary language. There is no doctrinal content to omit, restructure, or contradict.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Which is the fifth commandment?
> A. The fifth commandment is, Honor thy father and thy mother; that thy days may be long in the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee (Ex. 20:12).

- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves the full text of the commandment.
- Structural changes: None. Both are simple recitations of Exodus 20:12.
- The BLC modernizes the language ("Honor your father and your mother so that you may live long in the land the LORD your God is giving you") but preserves every element: the command to honor father and mother, the promise of long life, and the reference to the land the LORD gives. The shift from "thy/thee" to "your/you" and "giveth" to "is giving" is straightforward modernization with no theological import.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God)
> "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables; the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man." (19.2)

- The 1689 does not individually recite the fifth commandment. It affirms the Ten Commandments as a whole, delivered on Sinai, with the last six concerning duty to man.
- Omissions: N/A — the Confession does not provide individual commandment texts to compare against.
- Structural changes: N/A.
- The BLC's recitation of Exodus 20:12 is fully consistent with the 1689's affirmation of the Decalogue as binding moral law.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 123. Which is the fifth commandment?
> A. The fifth commandment is, Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is a Scripture quotation, not a doctrinal exposition).
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None. The BLC preserves every element of the WLC's answer in modernized form.
- The only differences are modernized English and the BLC's use of "LORD" (representing the divine name YHWH) where the WLC has "Lord" — this is actually a more precise rendering of the Hebrew text of Exodus 20:12 where the Tetragrammaton appears.

## Detailed Notes
- This is a Scripture-quotation question with no theological exposition. All three standards (BLC, Shorter, WLC) simply recite Exodus 20:12.
- The BLC's modernization is clean and preserves the full semantic content of the commandment.
- The BLC's use of "LORD" (all caps) to represent the divine name is a noteworthy improvement over the traditional "Lord" rendering, aligning with standard modern Bible translation practice (ESV, NIV, NASB, etc.).
- The proof text (Exodus 20:12) is identical across all standards.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Exodus 20:12** — The sole proof text, and the text being recited. The BLC's rendering is consistent with modern English translations of this verse. The key elements are all present: (1) the command to honor father and mother, (2) the promise of long life, (3) the specification "in the land the LORD your God is giving you."
