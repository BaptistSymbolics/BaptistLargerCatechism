# Q142: What does God require of us, that we may escape His wrath and curse, due to us for sin?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q142 faithfully mirrors the Baptist Shorter Catechism's parallel question, preserving its three requirements (faith, repentance, diligent use of means) in the same order and with substantially identical phrasing. The only notable change is the substitution of "mediation" for the Shorter's "redemption," which follows the WLC wording and is theologically unobjectionable. The addition of "ordinary" before "means" (also from the WLC) is a helpful clarification rather than a departure. The question shows strong solidarity with WLC Q153 while appropriately maintaining the Shorter's order of faith before repentance.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What doth God require of us that we may escape his wrath and curse, due to us for sin?
> A. To escape the wrath and curse of God due to us for sin, God requireth of us faith in Jesus Christ, repentance unto life, with the diligent use of all the outward means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption.

- Omissions: None. All three elements from the Shorter (faith in Jesus Christ, repentance unto life, diligent use of outward means) are preserved.
- Structural changes: None significant. The BLC follows the Shorter's order of faith before repentance, which differs from the WLC's order (see below). The conjunction "along with" replaces "with," which is stylistic only.
- **"Redemption" → "mediation":** The Shorter says "benefits of redemption" while the BLC says "benefits of His mediation." This follows the WLC's wording. Both terms are theologically sound: "redemption" names the accomplishment, "mediation" names the ongoing activity through which Christ applies redemption's benefits. The 1689 Confession uses both concepts (Christ as Mediator in Chapter 8; redemption throughout). This is not a substantive departure.
- **Addition of "ordinary":** The BLC adds "ordinary" to read "outward and ordinary means," which follows the WLC's Q154 language ("outward and ordinary means"). This is a helpful precision that distinguishes ordinary means from extraordinary divine action, consistent with the Confession's teaching in Chapter 14.1 that faith "is ordinarily wrought by the Ministry of the Word."
- **Proof texts:** The BLC adds several texts the Shorter does not cite (Acts 16:30-31; Matthew 3:7-8; Luke 13:3-5; John 3:16-18; Mark 1:15 for section 1; Matthew 28:19-20; Acts 2:42-46; 1 Timothy 4:16; 1 Corinthians 1:21; Ephesians 5:19-20; Ephesians 6:17-18 for section 2). These are appropriate expansions for a Larger Catechism. The Shorter's key citations (Acts 20:21; Proverbs 2:1-6; Proverbs 8:33ff) are preserved or closely paralleled.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 14 (Of Saving Faith), Paragraph 1:
> "The Grace of Faith, whereby the Elect are enabled to beleive to the saving of their souls, is the work of the Spirit of Christ in their hearts; and is ordinarily wrought by the Ministry of the Word; by which also, and by the administration of Baptisme, and the Lords Supper, Prayer and other Means appointed of God, it is increased, and strengthned."

Chapter 15 (Of Repentance unto Life and Salvation), Paragraph 1:
> "Such of the Elect as are converted at riper years, having sometimes lived in the state of nature, and therein served divers lusts and pleasures, God in their Effectual Calling giveth them Repentance unto Life."

Chapter 20 (Of the Gospel), Paragraph 4:
> "Although the Gospel be the only outward means, of revealing Christ, and saving Grace; and is, as such, abundantly sufficient thereunto; yet that men who are dead in Trespasses, may be born again, Quickned or Regenerated; there is moreover necessary, an effectual, insuperable work of the Holy Spirit, upon the whole Soul, for the producing in them a new spiritual Life; without which no other means will effect their Conversion unto God."

- Omissions: None. The BLC's three requirements map directly to the Confession's theology: (1) faith (Chapter 14), (2) repentance (Chapter 15), and (3) the use of outward means (Chapter 14.1's enumeration of Word, Baptism, Lord's Supper, Prayer).
- Structural changes: None. The Confession treats faith and repentance as distinct graces given in effectual calling, and the BLC preserves this by listing them as distinct requirements. The Confession's enumeration of specific means (Word, Baptism, Lord's Supper, Prayer) in Chapter 14.1 is summarized by the BLC's phrase "outward and ordinary means," which is appropriately general for a catechism answer (the specific means are addressed in subsequent BLC questions, mirroring the Shorter's structure).
- The Confession's emphasis in Chapter 20.4 that outward means alone are insufficient without the Holy Spirit's work is not contradicted by the BLC — the BLC simply states what God requires of us (faith, repentance, use of means), not that these human activities are self-sufficient.
- The BLC's use of "mediation" rather than "redemption" aligns with the Confession's extensive treatment of Christ as Mediator in Chapter 8 ("Of Christ the Mediator").

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q153. What doth God require of us, that we may escape his wrath and curse due to us by reason of the transgression of the law?
> A. That we may escape the wrath and curse of God due to us by reason of the transgression of the law, he requireth of us repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ, and the diligent use of the outward means whereby Christ communicates to us the benefits of his mediation.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None needed for this question — this is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement on soteriology.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **Question framing:** WLC says "due to us by reason of the transgression of the law"; BLC says "due to us for sin." The BLC follows the Shorter Catechism's simpler framing ("due to us for sin") rather than the WLC's more specific "transgression of the law." This is not theologically significant — sin is defined as transgression of the law (1 John 3:4; Shorter Catechism's definition of sin) — but the WLC's phrasing creates an explicit connection to the preceding section on the law. The BLC's simpler phrasing is appropriate for a Baptist catechism that follows the Shorter's wording.
  - **Order of faith and repentance:** The WLC puts repentance before faith ("repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ"); the BLC follows the Shorter in putting faith before repentance ("faith in Jesus Christ and repentance unto life"). Both orders are theologically defensible. The WLC's order may reflect a more experiential/temporal progression (conviction before trust); the Shorter/BLC order may reflect a logical priority of faith. Neither order implies one is more important than the other, and Acts 20:21 (cited by both) can support either arrangement.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"toward God" / "toward our Lord Jesus Christ":** The WLC's directional phrasing ("repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ") distinguishes the objects of each grace. The BLC retains "faith in Jesus Christ" but uses "repentance unto life" rather than "repentance toward God." However, this follows the Shorter Catechism exactly ("faith in Jesus Christ, repentance unto life"), so this is a deliberate alignment with the Baptist standard rather than an unexplained omission. The phrase "repentance unto life" emphasizes the fruit/destination of repentance rather than its direction, which is equally orthodox and well-attested in Scripture (Acts 11:18).
  - **"ordinary":** The WLC Q154 (not Q153 itself) uses "outward and ordinary means." The BLC includes "ordinary" in Q142, which is actually an enhancement of solidarity with the WLC tradition compared to the Shorter (which omits "ordinary").

## Detailed Notes
- This question serves as a theological hinge in the catechism, transitioning from the exposition of sin and misery to the means of deliverance. Its placement and content faithfully follow the catechetical tradition shared by the Baptist Shorter, the WLC, and the WSC.
- The BLC's two-section structure helpfully separates the internal graces (faith and repentance) from the external means, which is pedagogically useful while preserving the theological unity of the answer.
- The phrase "benefits of His mediation" rather than "benefits of redemption" is arguably a slight improvement in precision, since it emphasizes Christ's ongoing mediatorial activity in communicating benefits through the means of grace, not merely the accomplished fact of redemption. The Confession's Chapter 8 provides strong warrant for this language.
- The BLC's addition of proof texts beyond the Shorter's citations is appropriate. Mark 1:15 ("Repent, and believe in the gospel") is particularly apt as it captures both faith and repentance in a single dominical command, supporting the BLC's pairing of these two graces.

## Scripture Analysis
**Section 1 (Faith and Repentance):**
- Acts 20:21 — Paul's summary of his ministry: "repentance toward God, and faith toward our Lord Jesus Christ." This is the key proof text shared with both the Shorter and the WLC. Notably, this verse uses the WLC's order (repentance before faith), while the BLC follows the Shorter's reversed order.
- Acts 16:30-31 — The Philippian jailer: "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ." Directly supports faith as the requirement.
- Matthew 3:7-8 — John the Baptist: "Bring forth fruits meet for repentance." Supports repentance as a requirement.
- Luke 13:3-5 — Jesus: "Except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish." Supports the necessity of repentance.
- John 3:16-18 — Faith in Christ as the means of escaping condemnation. Strong support for faith as the means of deliverance from wrath.
- Mark 1:15 — "Repent ye, and believe the gospel." Captures both faith and repentance together.

**Section 2 (Outward Means):**
- Proverbs 2:1-5 — Seeking wisdom diligently. Parallels the Shorter's citation (Proverbs 2:1-6).
- Proverbs 8:33-36 — Attending to wisdom's instruction. Closely parallels the Shorter's citation (Proverbs 8:33ff).
- Matthew 28:19-20 — The Great Commission: baptism, teaching, and observance. Supports the ordinances as means.
- Acts 2:42-46 — The early church's devotion to the apostles' doctrine, fellowship, breaking of bread, and prayer. Comprehensive support for the ordinary means.
- 1 Timothy 4:16 — "Take heed unto thyself, and unto the doctrine; continue in them." Supports diligent use of means.
- 1 Corinthians 1:21 — "It pleased God by the foolishness of preaching to save them that believe." Supports the Word as a means.
- Ephesians 5:19-20 — Psalms, hymns, spiritual songs, thanksgiving. Supports corporate worship as a means.
- Ephesians 6:17-18 — The sword of the Spirit (the Word) and prayer. Supports Word and prayer as means.

The proof texts are well-chosen, covering the full range of ordinary means (Word, ordinances, prayer, corporate worship) and providing strong biblical warrant for both faith and repentance as requirements for escaping divine wrath.
