# Q17: What is the work of creation?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q17 faithfully preserves the Shorter Catechism's core content while adding "whether visible or invisible" from the 1689 Confession, and "out of nothing" from the Shorter's "of nothing." It maintains strong WLC solidarity, incorporating elements from both the WLC and the 1689 to produce a comprehensive answer. All key doctrinal elements are preserved.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is the work of creation?
> A. The work of creation is God's making all things of nothing, by the word of his power, in the space of six days, and all very good.

- Omissions: None. All elements of the Shorter are preserved.
- Structural changes: "of nothing" is repositioned to "out of nothing" and placed after "whether visible or invisible" rather than immediately after "all things." This is a stylistic rearrangement that does not alter meaning — it simply accommodates the insertion of the 1689's "visible or invisible" clause.
- Additions: "whether visible or invisible" is added from the 1689 Confession (Col. 1:16), appropriately expanding the Shorter's answer in a manner befitting a Larger Catechism.
- "Word" is capitalized in the BLC ("the Word of His power"), which may subtly emphasize the personal Word (Christ) as the agent of creation, consistent with John 1:1-3 and Colossians 1:16. The Shorter has "word" lowercase.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter IV, Paragraph 1:
> In the beginning it pleased God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, for the manifestation of the glory of his eternal power, wisdom, and goodness, to Create or make the world, and all things therein, whether visible or invisible, in the space of six days, and all very good.

- Omissions: The 1689 specifies (1) "In the beginning," (2) "it pleased God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit" (Trinitarian agency), and (3) "for the manifestation of the glory of his eternal power, wisdom, and goodness" (the purpose of creation). The BLC omits all three of these elements. However, these omissions are not concerning because: (a) "In the beginning" is implied; (b) Trinitarian agency is appropriately treated in other questions about the decrees and the Trinity; (c) the purpose of creation is addressed elsewhere in the catechetical structure (the decrees are "for his own glory"). The BLC's task here is to mirror and expand the Shorter's answer, not to compress the entire confession paragraph into one answer.
- Structural changes: None of theological significance. The BLC draws "whether visible or invisible" from the 1689, integrating it naturally into the Shorter's framework.
- The BLC adds "out of nothing" (creatio ex nihilo), which the 1689 does not explicitly state in this paragraph but which is implied and drawn from the Shorter Catechism and Hebrews 11:3.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q15:
> Q. 15. What is the work of creation?
> A. The work of creation is that wherein God did in the beginning, by the word of his power, make of nothing the world, and all things therein, for himself, within the space of six days, and all very good.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None in this question. Creation doctrine is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: The BLC drops two WLC phrases: (1) "in the beginning" and (2) "for himself" (the teleological purpose of creation). However, these are not unique to the WLC — the BLC is primarily structured to mirror the Shorter Catechism, which also lacks "in the beginning" and "for himself." The BLC follows the Shorter's pattern while adding "whether visible or invisible" from the 1689 Confession, which the WLC lacks.
- Key phrases dropped: "in the beginning" and "for himself." Of these, "for himself" is the more theologically significant (expressing God as the final cause of creation), but this is adequately addressed by the catechism's treatment of the decrees and God's glory elsewhere. The Shorter Catechism baseline also does not include this phrase.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC successfully functions as a Larger Catechism by expanding the Shorter's answer with the 1689's "whether visible or invisible" clause, drawn from Colossians 1:16. This is an appropriate expansion that makes explicit what the Shorter's "all things" implies.
- The phrase "out of nothing" (BLC) vs. "of nothing" (Shorter/WLC) is a modernization that preserves the doctrine of creatio ex nihilo clearly.
- The capitalization of "Word" in the BLC is a minor but theologically interesting choice, potentially pointing more explicitly to the personal Logos (Christ) as the agent of creation. This is consistent with the proof texts cited (Colossians 1:16, Psalm 33:6-9) and with the 1689's Trinitarian formula.
- The BLC's proof texts (Genesis 1; Colossians 1:16; Psalm 33:6-9; Hebrews 11:3; Revelation 4:11) are well-chosen and broader than the Shorter's (Genesis 1; Hebrews 11:3), reflecting the Larger Catechism's more comprehensive scope.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Genesis 1** — Shared with the Shorter; foundational creation narrative.
- **Colossians 1:16** — Supports "whether visible or invisible"; explicitly names Christ as creator of "all things... visible and invisible." This is the 1689's proof text for this clause. Excellent addition.
- **Psalm 33:6-9** — Supports "by the Word of His power"; "By the word of the LORD the heavens were made." Strengthens the connection between "Word" and divine creative power.
- **Hebrews 11:3** — Shared with the Shorter; supports creatio ex nihilo ("things which are seen were not made of things which do appear").
- **Revelation 4:11** — New addition; supports God as creator of all things ("for thou hast created all things, and for thy pleasure they are and were created"). Notably, this text also supports the WLC's "for himself" concept, partially compensating for the omission of that explicit phrase from the answer text.
