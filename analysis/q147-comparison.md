# Q147: How should those who are called preach the Word of God?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q147 closely follows WLC Q159 in structure, sequence, and theological content, demonstrating strong solidarity. The most notable change is replacing the WLC's "diligently" with "accurately" — a shift that narrows the concept from broad ministerial diligence (covering preparation, consistency, and effort) to precision of content alone. While "accurately" is a valid quality of preaching, dropping "diligently" removes a broader virtue the WLC specifically highlighted. All other changes are reasonable modernizations.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism does not address how the word should be preached by ministers. The closest question addresses the effectuality of the word from the hearer's/Spirit's perspective:

"Q. How is the word made effectual to salvation?
A. The Spirit of God maketh the reading, but especially the preaching of the word, an effectual means of convincing and converting sinners, and of building them up in holiness and comfort through faith unto salvation."

- Omissions: N/A (no parallel to compare)
- Structural changes: N/A
- The Shorter Catechism's emphasis on the Spirit making preaching effectual is not contradicted by the BLC, which includes "demonstration of the Spirit and of power."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter XXVI (Of the Church), Paragraph 10: "The work of Pastors being constantly to attend the Service of Christ, in his Churches, in the Ministry of the Word, and Prayer, with watching for their Souls, as they that must give an account to him..."

Chapter XXVI, Paragraph 11: "Although it be incumbent on the Bishops or Pastors of the Churches to be instant in Preaching the Word, by way of Office; yet the work of Preaching the Word, is not so peculiarly confined to them; but that others also gifted, and fitted by the Holy Spirit for it, and approved, and called by the Church, may and ought to perform it."

Chapter XXII (Of Religious Worship and the Sabbath Day), Paragraph 5: "The reading of the Scriptures, Preaching, and hearing the word of God, teaching and admonishing one another in Psalms, Hymns and Spiritual songs... are all parts of Religious worship of God, to be performed in obedience to him, with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear..."

Chapter XX (Of the Gospel), Paragraph 4: "Although the Gospel be the only outward means, of revealing Christ, and saving Grace; and is, as such, abundantly sufficient thereunto; yet that men who are dead in Trespasses, may be born again, Quickned or Regenerated; there is moreover necessary, an effectual, insuperable work of the Holy Spirit, upon the whole Soul..."

- Omissions: The 1689 does not provide detailed instruction on *how* to preach (this is properly catechetical rather than confessional material), so the BLC is expanding upon rather than expositing specific confessional text. No confessional content is omitted.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's emphasis on preaching the "whole counsel of God" (Acts 20:27) aligns with the Confession's emphasis on the sufficiency of Scripture and the pastoral duty of the ministry of the Word.
- The 1689's emphasis on "watching for their Souls, as they that must give an account" (26.10) resonates with the BLC's emphasis on love for "the souls of his people" and the aim to "convert, edify, and save."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q159: "How is the Word of God to be preached by those that are called thereunto?"
"They that are called to labour in the ministry of the Word, are to preach sound doctrine, diligently, in season and out of season; plainly, not in the enticing words of man's wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit, and of power; faithfully, making known the whole counsel of God; wisely, applying themselves to the necessities and capacities of the hearers; zealously, with fervent love to God and the souls of his people; sincerely, aiming at his glory, and their conversion, edification, and salvation."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question concerns the manner of preaching, not baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology. No Baptist-specific departures are expected or present.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"diligently" replaced with "accurately"**: The WLC's "diligently" (supported by Acts 18:25 — Apollos "taught diligently the things of the Lord") conveys comprehensive ministerial diligence in preparation, consistency, and effort. The BLC substitutes "accurately" (supported by Acts 18:24-28 and 2 Tim 2:15), which narrows the concept to precision of content. While accuracy is a valid preaching virtue, it does not cover the full semantic range of "diligently." This is the one substantive change.
  - **"plainly" replaced with "clearly"**: A straightforward modernization with no theological impact. Both convey the same idea.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"diligently"** — replaced rather than preserved. The WLC proof text (Acts 18:25) uses the word "diligently" in the KJV. The BLC uses the same passage (Acts 18:24-28) but to support "accurately" instead, which is a different reading emphasis from the same text.

### The "Diligently" vs. "Accurately" Question
This is the only change of substance. The WLC's "diligently" (Greek: akribōs in Acts 18:25) carries connotations of careful, thorough, persistent effort in the work of ministry. The BLC's "accurately" focuses specifically on correctness of content. Both are legitimate qualities of preaching, and both can be supported from Acts 18:24-28. However, "diligently" is the broader virtue — an accurate preacher who is not diligent (lazy, inconsistent, poorly prepared) fails the standard the WLC sets. The BLC partially recovers this ground through "zealously" and "faithfully," but "diligently" as a distinct adverb alongside those is lost.

This is classified as a minor concern rather than significant because: (1) accuracy is a valid and important preaching quality, (2) the concept of diligence is partially captured elsewhere in the answer, and (3) the overall theological thrust of the answer is fully preserved.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts closely mirror the WLC's:
- **Sound doctrine**: Titus 2:1,7-8 (WLC: Titus 2:1,8) — same passage, BLC adds v.7
- **Accurately/Diligently**: Acts 18:24-28; 2 Tim 2:15 (WLC: Acts 18:25; 2 Tim 2:15 is under "wisely") — same passages, but BLC reassigns 2 Tim 2:15 from "wisely" to "accurately"
- **In season/out of season**: 2 Tim 4:2 (WLC: same) — identical
- **Clearly/Plainly**: 1 Cor 14:19 (WLC: same) — identical
- **Demonstration of Spirit**: 1 Cor 2:4 (WLC: same) — identical
- **Faithfully**: Jer 23:28-29; 1 Cor 4:1-2; Matt 24:45-47 (WLC: Jer 23:28; 1 Cor 4:1-2) — BLC adds Matt 24:45-47
- **Whole counsel**: Acts 20:27 (WLC: same) — identical
- **Wisely**: Col 1:27-28 (WLC: Col 1:28; 2 Tim 2:15) — BLC drops 2 Tim 2:15 (moved to "accurately")
- **Needs/capacities**: 1 Cor 3:2; Heb 5:12-14; 1 Thess 2:7 (WLC: 1 Cor 3:2; Heb 5:12-14; Luke 12:42) — BLC substitutes 1 Thess 2:7 for Luke 12:42
- **Zealously**: Acts 18:24-28; 2 Tim 4:5 (WLC: Acts 18:25) — BLC adds 2 Tim 4:5
- **Love to God**: 2 Cor 5:13-14; Phil 1:15-17 (WLC: same) — identical
- **Souls of people**: Col 4:12; 2 Cor 12:15 + additional refs (WLC: Col 4:12; 2 Cor 12:15) — BLC expands
- **Sincerely**: 2 Cor 2:17; 4:2 (WLC: same) — identical
- **Glory**: 1 Thess 2:3-6; John 7:18 (WLC: 1 Thess 2:4-6; John 7:18) — nearly identical
- **Conversion**: 1 Cor 9:19-22 (WLC: same) — identical
- **Edification**: 2 Cor 12:19; Eph 4:12 (WLC: 2 Cor 12:19; Eph 4:12) — identical
- **Salvation**: 1 Tim 4:16; 2 Tim 2:10; Acts 26:16-18 (WLC references similar)

Notable: The reassignment of 2 Tim 2:15 ("rightly dividing the word of truth") from "wisely" to "accurately" is a defensible interpretive choice, though it weakens the WLC's original pairing of wisdom with right handling of Scripture.
