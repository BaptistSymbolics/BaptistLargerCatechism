# Q118: What is required of those in authority towards those under them?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q118 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q129, preserving all major duties of superiors in their proper theological order. The framing clause, triadic structures, and concluding purpose statements all map closely to the WLC. The one notable word choice — "blaming" for the WLC's "reproving" — introduces a slightly different connotation that weakens the corrective and restorative emphasis of the original.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** "Q. What is required in the fifth commandment? A. The fifth commandment requireth the preserving the honour and performing the duties belonging to every one in their several places and relations, as superiors, inferiors, or equals." (Eph. 5:21; 1 Pet. 2:17; Rom. 12:10)

The Shorter Catechism does not break out the duties of superiors into a separate question. It treats the fifth commandment as a single unit covering all relational duties. BLC Q118 is therefore an expansion of the Shorter, not a direct parallel. This expansion is appropriate for a "larger" catechism and follows the WLC's pattern of dedicating separate questions to each relational category (inferiors Q127, superiors Q129, equals Q131).

- Omissions: None — the Shorter's content is general and Q118 faithfully expands it.
- Structural changes: None problematic. The expansion into separate questions for each relational role is the expected larger catechism approach.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter XIX (Of the Law of God), paragraph 5: "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation."

Chapter XXIV (Of the Civil Magistrate), paragraph 1: "God the supream Lord, and King of all the World, hath ordained Civil Magistrates to be under him, over the people for his own glory, and the publick good; and to this end hath armed them with the power of the Sword, for defence and encouragement of them that do good, and for the punishment of evil doers."

Chapter XXVI (Of the Church), paragraphs 7-8: Churches receive "power and authority, which is any way needfull, for their carrying on that order in worship, and discipline."

- Omissions: None. The 1689 does not provide a detailed list of superiors' duties in the manner of a catechism. BLC Q118's content is consistent with the Confession's treatment of divinely ordained authority in family (Ch. XXV), church (Ch. XXVI), and commonwealth (Ch. XXIV).
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's framing — "the position of power they have received from God" — aligns with the 1689's consistent teaching that authority is divinely ordained and delegated.
- The 1689's emphasis on the sword power for civil magistrates (punishment of evildoers, defense of the good) finds its catechetical expression in BLC Q118's duties of rewarding the good and punishing the wrong.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q129: "What is required of superiors towards their inferiors? A. It is required of superiors, according to that power they receive from God, and that relation wherein they stand, to love, pray for, and bless their inferiors; to instruct, counsel, and admonish them; countenancing, commending, and rewarding such as do well; and discountenancing, reproving, and chastising such as do ill; protecting, and providing for them all things necessary for soul and body: and by grave, wise, holy, and exemplary carriage, to procure glory to God, honour to themselves, and so to preserve that authority which God hath put upon them."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question addresses general relational duties under the fifth commandment, not baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology. No Baptist-specific departures are expected or present.
- Unexpected divergences: None significant. The structure and content map point-for-point.
- Key phrases dropped: None dropped. All elements are preserved in modernized form.

## Detailed Notes

### "Reproving" vs. "Blaming"
The most notable word choice is the rendering of the WLC's "reproving" as "blaming." While both words involve addressing wrongdoing, they carry different connotations:

- **Reproving** (WLC) suggests corrective rebuke aimed at the offender's restoration. It implies a parental or pastoral concern — the superior addresses sin in order to bring the inferior back to right conduct. The word carries connotations of authority exercised for the good of the one reproved.
- **Blaming** (BLC) focuses more on assigning fault or responsibility. It can carry a more accusatory or negative tone and does not inherently contain the restorative dimension of reproof.

In the context of the triad "disapproving, blaming, and punishing," the word "blaming" fits grammatically but loses the specifically corrective and restorative nuance of "reproving." A word like "rebuking" or "correcting" would have better preserved the WLC's pastoral emphasis while still modernizing the language. This is a minor concern — the overall meaning is preserved, and the surrounding context (teaching, advising, warning) supplies much of the corrective framework — but it represents a slight weakening of precision.

### Structural Observations
The BLC breaks the WLC's single flowing sentence into multiple shorter sections, each with its own proof texts. This is a pedagogical improvement that makes the content more accessible without altering the theological substance. The order of duties is preserved exactly:

1. Love, pray, bless (relational foundation)
2. Teach, advise, warn (instructional duties)
3. Approve, praise, reward the good (positive reinforcement)
4. Disapprove, blame, punish the wrong (negative reinforcement)
5. Protect and provide for soul and body (material and spiritual care)
6. Exemplary conduct for God's glory, their own honor, and preservation of authority (modeling and purpose)

This sequence faithfully follows the WLC's order and preserves the theological logic: authority flows from God, expresses itself in love and instruction, exercises discernment between good and evil, provides material and spiritual care, and culminates in exemplary living for God's glory.

## Scripture Analysis
The proof texts are well-chosen and largely track the WLC's citations while adding further Baptist-friendly references:

- **Col. 3:19; Titus 2:4** for love — appropriate family context references
- **1 Sam. 12:23; Job 1:5** for prayer — Samuel's intercessory duty and Job's priestly family role
- **1 Kings 8:55-56; Heb. 7:7; Gen. 49:28** for blessing — Solomon's blessing, Melchizedek principle, Jacob's patriarchal blessing
- **Deut. 6:6-7** for teaching — the classic command for parental instruction
- **Eph. 6:4** for advising/warning — Paul's instruction to fathers
- **1 Pet. 3:7** for approving — interesting choice; this text is about husbands honoring wives, which works for the "approving" concept in a family authority context
- **1 Pet. 2:14; Rom. 13:3** for praising/rewarding — civil magistrate texts, appropriate for the broader "superiors" category
- **Rom. 13:3-4; Prov. 29:15; 1 Pet. 2:14** for disapproving/punishing — standard texts on civil and parental discipline
- **Job 29:12-16; Isa. 1:10, 17** for protection — Job's exemplary leadership and Isaiah's call to defend the vulnerable
- **Eph. 6:4; 1 Tim. 5:8** for provision of soul and body — combining spiritual nurture with material provision
- **1 Tim. 4:12; Titus 2:2-14** for exemplary behavior — Timothy's example and Titus's instructions for godly living
- **1 Kings 3:28** for honor to themselves — Solomon's wisdom earning the people's respect
- **Titus 2:15** for maintaining authority — Titus told to teach and exhort with all authority

The proof texts are appropriate, diverse across both Testaments, and cover family, church, and civil dimensions of authority. No concerns with the scriptural support.
