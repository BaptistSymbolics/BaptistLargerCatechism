# Q31: Does God leave all mankind to perish in the estate of sin and misery?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q31 faithfully combines the Shorter Catechism's answer with structural expansions from the WLC, producing a modernized larger catechism answer that preserves all key doctrinal content. The core elements of unconditional election, the covenant framework (Works/Grace), deliverance through a Redeemer, and the grounding of salvation in God's "mere good pleasure" are all retained. The shift from "elected some" to "chosen a people" is a stylistic variation without theological loss.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Did God leave all mankind to perish in the estate of sin and misery?
> A. God having out of his mere good pleasure, from all eternity, elected some to everlasting life, did enter into a covenant of grace, to deliver them out of the estate of sin and misery, and to bring them into an estate of salvation by a Redeemer. (Eph. 1:4, 5; Rom. 3:20-22; Gal. 3:21, 22)

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter's entire theological content is preserved.
- Structural changes: The BLC restructures the Shorter's single flowing sentence into a multi-clause format that inserts the WLC's "covenant of works" reference and "commonly called" labels. The Shorter's compact "elected some to everlasting life, did enter into a covenant of grace" becomes two distinct steps in the BLC: (1) "chosen a people to everlasting life" and (2) "did enter into a second covenant (commonly called the Covenant of Grace)." This restructuring does not alter theological meaning.
- "Elected some" becomes "chosen a people": This shifts from individual election language to corporate/collective language. Both are biblically warranted (Eph. 1:4-5 uses both individual and corporate framing). The substance — unconditional, eternal divine choice — is fully preserved.
- "Mere good pleasure" is retained from the Shorter, which is significant: this phrase grounds election in God's sovereign will rather than foreseen merit.
- "By a Redeemer" is retained at the end, preserving the Shorter's Christological focus.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 3, Paragraph 5:
> "Those of mankind that are predestinated to life, God before the foundation of the world was laid, according to his eternal and immutable purpose, and the secret Councel and good pleasure of his will, hath chosen in Christ unto everlasting glory, out of his meer free grace and love; without any other thing in the creature as a condition or cause moving him thereunto."

Chapter 7, Paragraph 2:
> "Moreover Man having brought himself under the curse of the Law by his fall, it pleased the Lord to make a Covenant of Grace wherein he freely offereth unto Sinners, Life and Salvation by Jesus Christ, requiring of them Faith in him, that they may be saved; and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal Life, his holy Spirit, to make them willing, and able to believe."

- Omissions: The 1689's mention of requiring faith and the promise of the Holy Spirit to enable belief (7.2) are not present in BLC Q31, but these are appropriately addressed in later questions in the catechetical sequence (as the Shorter also defers these topics).
- Structural changes: None of concern. The BLC correctly reflects the 1689's two-covenant structure (Works and Grace) and the unconditional nature of election.
- The BLC's "mere good pleasure" aligns with 1689 3.5's "good pleasure of his will."
- The BLC's "from all eternity" aligns with 1689 3.5's "before the foundation of the world was laid."
- The 1689's "chosen in Christ" is not explicitly stated in the BLC here, but the proof texts include Ephesians 1:4-5, which supplies this.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 30. Doth God leave all mankind to perish in the estate of sin and misery?
> A. God doth not leave all men to perish in the estate of sin and misery, into which they fell by the breach of the first covenant, commonly called the covenant of works; but of his mere love and mercy delivereth his elect out of it, and bringeth them into an estate of salvation by the second covenant, commonly called the covenant of grace.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None in this question. There are no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology differences that would apply here.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The BLC follows the WLC's structure very closely.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "mere love and mercy" (WLC) → "mere good pleasure" (BLC). This is not a WLC divergence but rather the BLC following the Shorter Catechism's formulation, which is its primary standard. Both phrases affirm unconditional grace; "good pleasure" emphasizes divine sovereignty while "love and mercy" emphasizes divine benevolence. The BLC's choice is appropriate given its relationship to the Shorter.
  - "delivereth his elect" (WLC) → "having chosen a people to everlasting life" (BLC). The BLC substitutes the Shorter's election language for the WLC's "elect" reference while expanding it structurally. The concept is preserved.
  - The WLC does not mention election explicitly in Q30 (it reserves that for Q31-32 on the covenant of grace made with Christ). The BLC, following the Shorter, integrates election directly into the answer.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC successfully fulfills its stated purpose of being a "larger catechism" version of the Shorter: it takes the Shorter's answer and expands it with the WLC's structural elements (covenant of works reference, "commonly called" labels for both covenants, the explicit mention of the first covenant breach as the cause of the estate of sin and misery).
- The proof texts are well-chosen and appropriate. The addition of 1 Thessalonians 5:9 and 2 Thessalonians 2:13-14 for the opening clause strengthens the biblical grounding beyond the Shorter's citations.
- The inclusion of Hosea 6:7 as a proof text for the covenant of works is a distinctively Reformed Baptist choice that supports the Adamic covenant of works reading.

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 Thessalonians 5:9; 2 Thessalonians 2:13-14**: Support the opening negative statement that God does not leave all to perish, with 2 Thess. 2:13-14 specifically referencing God's choice "from the beginning" for salvation.
- **Genesis 3:17; Romans 5:12, 15; Hosea 6:7**: Support the covenant of works and the fall. Hosea 6:7 ("like Adam, they transgressed the covenant") is a key proof text for the covenant of works framework.
- **Titus 3:4-7; Romans 3:20-22; Acts 13:48; Ephesians 1:4-5**: Support election and the covenant of grace. Acts 13:48 ("as many as were appointed to eternal life believed") is particularly apt for the "chosen a people to everlasting life" clause. Ephesians 1:4-5 supplies the "chosen in Christ" element.
- **Galatians 3:16; Romans 5:15-21; Isaiah 53:10-11; Isaiah 59:20-21**: Support deliverance through a Redeemer. Galatians 3:16 connects to the seed promise and Christological fulfillment.
