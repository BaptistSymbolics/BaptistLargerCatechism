# Q66: How does faith justify a sinner in the sight of God?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q66 is a close modernization of WLC Q73 that faithfully preserves all key theological content. It maintains the instrumental view of faith, the denial of faith-as-righteousness, and the denial of accompanying graces or works as grounds of justification. The only notable change is a structural rearrangement that places the positive statement (faith as instrument) before the negations, reversing the WLC order, but this does not alter doctrinal meaning.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** The Shorter Catechism does not have a direct parallel question asking how faith justifies. The closest is its definition of justification:
> Q. What is justification?
> A. Justification is an act of God's free grace, wherein he pardoneth all our sins, and accepteth us as righteous in his sight, only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us, and received by faith alone.

- Omissions: N/A — This is a different question. The Shorter addresses what justification is; BLC Q66 addresses how faith functions within justification. BLC Q66 presupposes the Shorter's content and expands on one specific aspect of it.
- Structural changes: None — different scope of question.
- The BLC appropriately expands the Shorter's phrase "received by faith alone" into a full explanation of faith's instrumental role, which is the proper function of a larger catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 11, Paragraphs 1-2

Paragraph 1: "Those whom God Effectually calleth, he also freely justifieth, not by infusing Righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting, and accepting their Persons as Righteous; not for any thing wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ's sake alone, not by imputing faith it self, the act of beleiving, or any other evangelical obedience to them, as their Righteousness; but by imputing Christs active obedience unto the whole Law, and passive obedience in his death, for their whole and sole Righteousnnss, they receiving, and resting on him, and his Righteousness, by Faith; which faith they have not of themselves, it is the gift of God."

Paragraph 2: "Faith thus receiving and resting on Christ, and his Righteousness, is the alone instrument of Justification: yet it is not alone in the person justified, but is ever accompanied with all other saving Graces, and is no dead faith, but worketh by love."

- Omissions: The BLC does not mention the 1689's explicit denial of justification "by infusing Righteousness into them" (the anti-Roman Catholic formulation), nor does it specify the distinction between Christ's "active obedience unto the whole Law, and passive obedience in his death." However, these elements belong to the definition of justification itself (addressed in other BLC questions), not to the specific question of how faith functions as an instrument. The BLC also omits the 1689's statement that faith "is not alone in the person justified, but is ever accompanied with all other saving Graces, and is no dead faith, but worketh by love" — though the BLC does reference accompanying graces and good works negatively (denying they are the basis of justification), it does not positively affirm their inseparable accompaniment. This is a minor difference appropriate to the narrower scope of the question being asked.
- Structural changes: None that alter meaning. The 1689's phrase "not by imputing faith it self, the act of beleiving, or any other evangelical obedience to them, as their Righteousness" is faithfully reflected in BLC section 3's denial that "the grace of faith or any action springing from it is imputed to the sinner for His justification."
- The BLC's language "receives Christ and applies Christ's righteousness" closely tracks the 1689's "receiving, and resting on him, and his Righteousness, by Faith" and the confession's language of faith as "the alone instrument of Justification."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q73:
> Q. 73. How doth faith justify a sinner in the sight of God?
> A. Faith justifies a sinner in the sight of God, not because of those other graces which do always accompany it, or of good works that are the fruits of it, nor as if the grace of faith, or any act thereof, were imputed to him for his justification; but only as it is an instrument by which he receiveth and applieth Christ and his righteousness.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this is pure soteriology with no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's "do always accompany it" is softened slightly to "accompanies faith" — the BLC drops "always" but this is stylistic rather than theological. The WLC's "Christ and his righteousness" appears as "Christ and applies Christ's righteousness" in the BLC — a minor rephrasing that actually makes the application of Christ's righteousness more explicit.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Structural rearrangement**: The WLC begins with the two negations (not because of accompanying graces/works; not as if faith were imputed for justification) and then states the positive (only as an instrument). The BLC reverses this, placing the positive instrumental statement first (section 1), then the first negation (section 2), then the second negation (section 3). This is a pedagogical rearrangement — leading with the positive affirmation before the denials — that does not alter theological content.

2. **"Receives Christ and applies Christ's righteousness"**: The BLC splits the WLC's single clause "receiveth and applieth Christ and his righteousness" into "receives Christ and applies Christ's righteousness." This is faithful to the original meaning: faith's object is both Christ himself and his righteousness, and faith both receives and applies.

3. **"Any other grace that accompanies faith"**: The BLC's phrasing is slightly different from the WLC's "those other graces which do always accompany it." The WLC's "always" affirms the Reformed doctrine that justifying faith is never alone (i.e., always accompanied by other graces). The BLC's phrasing still conveys the same concept but without the emphatic "always." This is a very minor precision reduction.

4. **Proof texts**: The BLC's proof texts are appropriate and well-chosen. John 1:12 (receiving Christ), Philippians 3:9 (righteousness through faith), Galatians 2:16 (justified by faith in Christ), Galatians 3:11 (not justified by law), Romans 3:28 (justified by faith apart from works), Romans 4:5 (faith counted as righteousness), and Romans 10:10 (belief unto righteousness) all support the claims made.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Section 1** (faith as instrument): John 1:12 — receiving Christ; Phil 3:9 — righteousness through faith in Christ; Gal 2:16 — justified by faith in Christ. These are standard justification proof texts and well-matched to the instrumental claim.
- **Section 2** (not by accompanying graces/works): Gal 3:11 — no one justified by the law; Rom 3:28 — justified by faith apart from works of the law. Appropriate for denying works-based justification.
- **Section 3** (faith not imputed as righteousness): Rom 4:5 — faith counted as righteousness (supporting that it is faith's object, not faith itself, that is the ground); Rom 10:10 — with the heart one believes unto righteousness. These are appropriate, though Rom 4:5 requires careful reading since it could superficially seem to say faith *is* imputed, when the Reformed understanding is that faith is the instrument by which Christ's righteousness is received and imputed.
