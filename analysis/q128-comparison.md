# Q128: What is forbidden in the seventh commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q128 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q139, preserving all key categories of sins forbidden by the seventh commandment. It maintains strong structural solidarity with the WLC while rendering the language in accessible modern English. The content aligns well with the Shorter Catechism's summary and the 1689 Confession's teaching on marriage.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is forbidden in the seventh commandment?
> A. The seventh commandment forbiddeth all unchaste thoughts, words, and actions (Mt. 15:19, 5:28; Eph. 5:3, 4).

- Omissions: None. The Shorter provides a brief summary ("all unchaste thoughts, words, and actions"), and the BLC properly expands this into the detailed categories that the Larger Catechism format requires. Every element of the Shorter's triad (thoughts, words, actions) is covered extensively in the BLC.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC preserves the Shorter's logic — the seventh commandment addresses sins of thought (imaginations, thoughts, purposes, inclinations), word (corrupt talk, listening to such), and deed (adultery, fornication, etc.) — and adds the comprehensive detail expected of a Larger Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 25, "Of Marriage"

> 1. Marriage is to be between one Man and one Woman; neither is it lawful for any man to have more then one Wife, nor for any Woman to have more then one Husband at the same time.
> 2. Marriage was ordained for the mutual help of Husband and Wife, for the increase of Man-kind, with a legitimate issue, and for preventing of uncleanness.
> 3. It is lawful for all sorts of people to Marry, who are able with judgment to give their consent; yet it is the duty of Christians to marry in the Lord, and therefore such as profess the true Religion, should not Marry with Infidels, or Idolaters; neither should such as are godly be unequally yoked, by marrying with such as are wicked, in their life, or maintain damnable Heresie.
> 4. Marriage ought not to be within the degrees of consanguinity, or Affinity forbidden in the word; nor can such incestuous Marriage ever be made lawful, by any law of Man or consent of parties, so as those persons may live together as Man and Wife.

- Omissions: None of substance. The 1689 does not provide a catechetical list of sins against the seventh commandment; it addresses marriage positively. The BLC's content aligns with what the Confession implies negatively:
  - 1689 25.1 (one man, one woman) → BLC forbids "having more than one wife or husband at the same time"
  - 1689 25.2 (preventing uncleanness) → BLC forbids all forms of unchastity
  - 1689 25.3 (lawful for all to marry) → BLC forbids "prohibiting lawful marriages" and "restrictive vows of celibacy"
  - 1689 25.3 (not marry with infidels) → BLC forbids "allowing unlawful ones" (citing Lev. 18 and Mal. 2:11-12)
  - 1689 25.4 (not within forbidden degrees) → BLC names "incest" explicitly
- Structural changes: None. The BLC derives its prohibitions consistently from the Confession's positive teaching on marriage.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 139. What are the sins forbidden in the seventh commandment?
> A. The sins forbidden in the seventh commandment, besides the neglect of the duties required, are, adultery, fornication, rape, incest, sodomy, and all unnatural lusts; all unclean imaginations, thoughts, purposes, and affections; all corrupt or filthy communications, or listening thereunto; wanton looks, impudent or light behaviour, immodest apparel; prohibiting of lawful, and dispensing with unlawful marriages; allowing, tolerating, keeping of stews, and resorting to them; entangling vows of single life, undue delay of marriage, having more wives or husbands than one at the same time; unjust divorce, or desertion; idleness, gluttony, drunkenness, unchaste company; lascivious songs, books, pictures, dancings, stage plays; and all other provocations to, or acts of uncleanness, either in ourselves or others.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None identified. This question touches moral law, not baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology, so no Baptist-specific departures are expected or present.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance. The BLC tracks the WLC answer almost point-for-point.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - WLC "affections" in "all unclean imaginations, thoughts, purposes, and affections" → BLC has "inclinations" instead of "affections." This is a defensible modernization; "inclinations" captures the same meaning as "affections" in the older sense (disordered desires/dispositions).
  - WLC "keeping of stews" → BLC renders this as "organizing prostitution," which is a faithful modernization (a "stew" being an archaic term for a brothel).
  - WLC "stage plays" → BLC has "theatrical presentations," a natural modernization.
  - WLC "impudent" → BLC has "shameless," a reasonable synonym.
  - WLC "dispensing with" unlawful marriages → BLC has "allowing" unlawful ones. This is acceptable; "dispensing with" in the WLC sense means granting dispensation for (i.e., permitting), not disposing of.

## Detailed Notes
The BLC follows the WLC's exact ordering and categorization with remarkable fidelity:

1. **Opening clause**: WLC "besides the neglect of the duties required" → BLC "In addition to failing to do what is required" — faithful modernization.
2. **Sexual sins**: adultery, fornication, rape, incest, sodomy, unnatural lusts/desires — identical categories.
3. **Internal sins**: imaginations, thoughts, purposes, affections/inclinations — preserved with minor synonym change.
4. **Communication sins**: corrupt talk, listening — preserved.
5. **Appearance sins**: wanton/lewd looks, impudent/shameless behavior, immodest apparel/dress — preserved.
6. **Marriage prohibitions**: prohibiting lawful marriages, dispensing with/allowing unlawful ones — preserved.
7. **Prostitution**: allowing, tolerating, keeping stews/organizing prostitution, resorting to them/visiting prostitutes — preserved and modernized.
8. **Celibacy and delay**: entangling/restrictive vows of single life/celibacy, undue/unnecessary delay of marriage — preserved.
9. **Polygamy**: more than one wife or husband — preserved.
10. **Divorce and desertion**: unjust divorce, desertion — preserved.
11. **Occasions of sin**: idleness, gluttony, drunkenness, unchaste/impure company — preserved.
12. **Media**: lascivious/obscene songs, books, pictures, dancing, stage plays/theatrical presentations — preserved and modernized.
13. **Catch-all**: all other provocations/encouragements to uncleanness — preserved.

The BLC adds "pornographic" to the media section ("obscene or pornographic songs, books, pictures"), which is a contextually appropriate modern expansion that the WLC's "lascivious" would have encompassed.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts largely parallel the WLC's traditional citations. Notable observations:

- The proof texts are well-chosen and match the specific sins they support.
- Some texts are shared with the WLC tradition (e.g., Rom. 1:24-27 for unnatural desires, Mt. 5:28 for impure thoughts, 1 Tim. 4:3 for prohibiting marriage, Mal. 2:16 for divorce).
- The BLC adds some additional texts not in the WLC (e.g., Proverbs 4:23, 27 for the opening clause; Proverbs 19:27 for corrupt talk) that are contextually appropriate.
- Matthew 19:10-12 for "restrictive vows of celibacy" is appropriate, as Christ there discusses the gift of celibacy as exceptional rather than a universal requirement.
- The citation of Ezekiel 16:49 for idleness is appropriate (Sodom's sin included "abundance of idleness").
- The citation of Mark 6:22 for dancing in the media section appropriately references Herodias's daughter's dance, which led to sin.
