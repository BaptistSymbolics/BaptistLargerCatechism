# Q158: If Abraham's physical descendants were circumcised under the Old Covenant, why shouldn't the children of believing parents be baptized under the New Covenant?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q158 faithfully presents the core Baptist covenant theology distinguishing the national covenant with Abraham from the covenant of grace, and correctly concludes that children of believers are not in the covenant of grace by birth but only by faith. However, it omits the Shorter Catechism's key appeal to the regulative principle ("there is neither command or example in the holy scriptures, or certain consequence from them to baptize such"), which the Shorter treats as the primary and sufficient argument. The BLC's reliance on a two-covenant Abrahamic framework, while theologically sound and grounded in the 1689 appendix, represents an expansion beyond what the standards require as the decisive argument.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- "Q. Are the infants of such as are professing believers to be baptized? A. The infants of such as are professing believers are not to be baptized, because there is neither command or example in the holy scriptures, or certain consequence from them to baptize such (Ex. 23:13; Pr. 30:6; Lk. 3:7, 8)."
- Also relevant: "Q. To whom is baptism to be administered? A. Baptism is to be administered to all those who actually profess repentance towards God (Acts 2:38; Mt. 3:6), faith in and obedience to our Lord Jesus Christ, and to none other (Acts 8:12, 36, 37, 38; 10:47, 48)."

- **Omissions:** The BLC completely omits the Shorter's decisive appeal to the regulative principle — that there is "neither command or example in the holy scriptures, or certain consequence from them" to baptize infants. This is the Shorter's entire argument and it is absent from Q158. The Shorter treats the lack of scriptural warrant as sufficient and primary; the BLC bypasses this for a covenant-theological argument.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter answers the infant baptism question with a simple appeal to the sufficiency and authority of Scripture (regulative principle). The BLC restructures the answer around a two-part Abrahamic covenant framework, making covenant theology the primary argument rather than scriptural warrant. This is a legitimate theological expansion, but the absence of the regulative principle argument is notable since it is the Shorter's sole basis.
- **Agreement:** Both conclude that infants of believers should not be baptized. The BLC's emphasis that children are only in the covenant of grace "by virtue of faith in Christ" aligns with the Shorter's restriction of baptism to those who "actually profess repentance towards God, faith in and obedience to our Lord Jesus Christ."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter VII (Of God's Covenant), Paragraph 2: "Moreover Man having brought himself under the curse of the Law by his fall, it pleased the Lord to make a Covenant of Grace wherein he freely offereth unto Sinners, Life and Salvation by Jesus Christ, requiring of them Faith in him, that they may be saved; and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal Life, his holy Spirit, to make them willing, and able to believe."

Chapter XXIX (Of Baptism), Paragraph 2: "Those who do actually professe repentance towards God, faith in, and obedience, to our Lord Jesus, are the only proper subjects of this ordinance."

1689 Appendix on Baptism (key section): "Abraham had a twofold seed, natural, of the Jews; and faithful, of the believing Gentiles: his natural seed was signed with the sign of circumcision, first indeed for the distinguishing of them from all other Nations whilst they as yet were not the seed of Abraham, but especially for the memorial of the justification of the Gentiles by faith, when at length they should become his seed. Therefore circumcision was of right to cease, when the Gentiles were brought in to the faith, forasmuch as then it had obtained its last and chief end, & thenceforth circumcision is nothing."

Also from the Appendix: "If our brethren do suppose baptism to be the seal of the Covenant which God makes with every beleiver (of which the Scriptures are altogether silent) it is not our concern to contend with them herein; yet we conceive the seal of that Covenant is the indwelling of the Spirit of Christ in the particular and individual persons in whom he resides, and nothing else."

- **Omissions:** The BLC omits the 1689's key confessional statement that "Those who do actually professe repentance towards God, faith in, and obedience, to our Lord Jesus, are the only proper subjects of this ordinance" — the word "only" is decisive. The BLC also omits the Appendix's argument that the sign of circumcision was governed by the "will of the Law-giver" (regulative principle applied to ordinances), and the Appendix's point that baptism cannot simply be "substituted in the place of circumcision" because it differs in latitude, extent, and terms.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC's two-part Abrahamic covenant framework (national covenant vs. covenant of grace) aligns well with the 1689 Appendix's distinction between Abraham's "twofold seed" (natural and faithful). This is a faithful exposition of the Appendix material. However, the Confession proper (Ch. XXIX.2) grounds the argument in the positive requirement of profession, not primarily in covenant-theological distinctions.
- **Agreement:** The BLC's core theological claim — that physical descent from Abraham does not make one a member of the covenant of grace, and that faith in Christ is required — is fully consistent with the 1689. The distinction between national/temporal promises and spiritual/eternal promises in the Abrahamic covenant draws from the Appendix's extended argument.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- WLC Q166: "Unto whom is baptism to be administered? A. Baptism is not to be administered to any that are out of the visible church, and so strangers from the covenant of promise, till they profess their faith in Christ, and obedience to him, but infants descending from parents, either both, or but one of them, professing faith in Christ, and obedience to him, are in that respect within the covenant, and to be baptized."
- Also relevant — WLC Q34: "How was the covenant of grace administered under the Old Testament? A. The covenant of grace was administered under the Old Testament, by promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the passover, and other types and ordinances, which did all fore-signify Christ then to come..."

- **Solidarity:** WEAK — This is expected and appropriate. Q158 is one of the BLC's most distinctively Baptist questions, directly countering the WLC's paedobaptist conclusion.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** The WLC holds that infants of believers "are in that respect within the covenant, and to be baptized." The BLC directly and intentionally rejects this, arguing that children are not within the covenant of grace by birth but only by faith. The WLC treats circumcision as an administration of the one covenant of grace (Q34); the BLC distinguishes the national covenant (circumcision) from the covenant of grace, which is a fundamental Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement on covenant theology. These are all expected and appropriate divergences.
- **Unexpected divergences:** None. The divergences are entirely in the expected areas of baptismal subjects, covenant theology, and the relationship between circumcision and baptism.
- **Key phrases dropped:** The WLC's framework of "visible church" membership by covenant inheritance is rejected rather than dropped — this is intentional theological disagreement, not oversight.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The question format is unusual.** BLC Q158 is framed as a paedobaptist objection requiring a Baptist response, which is a catechetical approach (answering objections) rather than the standard confessional approach of positively stating doctrine. This is a legitimate pedagogical choice but notably different from both the Shorter ("Are the infants of such as are professing believers to be baptized? — No, because...") and the WLC (which simply states who should be baptized).

2. **The two-covenant Abrahamic framework is sound but represents the BLC's theological construction.** While rooted in the 1689 Appendix's "twofold seed" language, the BLC presents this more systematically as "two parts" of the Abrahamic covenant — a national covenant with temporal promises conditioned on circumcision/obedience, and a spiritual covenant with eternal promises conditioned on faith. The 1689 Appendix discusses Abraham's "twofold seed" but does not use the precise "two parts" / "two covenants" framework as explicitly.

3. **The first section's claim needs precision.** The BLC states "The children of believers are not Abraham's true offspring by virtue of physical birth, but only by virtue of faith in Christ." While theologically sound (per Galatians 3:7-9), the phrasing "Abraham's true offspring" could be more precise — the Shorter and 1689 focus on membership in the *covenant of grace* rather than on who constitutes Abraham's "true offspring."

4. **The final section is the strongest.** The conclusion — "it's an error to confound the national covenant with the covenant of grace... and the commonwealth of Israel... with the Church" — directly addresses the core Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement and aligns with both the 1689 Appendix and broader Reformed Baptist covenant theology.

5. **Missing regulative principle argument.** The most significant concern is that neither the Shorter's appeal to the absence of scriptural command/example nor the 1689 Chapter XXIX's positive restriction ("only proper subjects") appears in Q158. The Shorter treats this as the decisive argument. A comprehensive BLC answer on infant baptism should include both the covenant-theological argument (which it has) and the regulative principle argument (which it lacks).

## Scripture Analysis
- **Galatians 3:7-9** — Appropriate for the claim that Abraham's true children are those of faith. Well-chosen.
- **Genesis 15:18; 17:7-8; 12:6-7; 13:14-17** — Land promises to Abraham's natural seed. Supports the "national covenant" claim, though the texts also contain spiritual promises intertwined with temporal ones. The BLC's clean separation into "two parts" is somewhat more systematic than the Genesis text itself.
- **Genesis 17:9-11; Deuteronomy 28** — Circumcision as sign and covenantal blessings/curses conditioned on obedience. Appropriate support.
- **Romans 2:28-29; 4:9-14; Philippians 3:3; Galatians 3:26-28; 4:21-30** — Strong proof texts for the spiritual-seed argument. Galatians 4:21-30 (Hagar/Sarah allegory) is particularly apt for distinguishing the two covenants.
- **No proof texts for the final section.** The concluding section, which states the doctrinal conclusion ("it's an error to confound the national covenant with the covenant of grace"), cites no Scripture. This is the weightiest theological claim in the answer and would benefit from proof text support (e.g., Jeremiah 31:31-34 for the new covenant's distinctiveness, or Hebrews 8:6-13).
