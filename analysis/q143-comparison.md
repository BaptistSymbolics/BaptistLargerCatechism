# Q143: What are the outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicates to us the benefits of redemption?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q143 closely follows the Shorter Catechism's parallel question but replaces "especially the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer" with the generic phrase "particularly the Word, sacraments, and prayer." This collapses "baptism" and "the Lord's supper" into the single term "sacraments," obscuring the Shorter Catechism's deliberate enumeration of the two specific Baptist ordinances. The BLC also replaces the Shorter's "especially" with "particularly" and substitutes "his ordinances" for a phrase that in the Shorter names them as Christ's ordinances more directly. More critically, this change replaces the Shorter's distinctively Baptist specificity (naming baptism and the Lord's supper individually) with Presbyterian-sounding generic language ("sacraments") that the Baptist tradition has historically been cautious about.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> Q. What are the outward means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption?
> A. The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption are his ordinances, especially the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer; all which means are made effectual to the elect for salvation (Mt. 28:19, 20; Acts 2:42, 46, 47).

- **Omissions:**
  - The Shorter explicitly names "baptism" and "the Lord's supper" as individual ordinances. The BLC collapses these into "sacraments," losing the specific enumeration.
  - The Shorter says "all which **means** are made effectual" — the BLC says "all of which are made effectual," which is grammatically different but theologically equivalent.

- **Structural changes:**
  - "especially" → "particularly" — a minor wording change, theologically equivalent.
  - "the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer" → "the Word, sacraments, and prayer" — a significant structural change. The Shorter's four-fold enumeration (word, baptism, Lord's supper, prayer) becomes a three-fold enumeration (Word, sacraments, prayer) where two distinct ordinances are collapsed into a single category.

- **Key concern:** The Baptist Shorter Catechism deliberately names "baptism" and "the Lord's supper" individually rather than using the term "sacraments." This is theologically significant because: (1) it makes the two ordinances visible and specific, (2) it avoids the term "sacraments" which carries connotations in sacramental theology that Baptists have historically been cautious about, and (3) the Shorter Catechism's enumeration matches the Baptist practice of treating these as distinct ordinances rather than as a generic sacramental category. The BLC's departure here actually moves **away** from the Shorter's Baptist specificity and **toward** the WLC's Presbyterian phrasing.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 14 (Of Saving Faith), Paragraph 1:
> "The Grace of Faith, whereby the Elect are enabled to beleive to the saving of their souls, is the work of the Spirit of Christ in their hearts; and is ordinarily wrought by the Ministry of the Word; by which also, and by the administration of Baptisme, and the Lords Supper, Prayer and other Means appointed of God, it is increased, and strengthned."

Chapter 20 (Of the Gospel), Paragraph 4:
> "Although the Gospel be the only outward means, of revealing Christ, and saving Grace; and is, as such, abundantly sufficient thereunto; yet that men who are dead in Trespasses, may be born again, Quickned or Regenerated; there is moreover necessary, an effectual, insuperable work of the Holy Spirit, upon the whole Soul, for the producing in them a new spiritual Life; without which no other means will effect their Conversion unto God."

Chapter 22 (Of Religious Worship), Paragraph 5:
> "The reading of the Scriptures, Preaching, and hearing the word of God, teaching and admonishing one another in Psalms, Hymns and Spiritual songs, singing with grace in our Hearts to the Lord; as also the Administration of Baptism, and the Lords Supper are all parts of Religious worship of God, to be performed in obedience to him, with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear."

Chapter 28 (Of Baptism and the Lords Supper), Paragraph 1:
> "Baptism and the Lords Supper are ordinances of positive, and soveraign institution; appointed by the Lord Jesus the only Law-giver, to be continued in his Church to the end of the world."

- **Omissions:**
  - The 1689 consistently names "Baptism" and "the Lord's Supper" individually (14.1, 22.5, 28.1), never collapsing them into a generic term "sacraments." The BLC's use of "sacraments" departs from the Confession's consistent practice.
  - The 1689 14.1 also mentions "other Means appointed of God" beyond the Word, Baptism, Lord's Supper, and Prayer. The BLC does not reference this broader category, though this is a minor point since the catechism is appropriately summarizing.

- **Structural changes:**
  - The 1689 treats baptism and the Lord's Supper as named, distinct ordinances throughout. The BLC restructures this by subsuming them under "sacraments," which the 1689 does not use as a category label in this way.

- **Terminological note:** The 1689 Confession does not use the term "sacraments" as a generic label for baptism and the Lord's Supper in the way the BLC does. It titles Chapter 28 "Of Baptism and the Lords Supper" (not "Of the Sacraments"), consistently naming them individually. While "sacrament" is not a forbidden term in Baptist theology, the 1689's deliberate avoidance of it as a categorical label is noteworthy, and the BLC's introduction of it departs from the Confession's practice.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> WLC Q154. What are the outward means whereby Christ communicates to us the benefits of his mediation?
> A. The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicates to his church the benefits of his mediation, are all his ordinances; especially the Word, sacraments, and prayer; all which are made effectual to the elect for their salvation.

- **Solidarity: STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC's phrasing almost verbatim, including the use of "sacraments" as a generic category.

- **Intentional Baptist divergences:**
  - "benefits of his mediation" (WLC) → "benefits of redemption" (BLC). This is a reasonable Baptist adaptation — both phrases refer to the same reality, with "redemption" being slightly more specific.
  - "communicates to his church" (WLC) → "communicates to us" (BLC). The WLC specifies the church as the locus; the BLC personalizes it. This may be intentional to avoid ecclesiological implications about the visible church as the exclusive context.
  - "their salvation" (WLC) → "salvation" (BLC). Minor simplification.

- **Unexpected divergences:** None. The BLC tracks the WLC very closely.

- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "to his church" — dropped from the WLC without clear theological reason. The WLC's phrase emphasizes that the means of grace operate within the context of the church, which is an important theological point that both Baptists and Presbyterians affirm.

## Detailed Notes

1. **"Sacraments" vs. named ordinances:** This is the central issue. The BLC appears to have followed the WLC's phrasing ("sacraments") rather than the Baptist Shorter Catechism's phrasing ("baptism, the Lord's supper"). This is ironic for a document meant to exposit the 1689 Baptist Confession, because:
   - The Baptist Shorter Catechism names the two ordinances explicitly.
   - The 1689 Confession names them explicitly throughout.
   - The WLC uses "sacraments" because Presbyterians treat the sacraments as a unified theological category tied to covenant administration.
   - Baptists have historically preferred "ordinances" and have been cautious with "sacraments" precisely because the term can import sacramental theology assumptions foreign to Baptist convictions.

2. **The BLC should follow the Shorter, not the WLC, on this point.** Since the BLC's stated purpose is to align with the 1689 and the Shorter Catechism while showing solidarity with the WLC, it should follow the Shorter's enumeration ("baptism, the Lord's supper") rather than the WLC's generic category ("sacraments"). This is one of those places where Baptist distinctiveness should override WLC solidarity.

3. **"To his church" omission:** The WLC's specification that Christ communicates benefits "to his church" is theologically significant — it locates the means of grace within the context of the visible church. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not include this phrase either, so the BLC's omission is consistent with the Shorter. However, the 1689 22.5 does locate these activities within corporate worship, so an explicit church reference would not have been out of place.

4. **"Redemption" vs. "mediation":** The BLC's substitution of "redemption" for the WLC's "mediation" follows the Shorter Catechism, which also uses "redemption." This is appropriate and shows proper priority of the Shorter over the WLC.

## Scripture Analysis

**BLC proof texts:** Matthew 28:19-20; Acts 2:42-47; 1 Timothy 4:16; 1 Corinthians 1:21; Ephesians 5:19-20; Ephesians 6:17-18

**Shorter Catechism proof texts:** Mt. 28:19, 20; Acts 2:42, 46, 47

- The BLC expands the Shorter's proof texts significantly, adding 1 Timothy 4:16 (devotion to Scripture and doctrine), 1 Corinthians 1:21 (the foolishness of preaching), Ephesians 5:19-20 (psalms, hymns, spiritual songs), and Ephesians 6:17-18 (sword of the Spirit and prayer). These are all appropriate and well-chosen additions.
- Matthew 28:19-20 and Acts 2:42-47 are shared with the Shorter and are foundational texts for the means of grace. Notably, Matthew 28:19-20 explicitly mentions baptism, which reinforces the argument that baptism should be named individually in the answer.
- The proof texts support Word, prayer, and corporate worship well. They do not specifically support the generic term "sacraments" — rather, they support the individual naming of baptism (Matt 28:19-20) and the breaking of bread (Acts 2:42).
