# Q54: How was Christ exalted in His ascension?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q54 is a close modernization of WLC Q53, preserving all substantive theological content about Christ's ascension. The question faithfully exposits the relevant Confession material and the Shorter Catechism's summary reference to the ascension, while expanding it with appropriate detail drawn from the WLC. No omissions, contradictions, or structural alterations of concern are present.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Wherein consisteth Christ's exaltation?
> A. Christ's exaltation consisteth in his rising again from the dead on the third day (1 Cor. 15:4), in ascending up into heaven (Mark 16:19), in sitting at the right hand of God the Father (Eph. 1:20), and in coming to judge the world at the last day (Acts 1:11; 17:31).

- Omissions: None. The Shorter merely lists "ascending up into heaven" as one of the four aspects of exaltation. The BLC Q54 properly expands this single phrase into a full exposition, which is exactly the role of a Larger Catechism.
- Structural changes: None. The Shorter's four-part structure of exaltation (resurrection, ascension, session, return) is preserved across BLC Q52-Q55 (or similar range), with Q54 taking up the ascension specifically.
- The Shorter's reference to Christ's return "at the last day" is echoed in BLC Q54's closing phrase "until His second coming at the end of the world."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8 ("Of Christ the Mediator"), Paragraph 4:
> "on the third day he arose from the dead, with the same body in which he suffered; with which he also ascended into heaven: and there sitteth at the right hand of his Father, making intercession; and shall return to judge Men and Angels, at the end of the World."

- Omissions: None. The Confession's treatment of the ascension is brief — "ascended into heaven: and there sitteth at the right hand of his Father" — and the BLC appropriately expands on this with detail about the nature and purposes of the ascension. The Confession's mention of "making intercession" belongs to the session at God's right hand, not the ascension per se, and would properly be treated in a subsequent question.
- Structural changes: None. The Confession's narrative flow (resurrection → ascension → session → return) is maintained in the BLC's question structure.
- The BLC's emphasis on ascending "in our nature and as our head" is consistent with the Confession's incarnational framework in 8.2 (Christ taking man's nature) and the mediatorial office described throughout Chapter 8.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q53:
> Q. 53. How was Christ exalted in his ascension?
> A. Christ was exalted in his ascension, in that having after his resurrection often appeared unto and conversed with his apostles, speaking to them of the things pertaining to the kingdom of God, and giving them commission to preach the gospel to all nations, forty days after his resurrection, he, in our nature, and as our head, triumphing over enemies, visibly went up into the highest heavens, there to receive gifts for men, to raise up our affections thither, and to prepare a place for us, where he himself is, and shall continue till his second coming at the end of the world.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None present. This question involves Christology, not baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology — no Baptist divergence is expected or found.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped: None materially. All WLC elements are preserved:
  - "often appeared" → "appeared numerous times" (modernized)
  - "giving them commission to preach the gospel to all nations" → "commissioning them to preach the gospel to all nations" (modernized)
  - "in our nature, and as our head" → retained verbatim
  - "triumphing over enemies" → retained (repositioned to follow "forty days" clause but same content)
  - "visibly went up into the highest heavens" → "visibly ascended to the highest heavens" (modernized)
  - "there to receive gifts for men" → "There, He receives gifts for men" (modernized)
  - "to raise up our affections thither" → "raises our minds and affections" (slight expansion adding "minds," which is theologically appropriate given Colossians 3:1-2 includes both seeking and setting the mind)
  - "to prepare a place for us" → retained
  - "shall continue till his second coming at the end of the world" → "shall continue to be until His second coming at the end of the world" (modernized)

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC restructures the WLC's single flowing sentence into eight discrete proof-texted sections. This is a formatting difference, not a theological one, and aids clarity.
- The WLC's word "often" is changed to "numerous times" — equivalent meaning.
- The BLC adds "and conversed with" where the WLC has "appeared unto and conversed with" — the BLC has "appeared numerous times and conversed with His apostles," which preserves both elements.
- The phrase "raises our minds and affections" (vs. WLC's "raise up our affections thither") is a minor but defensible expansion. The word "minds" is implied by "affections" in older usage but is made explicit here, and the proof text (Colossians 3:1-2) supports both cognitive ("seek those things which are above") and affective ("set your affection on things above") dimensions.
- The repositioning of "forty days after His resurrection" to precede the ascension clause (rather than following the commissioning clause) slightly changes the sentence flow but not the meaning. Both the WLC and BLC indicate the ascension occurred forty days after the resurrection.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Acts 1:2-3** — Appropriately cited for Christ's post-resurrection appearances and teaching about the kingdom.
- **Matthew 28:19-20** — Appropriately cited for the Great Commission. The WLC cites the same reference.
- **John 20:17; Hebrews 6:20** — Used for "in our nature and as our head." The WLC footnotes only cite Hebrews 6:20. The BLC adds John 20:17 ("I ascend to my Father and your Father"), which strengthens the "our nature" connection — a helpful addition.
- **Ephesians 4:8** — Cited for "triumphing over enemies," matching the WLC's proof text.
- **Acts 1:9-11; Ephesians 4:7-8; Psalm 68:18; Ephesians 4:10; Acts 2:33** — Cited for "receives gifts for men." This expands the WLC's proof texts (which include Acts 1:9-11, Ephesians 4:10, Psalm 68:18). The addition of Ephesians 4:7-8 and Acts 2:33 is appropriate and strengthens the citation.
- **Colossians 3:1-2** — Cited for "raises our minds and affections," matching the WLC.
- **John 14:3** — Cited for "prepares a place for us," matching the WLC.
- **Acts 3:21** — Cited for Christ continuing in heaven until His return, matching the WLC.
- All proof texts are appropriate and well-chosen. No problematic or missing citations.
