# Q109: How is the Sabbath to be sanctified?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q109 covers the same ground as the Shorter Catechism, 1689 Confession 22.8, and WLC Q117, preserving the core elements of Sabbath sanctification: holy rest, cessation from lawful employments/recreations, public and private worship, works of necessity and mercy, and advance preparation. However, the BLC introduces two notable additions not found in any standard—the word "joyfully" and the phrase "carefully plan ahead"—and omits the 1689's important specification that rest extends to "words, and thoughts" about worldly matters, not merely employments. The structural reordering also places the mercy exception before the worship duty, slightly weakening the emphasis on worship as the primary positive obligation.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. How is the Sabbath to be sanctified? A. The Sabbath is to be sanctified by a holy resting all that day (Ex. 20:8, 10), even from such worldly employments and recreations as are lawful on other days (Ex. 16:25-28; Neh. 13:15-22); and spending the whole time in the public and private exercises of God's worship (Lk. 4:16; Acts 20:7; Ps. 92:title; Is. 66:23), except so much as is to be taken up in the works of necessity and mercy (Mt. 12:1-13)."

- **Omissions:** The Shorter says "spending the whole time in the public and private exercises of God's worship"—the BLC renders this as "joyfully spend all our time publicly and privately worshiping God." The word "joyfully" is an addition not found in the Shorter. While not contradictory (Isaiah 58:13 does reference delight), it introduces a subjective qualification absent from the standard. The Shorter's phrase "exercises of God's worship" (which encompasses a broader range of duties than simply "worshiping God") is narrowed slightly.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter places the necessity/mercy exception as a parenthetical qualifier at the end ("except so much as is to be taken up in..."). The BLC elevates it to a separate section placed *before* the worship command, which subtly alters the emphasis. In the Shorter, worship is the primary positive command with mercy as a permitted exception; in the BLC, the exception precedes the command.
- **Addition:** The BLC adds an entire final section on preparation ("prepare our hearts and carefully plan ahead so that our worldly business is taken care of beforehand"). This is not in the Shorter but is drawn from the 1689 and WLC, which is appropriate for a Larger Catechism expanding on the Shorter.
- **Agreement:** The core elements—holy rest for the whole day, cessation from lawful worldly employments and recreations, public and private worship, and the exception for necessity and mercy—are all preserved.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 22, Paragraph 8 — "The Sabbath is then kept holy unto the Lord, when men after a due preparing of their hearts, and ordering their common affairs aforehand, do not only observe an holy rest all the day, from their own works, words, and thoughts, about their worldly employment, and recreations, but also are taken up the whole time in the publick and private exercises of his worship, and in the duties of necessity and mercy."

- **Omissions:** The 1689 specifies rest "from their own works, **words, and thoughts**, about their worldly employment, and recreations." The BLC omits the crucial phrase "words, and thoughts," reducing the scope of Sabbath rest to merely ceasing from "worldly employments and recreations" without addressing the inward and verbal dimensions. This is a meaningful omission—the 1689 teaches that even *thinking and speaking* about worldly business profanes the Sabbath, a teaching with substantial pastoral import.
- **Structural changes:** The 1689 places preparation *first* ("when men after a due preparing of their hearts, and ordering their common affairs aforehand") and then the duties follow logically. The BLC reverses this, placing preparation last. While not theologically damaging, it weakens the 1689's emphasis that preparation is a *precondition* for proper Sabbath-keeping, not an afterthought.
- **Addition:** The BLC adds "joyfully" to the worship command and "carefully plan ahead" to the preparation command—neither phrase appears in the 1689. The 1689 uses the more measured "due preparing" and "ordering... aforehand."
- **Agreement:** The BLC preserves the 1689's key teaching that advance preparation of worldly affairs is part of Sabbath sanctification, which the Shorter omits. The public and private worship elements are preserved.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q117: "How is the sabbath or the Lord's day to be sanctified? A. The sabbath or Lord's day is to be sanctified by an holy resting all the day, not only from such works as are at all times sinful, but even from such worldly employments and recreations as are on other days lawful; and making it our delight to spend the whole time (except so much of it as is to be taken up in works of necessity and mercy) in the public and private exercises of God's worship: and, to that end, we are to prepare our hearts, and with such foresight, diligence, and moderation, to dispose and seasonably dispatch our worldly business, that we may be the more free and fit for the duties of that day."

- **Solidarity:** MODERATE
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected or found. This is a matter of common Reformed agreement on the fourth commandment with no ecclesiological or baptismal implications.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC specifies rest "not only from such works as are at all times sinful, but even from such worldly employments and recreations as are on other days lawful." The BLC drops the first half of this distinction—resting from works that are "at all times sinful"—retaining only the rest from lawful employments. While resting from sinful works may seem obvious, the WLC's explicit mention serves the pedagogical purpose of establishing a baseline before extending to lawful works. This is a notable structural loss.
  - The WLC says "making it our delight"—the BLC renders this as "joyfully." This captures a similar idea but loses the WLC's echo of Isaiah 58:13 ("call the sabbath a delight"), which is a more precise formulation.
  - The WLC's preparation language is richer: "with such foresight, diligence, and moderation, to dispose and seasonably dispatch our worldly business, that we may be the more free and fit for the duties of that day." The BLC's "carefully plan ahead so that our worldly business is taken care of beforehand in order that we may more readily spend the day the way God requires" captures the idea but loses "foresight, diligence, and moderation" (three specific virtues) and "seasonably dispatch" (the concept of timeliness).
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "not only from such works as are at all times sinful" — pedagogically important distinction dropped
  - "making it our delight" — replaced with "joyfully" (weaker echo of Isa. 58:13)
  - "foresight, diligence, and moderation" — specific preparation virtues dropped
  - "seasonably dispatch" — timeliness concept dropped
  - "the more free and fit for the duties of that day" — replaced with "more readily spend the day the way God requires" (adequate but less precise)

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "words and thoughts" omission** is the most significant finding. The 1689's specification that Sabbath rest extends to "words, and thoughts" about worldly matters—not just ceasing from worldly work—is a substantial pastoral teaching. It addresses the common problem of physical rest from labor while mental preoccupation with business continues. The BLC's silence here represents a real loss of confessional content.

2. **The "joyfully" addition** is interesting. While the WLC says "making it our delight" (drawn from Isaiah 58:13), neither the Shorter nor the 1689 includes any reference to subjective disposition in their sanctification language. The BLC's "joyfully" appears to combine the WLC's "delight" language with modernization, but it places this as an adverb modifying worship rather than as the WLC's distinct clause about attitude toward the day itself.

3. **The structural reordering** of the mercy/necessity exception is worth noting. Both the Shorter and WLC place this exception parenthetically within or after the worship command, subordinating it. The BLC gives it its own section placed before worship, which could subtly suggest that identifying exceptions is a prior concern to engaging in worship.

4. **The preparation section** is a welcome expansion from the Shorter, properly drawing on the 1689 and WLC. However, the BLC's "carefully plan ahead" is informal compared to the 1689's "due preparing of their hearts, and ordering their common affairs aforehand." Notably, the 1689 mentions preparing "hearts" alongside ordering "common affairs"—the BLC collapses both into "prepare our hearts and carefully plan ahead," which could be read as two separate actions but loses the 1689's clear pairing of spiritual and practical preparation.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Exodus 20:8-10** — Appropriately cited for holy rest.
- **Exodus 16:25-29; Nehemiah 13:15-22; Jeremiah 17:21-22** — Good support for cessation from worldly employments. Jeremiah 17:21-22 is an addition beyond the Shorter's citations.
- **Matthew 11:1-13** — This citation is puzzling for the necessity/mercy exception. The standard citation is Matthew 12:1-13 (disciples plucking grain and healing on the Sabbath). Matthew 11:1-13 concerns John the Baptist's inquiry from prison. This appears to be a **citation error**—likely meant to be Matthew 12:1-13, which is correctly cited alongside it. The Shorter cites Mt. 12:1-13; the 1689 cites Mat. 12.1-13.
- **Isaiah 58:13,18** — Isaiah 58:18 does not exist (Isaiah 58 has only 14 verses). This is likely a **citation error**—perhaps Isaiah 58:13-14 was intended, which would be appropriate (calling the Sabbath a delight and being honored for it).
- **Isaiah 66:23; Luke 4:15-16; Acts 20:7; 1 Corinthians 16:1-2; Leviticus 23:3** — Appropriate citations for public worship and Sabbath assembly.
- **Exodus 20:8; Luke 23:54-56; Exodus 16:22-29; Nehemiah 13:19** — Good support for preparation. Luke 23:54-56 (preparation day before Sabbath) is a particularly apt addition.
- **Two apparent Scripture citation errors** (Matthew 11:1-13 and Isaiah 58:18) should be corrected.
