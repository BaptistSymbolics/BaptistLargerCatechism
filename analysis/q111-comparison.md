# Q111: What are the reasons annexed to the fourth commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
The BLC substantially covers all four reasons from the Shorter Catechism and follows the WLC's expanded structure closely. However, it drops the Shorter's qualifier "lawful" from "lawful employments," which subtly weakens the teaching that even the six permitted days are governed by moral law. The BLC also replaces the Shorter's precise term "propriety" (ownership claim) with a looser paraphrase.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What are the reasons annexed to the fourth commandment? A. The reasons annexed to the fourth commandment, are God's allowing us six days of the week for our own lawful employments (Ex. 20:9), his challenging a special propriety in a seventh, his own example, and his blessing the Sabbath day (Ex. 20:11)."

- **Omissions:**
  - The word **"lawful"** before "employments" is dropped. The Shorter says "our own lawful employments"; the BLC says merely "our own affairs." This qualifier is theologically significant: it teaches that even on the six permitted days, only lawful activities are sanctioned. Dropping it could imply the six days are unrestricted.
  - The phrase **"challenging a special propriety"** is reduced to "insists that this day belongs to him." While the meaning is broadly similar, "propriety" (a property/ownership claim) is a precise theological term the Shorter deliberately uses. The BLC's paraphrase is adequate but less precise.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter lists four reasons in compact form (equity, propriety, example, blessing). The BLC expands these into three sections, combining God's example and blessing into one section. This follows the WLC's pattern and is acceptable.
- The four core reasons (equity, propriety, example, blessing) are all present in substance.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 22, Paragraphs 7-8

Paragraph 7: "As it is of the Law of nature, that in general a proportion of time by Gods appointment, be set a part for the Worship of God; so by his Word in a positive-moral, and perpetual Commandement, binding all men, in all Ages, he hath particularly appointed one day in seven for a Sabbath to be kept holy unto him, which from the beginning of the World to the Resurrection of Christ, was the last day of the week; and from the resurrection of Christ, was changed into the first day of the week which is called the Lords day; and is to be continued to the end of the World, as the Christian Sabbath; the observation of the last day of the week being abolished."

Paragraph 8: "The Sabbath is then kept holy unto the Lord, when men after a due preparing of their hearts, and ordering their common affairs aforehand, do not only observe an holy rest all the day, from their own works, words, and thoughts, about their worldly employment, and recreations, but also are taken up the whole time in the publick and private exercises of his worship, and in the duties of necessity and mercy."

- **Omissions:** The 1689 does not directly address the "reasons annexed" to the fourth commandment in the same catechetical framework, so direct comparison is limited. The Confession's emphasis on the Sabbath as a "positive-moral, and perpetual Commandement, binding all men, in all Ages" is not contradicted by Q111 but is not explicitly echoed either.
- **Structural changes:** None problematic. The BLC's focus on the reasons from Exodus 20:9-11 is appropriate for a catechism question specifically about the annexed reasons.
- The BLC is consistent with the Confession's Sabbath theology. The Confession emphasizes the perpetual and binding nature of the command, while Q111 addresses the motivating reasons — complementary rather than overlapping concerns.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q120: "What are the reasons annexed to the fourth commandment, the more to enforce it? A. The reasons annexed to the fourth commandment, the more to enforce it, are taken from the equity of it, God allowing us six days of seven for our own affairs, and reserving but one for himself in these words, Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work: from God's challenging a special propriety in that day, The seventh day is the sabbath of the Lord thy God: from the example of God, who in six days made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: and from that blessing which God put upon that day, not only in sanctifying it to be a day for his service, but in ordaining it to be a means of blessing to us in our sanctifying it; Wherefore the Lord blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC closely follows the WLC's structure and expository approach, preserving all four reasons and the Scripture quotations.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected or found in this question (no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology involved).
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC's phrase "the more to enforce it" in the question is replaced with "to emphasize its importance" — a minor modernization that is functionally equivalent.
  - The WLC's "equity of it" becomes "inherent fairness" — acceptable modernization.
  - The WLC says "God's challenging a special propriety in that day" — this precise language of God "challenging" (i.e., claiming) a "propriety" (ownership right) is dropped. The BLC says "God also insists that this day belongs to him." The meaning is preserved but the theological precision is reduced.
  - The WLC's dual aspect of blessing ("not only in sanctifying it to be a day for his service, but in ordaining it to be a means of blessing to us in our sanctifying it") is preserved well in the BLC's "not just by making it a holy day for serving him but also by establishing that our keeping the Sabbath holy will be a blessing to us as well."
- **Key phrases dropped:** "Challenging a special propriety" is the most notable WLC phrase dropped, though its meaning is paraphrased.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Lawful" qualifier:** This is the most notable omission. The Shorter Catechism says "lawful employments" — the BLC says "our own affairs." While the BLC is a modernization, "lawful" is not archaic vocabulary; it is a deliberate theological qualifier teaching that even the six working days are under God's moral governance. Its omission, while not a contradiction, weakens a point the Shorter considered important enough to include.

2. **"Propriety" language:** Both the Shorter and WLC use "propriety" (property right, ownership claim) to describe God's relationship to the Sabbath day. The BLC's "this day belongs to him" conveys the same idea but lacks the formal force of a property/ownership claim. This is a defensible modernization choice.

3. **Combined section:** The BLC combines God's example and God's blessing into one section. The WLC and Shorter treat these as distinct reasons (four total). The BLC's three-section structure still identifies all four reasons but gives example and blessing less distinct treatment.

4. **Overall quality:** The BLC is a faithful modernization of the WLC answer. The four reasons from Exodus 20:9-11 are all represented with appropriate Scripture quotations.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Exodus 20:9** — Cited for the equity/fairness argument. Matches both Shorter and WLC usage.
- **Exodus 20:10** — Cited for God's propriety claim. Matches both Shorter and WLC usage.
- **Exodus 20:11** — Cited for God's example and blessing. Matches both Shorter and WLC usage.
- The proof texts are identical in scope to the Shorter and WLC. No Scripture references are missing or inappropriately added.
