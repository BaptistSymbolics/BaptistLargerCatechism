# Q178: Why must we pray in Christ's name?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q178 is a close modernization of WLC Q181, preserving its three-part logical structure (sinful distance from God, Christ's unique appointment as Mediator, therefore prayer in His name only) with minimal alteration. The 1689 Confession's teaching on mediation and prayer in Christ's name is faithfully reflected. The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question but its definition of prayer (Q98) includes "in the name of Christ," which BLC Q178 properly exposits.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest is the definition of prayer:
> "Q. What is prayer? A. Prayer is an offering up our desires to God, by the assistance of the Holy Spirit, for things agreeable to his will, in the name of Christ, believing, with confession of our sins, and thankful acknowledgments of his mercies." (Q98)

The Shorter does not have a separate question explaining *why* we pray in Christ's name. BLC Q178 serves as an expansion/exposition of the Shorter's phrase "in the name of Christ," explaining the theological rationale behind it.

- Omissions: None — the BLC is expanding on content the Shorter mentions but does not elaborate.
- Structural changes: None — BLC Q178 provides the theological "why" that the Shorter's definition assumes but does not unpack.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 22, Paragraph 2:
> "Religious Worship is to be given to God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and to him alone; not to Angels, Saints, or any other Creatures; and since the fall, not without a Mediator, nor in the Mediation of any other but Christ alone."

Chapter 22, Paragraph 3:
> "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will..."

Chapter 8, Paragraph 9:
> "This office of Mediator between God and Man, is proper onely to Christ, who is the Prophet, Priest, and King of the Church of God; and may not be either in whole, or any part thereof transfer'd from him to any other."

- Omissions: None of substance. The 1689's core points — (1) worship since the fall requires a mediator, (2) no mediator but Christ alone, (3) prayer must be made in the name of the Son — are all present in BLC Q178.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC follows the same logical flow: sin creates distance → mediation required → Christ alone qualifies → pray in His name only.

The BLC's phrase "Human sinfulness has so far separated us from God" captures the 1689's "since the fall, not without a Mediator." The BLC's "no one in heaven or earth has been appointed to or is fit for that glorious job except Christ alone" faithfully reflects both 22:2 ("nor in the Mediation of any other but Christ alone") and 8:9 ("proper onely to Christ... may not be either in whole, or any part thereof transfer'd from him to any other").

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q181:
> "Why are we to pray in the name of Christ? A. The sinfulness of man, and his distance from God by reason thereof, being so great, as that we can have no access into his presence without a mediator; and there being none in heaven or earth appointed to, or fit for, that glorious work but Christ alone, we are to pray in no other name but his only."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — BLC Q178 is essentially a straightforward modernization of WLC Q181.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is not a topic where Baptist-Presbyterian differences apply.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's phrase "his distance from God by reason thereof" is slightly condensed in the BLC to "separated us from God" — but the concept of distance/separation caused by sin is retained. The WLC says "glorious work" where the BLC says "glorious job" — a minor vocabulary modernization. The BLC adds "to bridge that separation" which is a small explanatory gloss on the mediator's role, consistent with the WLC's meaning.

The three-part structure is perfectly preserved:
1. WLC: "The sinfulness of man, and his distance from God by reason thereof, being so great, as that we can have no access into his presence without a mediator" → BLC: "Human sinfulness has so far separated us from God that we cannot gain access to His presence without a mediator to bridge that separation"
2. WLC: "and there being none in heaven or earth appointed to, or fit for, that glorious work but Christ alone" → BLC: "and since no one in heaven or earth has been appointed to or is fit for that glorious job except Christ alone"
3. WLC: "we are to pray in no other name but his only" → BLC: "we must pray in His name only and in no other"

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC slightly rephrases the conclusion from "pray in no other name but his only" to "pray in His name only and in no other." Both formulations make the same exclusive claim; the BLC's version is arguably slightly more emphatic by stating it both positively ("in His name only") and negatively ("and in no other").
- The word "job" for "work" is a legitimate modernization, though "work" would arguably carry more theological weight and dignity. This is a stylistic preference, not a theological concern.
- The added phrase "to bridge that separation" is a helpful explanatory gloss that makes the mediator's function more explicit. It does not introduce new doctrine but clarifies what mediation accomplishes.
- BLC Q178 corresponds to WLC Q181 specifically. The BLC does not appear to have a separate parallel to WLC Q180 ("What is it to pray in the name of Christ?"), which teaches that praying in Christ's name is not "by bare mentioning of his name" but involves drawing encouragement, boldness, strength, and hope of acceptance from Christ and his mediation. This is a notable structural observation — the WLC splits this into two questions (what it means, Q180; and why, Q181), while the BLC covers only the "why." The "what it means" content may be addressed elsewhere in the BLC.

## Scripture Analysis
- **John 14:6; Isaiah 59:2; Ephesians 3:12** (section 1): Strong support for the separation caused by sin and Christ as the way of access. Isaiah 59:2 directly teaches that sin separates from God. John 14:6 establishes Christ as the exclusive way. Ephesians 3:12 teaches boldness and access through faith in Christ. These align well with the WLC's proof texts (which cite John 14:6 and Ephesians 3:12 among others).
- **John 6:27; Hebrews 7:25-27; 1 Timothy 2:5** (section 2): Excellent proof texts for Christ's unique appointment and fitness as Mediator. 1 Timothy 2:5 is the classic text on Christ as the one mediator. Hebrews 7:25-27 establishes His perfect, perpetual priesthood. John 6:27 speaks of the Son whom the Father has sealed/appointed.
- **Colossians 3:17; Hebrews 13:15** (section 3): Appropriate for the conclusion that we must do all in Christ's name. Colossians 3:17 ("whatsoever ye do... do all in the name of the Lord Jesus") and Hebrews 13:15 ("by him... let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually") both support exclusive prayer through Christ.

The proof texts are well-chosen and theologically appropriate, overlapping significantly with both the WLC's Scripture references and the 1689's citations for the relevant paragraphs.
