# Q97: What is forbidden in the first commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q97 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q105, preserving virtually all content in updated English. It expands from the Shorter Catechism's compact summary as expected of a "Larger" catechism. The BLC tracks the WLC's structure and catalog of sins with high fidelity, and all key doctrinal elements from the 1689 Confession's relevant chapters are reflected.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is forbidden in the first commandment?
> A. The first commandment forbiddeth the denying (Ps. 14:1), or not worshipping and glorifying the true God (Rom. 1:21), as God and our God (Ps. 81:10, 11), and the giving of that worship and glory to any other, which is due unto him alone (Rom. 1:25, 26).

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's four core prohibitions are all present in the BLC:
  1. "denying" God → BLC: "atheism, denying or not believing in God"
  2. "not worshipping and glorifying the true God" → BLC: "failing or neglecting to do anything this commandment requires relating to God" (plus the extensive catalog of specific failures)
  3. "as God and our God" → BLC: "not having and affirming him as God and our God"
  4. "giving of that worship and glory to any other" → BLC: "idolatry, believing in or worshiping any other gods along with or other than the one true God" plus the extensive catalog of misdirected worship (saints, angels, created beings, the devil)
- Structural changes: None significant. The BLC properly expands each of the Shorter's compact categories into the detailed catalog expected of a Larger catechism.
- The Shorter's phrase "not worshipping and glorifying" is captured in the BLC's general clause about "failing or neglecting to do anything this commandment requires," which properly points back to BLC Q96 (the duties required).

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 2, Paragraph 2: "to him is due from Angels and men, whatsoever worship, service, or obedience as Creatures they owe unto the Creator, and whatever he is further pleased to require of them."

Chapter 22, Paragraph 1: "The light of Nature shews that there is a God, who hath Lordship, and Soveraigntye over all; is just, good, and doth good unto all; and is therefore to be feared, loved, praised, called upon, trusted in, and served, with all the Heart, and all the Soul, and with all the Might. But the acceptable way of Worshipping the true God, is instituted by himself; and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be Worshipped according to the imaginations, and devices of Men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representations, or any other way, not prescribed in the Holy Scriptures."

Chapter 22, Paragraph 2: "Religious Worship is to be given to God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and to him alone; not to Angels, Saints, or any other Creatures; and since the fall, not without a Mediator, nor in the Mediation of any other but Christ alone."

- Omissions: None. The key 1689 themes are all reflected:
  - God alone is due worship (Ch. 2.2) → reflected throughout the BLC's catalog
  - Worship not according to imaginations of men or suggestions of Satan (Ch. 22.1) → BLC covers "making an agreement with, consulting, or following the suggestions of the devil"
  - Not to angels, saints, or creatures (Ch. 22.2) → BLC explicitly includes "praying to or worshiping saints, angels, or any other created being"
  - Not making men lords of faith and conscience (implied in Ch. 22.1's regulative principle) → BLC: "making men the rulers of our faith and conscience"
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's enumeration is consistent with the Confession's theological framework.
- The 1689's emphasis on the regulative principle (Ch. 22.1) is not explicitly stated in the BLC Q97, but this is appropriate since the regulative principle pertains more to the *second* commandment (manner of worship) than the first (object of worship). The BLC properly distinguishes these domains.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 105. What are the sins forbidden in the first commandment?
> A. The sins forbidden in the first commandment are, atheism, in denying or not having a God; idolatry, in having or worshipping more gods than one, or any with or instead of the true God; the not having and avouching him for God, and our God; the omission or neglect of anything due to him, required in this commandment; ignorance, forgetfulness, misapprehensions, false opinions, unworthy and wicked thoughts of him; bold and curious searching into his secrets; all profaneness, hatred of God; self-love, self-seeking, and all other inordinate and immoderate setting of our mind, will, or affections upon other things, and taking them off from him in whole or in part; vain credulity, unbelief, heresy, misbelief, distrust, despair, incorrigibleness, and insensibleness under judgments, hardness of heart, pride, presumption, carnal security, tempting of God; using unlawful means, and trusting in lawful means; carnal delights and joys; corrupt, blind, and indiscreet zeal; lukewarmness, and deadness in the things of God; estranging ourselves, and apostatizing from God; praying, or giving any religious worship, to saints, angels, or any other creatures; all compacts and consulting with the devil, and hearkening to his suggestions; making men the lords of our faith and conscience; slighting and despising God and his commands; resisting and grieving of his Spirit, discontent and impatience at his dispensations, charging him foolishly for the evils he inflicts on us; and ascribing the praise of any good we either are, have or can do, to fortune, idols, ourselves, or any other creature.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None detected. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology distinctions.
- Unexpected divergences: None significant.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - WLC "not having a God" (in the atheism clause) — BLC renders this as "not believing in God," which is a reasonable modernization though slightly shifts from ontological denial to epistemic unbelief. The difference is minimal since both are captured.
  - WLC "avouching" → BLC "affirming" — straightforward modernization, no loss of meaning.
  - WLC "self-seeking" → BLC "self-interest" — reasonable synonym.
  - WLC "incorrigibleness" → BLC "refusing correction" — good modernization that captures the meaning.
  - WLC "insensibleness under judgments" → BLC "resisting God's judgment" — slight shift from passive insensibility to active resistance, but both are covered since the BLC also has "hardness of heart" and "worldly complacency" which capture the passive dimension.
  - WLC "presumption" → BLC "willfulness" — the WLC's "presumption" specifically refers to presuming on God's mercy (presumptuous sin); BLC's "willfulness" may not fully capture this theological nuance, though combined with "putting God to the test" the range is still covered.
  - WLC "carnal security" → BLC "worldly complacency" — reasonable modernization.
  - WLC "hearkening to his suggestions" (regarding the devil) → BLC "following the suggestions of the devil" — preserved.
  - WLC "discontent and impatience at his dispensations" → BLC "being dissatisfied and offended by the things God provides in our lives" — good modernization.
  - WLC "fortune" → BLC "luck" — appropriate modernization of the same concept.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Structure:** The BLC follows the WLC's order almost exactly, which is noteworthy given that this is a very long answer. The catalog proceeds through the same logical sequence: atheism → idolatry → not claiming God → neglect → intellectual sins → emotional sins → spiritual sins → false worship → occult → human authority → despising God → grieving the Spirit → discontent → misattribution. This demonstrates strong structural solidarity.

2. **Modernization quality:** The BLC's rendering into modern English is well-executed throughout. Complex archaic terms like "incorrigibleness," "carnal security," "avouching," and "vain credulity" are rendered into accessible equivalents without losing theological content.

3. **Expansion from WLC:** The BLC adds "and spiritual deadness" alongside "lukewarmness" — the WLC has "deadness in the things of God" as a separate item paired with lukewarmness, and the BLC preserves this pairing while slightly condensing the phrasing.

4. **Minor note on atheism:** The WLC says "denying or not having a God" — where "not having" suggests practical atheism (living without reference to God). The BLC's "denying or not believing in God" shifts slightly toward intellectual atheism. However, practical atheism is well-covered by the many subsequent clauses about neglect, forgetfulness, and lukewarmness.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts largely correspond to the WLC's citations, with some updates and additions:
- Psalm 14:1 for atheism — shared with both the Shorter and WLC
- Psalm 81:11 for not having God as our God — shared (WLC/Shorter cite Ps. 81:10-11)
- Deuteronomy 29:29 for curious searching into God's secrets — shared with WLC
- The BLC adds Ephesians 2:12 for atheism, which is a strong addition (being "without God in the world")
- 1 Thessalonians 1:9 for idolatry is apt (turning from idols to serve the living God)
- The proof texts are well-chosen throughout and appropriately support each clause
- No problematic proof text choices were identified
