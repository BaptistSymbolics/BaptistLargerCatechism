# Q52: What was the estate of Christ's exaltation?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q52 introduces the word "imminent" to describe Christ's return, a theological addition absent from all three standards. This injects a contested eschatological position into what is otherwise a simple enumeration of exaltation stages. Additionally, the BLC omits "on the third day" from the resurrection (present in both the Shorter and WLC), omits "making intercession" from the session at God's right hand (present in the 1689), omits the judgment of "Angels" (1689), and omits "at the last day" / "at the end of the World" (Shorter and 1689). The addition of "imminent" combined with the omission of "at the last day" produces a structural shift in eschatological framing that is novel relative to all three standards.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Wherein consisteth Christ's exaltation?
> A. Christ's exaltation consisteth in his rising again from the dead on the third day (1 Cor. 15:4), in ascending up into heaven (Mark 16:19), in sitting at the right hand of God the Father (Eph. 1:20), and in coming to judge the world at the last day (Acts 1:11; 17:31).

- Omissions:
  - **"on the third day"** — The Shorter specifies Christ rose "on the third day." The BLC says only "His resurrection." This temporal marker is theologically significant: it is a creedal affirmation (Nicene Creed, Apostles' Creed), a fulfillment of prophecy (Hosea 6:2, Jonah 1:17/Matt. 12:40), and is explicitly stated in Scripture (1 Cor. 15:4). Its omission weakens the answer.
  - **"at the last day"** — The Shorter says "coming to judge the world at the last day." The BLC replaces this with "His imminent return to judge the world." The phrase "at the last day" is a biblical expression (John 6:39-40, 44, 54; 11:24; 12:48) that locates the judgment eschatologically. Its replacement with "imminent" changes the eschatological framing.
  - **"rising again from the dead"** — The Shorter's fuller phrase is reduced to the single word "resurrection."
  - **"ascending up into heaven"** — The Shorter specifies the destination; the BLC says only "His ascension."
  - **"at the right hand of God the Father"** — The Shorter identifies God specifically as "God the Father"; the BLC says only "the Father." This is a very minor difference.
- Structural changes: The four-fold structure is preserved (resurrection, ascension, session, return). However, the addition of "imminent" to the fourth element introduces a theological qualifier not present in the Shorter's simple "coming to judge the world at the last day."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8, Paragraph 4:
> "...on the third day he arose from the dead, with the same body in which he suffered; with which he also ascended into heaven: and there sitteth at the right hand of his Father, making intercession; and shall return to judge Men and Angels, at the end of the World."

Also Chapter 32, Paragraph 1:
> "God hath appointed a Day wherein he will judge the world in Righteousness, by Jesus Christ; to whom all power and judgement is given of the Father; in which Day not only the Apostate Angels shall be judged; but likewise all persons that have lived upon the Earth, shall appear before the Tribunal of Christ..."

- Omissions:
  - **"on the third day"** — Present in the 1689, absent from the BLC.
  - **"with the same body in which he suffered"** — This is detailed content appropriate for later expansion questions rather than this summary question, so its absence here is not problematic.
  - **"making intercession"** — The 1689 explicitly connects the session at God's right hand with Christ's ongoing intercessory work. The BLC omits this entirely. This is a significant omission: intercession is a central aspect of Christ's present exaltation and His priestly office (Rom. 8:34; Heb. 7:25). The WLC likewise includes intercession in its expanded treatment (Q54-55), but the 1689 includes it in this summary statement.
  - **"Men and Angels"** — The 1689 says Christ will "judge Men and Angels"; the BLC says only "judge the world." Chapter 32.1 further specifies "the Apostate Angels shall be judged." This narrows the scope of Christ's judicial authority.
  - **"at the end of the World"** — The 1689's eschatological temporal marker is replaced by the BLC's "imminent." The 1689's language locates judgment at the terminus of history; "imminent" introduces a different eschatological expectation.
- Structural changes: The 1689 presents the exaltation stages as a continuous historical-eschatological narrative culminating "at the end of the World." The BLC's insertion of "imminent" shifts the emphasis from the eschatological terminus to the nearness of the return, introducing a perspective not found in the Confession.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q51:
> Q. 51. What was the estate of Christ's exaltation?
> A. The estate of Christ's exaltation comprehendeth his resurrection, ascension, sitting at the right hand of the Father, and his coming again to judge the world.

- Solidarity: **MODERATE** — The BLC follows the WLC's basic structure closely but introduces "imminent" which the WLC does not contain.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected or present. This is pure Christological doctrine.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"imminent return"** vs. **"coming again"** — The WLC simply says "his coming again to judge the world." The BLC's "His imminent return to judge the world" adds a theological qualifier ("imminent") that goes beyond both the WLC and the Shorter. The imminence of Christ's return is a debated eschatological question across Reformed traditions. While all confessional traditions affirm Christ will return, the specific characterization of that return as "imminent" (which implies it could happen at any moment) is a position associated with certain eschatological frameworks and is not part of the confessional consensus. The WLC's subsequent expansion (Q56) describes Christ coming "at the last day" — not "imminently."
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"comprehendeth"** — The WLC's "comprehendeth" (encompassing) is changed to "consists of." This is a minor modernization, though "comprehendeth" may slightly better convey that these stages together constitute the exaltation.
  - **"coming again"** — The WLC's simple "coming again" is replaced with "imminent return," which is not a modernization but a theological alteration.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "imminent" addition is the most significant issue.** No standard — Shorter, 1689, or WLC — characterizes Christ's return as "imminent." The confessional tradition carefully describes Christ as "coming again to judge the world" (WLC), coming "at the last day" (Shorter), or returning "at the end of the World" (1689). The 1689 Chapter 32.3 does teach that the day is "unknown to Men" and that believers should "be always watchful," but this is watchfulness due to uncertainty about timing, not a claim of imminence. These are distinct theological concepts: (a) not knowing when Christ will return, and (b) His return being "imminent" (could happen at any moment). The confessional tradition affirms (a) but does not commit to (b). Inserting "imminent" into a catechism answer that is meant to mirror the standards introduces a contested eschatological position.

2. **The omission of "on the third day"** is notable because this phrase is confessionally, creedally, and scripturally established. The BLC's own proof text (1 Cor. 15:3-4) explicitly states "he rose again the third day." Since this is a summary question, brevity is understandable, but the third-day specification is preserved in both the Shorter and the WLC's expanded treatment (Q52), and is a core creedal affirmation.

3. **The omission of "making intercession"** from the 1689's summary is noteworthy. The 1689 explicitly ties the session to intercession in its compact treatment. This could be addressed in subsequent BLC questions that expand on the session, but its absence from the summary question represents a departure from the 1689's emphasis.

4. **The replacement of "at the last day" with "imminent"** is a double change: it both removes a biblical temporal marker and adds a non-confessional theological qualifier.

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 Corinthians 15:3-4** — For the resurrection. Appropriate; matches both the Shorter and WLC proof texts. However, this verse explicitly says "the third day" which the BLC answer omits from its text.
- **Psalm 68:18** — For the ascension. This matches WLC Q53 proof text 224. Appropriate.
- **Acts 1:11** — For the ascension and the return. Used by both the Shorter and WLC. Appropriate. However, Acts 1:11 says "this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner" — it does not characterize the return as "imminent."
- **Ephesians 4:8** — For the ascension. Matches WLC Q53 proof text (cf. Psalm 68:18). Appropriate.
- **Ephesians 1:20** — For the session. Matches the Shorter and WLC. Appropriate.
- **Psalm 110:1** — For the session. Standard proof text, matches WLC. Appropriate.
- **Acts 2:33-34** — For the session. Matches WLC proof texts. Appropriate.
- **Hebrews 1:3** — For the session. Good addition; explicitly mentions Christ sitting "on the right hand of the Majesty on high."
- **Acts 17:31** — For the judgment. Matches both the Shorter and WLC. Appropriate. This verse says God "hath appointed a day" — supporting "the last day" language, not "imminent."
- **Matthew 16:27** — For the return/judgment. Not used by the Shorter or WLC for this question. The verse says "the Son of man shall come in the glory of his Father with his angels; and then he shall reward every man according to his works." It does not support the word "imminent."
- None of the cited proof texts support the characterization of Christ's return as "imminent." The proof texts consistently point to a future coming on an appointed day, in keeping with the confessional language "at the last day" / "at the end of the World."
