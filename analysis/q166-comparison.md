# Q166: What are Christ's directions for giving and receiving the bread and wine in the sacrament of the Lord's Supper?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q166 is a close and faithful modernization of WLC Q169, preserving the essential structure and content of Christ's directions for administering the Lord's Supper. It aligns well with 1689 Confession 30.3. There is no direct Shorter Catechism parallel for this specific question. Two minor concerns arise: (1) the BLC replaces the WLC/1689's precise "word of institution" with the vaguer "biblical declaration," and (2) the BLC continues to use "sacrament" rather than "ordinance," departing from the 1689's consistent terminology (an issue already flagged in Q165). The phrase "they communicating also themselves" from 1689 30.3 (requiring that ministers also partake) is omitted in the BLC, following the WLC's lead.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism does not have a separate question about Christ's directions for the giving and receiving of bread and wine. The closest Shorter question is: "Q. What is the Lord's supper? A. The Lord's supper is an ordinance of the New Testament, instituted by Jesus Christ; wherein by giving and receiving bread and wine, according to his appointment, his death is shown forth, and the worthy receivers are, not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith, made partakers of his body and blood, with all his benefits, to their spiritual nourishment and growth in grace." This addresses the nature of the Supper generally rather than the specific liturgical directions.

- **Omissions:** Not strictly applicable since there is no parallel question. However, it is worth noting that the Shorter's phrase "according to his appointment" (used in its definition of the Supper) carries stronger covenantal weight than the BLC's "according to the same directions." The Shorter's language emphasizes divine appointment; the BLC's language suggests procedural instructions.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC rightly expands this topic into a dedicated question (following the WLC's structure), since the Shorter compresses all Lord's Supper content into fewer questions.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 30, Paragraph 3: "The Lord Jesus hath in this Ordinance, appointed his Ministers to Pray, and bless the Elements of Bread and Wine, and thereby to set them apart from a common to an holy use, and to take and break the Bread; to take the Cup, and (they communicating also themselves) to give both to the Communicants."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"they communicating also themselves"** — The 1689 explicitly requires that ministers who administer the elements must also partake themselves. The BLC omits this requirement entirely. This follows the WLC's lead (Q169 also omits it), but it is a real doctrinal element in the Confession. This is a minor omission since the point is arguably implied and may be addressed elsewhere, but the 1689 makes it explicit within the administration directions.
  - **"Pray, and bless the Elements"** — The 1689 specifies both prayer and blessing as the means of setting apart the elements. The BLC mentions "prayer" and "thanksgiving" but replaces "bless" with "biblical declaration," which captures the verbal aspect but loses the specific act of blessing.
  - **"from a common to an holy use"** — The 1689 describes setting the elements apart "from a common to an holy use," emphasizing the transition from common to holy. The BLC uses "from their ordinary use," which captures the separation from common use but omits the positive destination ("holy use"). This is a subtle loss of the consecration concept.
  - **"Ordinance"** — The 1689 uses "Ordinance" consistently throughout Chapter 30. The BLC uses "sacrament," following the WLC rather than the Baptist confessional standard.
- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC follows the same logical order as the 1689: ministers set apart elements → break bread → give to communicants → communicants eat and drink in remembrance.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 169. How hath Christ appointed bread and wine to be given and received in the sacrament of the Lord's supper? A. Christ hath appointed the ministers of his Word, in the administration of this sacrament of the Lord's supper, to set apart the bread and wine from common use, by the word of institution, thanksgiving, and prayer; to take and break the bread, and to give both the bread and the wine to the communicants: who are, by the same appointment, to take and eat the bread, and to drink the wine, in thankful remembrance that the body of Christ was broken and given, and his blood shed, for them."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC is clearly modeled directly on WLC Q169 and preserves its structure, sequence, and theological content with high fidelity.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None — this is an area of expected Baptist-Presbyterian agreement. The administration of the Lord's Supper is not a point of ecclesiological divergence.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - **"word of institution" → "biblical declaration"** — The WLC's "word of institution" is a precise theological term referring to Christ's words at the Last Supper (the verba institutionis). The BLC replaces this with "biblical declaration," which is vaguer and could refer to any Scripture reading. "Word of institution" is the standard Reformed term across confessional traditions and there is no Baptist reason to depart from it. This is a weakening of precision.
  - **"by the same appointment" → "according to the same directions"** — The WLC's "appointment" carries greater theological weight (divine appointment/ordination) than the BLC's "directions" (which suggests instructions rather than sovereign institution). This parallels the Q165 issue of "appointment" being weakened.
  - **"to take and eat the bread, and to drink the wine"** — The WLC explicitly says communicants are "to take and eat the bread, and to drink the wine." The BLC says communicants "are to eat the bread and drink the wine." The BLC omits the communicants' act of "taking," which the WLC includes to mirror the minister's action of taking and breaking. This is a very minor omission.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - **"word of institution"** — replaced with "biblical declaration," losing theological precision
  - **"common use"** — replaced with "ordinary use," a reasonable modernization with minimal theological impact
  - **"by the same appointment"** — weakened to "according to the same directions"
  - **"to take"** (on the communicants' side) — omitted

## Detailed Notes

### "Biblical declaration" vs. "word of institution"
The WLC's "word of institution" (and the broader Reformed tradition's verba institutionis) refers specifically to Christ's words of institution from the Last Supper narratives (1 Cor 11:23-25; Matt 26:26-28; Mark 14:22-24; Luke 22:19-20). This is a well-established technical term in Reformed sacramental theology describing the recitation of Christ's own words as part of the consecration. The BLC's "biblical declaration" is ambiguous — it could mean any Scripture reading or declaration, not specifically the words of institution. While "biblical declaration" is not wrong, it sacrifices the precision that both the WLC and broader Reformed tradition maintain. There is no Baptist reason to avoid "word of institution."

### Ministers' self-communion
The 1689 Confession (30.3) uniquely specifies that ministers, in giving the elements to communicants, are also "communicating also themselves" — i.e., the minister must also partake. Neither the WLC Q169 nor the BLC includes this detail. Since the BLC aims to exposit the 1689, the omission of a confessional requirement (even a minor procedural one) is worth noting. However, this could reasonably be addressed under a broader treatment of who should partake.

### "Ordinary use" vs. "common use"
The BLC's "ordinary use" for the WLC's "common use" is a defensible modernization. Both communicate the same idea — that the bread and wine are set apart from their everyday, non-sacramental purpose. However, the 1689's fuller phrase "from a common to an holy use" is more theologically rich, emphasizing not just separation from the profane but consecration to the sacred. The BLC captures the negative side (separation from ordinary) but not the positive side (dedication to holy use).

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 Corinthians 11:23-24** — The primary institution text. Covers the minister's actions (taking, giving thanks, breaking) and the command to eat in remembrance. Appropriate and matches WLC footnote 1083.
- **Matthew 26:26-28** — Synoptic institution narrative. Covers taking, blessing, breaking, giving, and the blood of the covenant. Appropriate.
- **Mark 14:22-24** — Parallel Synoptic account. Appropriate.
- **Luke 22:19-20** — Includes "this do in remembrance of me" and the new covenant language. Appropriate.
- The BLC's proof texts closely match the WLC's footnote 1083, which cites 1 Corinthians 11:23-24 and Matthew 26:26-28. The BLC adds Mark 14:22-24 and Luke 22:19-20, providing fuller Synoptic coverage, which is a minor improvement.
