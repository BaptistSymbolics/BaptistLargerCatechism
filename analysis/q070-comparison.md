# Q70: What is the difference between justification and sanctification?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q70 is a close modernization of WLC Q77 and is broadly consistent with the 1689 Confession's chapters on justification and sanctification. However, it omits the WLC's important clause that sanctification is "neither equal in all" believers, and it introduces the phrase "Spirit-empowered effort of the believer" where the WLC simply says sin is "subdued" — adding an anthropological emphasis not present in the standard. The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question on the distinction, but the BLC's treatment of the individual doctrines is consistent with the Shorter's definitions.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter treats justification and sanctification in separate questions rather than contrasting them:

- **Q. What is justification?** "Justification is an act of God's free grace, wherein he pardoneth all our sins (Rom. 3:24, 25; and 4:6, 7, 8), and accepteth us as righteous in his sight (2 Cor. 5:19, 21), only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us (Rom. 5:17-19), and received by faith alone (Gal. 2:16; Phil. 3:9)."
- **Q. What is sanctification?** "Sanctification is the work of God's free grace (2 Thess. 2:13), whereby we are renewed in the whole man after the image of God (Eph. 4:23, 24), and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness (Rom. 6:4,6; 8:1)."

- **Omissions from Shorter's justification definition:**
  - The Shorter says God "accepteth us as righteous in his sight" — the BLC mentions imputation of Christ's righteousness but omits the positive acceptance language.
  - The Shorter specifies justification is "only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us, and received by faith alone." The BLC omits faith as the instrument of reception entirely. While this question focuses on the *distinction* between the two doctrines rather than defining justification per se, the absence of faith is notable given that the BLC should presumably have covered this in its own "What is justification?" question.
  - The Shorter calls justification "an act of God's free grace" — the BLC does not use this language here.
- **Omissions from Shorter's sanctification definition:**
  - The Shorter says we are "renewed in the whole man after the image of God" — the BLC omits the scope ("whole man") and telos ("image of God") of sanctification.
  - The Shorter calls sanctification "the work of God's free grace" — the BLC omits this language. The Shorter's emphasis on free grace as the ground of sanctification is absent.
- **Structural changes:** None significant. Since there is no direct parallel question, BLC Q70 follows the WLC's structure of contrasting the two doctrines.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

**Chapter 11 (Of Justification), Paragraph 1:** "Those whom God Effectually calleth, he also freely justifieth, not by infusing Righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting, and accepting their Persons as Righteous; not for any thing wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ's sake alone, not by imputing faith it self, the act of beleiving, or any other evangelical obedience to them, as their Righteousness; but by imputing Christs active obedience unto the whole Law, and passive obedience in his death, for their whole and sole Righteousness, they receiving, and resting on him, and his Righteousness, by Faith; which faith they have not of themselves, it is the gift of God."

**Chapter 13 (Of Sanctification), Paragraph 1:** "They who are united to Christ, Effectually called, and regenerated, having a new heart, and a new Spirit created in them, through the vertue of Christ's death, and Resurrection; are also farther sanctified, really, and personally, through the same vertue, by his word and Spirit dwelling in them; the dominion of the whole body of sin is destroyed, and the several lusts thereof, are more and more weakned, and mortified; and they more and more quickened, and strengthned in all saving graces, to the practice of all true holyness, without which no man shall see the Lord."

**Chapter 13, Paragraph 2:** "This Sanctification is throughout, in the whole man, yet imperfect in this life; there abideth still some remnants of corruption in every part, whence ariseth a continual, and irreconcilable war; the Flesh lusting against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the Flesh."

**Chapter 13, Paragraph 3:** "In which war, although the remaining corruption for a time may much prevail; yet through the continual supply of strength from the sanctifying Spirit of Christ the regenerate part doth overcome; and so the Saints grow in Grace, perfecting holiness in the fear of God, pressing after an heavenly life, in Evangelical Obedience to all the commands which Christ as Head and King, in his Word hath prescribed to them."

- **Omissions from 1689 on justification:**
  - 1689 11.1 carefully distinguishes "not by infusing Righteousness into them" — the BLC captures this imputation/infusion contrast well.
  - 1689 11.1 specifies "Christs active obedience unto the whole Law, and passive obedience in his death" — the BLC speaks only of "the righteousness of Christ," which is less precise but not contradictory.
  - 1689 11.1 says justification is "not for any thing wrought in them, or done by them" — the BLC's "sin is pardoned apart from the work of the believer" captures this idea but with different phrasing.
  - 1689 11.1 includes faith as the instrument: "receiving, and resting on him, and his Righteousness, by Faith" — the BLC omits faith entirely in this question.
- **Omissions from 1689 on sanctification:**
  - 1689 13.1 names the means: "by his word and Spirit dwelling in them" — the BLC mentions only the Spirit, omitting the Word as means of sanctification.
  - 1689 13.1 describes sanctification as happening "through the vertue of Christ's death, and Resurrection" — the BLC omits the christological ground of sanctification.
  - 1689 13.2 says sanctification is "throughout, in the whole man" — the BLC omits this scope language.
  - 1689 13.2-3 describes the "continual, and irreconcilable war" between flesh and Spirit — the BLC omits the warfare motif.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC introduces "Spirit-empowered effort of the believer" as its description of how sin is subdued in sanctification. The 1689 13.3 speaks of "the continual supply of strength from the sanctifying Spirit of Christ" where "the regenerate part doth overcome." The BLC's language is not contradictory, but it foregrounds human effort more than the 1689 does. The Confession's framing emphasizes the Spirit's supply of strength and the regenerate nature overcoming; the BLC's framing emphasizes the believer's effort empowered by the Spirit. This is a subtle but real shift in emphasis.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

**WLC Q77. Wherein do justification and sanctification differ?** "Although sanctification be inseparably joined with justification, yet they differ, in that God in justification imputeth the righteousness of Christ; in sanctification of his Spirit infuseth grace, and enableth to the exercise thereof; in the former, sin is pardoned; in the other, it is subdued: the one doth equally free all believers from the revenging wrath of God, and that perfectly in this life, that they never fall into condemnation; the other is neither equal in all, nor in this life perfect in any, but growing up to perfection."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC closely follows the WLC's structure and language throughout.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent. This is a question of soteriology where Baptists and Presbyterians agree.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"neither equal in all" OMITTED:** The WLC states that sanctification is "neither equal in all, nor in this life perfect in any." The BLC drops "neither equal in all" entirely, saying only that sanctification "is not completed in any believer in this life." This omits an important doctrinal point — that believers exist at different levels of sanctification. The WLC's proof text for this (1 John 2:12-14, addressing "little children," "young men," and "fathers") is retained by the BLC but no longer supports the point about inequality, since the BLC uses it generically for imperfection. The omission removes a pastorally significant teaching about varying degrees of spiritual maturity.
  2. **"Spirit-empowered effort of the believer" ADDED:** Where the WLC simply says sin "is subdued," the BLC adds "by Spirit-empowered effort of the believer." While theologically defensible (and supported by the cited texts Rom. 6:6-14; Rom. 8:13; Phil. 2:12-13), this is an addition beyond the WLC. The WLC leaves the agent of subduing implicit; the BLC makes it explicitly the believer's effort (albeit Spirit-empowered). This is a clarifying expansion, not a contradiction.
  3. **"revenging wrath" simplified:** The WLC says justification frees believers from "the revenging wrath of God." The BLC simplifies to "the wrath of God," dropping the qualifier "revenging." This is a minor loss of precision — the WLC's language distinguishes God's punitive wrath (from which believers are freed) from his fatherly displeasure (which they may still experience, per 1689 11.5).
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "neither equal in all" — significant omission (see above)
  - "revenging" (qualifying "wrath of God") — minor precision loss

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "neither equal in all" omission** is the most significant issue. The WLC teaches two things about sanctification's imperfection: (a) it is unequal among believers, and (b) it is incomplete in any individual believer. The BLC captures only (b). This is a genuine doctrinal omission, not merely a stylistic simplification. The 1689 Confession implicitly supports inequality of sanctification through its language about believers being "more and more" sanctified and corruption "for a time" prevailing in some, but the point is clearer in the WLC. The BLC's proof texts (1 John 2:12-14; Heb. 5:12-14) actually support the inequality point, making the textual omission puzzling.

2. **"Spirit-empowered effort of the believer"** is a genuine clarifying expansion. The proof texts (especially Phil. 2:12-13 — "work out your own salvation...for it is God who works in you") support the cooperative language. However, the WLC and the 1689 both frame sanctification more passively from the human side — the WLC says sin "is subdued" (divine passive), and the 1689 says the Spirit's strength enables "the regenerate part" to overcome. The BLC's language, while orthodox, shifts the emphasis slightly toward human agency in sanctification.

3. **Omission of the Word as means of sanctification:** The 1689 13.1 says believers are sanctified "by his word and Spirit dwelling in them." The BLC mentions only "His Spirit" infusing grace. This omits the ordinary means (the Word) through which sanctification progresses — a point the 1689 specifically makes.

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 Corinthians 6:11; 1 Corinthians 1:30** — Appropriate for the inseparable connection between justification and sanctification. Standard proof texts shared with WLC.
- **Romans 4:6-8** — Supports imputation of righteousness. The WLC cites the same passage.
- **Ezekiel 36:27; Hebrews 9:13-14** — Ezekiel 36:27 is the WLC's proof text for the Spirit infusing grace and enabling its exercise. Hebrews 9:13-14 (Christ's blood purifying conscience) is a BLC addition not in the WLC — a reasonable supplementary text.
- **Romans 3:24-25** — Standard proof text for pardon of sin in justification, shared with WLC.
- **Romans 6:6-14; Romans 8:13; Philippians 2:12-13** — These expand beyond the WLC's Romans 6:6, 14 for the subduing of sin. Romans 8:13 and Philippians 2:12-13 are good additions that support the BLC's "Spirit-empowered effort" language.
- **Romans 8:33-34** — Standard proof text for freedom from condemnation, shared with WLC.
- **1 John 2:12-14; Hebrews 5:12-14; 1 John 1:8-10** — 1 John 2:12-14 is the WLC's proof text for the "neither equal in all" point, which the BLC omits from the text but retains as a proof text. Hebrews 5:12-14 (varying levels of maturity) is a BLC addition that also supports inequality of sanctification — again, the proof text supports a point the BLC text doesn't make. 1 John 1:8-10 is the WLC's proof text for imperfection.
- **2 Corinthians 7:1; Philippians 3:12-14** — Both support growing toward perfection. 2 Corinthians 7:1 is shared with the WLC. Philippians 3:12-14 is a helpful BLC addition.
