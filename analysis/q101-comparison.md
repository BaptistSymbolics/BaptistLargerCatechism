# Q101: What is forbidden in the second commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q101 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q109, preserving the structure and nearly all content of the Westminster original while updating language for clarity. However, the BLC drops the WLC's distinct phrase "tolerating a false religion" and substitutes "regulations" for "ordinances" in the final clause. The omission of "tolerating" removes a specific concept (passive tolerance as distinct from active approval), and the shift from "ordinances" to "regulations" loses the theological specificity of a term that denotes God's appointed means of worship.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
Q. "What is forbidden in the second commandment?"
A. "The second commandment forbiddeth the worshipping of God by images (Deut. 4:15-19; Ex. 32:5, 8), or any other way not appointed in his word (Deut. 7:31, 32)."

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's compact answer is fully preserved and greatly expanded in the BLC, as is appropriate for a larger catechism format. The two core prohibitions — worship by images and worship not appointed in God's word — are both present and elaborated.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC expands each element of the Shorter's answer into detailed subcategories without altering the theological relationships.
- The BLC faithfully unpacks the Shorter's compressed statement, giving detailed exposition of both image-worship and will-worship.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraph 1 — "But the acceptable way of Worshipping the true God, is instituted by himself; and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be Worshipped according to the imaginations, and devices of Men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representations, or any other way, not prescribed in the Holy Scriptures."

- Omissions: The 1689's reference to "the suggestions of Satan" as a source of false worship is not explicitly stated in the BLC. The BLC covers human invention ("our own invention") and received tradition but does not name satanic suggestion as a distinct source.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC faithfully reflects the regulative principle as articulated in the Confession — that worship must be instituted by God and not devised by human imagination.
- The BLC's language about worship "not established by God Himself" and prohibitions against human invention align well with the Confession's statement that acceptable worship is "instituted by himself" and "limited by his own revealed will."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q109: "What are the sins forbidden in the second commandment?"
"The sins forbidden in the second commandment are, all devising, counselling, commanding, using, and anywise approving, any religious worship not instituted by God himself; tolerating a false religion; the making any representation of God, of all or of any of the three persons, either inwardly in our mind, or outwardly in any kind of image or likeness of any creature whatsoever; all worshipping of it, or God in it or by it; the making of any representation of feigned deities, and all worship of them, or service belonging to them, all superstitious devices, corrupting the worship of God, adding to it, or taking from it, whether invented and taken up of ourselves, or received by tradition from others, though under the title of antiquity, custom, devotion, good intent, or any other pretence whatsoever; simony; sacrilege; all neglect, contempt, hindering, and opposing the worship and ordinances which God hath appointed."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC's structure almost exactly, proceeding through the same categories in the same order with modernized vocabulary.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None apparent. This question does not touch baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology.
- Unexpected divergences:
  1. **"tolerating a false religion" — dropped entirely.** The WLC lists this as a distinct sin between "approving" unauthorized worship and making representations of God. The BLC omits it without replacement. This is theologically significant: the WLC deliberately distinguishes passive tolerance of false religion from active approval. Baptists historically affirm religious liberty in the civil sphere (1689 Ch. 21.3 affirms the civil magistrate should not compel religion), so this omission may be intentional — reflecting a Baptist concern that "tolerating a false religion" could be read to endorse civil suppression of heterodox worship. If intentional, this is a principled Baptist adjustment, but it is not flagged or explained.
  2. **"ordinances" → "regulations"** in the final clause. The WLC concludes with "the worship and ordinances which God hath appointed." The BLC renders this "the worship and regulations established by God." "Ordinances" in Reformed theology specifically denotes God's appointed means of worship (Word, sacraments, prayer, etc.), carrying sacramental and liturgical weight. "Regulations" is more generic and could be read as mere rules rather than divinely instituted worship practices. This weakens the specificity of the original.
  3. **"corrupting the worship of God" → "any departure from the true worship of God."** The WLC's "corrupting" implies internal degradation of existing worship; the BLC's "departure" implies leaving it. The BLC phrasing is arguably broader, which is acceptable, but the nuance of corruption-from-within is lessened.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "tolerating a false religion" — entirely absent
  - "corrupting the worship of God" — replaced with "departure from," losing the specific nuance of internal corruption
  - "ordinances which God hath appointed" — changed to "regulations established by God"

## Detailed Notes
1. **Vocabulary modernization is generally well done.** "Devising" → "imagining," "counselling" → "recommending," "commanding" → "demanding," "using" → "practicing," "anywise approving" → "in any way approving," "feigned deities" → "invented gods," "pretence" → "excuse" — all are natural, accurate modernizations.
2. **The "tolerating" omission is the most significant finding.** The WLC places "tolerating a false religion" immediately after the prohibition of approving unauthorized worship, making it a distinct category. The BLC's omission could be read as a deliberate Baptist correction (Baptists have historically championed religious liberty and opposed state-enforced religious conformity), but this removes a concept that also operates at the ecclesiastical level — churches should not tolerate false religion within their fellowship. The Baptist concern about civil tolerance does not require dropping the concept entirely.
3. **The BLC's sectioning into separate proof-texted clauses** works well pedagogically, making the long WLC answer more accessible while preserving its substance.
4. **"Sacrilege" → "anything sacrilegious"** — a minor broadening that is acceptable.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts closely parallel those traditionally assigned to WLC Q109, with appropriate modernization of references.
- Numbers 15:39 for "imagining" is appropriate (the passage warns against following the imagination of one's own heart).
- Deuteronomy 13:6-8 for "recommending" is apt (enticement to serve other gods).
- Hosea 5:11 and Micah 6:16 for "demanding" work well (Ephraim walked after commandments of men; statutes of Omri).
- The proof texts for the image-making sections (Deut. 4:15-19; Acts 17:29; Rom. 1:21-25) match the WLC's traditional citations.
- Acts 17:22 for "superstitious contrivances" is interesting — Paul's address at Mars Hill regarding their superstitious devotion.
- 1 Peter 1:18 for "antiquity" as a false justification is effective (vain manner of life received by tradition from your fathers).
- The proof text selections are sound and well-matched throughout.
