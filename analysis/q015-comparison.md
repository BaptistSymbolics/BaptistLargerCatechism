# Q15: What has God particularly decreed concerning angels and men?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q15 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q13, preserving its tripartite structure (election of angels, election of men in Christ with means, reprobation of the rest). All key theological content from both the 1689 Confession and the WLC is retained. One minor lexical change ("counsel" → "purposes") slightly weakens precision but does not alter doctrine.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest is:
> Q. Did God leave all mankind to perish in the estate of sin and misery?
> A. God having out of his mere good pleasure, from all eternity, elected some to everlasting life (Eph. 1:4, 5), did enter into a covenant of grace, to deliver them out of the estate of sin and misery, and to bring them into an estate of salvation by a Redeemer (Rom. 3:20-22; Gal. 3:21, 22).

- Omissions: N/A — no direct parallel, so the BLC is not expected to mirror the Shorter here.
- Structural changes: N/A.
- The Shorter's Q7 (decrees generally) and Q13 (election in context of covenant of grace) address related territory but neither isolates the particular decree concerning angels and men. The BLC appropriately expands beyond the Shorter's scope here, following the WLC's more detailed treatment.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 3, Paragraphs 3–6.

> **3.3:** "By the decree of God for the manifestation of his glory some men and Angels, are predestinated, or fore-ordained to Eternal Life, through Jesus Christ to the praise of his glorious grace; others being left to act in their sin to their just condemnation, to the praise of his glorious justice."

> **3.5:** "Those of mankind that are predestinated to life, God before the foundation of the world was laid, according to his eternal and immutable purpose, and the secret Councel and good pleasure of his will, hath chosen in Christ unto everlasting glory, out of his meer free grace and love; without any other thing in the creature as a condition or cause moving him thereunto."

> **3.6:** "As God hath appointed the Elect unto glory, so he hath by the eternal and most free purpose of his will, fore-ordained all the means thereunto, wherefore they who are elected, being faln in Adam, are redeemed by Christ, are effectually called unto faith in Christ, by his spirit working in due season, are justifyed, adopted, sanctified, and kept by his power through faith unto salvation; neither are any other redeemed by Christ, or effectually called, justified, adopted, sanctified, and saved, but the Elect only."

- Omissions: The 1689's negative exclusion "without any other thing in the creature as a condition or cause moving him thereunto" (3.5) is not explicitly stated, though the BLC's "originates solely from His love" captures the positive side of this. The detailed enumeration of means in 3.6 (redeemed, effectually called, justified, adopted, sanctified, kept through faith) is compressed to "along with the means thereof," but this is appropriate for a catechism question on the decree itself — later questions would treat these applications.
- Structural changes: The 1689 3.3 says "others being left to act in their sin to their just condemnation," emphasizing the reprobate's active agency in sin. The BLC follows the WLC's formulation "passed by and foreordained the rest to be inflicted with dishonor and wrath for their sin," which uses the language of preterition ("passed by") rather than the 1689's "left to act in their sin." Both are orthodox; the WLC formulation actually adds "foreordained" alongside "passed by," making explicit what the 1689 leaves somewhat implicit (the positive act of foreordination to wrath alongside the negative act of passing by).
- The 1689's use of "through Jesus Christ" in 3.3 applies syntactically to both men and angels. The BLC correctly restricts the Christological mediation to men ("in Christ has chosen some men"), following the WLC's more precise formulation.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q13:
> Q. What hath God especially decreed concerning angels and men?
> A. God, by an eternal and immutable decree, out of his mere love, for the praise of his glorious grace, to be manifested in due time, hath elected some angels to glory; and in Christ hath chosen some men to eternal life, and the means thereof: and also, according to his sovereign power, and the unsearchable counsel of his own will, (whereby he extendeth or withholdeth favor as he pleaseth,) hath passed by and foreordained the rest to dishonor and wrath, to be for their sin inflicted, to the praise of the glory of his justice.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC is a very close modernization of WLC Q13. Structure, content, and proof texts are virtually identical.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "mere love" → "solely from His love": "Mere" in archaic usage means "pure/unmixed," carrying a slightly different nuance than "solely" (which emphasizes exclusivity of source). Both are acceptable modernizations; "solely" may even be clearer for modern readers.
  - "counsel of his own will" → "purposes of His will": "Counsel" (Greek: βουλή) implies deliberative wisdom and is a standard confessional term (used in 1689 3.5, WLC Q12, etc.). "Purposes" is a weaker synonym that loses the deliberative connotation. This is the most notable change but does not rise to a doctrinal concern.
  - "the praise of the glory of his justice" → "the praise of His glorious justice": The WLC's double genitive ("praise of the glory of his justice") is more emphatic, but the BLC's simplification preserves the essential content.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Tripartite structure preserved:** The BLC faithfully maintains the WLC's three-part structure: (a) election of angels to glory, (b) election of men in Christ to eternal life with its means, (c) reprobation — preterition and foreordination to wrath for sin, to the praise of divine justice.
2. **"Counsel" to "purposes":** This is the most notable lexical change. The 1689 uses "Councel" repeatedly in Chapter 3 (paragraphs 1, 5, 6), and the WLC uses "counsel" in both Q12 and Q13. The term carries theological weight from Acts 2:23 ("determinate counsel"), Ephesians 1:11 ("counsel of his will"), and Hebrews 6:17 ("immutability of his counsel"). "Purposes" is not wrong, but it flattens a term with rich confessional and biblical resonance. A modernization like "counsel" → "counsel" (retaining the word) or → "deliberate purpose" would have been more precise.
3. **Reprobation carefully stated:** The BLC preserves the important theological distinctions: (a) the act of "passing by" (preterition), (b) the positive "foreordaining" to wrath, (c) the grounding of infliction "for their sin" (infralapsarian note — wrath is for sin, not arbitrary), and (d) the doxological purpose "to the praise of His glorious justice."
4. **Election of angels distinguished from election of men:** The BLC correctly maintains that angelic election is to "glory" without Christological mediation, while human election is "in Christ" to "eternal life." This follows the WLC's careful distinction.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts match the WLC's exactly:
- **Section 1 (angelic election):** 1 Timothy 5:21 (elect angels); Matthew 16:27, 25:41 (angels); 2 Peter 2:4, Jude 6 (fallen angels — establishing the reality of non-elect angels). The BLC adds Matthew 16:27, 2 Peter 2:4, and Jude 6 beyond WLC's footnote 45, which cited only 1 Timothy 5:21. These additions are appropriate expansions.
- **Section 2 (human election):** Ephesians 1:4-6, 2:10; 2 Thessalonians 2:13-14; 1 Peter 1:2 — matching WLC footnote 46. Ephesians 2:10 is added, appropriately supporting the "means thereof."
- **Section 3 (reprobation):** Romans 9:17-22; Matthew 11:25-26; 2 Timothy 2:20; Jude 4; 1 Peter 2:8 — closely matching WLC footnote 47.
