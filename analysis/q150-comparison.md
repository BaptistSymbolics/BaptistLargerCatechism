# Q150: How do the sacraments become effectual means of salvation?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q150 faithfully preserves the core theology of the Shorter Catechism and 1689 Confession regarding sacramental efficacy: no power in the elements or administrator, efficacy solely from Christ's blessing and the Spirit's working, received by faith. However, the BLC adds the word "rightly" to the Shorter's "by faith receive them," introducing a qualification not present in the primary standard. While theologically defensible (and consistent with the broader catechetical tradition on worthy receiving), this addition subtly shifts the emphasis from faith as the sole instrument of reception to the manner or quality of that reception, which is a slight structural change.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** BSC Q91: "How do baptism and the Lords supper become effectual means of salvation? A. Baptism and the Lords supper become effectual means of salvation, not for any virtue in them, or in him that doth administer them, but only by the blessing of Christ, and the working of the Spirit in those that by faith receive them."

- **Omissions:** None. All key elements of the Shorter are preserved: no virtue in the elements, no virtue in the administrator, blessing of Christ, working of the Spirit, faith as instrument of reception.
- **Structural changes:** The order of "blessing of Christ" and "working of the Spirit" matches the Shorter. The BLC separates these into distinct sections (Christ's blessing in one clause, Spirit's working in another), which is an expansion but preserves the Shorter's theological structure.
- **Addition of "rightly":** The Shorter says "by faith receive them." The BLC says "by faith rightly receive them." This adds a qualifier not present in the Shorter. While the concept of right/worthy receiving appears elsewhere in the catechetical tradition (BSC Q97 on worthy receiving of the Lord's Supper), inserting it into the general formula for sacramental efficacy is an addition to the Shorter's baseline. The Shorter's formula is elegantly sufficient: faith is the instrument, and to receive "by faith" already implies right reception. Adding "rightly" could be read as introducing a condition beyond faith itself.
- **"Sacraments" vs. "Baptism and the Lords supper":** The Shorter names the two ordinances specifically; the BLC uses the general term "sacraments." This follows the WLC's language rather than the Shorter's specificity. This is acceptable as a Larger Catechism convention.
- **Modernization:** "Any inherent potency coming from the devoutness or intention of whoever administers them" is an expanded modernization of the Shorter's concise "in him that doth administer them." This is more elaborate than necessary but not theologically problematic.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**
- Chapter XXVIII.1: "Baptism and the Lords Supper are ordinances of positive, and soveraign institution; appointed by the Lord Jesus the only Law-giver, to be continued in his Church to the end of the world."
- Chapter XIV.1: "The Grace of Faith, whereby the Elect are enabled to beleive to the saving of their souls, is the work of the Spirit of Christ in their hearts; and is ordinarily wrought by the Ministry of the Word; by which also, and by the administration of Baptisme, and the Lords Supper, Prayer and other Means appointed of God, it is increased, and strengthned."

- **Omissions:** None significant. The 1689 does not have a direct parallel to this question (it does not contain a specific statement about how ordinances become effectual means), so the BLC is drawing primarily from the Shorter Catechism tradition. The 1689's teaching in XIV.1 that the ordinances increase and strengthen faith is compatible with the BLC's formulation.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC's affirmation that efficacy comes from Christ's blessing and the Spirit's working, not from the elements or administrator, is fully consistent with the 1689's framework of sovereign institution (XXVIII.1) and Spirit-wrought faith (XIV.1).
- **Agreement:** The BLC's emphasis that the sacraments have no inherent power aligns with the 1689's careful framing of ordinances as means appointed by God (not autonomous channels of grace). The 1689's insistence on "positive, and soveraign institution" by Christ maps to the BLC's "Christ who established them."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q161: "How do the sacraments become effectual means of salvation? A. The sacraments become effectual means of salvation, not by any power in themselves, or any virtue derived from the piety or intention of him by whom they are administered, but only by the working of the Holy Ghost, and the blessing of Christ, by whom they are instituted."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None in this question. The sacramental efficacy formula is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **Order reversal:** The WLC says "working of the Holy Ghost, and the blessing of Christ"; the BLC reverses this to "blessing of Christ... and the working of the Holy Spirit." This follows the Shorter Catechism's order rather than the WLC's, which is a defensible choice (prioritizing the primary Baptist standard), but it departs from WLC structural solidarity without theological necessity.
  2. **Addition of "in those who by faith rightly receive them":** The WLC does *not* include this qualifier in Q161. The WLC deliberately omits the subjective condition from its answer about sacramental efficacy, focusing exclusively on the objective grounds (Spirit's working and Christ's blessing). The BLC imports the Shorter's "by faith receive them" clause (good — this is the Baptist standard), but then adds "rightly" which is found in neither the Shorter nor the WLC at this point. The WLC addresses worthy/right reception separately in Q170-171 and Q177.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "by whom they are instituted" — replaced with "who established them." This is simple modernization, not a theological departure.
  - The WLC's "any virtue derived from" is expanded to "any inherent potency coming from." More elaborate but equivalent.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "rightly" addition is the primary concern.** The Shorter Catechism's formula "by faith receive them" is theologically precise: faith is both the necessary and sufficient instrument for effectual reception. Adding "rightly" introduces ambiguity — does it mean something beyond faith? The broader catechetical context (BSC Q97 on worthy receiving) suggests "rightly" refers to self-examination, discerning the Lord's body, etc. But inserting this qualification into the general efficacy formula risks conflating the instrument of reception (faith) with the conditions of worthy participation (self-examination, repentance, love, new obedience). The Shorter wisely reserves these conditions for the specific questions on the Lord's Supper.
2. **The BLC successfully synthesizes the Shorter and WLC traditions.** It takes the Shorter's structure (including the "by faith receive them" clause that the WLC omits) while adopting the WLC's expanded language about the administrator ("piety or intention" rather than just "virtue in him"). This synthesis is well-executed apart from the "rightly" addition.
3. **"Sacraments" terminology:** The BLC uses "sacraments" following the WLC rather than the Shorter's "baptism and the Lord's supper." This is standard Larger Catechism convention and is not a concern, especially since 1689 XXVIII treats them together as "ordinances of positive, and soveraign institution."

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 Corinthians 3:6-7** (no power in the administrator): Appropriate. Paul's point that the planter and waterer are nothing, but God gives the growth, well supports the denial of inherent virtue in the administrator.
- **Matthew 28:19; 1 Peter 3:21** (blessing of Christ who established them): Matt 28:19 supports Christ's institution. 1 Pet 3:21 ("baptism... now saves you") is appropriate for connecting Christ's work to sacramental efficacy, though it requires careful handling to avoid implying baptismal regeneration — the BLC's broader context guards against this.
- **1 Corinthians 12:3, 13; Titus 3:5** (working of the Holy Spirit): All appropriate for the Spirit's role in making the ordinances effectual.
- **1 Corinthians 11:28; Galatians 3:26-27; Colossians 2:11-12** (by faith rightly receive them): 1 Cor 11:28 ("let a person examine himself") notably supports "rightly" receiving, which may explain why the BLC added that word — the proof text pulled the language. Gal 3:26-27 and Col 2:11-12 support faith as the instrument of sacramental benefit. The proof texts are well-chosen but 1 Cor 11:28 specifically concerns the Lord's Supper, not sacraments generally — using it here to ground a general principle is a minor overextension.
