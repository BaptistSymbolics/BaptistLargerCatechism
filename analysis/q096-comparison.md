# Q96: What is required in the first commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q96 closely follows the WLC Q104 structure and is a faithful expansion of the Shorter Catechism's answer. It preserves all major theological content and the overall flow of duties. However, the WLC's "thinking" (cogitative duty toward God) is replaced with "valuing" (an affective/estimative duty), which shifts the concept from intellectual contemplation to assessment of worth. While "valuing" is a legitimate duty, its substitution for "thinking" drops the WLC's distinct emphasis on the mind's contemplative engagement with God as a separate duty from meditation.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** Q. "What is required in the first commandment?" A. "The first commandment requireth us to know and acknowledge God to be the only true God and our God, and to worship and glorify him accordingly."

The BLC faithfully preserves the Shorter's core structure:
- "know and recognize God as the only true God and our God" mirrors "know and acknowledge God to be the only true God and our God"
- "worship and glorify him as such" mirrors "worship and glorify him accordingly"

The BLC then expands the Shorter's concise answer into the fuller exposition drawn from the WLC, which is the expected pattern for a Larger Catechism.

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's complete content is preserved and expanded.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC maintains the Shorter's foundation and builds upon it.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 2, Paragraph 2: "to him is due from Angels and men, whatsoever worship, service, or obedience as Creatures they owe unto the Creator, and whatever he is further pleased to require of them."

Chapter 22, Paragraph 1: "The light of Nature shews that there is a God, who hath Lordship, and Soveraigntye over all; is just, good, and doth good unto all; and is therefore to be feared, loved, praised, called upon, trusted in, and served, with all the Heart, and all the Soul, and with all the Might."

The BLC aligns well with the 1689's teaching:
- The Confession's list "feared, loved, praised, called upon, trusted in, and served" is fully covered in the BLC's expanded list of duties (fearing, loving, calling on Him, trusting, giving praise, obeying).
- The Confession's "with all the Heart, and all the Soul, and with all the Might" corresponds to the BLC's "completely obeying and submitting to Him in our whole person."
- The Confession's emphasis on "whatsoever worship, service, or obedience" is reflected in the BLC's comprehensive catalogue of duties.

- Omissions: None of substance. The 1689 does not provide a detailed exposition of first commandment duties comparable to what a catechism offers, so the BLC appropriately goes beyond the Confession's general statements.
- Structural changes: None.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q104: "What are the duties required in the first commandment? A. The duties required in the first commandment are, the knowing and acknowledging of God to be the only true God, and our God; and to worship and glorify him accordingly, by thinking, meditating, remembering, highly esteeming, honouring, adoring, choosing, loving, desiring, fearing of him; believing him; trusting, hoping, delighting, rejoicing in him; being zealous for him; calling upon him, giving all praise and thanks, and yielding all obedience and submission to him with the whole man; being careful in all things to please him, and sorrowful when in any thing he is offended; and walking humbly with him."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question addresses moral duty toward God where no Baptist-Presbyterian distinctives apply.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - WLC "thinking" → BLC "valuing": The WLC's "thinking" (with proof text Mal. 3:16, referring to "them that thought upon his name") denotes the cogitative act of deliberately directing one's thoughts toward God. The BLC replaces this with "valuing," which is more of an estimative/affective disposition. While "valuing" is a legitimate duty toward God, it is not the same concept as "thinking." This substitution drops the WLC's distinct emphasis on intellectual contemplation as a separate duty alongside meditation.
  - WLC "choosing" → BLC "preferring": These are close synonyms; "preferring" captures the sense of deliberate selection. The BLC cites Joshua 24:15-22 which strongly supports "choosing." Both terms convey the idea adequately, though "choosing" is arguably more decisive and covenantal.
  - WLC "highly esteeming" → BLC "highly regarding": Acceptable modernization.
- Key phrases dropped: "thinking" as a distinct duty is lost (replaced rather than modernized). All other WLC phrases are preserved in recognizable modernized form.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Thinking" vs. "Valuing"**: The WLC's proof text for "thinking" is Malachi 3:16 ("Then they that feared the LORD spake often one to another: and the LORD hearkened, and heard it, and a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared the LORD, and that thought upon his name"). The BLC also cites Malachi 3:16 for "valuing," which suggests the authors interpreted "thought upon his name" as expressing value/esteem rather than cognitive engagement. This is a defensible reading but narrows the concept. The WLC likely intended "thinking" as the broadest cogitative duty — the deliberate turning of the mind to God — which then gets specified in "meditating" and "remembering." Replacing "thinking" with "valuing" collapses this into the affective/estimative cluster alongside "highly regarding" and creates some redundancy.

2. **"Choosing" vs. "Preferring"**: Both convey active selection. "Preferring" may slightly soften the covenantal decisiveness of "choosing" (cf. Joshua 24:15, "choose you this day"), but the theological substance is preserved.

3. **Structural fidelity**: The BLC preserves the WLC's overall progression remarkably well: (a) knowing God as the true God, (b) worshipping accordingly, (c) internal duties of mind and affection, (d) external duties of zeal and prayer, (e) whole-person obedience, (f) careful living, (g) sorrow for offense, (h) humble walking. This structural parallel is commendable.

4. **Modernization quality**: The BLC's modernization is generally strong. "Acknowledge" → "recognize," "accordingly" → "as such," "yielding all obedience and submission to him with the whole man" → "completely obeying and submitting to Him in our whole person" are all clear and faithful renderings.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts closely follow the WLC's citations with appropriate adjustments:
- Malachi 3:16 is retained but shifted from "thinking" to "valuing" — see note above.
- Psalm 63:6 is appropriately cited for "meditating on" (WLC uses the same).
- Psalm 71:19 is cited for "highly regarding" — the WLC cites Psalm 71:19 for "highly esteeming." Good parallel.
- Joshua 24:15-22 is cited for "preferring" — the WLC cites this passage range for "choosing." The passage strongly supports both concepts.
- Philippians 4:6 for "call on Him, giving Him all praise and thanks" is somewhat narrow; the WLC does not provide a single proof text here, and Phil 4:6 emphasizes prayer with thanksgiving more than "all praise." However, this is a minor proof text concern.
- The addition of Psalm 96 (cited without verse numbers) for "preferring" or "adoring" is appropriate — it is a psalm of worship that declares God's supremacy over all gods.
- Psalm 45:2-3 is cited for "adoring" — the WLC cites Psalm 45:11 for "adoring" (which speaks of the king desiring beauty and worship). The BLC's selection of vv. 2-3 shifts to the beauty of the king rather than the worship response, but both are within the psalm's scope.
- Nehemiah 13:8 for "being genuinely sorry when we offend Him" is an interesting addition — Nehemiah's response of displeasure at Tobiah's occupation of the temple chamber. This reflects zeal/grief at offense to God, which fits the concept.
