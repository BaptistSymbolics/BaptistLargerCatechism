# Q28: What is the misery of that estate whereinto man fell?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q28 closely follows the WLC Q27 structure and substantially preserves the Shorter Catechism's content, but it restructures the Shorter's compact final clause ("liable to all miseries in this life, to death itself, and to the pains of hell for ever") into a more generic phrase ("liable to all miseries and punishments in the world and that which is to come"). This loses the Shorter's explicit naming of "death itself" and "the pains of hell for ever" as distinct categories. The BLC also drops the Shorter's causal connective "and so" and the WLC's "justly," both of which serve theological functions.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is the misery of that estate whereinto man fell?
> A. All mankind by their fall lost communion with God, are under his wrath and curse, and so made liable to all miseries in this life, to death itself, and to the pains of hell for ever.

- **Omissions:**
  - "death itself" as an explicitly named consequence is absent. The Shorter singles out death as a distinct category of misery, following the threefold structure: miseries in this life / death itself / pains of hell. The BLC collapses this into "all miseries and punishments in the world and that which is to come," which is a twofold division (this world / world to come) that subsumes death without naming it.
  - "the pains of hell for ever" is replaced by the vaguer "that which is to come." While eschatological punishment is implied and supported by the proof texts (Matt 25:41-46, Jude 7), the Shorter's vivid and explicit language about hell and its eternal nature is lost.
  - The causal connective "and so" is dropped. In the Shorter, "and so made liable" connects the liability to miseries as a consequence flowing from being under God's wrath and curse. The BLC's "and made liable" weakens this logical relationship.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter's threefold misery taxonomy (miseries in this life / death / hell forever) is restructured into a twofold scheme (this world / world to come). This is not contradictory but loses specificity.
- **Additions:** The BLC adds two clauses not in the Shorter: "such that we are by nature children of wrath" and "and bond slaves of Satan." These are legitimate expansions drawn from the WLC and 1689, not novelties.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 6, Paragraph 3:
> "They being the root, and by Gods appointment, standing in the room, and stead of all mankind; the guilt of the Sin was imputed, and corrupted nature conveyed, to all their posterity descending from them by ordinary generation, being now conceived in Sin, and by nature children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal, unless the Lord Jesus set them free."

Also Chapter 6, Paragraph 2:
> "Our first Parents by this Sin, fell from their original righteousness and communion with God, and we in them, whereby death came upon all; all becoming dead in Sin, and wholly defiled, in all the faculties, and parts, of soul, and body."

- **Omissions:**
  - The 1689's threefold taxonomy of miseries as "spiritual, temporal and eternal" is not preserved. The BLC's "in the world and that which is to come" partially maps to temporal and eternal but drops the spiritual category entirely.
  - "The subjects of death" — the 1689 explicitly names subjection to death, which the BLC does not.
  - "The servants of Sin" — the 1689 lists servitude to sin alongside being children of wrath and bond slaves to Satan. The BLC omits bondage to sin as a distinct concept. The proof text at John 8:34 ("whoever commits sin is a slave of sin") is cited under the first section about losing communion with God, but the answer text itself never identifies bondage to sin.
  - "Unless the Lord Jesus set them free" — the 1689's hopeful qualifier pointing to redemption is absent, though this may be considered a matter for subsequent questions rather than an omission.
  - "Conceived in Sin" — the 1689's language about the manner of corruption's transmission is not included, though this belongs more properly to the sinfulness question (Q27) than the misery question.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC restructures "children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death" into "children of wrath... bond slaves of Satan." This replaces servitude to sin with servitude to Satan, which is a different (though related) concept.
- **Agreement:** Strong agreement on loss of communion with God, being under wrath and curse, being children of wrath by nature, and liability to punishments.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q27:
> Q. 27. What misery did the fall bring upon mankind?
> A. The fall brought upon mankind the loss of communion with God, his displeasure and curse; so as we are by nature children of wrath, bond slaves to Satan, and justly liable to all punishments in this world, and that which is to come.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC clearly uses the WLC Q27 as its structural template, preserving the same sequence of concepts: loss of communion, wrath/curse, children of wrath, bond slaves to Satan, liable to punishments.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None applicable to this question (no baptismal, ecclesiological, or covenant theology content).
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - "his displeasure" is dropped. The WLC distinguishes "displeasure and curse" while the BLC has only "wrath and curse." The 1689 similarly omits "displeasure," so this may follow the Baptist confessional tradition, though the distinction between displeasure (God's personal disfavor) and wrath (the judicial consequence) is theologically meaningful.
  - "justly" is dropped from "justly liable." The WLC's "justly" emphasizes that human liability to punishment is deserved, not arbitrary — a point of theological precision regarding God's justice. This is an unexplained omission.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "justly" — no theological reason to omit this important qualifier about divine justice.
  - "displeasure" — potentially following 1689 precedent, but still a loss of precision.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "children of wrath" / "bond slaves of Satan" expansion:** The BLC splits the WLC's compound clause into two separate sections with individual proof texts. This is a legitimate pedagogical expansion that gives each concept more emphasis.

2. **Proof text shifts:** The Shorter cites Eph 2:2-3 and Gal 3:10 for "wrath and curse." The BLC reassigns Eph 2:2-3 to the "children of wrath" section and replaces Gal 3:10 entirely with Gen 3:16-19, Eccl 2:22-23, and Rom 8:18-23 for "wrath and curse." The loss of Gal 3:10 ("Cursed is everyone who does not continue in all things written in the book of the law") is notable, as it grounds the curse in the broken law — a key Reformed connection.

3. **The "servants of sin" gap:** The 1689 lists three forms of bondage: children of wrath, servants of sin, subjects of death. The WLC lists: children of wrath, bond slaves to Satan. The BLC follows the WLC's pairing rather than the 1689's, but neither standard is contradicted — just the 1689's distinct emphasis on sin-as-master is not preserved.

4. **"Death itself" omission:** This is the most significant loss from the Shorter. The Shorter's progression from "miseries in this life" to "death itself" to "pains of hell" follows a climactic pattern that underscores death as a specific judicial consequence (cf. Gen 2:17, Rom 6:23). The BLC's proof texts include both Gen 2:17 and Rom 6:23, so the theology is implicitly present, but the catechetical answer — the part memorized and recited — does not name death explicitly.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Gen 3:8, 10, 24** (loss of communion): Appropriate — hiding from God, fear, expulsion from Eden.
- **John 8:34, 42, 44** (loss of communion): Partially misplaced. John 8:34 ("slave of sin") would better support a "servants of sin" clause. John 8:42, 44 (not being of God, being of the devil) relates more to spiritual paternity than communion.
- **Eph 2:12** (loss of communion): Appropriate — "without God in the world."
- **Gen 3:16-19; Eccl 2:22-23; Rom 8:18-23** (wrath and curse): These emphasize the temporal consequences of the curse (pain, toil, groaning creation) but do not ground the curse in the broken law. A verse like Gal 3:10 (used by the Shorter) would strengthen this section.
- **Eph 2:2-3; John 3:36; Rom 1:18; Eph 5:6** (children of wrath): Strong selection, especially Eph 2:3 ("by nature children of wrath").
- **2 Tim 2:26** (bond slaves of Satan): Appropriate — "the snare of the devil, having been taken captive by him."
- **Gen 2:17; Rom 6:23; Matt 25:41-46; Jude 7** (liable to miseries/punishments): Strong selection. Gen 2:17 and Rom 6:23 both explicitly name death as the penalty, compensating somewhat for the answer's failure to name death explicitly.
