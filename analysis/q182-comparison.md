# Q182: How should we pray?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q182 closely tracks WLC Q185 in structure, content, and proof texts, faithfully modernizing the list of dispositions and qualities required in prayer. However, the BLC renders the WLC's "enlarged hearts" as "confidence," which narrows the concept and loses the richer sense of expansive spiritual affection. The 1689's "humility" is present only implicitly (through "unworthiness"), and the Shorter Catechism's emphasis on praying "in the name of Christ" and "by the assistance of the Holy Spirit" is not repeated here—though these are addressed in BLC Q178 and Q179 respectively.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- Q98: "What is prayer? A. Prayer is an offering up our desires to God, by the assistance of the Holy Spirit, for things agreeable to his will, in the name of Christ, believing, with confession of our sins, and thankful acknowledgments of his mercies."

The Shorter Catechism's prayer question is a *definition* of prayer rather than a direct parallel to "how should we pray," but it establishes key elements that any full treatment of prayer must preserve:

- Omissions: The Shorter's "by the assistance of the Holy Spirit" and "in the name of Christ" are absent from Q182—but these are covered in BLC Q179 and Q178 respectively, so this is not a standalone omission from the catechism as a whole. The Shorter's "confession of our sins" is captured by the BLC's "deep sense of our... sins" and "hearts filled with repentance." The Shorter's "believing" maps to the BLC's "faith." The Shorter's "thankful acknowledgments of his mercies" maps to the BLC's "thanks."
- Structural changes: None significant. The BLC expands the Shorter's compact definition into a fuller enumeration of prayer qualities, which is appropriate for a larger catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22 (Of Religious Worship and the Sabbath Day), Paragraph 3:
> "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance; and when with others, in a known tongue."

- Omissions: (1) "in the Name of the Son" — addressed in BLC Q178, not here. (2) "by the help of the Spirit" — addressed in BLC Q179, not here. (3) "according to his Will" — partially captured by "humbly submit to His will" at the end, though the 1689 places it as a condition for prayer being *accepted*, not merely as an attitude of submission. (4) **"humility"** — the 1689 lists humility as a distinct quality; the BLC replaces this with "a deep sense of our unworthiness," which captures the concept but loses the standalone virtue. (5) "when with others, in a known tongue" — not addressed (the WLC also addresses this separately in Q185's proof texts, so this is less concerning).
- Structural changes: The 1689 structures its list as conditions for prayer being *accepted* ("that it may be accepted, it is to be made... with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance"). The BLC structures these as attitudes the pray-er *should have*. This is a subtle but acceptable reframing for catechetical instruction. The BLC adds "sincerity" which is not in the 1689 list but is in the WLC.
- The BLC's list is a superset of the 1689's qualities (adding sincerity, thanks, repentance, perseverance in waiting), which is appropriate for a larger catechism expanding on the confession.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q185: "How are we to pray? A. We are to pray with an awful apprehension of the majesty of God, and deep sense of our own unworthiness, necessities, and sins; with penitent, thankful, and enlarged hearts; with understanding, faith, sincerity, fervency, love, and perseverance, waiting upon him, with humble submission to his will."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement; prayer is not a topic where Baptist distinctives apply).
- Unexpected divergences:
  1. **"enlarged hearts" → "confidence"**: This is the most notable change. The WLC's "enlarged hearts" (proof text: 1 Samuel 1:15, 2:1; Psalm 81:10; Ephesians 3:20-21) conveys a broader concept of the heart being expanded in spiritual affection, desire, and capacity—not merely confidence. The BLC's "confidence" captures only one dimension of this concept. The proof texts (1 Samuel 1:15—Hannah pouring out her soul; 1 Samuel 2:1—Hannah's heart rejoicing; Psalm 81:10—"open thy mouth wide, and I will fill it") actually support the WLC's broader "enlarged hearts" more naturally than the BLC's narrower "confidence."
  2. **"necessities" → "inadequacies"**: The WLC says "necessities" (proof text: Luke 15:17-19, the prodigal son recognizing his need). The BLC renders this as "inadequacies," which shifts from a sense of *need* to a sense of *inability*. This is a minor nuance; "inadequacies" is defensible but less precise than "necessities."
- Key phrases dropped: "enlarged hearts" (replaced with the narrower "confidence"). No other significant WLC phrases are dropped.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Enlarged hearts" vs. "Confidence"**: This is the primary concern. The WLC's "enlarged hearts" is a rich, well-attested concept in Reformed piety denoting the expansion of the soul in its desires, affections, and expectations toward God. It includes confidence but also encompasses the breadth of spiritual longing, rejoicing, and holy ambition in prayer (cf. Psalm 119:32, "I will run the way of thy commandments, when thou shalt enlarge my heart"). Reducing this to "confidence" flattens the concept. The BLC's own proof texts (1 Samuel 1:15, 2:1; Psalm 81:10; Ephesians 3:20-21) actually better support "enlarged hearts" than mere "confidence."

2. **Structural fidelity**: The BLC follows the WLC's structure precisely: (a) majesty of God → (b) unworthiness/needs/sins → (c) penitent, thankful, enlarged hearts → (d) virtues list → (e) waiting → (f) submission. This strong structural alignment shows clear dependence on the WLC.

3. **Comprehensiveness**: The BLC successfully preserves all the WLC's list items with the exception of the "enlarged hearts" rendering issue. The modernization is generally well done.

4. **Separation into sections**: The BLC breaks up the WLC's more compact prose into individually proof-texted sections, which is pedagogically helpful and allows more specific scriptural support for each quality.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC generally uses the same proof texts as the WLC (Ecclesiastes 5:1 for majesty, Genesis 18:27/32:10 for unworthiness, Luke 15:17-19 for inadequacies/necessities, Luke 18:13-14 for sins, Psalm 51:17 for repentance, Philippians 4:6 for thanks, 1 Corinthians 14:15 for understanding, Mark 11:24/James 1:6 for faith, Psalm 145:18/17:1 for sincerity, James 5:16 for fervor, 1 Timothy 2:8 for love, Ephesians 6:18 for perseverance, Micah 7:7 for waiting, Matthew 26:39 for submission).
- The BLC adds Psalm 33:8 and Psalm 95:6 for God's majesty (appropriate additions).
- The BLC adds Psalm 144:3 for unworthiness and Psalm 86:1 for inadequacies (both appropriate).
- The BLC adds Zechariah 12:10-14 for repentance and 1 Thessalonians 5:18 for thanks (appropriate).
- The BLC adds Hebrews 10:22 for both faith and sincerity (appropriate; the WLC also references it).
- The BLC adds John 4:24 for sincerity (appropriate, emphasizing worship "in spirit and in truth").
- The BLC adds Matthew 5:23-24 for love (appropriate—reconciliation before worship).
- For "confidence," the BLC uses 1 Samuel 1:15, 1 Samuel 2:1, Psalm 81:10, and Ephesians 3:20-21. These are the WLC's proof texts for "enlarged hearts" (footnote 1189). As noted above, these texts more naturally support the broader "enlarged hearts" concept than the narrower "confidence."
