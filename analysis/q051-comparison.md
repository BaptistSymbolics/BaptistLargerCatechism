# Q51: How did Christ humble Himself after His death?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q51 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q50 on Christ's humiliation after death. The content aligns well with both the Shorter Catechism's compressed treatment and the 1689 Confession's statement on Christ remaining in the state of the dead. The BLC preserves all key elements: burial, the state of the dead, the power of death, the third-day terminus, and the creedal reference to the descent clause.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Wherein did Christ's humiliation consist?
> A. Christ's humiliation consisted in his being born, and that in a low condition, made under the law, undergoing the miseries of this life, the wrath of God, and the cursed death of the cross; in being buried, and continuing under the power of death for a time. (1 Cor. 15:3,4; Acts 2:24-27,31; Mt. 12:40)

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter's compressed phrase "continuing under the power of death for a time" is expanded in the BLC (following the WLC) to "continuing in the state of the dead and under the power of death until the third day." This is a faithful expansion, not an omission.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC isolates the post-death humiliation (burial and state of the dead) as a separate question, following the WLC's structure of breaking Christ's humiliation into stages. The Shorter treats all humiliation in a single answer; the BLC's division is a legitimate structural choice for a larger catechism.
- The Shorter does not include the Apostles' Creed descent clause reference. The BLC adds this following the WLC, which is a permissible expansion.
- The Shorter says "for a time"; the BLC specifies "until the third day" — a more precise rendering that matches the WLC and is theologically accurate.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8, Paragraph 4:
> "This office the Lord Jesus did most willingly undertake, which that he might discharge he was made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it, and underwent the punishment due to us, which we should have born and suffered, being made Sin and a Curse for us: enduring most grievous sorrows in his Soul; and most painful sufferings in his body; was crucified, and died, and remained in the state of the dead; yet saw no corruption: on the third day he arose from the dead, with the same body in which he suffered..."

- Omissions: The 1689's phrase "yet saw no corruption" is not included in BLC Q51. However, this is not a true omission from this question — it pertains more naturally to the resurrection and exaltation (the positive fact that corruption was prevented), and would be expected in the BLC's treatment of Christ's exaltation/resurrection rather than His humiliation after death. The WLC likewise does not include "saw no corruption" in Q50 but reserves it for Q52 on the resurrection.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's description of Christ remaining "in the state of the dead and under the power of death until the third day" faithfully mirrors the 1689's "remained in the state of the dead" with the third-day temporal marker drawn from the same paragraph.
- The 1689 does not include the Apostles' Creed descent reference. The BLC's inclusion follows the WLC tradition and is not in tension with the Confession, as it presents the creedal language as an alternative expression of the same reality (the state of the dead), not as teaching a literal descent into hell as a place of torment.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q50:
> Q. 50. Wherein consisted Christ's humiliation after his death?
> A. Christ's humiliation after his death consisted in his being buried, and continuing in the state of the dead, and under the power of death till the third day; which hath been otherwise expressed in these words, He descended into hell.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is pure Christological doctrine with no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology in view.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped: None. The BLC preserves every element of WLC Q50. The only differences are minor modernizations:
  - "Wherein consisted" → "How did Christ humble Himself" (question phrasing)
  - "consisted in" → "involved" (modernization)
  - "till" → "until" (modernization)
  - "hath been otherwise expressed in these words" → "has been otherwise expressed in these words in the Apostles' creed" (the BLC explicitly identifies the source as the Apostles' Creed, which is a helpful clarification over the WLC's unattributed reference)
  - "He descended into hell" → "He descended into hell" (identical)

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's explicit attribution of the "descended into hell" language to the Apostles' Creed is a modest but helpful improvement over the WLC, which leaves the source unnamed. This aids catechetical clarity without altering doctrine.
- The BLC's use of "involved" rather than "consisted in" is a natural modernization. "Consisted in" can be confusing to modern readers; "involved" conveys the same meaning.
- The question wording shifts from "Wherein consisted Christ's humiliation after his death?" to "How did Christ humble Himself after His death?" This is a consistent pattern in the BLC of using active voice and more accessible phrasing. The shift from passive ("humiliation") to active ("humble Himself") helpfully preserves the voluntary nature of Christ's humiliation (cf. Phil. 2:8).

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 Corinthians 15:3-4** — Used for burial. Matches both the Shorter Catechism and WLC proof text 200. Appropriate.
- **Acts 2:24-31** — Used for the state of the dead. Matches WLC proof text 201 (Acts 2:24-27, 31). The BLC slightly expands the range to include vv. 28-30, which contain Peter's quotation of Psalm 16, strengthening the connection. Appropriate.
- **Romans 6:9** — Used for the state of the dead. Matches WLC proof text 201. Appropriate.
- **Matthew 12:40** — Used for the state of the dead. Matches WLC proof text 201. Appropriate.
- The BLC omits **Psalm 16:10** which the WLC includes in proof text 201. This is a minor point since Acts 2:24-31 is Peter's extended quotation and exposition of Psalm 16:10, so the content is effectively covered. However, including the Psalm directly would strengthen the Old Testament witness.
- No proof texts are cited for the Apostles' Creed descent clause, which is appropriate since the creed itself is being referenced as a historical expression, not as a proof from Scripture.
