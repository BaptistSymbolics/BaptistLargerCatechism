# Q186: What do we pray for in the second petition?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q186 closely follows WLC Q191 in structure and content, achieving strong solidarity. However, it omits the Shorter Catechism's critical theological framework of the three kingdoms (Satan's kingdom, kingdom of grace, kingdom of glory), which organizes the entire petition's theology. The Shorter's phrase "the kingdom of grace may be advanced, ourselves and others brought into it and kept in it" is replaced with a scattered list of particular requests that loses the unifying theological concept. Additionally, the informal phrasing "hurry up and come again" trivializes the solemn eschatological petition found in both the Shorter ("kingdom of glory may be hastened") and the WLC ("hasten the time of his second coming").

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** BSC Q102 (second petition of the Lord's Prayer):
> "Q. What do we pray for in the second petition? A. In the second petition, which is, Thy kingdom come (Mt. 6:10), we pray that Satan's kingdom may be destroyed (Ps. 68:1, 18), and that the kingdom of grace may be advanced (Rev. 12:10, 11), ourselves and others brought into it and kept in it (2 Thes. 3:1; Rom. 10:1; Jn. 17:19, 20), and that the kingdom of glory may be hastened (Rev. 22:10)."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"The kingdom of grace may be advanced"** — This is the central organizing concept of the Shorter's answer. The Shorter structures the entire petition around three kingdoms: (1) Satan's kingdom destroyed, (2) kingdom of grace advanced, (3) kingdom of glory hastened. The BLC drops the explicit "kingdom of grace" language entirely, replacing it with particular applications (gospel preached, Jews converted, Gentiles brought in, church supplied, etc.) without naming the unifying theological concept.
  - **"Ourselves and others brought into it and kept in it"** — The Shorter's phrase about being "kept in" the kingdom of grace implies perseverance and preservation. The BLC addresses conversion and building up of the converted, but does not explicitly pray for believers to be "kept in" the kingdom — a subtle but real omission of the perseverance dimension.
  - **"The kingdom of glory may be hastened"** — The Shorter's solemn, reverent phrasing is replaced with "that he would hurry up and come again," which trivializes the eschatological petition (see Detailed Notes below).

- **Structural changes:** The Shorter's elegant three-kingdom structure (destruction of Satan's kingdom → advancement of the kingdom of grace → hastening of the kingdom of glory) is replaced by a longer enumerated list. While the BLC covers more specific content (mirroring the WLC), it loses the Shorter's theological architecture that the BLC should be expositing in expanded form.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

- **Chapter 24, Paragraph 3** (Civil Magistrate): "Civil Magistrates being set up by God, for the ends aforesaid; subjection in all lawful things commanded by them, ought to be yeilded by us, in the Lord; not only for wrath but for Conscience sake; and we ought to make supplications and prayers for Kings, and all that are in Authority, that under them we may live a quiet and peaceable life, in all godliness and honesty."
- **Chapter 26, Paragraph 3** (The Church): "The purest Churches under heaven are subject to mixture, and error; and som have so degenerated as to become no Churches of Christ, but Synagogues of Satan; nevertheless Christ always hath had, and ever shall have a Kingdome in this world, to the end thereof, of such as believe in him, and make profession of his Name."
- **Chapter 26, Paragraphs 8-9** (Church Officers): Officers appointed by Christ are "Bishops or Elders and Deacons," chosen by the church and set apart by prayer.
- **Chapter 32, Paragraph 3** (Last Judgment): Believers should "be always watchful, because they know not at what hour, the Lord will come; and may ever be prepared to say, Come Lord Jesus, Come quickly, Amen."

- **Omissions:** No significant omissions from the 1689 on matters covered. The BLC's mention of the church being "recognized and supported by the civil authorities" aligns with 1689 Chapter 24.3's call to pray for authorities. The 1689's emphasis on Christ's perpetual kingdom (26.3) is reflected in the BLC's content.

- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC's prayer content is compatible with the 1689's teaching on prayer, the church, civil magistrates, and eschatology.

- **Concern on tone:** The 1689 Chapter 32.3 uses the reverent language "Come Lord Jesus, Come quickly, Amen" (from Rev. 22:20). The BLC's "that he would hurry up and come again" does not match this solemn register.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q191:
> "In the second petition, (which is, Thy kingdom come,) acknowledging ourselves and all mankind to be by nature under the dominion of sin and Satan, we pray, that the kingdom of sin and Satan may be destroyed, the gospel propagated throughout the world, the Jews called, the fullness of the Gentiles brought in; the church furnished with all gospel-officers and ordinances, purged from corruption, countenanced and maintained by the civil magistrate: that the ordinances of Christ may be purely dispensed, and made effectual to the converting of those that are yet in their sins, and the confirming, comforting, and building up of those that are already converted: that Christ would rule in our hearts here, and hasten the time of his second coming, and our reigning with him forever: and that he would be pleased so to exercise the kingdom of his power in all the world, as may best conduce to these ends."

- **Solidarity: STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC's structure and content very closely, covering nearly every element in the same order.

- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent. This petition does not touch on baptism, ecclesiology in its distinctive Baptist sense, or covenant theology in ways that would require divergence.

- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - **"hurry up and come again"** vs. WLC's "hasten the time of his second coming" — The BLC's colloquial phrasing departs from the WLC's solemn tone without theological reason. "Hasten" is already accessible modern English; "hurry up" introduces a casualness that is alien to both the WLC and the biblical text (Rev. 22:20).
  - **"that he would hurry up and come again, when we shall reign with him forever"** vs. WLC's "hasten the time of his second coming, and our reigning with him forever" — The WLC presents the second coming and reigning as two coordinate prayers (hasten the coming AND our reigning). The BLC's "when we shall reign" makes the reigning a temporal clause subordinate to the coming, subtly shifting from a prayer for reigning to a statement of fact about what will happen when he comes. This is a minor structural change.
  - **"that he would be pleased so to rule over everything that goes on in the world as may best bring about all these results"** vs. WLC's "that he would be pleased so to exercise the kingdom of his power in all the world, as may best conduce to these ends" — The BLC drops the phrase **"the kingdom of his power"**, which is theologically significant. The WLC distinguishes three aspects of Christ's kingdom throughout the petition: the kingdom of sin/Satan (to be destroyed), the kingdom of grace (the church content), and the kingdom of power/providence (the concluding clause). By replacing "exercise the kingdom of his power" with "rule over everything that goes on in the world," the BLC obscures this important theological distinction.

- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - **"the kingdom of his power"** — This phrase reflects the Reformed scholastic distinction between Christ's *regnum potentiae* (kingdom of power over all creation), *regnum gratiae* (kingdom of grace in the church), and *regnum gloriae* (kingdom of glory in heaven). Its omission weakens the theological precision of the concluding petition.
  - **"purely dispensed"** — The WLC says ordinances should be "purely dispensed." The BLC says "administered fully," which shifts the emphasis from purity of administration to completeness. "Purely" carries the connotation of administering ordinances according to Christ's institution without corruption — a distinct concept from administering them "fully."

## Detailed Notes

1. **"Hurry up and come again"** — This is the most conspicuous issue. The phrase "hurry up" is colloquial English that connotes impatience or casual urgency. The biblical text (Rev. 22:20, "Come, Lord Jesus") and the confessional tradition ("Come Lord Jesus, Come quickly, Amen" in the 1689; "hasten the time of his second coming" in the WLC) express eager longing in reverent language. A modernization should preserve the register of the original. Something like "hasten his coming again" would modernize without trivializing. This is not merely a stylistic preference — catechisms form the devotional vocabulary of believers, and the language of prayer should teach reverence.

2. **Loss of the three-kingdom framework** — Both the Shorter Catechism and the WLC implicitly organize the second petition around three aspects of Christ's kingdom: (a) the kingdom of sin/Satan to be destroyed, (b) the kingdom of grace to be advanced, (c) the kingdom of glory to be hastened, with the kingdom of power governing all. The Shorter makes this explicit with its three-fold structure. The BLC, by following the WLC's expanded detail without preserving the Shorter's framing concepts or the WLC's "kingdom of his power" language, loses this theological architecture. Since the BLC is meant to exposit the Shorter, preserving the Shorter's three-kingdom framework in expanded form is essential.

3. **"Recognized and supported by the civil authorities"** vs. WLC's "countenanced and maintained by the civil magistrate" — This is an acceptable modernization. "Recognized and supported" conveys the same meaning as "countenanced and maintained."

4. **"Evangelical officers and regulations"** vs. WLC's "gospel-officers and ordinances" — "Evangelical" for "gospel" is acceptable, though slightly unusual. However, "regulations" for "ordinances" is a potentially misleading modernization. "Ordinances" in Reformed usage has a specific theological meaning (the means of grace Christ has appointed — preaching, sacraments, prayer, discipline). "Regulations" connotes rules or procedures, which is a narrower and more administrative concept. This same word appears in the next section where "regulations of Christ" replaces "ordinances of Christ."

5. **"The Jews be converted" vs. "the Jews called"** — The WLC uses "called" (reflecting effectual calling), while the BLC uses "converted." This is a minor shift but worth noting: "called" is more precisely Reformed terminology (effectual calling being the work of God), while "converted" can be read as emphasizing the human response side. The Shorter does not address Jews specifically.

## Scripture Analysis

- The BLC's proof texts closely mirror the WLC's footnote references, showing careful attention to the WLC's scriptural apparatus.
- **Matthew 6:10** — Correctly cited for the petition itself.
- **Ephesians 2:2-3** — Correctly cited for dominion of sin and Satan (matches WLC footnote 1223).
- **Psalm 68:1, 18; Revelation 12:9-11** — For destruction of Satan's kingdom. The WLC uses Ps. 68:1, 18 and Rev. 12:10-11. The BLC adds Rev. 12:9 (Satan cast down), which is a reasonable expansion.
- **Romans 10:1; Psalm 67:2** — For conversion of Jews. The WLC cites Rom. 10:1. The BLC adds Ps. 67:2, which is appropriate.
- **Romans 11:25-26** — For the fullness of the Gentiles. This is a more precise reference than any single WLC footnote for this point and is theologically apt.
- **Revelation 22:20** — Correctly cited for the coming of Christ, matching the WLC.
- **1 Timothy 2:1-2** — Correctly cited for prayer regarding civil authorities, matching both the WLC and the 1689 (Chapter 24.3).
- **Isaiah 64:1-2; Revelation 4:8-11; 2 Chronicles 20:6, 10-12** — For the concluding petition about Christ ruling over all things. These are reasonable proof texts though the WLC's footnote references differ somewhat.
