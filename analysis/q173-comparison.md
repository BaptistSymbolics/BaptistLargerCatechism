# Q173: In what ways do the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's Supper coincide?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q173 is a close modernization of WLC Q176, preserving the five-point structure of agreement between the two ordinances. However, the BLC retains the WLC's assertion that baptism and the Lord's Supper are "seals of the same covenant," a phrase which the 1689 Confession's own appendix explicitly rejects. The appendix states that "the seal of that Covenant is the indwelling of the Spirit of Christ in the particular and individual persons in whom he resides, and nothing else," directly contradicting the claim that baptism and the Lord's Supper function as covenant seals. Additionally, the BLC drops the WLC's exclusive qualifier "and by none other" regarding ministerial administration, weakening an important restriction.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not have a question specifically comparing baptism and the Lord's Supper or identifying their points of agreement. The closest questions address them together only in terms of efficacy:

- "Q. How do baptism and the Lords supper become effectual means of salvation? A. Baptism and the Lords supper become effectual means of salvation, not for any virtue in them, or in him that doth administer them, but only by the blessing of Christ (1 Pet. 3:21; Mt. 3:11; 1 Cor. 3:6, 7), and the working of the Spirit in those that by faith receive them (1 Cor. 12:3; Mt. 28:19)."

- "Q. What are the outward means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption? A. The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption are his ordinances, especially the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer; all which means are made effectual to the elect for salvation (Mt. 28:19, 20; Acts 2:42, 46, 47)."

- Omissions: N/A — no direct parallel to measure against.
- Structural changes: N/A.
- Notably, the Shorter Catechism never uses "seal" language for baptism or the Lord's Supper. It characterizes them as "ordinances" and "outward means" made effectual "by the blessing of Christ" and "the working of the Spirit in those that by faith receive them." The BLC introduces "seals of the same covenant" language that has no basis in the Shorter Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 28 (Of Baptism and the Lord's Supper):
"1. Baptism and the Lords Supper are ordinances of positive, and soveraign institution; appointed by the Lord Jesus the only Law-giver, to be continued in his Church to the end of the world."
"2. These holy appointments are to be administred by those only, who are qualified and thereunto called according to the commission of Christ."

Chapter 29.1 (Of Baptism):
"Baptism is an Ordinance of the New Testament, ordained by Jesus Christ, to be unto the party Baptized, a sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, and resurrection; of his being engrafted into him; of remission of sins; and of his giving up unto God through Jesus Christ to live and walk in newness of Life."

1689 Appendix (on the relationship between baptism and circumcision/sealing):
"If our brethren do suppose baptism to be the seal of the Covenant which God makes with every beleiver (of which the Scriptures are altogether silent) it is not our concern to contend with them herein; yet we conceive the seal of that Covenant is the indwelling of the Spirit of Christ in the particular and individual persons in whom he resides, and nothing else."

- Omissions:
  - **"Positive and sovereign institution"** — The 1689 calls these ordinances of "positive and sovereign institution," emphasizing that they derive their authority purely from Christ's explicit command (positive law) rather than from natural law or inherited covenantal logic. The BLC says only that "God is the author of both," which is accurate but loses the "positive and sovereign institution" language that guards against sacramentalism.
  - **"The only Law-giver"** — The 1689 identifies Christ as "the only Law-giver" who appointed these ordinances. This emphasizes the regulative principle applied to ordinances. The BLC omits this.

- Structural changes: None significant — the basic elements align.

- **Critical theological conflict — "Seals of the same covenant":** This is the central problem. The BLC's third section states "Both are seals of the same covenant," citing Romans 4:11, Colossians 2:11-12, and Matthew 26:27-28. However:
  1. The 1689 Confession **never** calls baptism or the Lord's Supper "seals of the covenant." Chapter 28 calls them "ordinances of positive, and soveraign institution." Chapter 29 calls baptism "a sign" — not a seal.
  2. The 1689 Appendix **explicitly rejects** the position that baptism is "the seal of the Covenant," stating the Scriptures are "altogether silent" on this point and that "the seal of that Covenant is the indwelling of the Spirit of Christ... and nothing else."
  3. Romans 4:11 (cited by the BLC as proof) is the very passage the 1689 Appendix discusses at length, arguing it refers to circumcision sealing a specific promise about Abraham being "the Father of many Nations" — not establishing a general principle that ordinances are covenant seals.
  4. This is not merely a semantic issue. In Reformed Baptist theology, the distinction between ordinances as "signs" and the Spirit as the "seal" is a load-bearing doctrinal point that differentiates the Baptist position from the paedobaptist framework. Calling baptism a "seal of the covenant" is precisely the reasoning paedobaptists use to argue for infant baptism (as circumcision was a covenant seal applied to infants).

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q176:

"Q. 176. Wherein do the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's supper agree? A. The sacraments of baptism and the Lord's supper agree, in that the author of both is God; the spiritual part of both is Christ and his benefits; both are seals of the same covenant, are to be dispensed by ministers of the gospel, and by none other; and to be continued in the church of Christ until his second coming."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC's five-point structure almost verbatim, with only minor modernization of phrasing.
- Intentional Baptist divergences:
  - **"Sacraments" → "sacraments"**: The BLC retains the term "sacraments" in the question, matching the WLC. This is notable since the 1689 and Baptist Shorter Catechism prefer "ordinances."
  - **"Coincide" for "agree"**: A minor vocabulary update.
  - **"Ordinarily to be administered" for "to be dispensed"**: The BLC adds the qualifier "ordinarily," which is a minor softening.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"Seals of the same covenant" retained without Baptist qualification:** This is the most significant issue. The WLC's "seals of the same covenant" reflects Westminster's covenant theology in which baptism replaces circumcision as a covenant seal. This is precisely the area where Baptists are expected to diverge from Presbyterians, yet the BLC copies this phrase without modification. A faithful Baptist adaptation should have replaced "seals" with "signs" or at minimum qualified the covenant-seal language in Baptist terms.
  - **"And by none other" dropped:** The WLC says the sacraments "are to be dispensed by ministers of the gospel, and by none other." The BLC says they "are ordinarily to be administered by ministers of the gospel." Dropping "and by none other" removes an important exclusive restriction. Adding "ordinarily" further weakens it by implying exceptions may exist.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"And by none other"** — An explicit exclusive qualifier on ministerial administration, dropped without theological reason.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "seals of the same covenant" problem is the most serious issue in this question.** This is not a case where the BLC merely retains WLC language in an area of Baptist-Presbyterian agreement. It retains WLC language in the precise area where the 1689 Confession explicitly marks out a distinctive Baptist position. The 1689 Appendix devotes extensive space (several paragraphs) to arguing against the seal-of-the-covenant framework for baptism, making this a core confessional distinctive — not a peripheral matter.

2. **The BLC's use of "sacraments" in the question is worth noting** but less serious. While the 1689 and Baptist Shorter prefer "ordinances," the term "sacrament" is not inherently objectionable in Baptist usage and was used by some early Particular Baptists. However, combined with "seals of the same covenant," the cumulative effect is a question that reads as essentially Presbyterian rather than distinctively Baptist.

3. **The qualifier "ordinarily" before ministerial administration** introduces ambiguity about whether non-ministers may administer baptism or the Lord's Supper in extraordinary circumstances. The 1689 28.2 states flatly that "These holy appointments are to be administred by those only, who are qualified and thereunto called according to the commission of Christ" — no "ordinarily" qualifier. The WLC is equally firm: "by ministers of the gospel, and by none other." The BLC is weaker than both its standards.

4. **The BLC should have adapted the WLC's "seals" language to reflect the Baptist position.** Options that would maintain WLC structural solidarity while being confessionally faithful include: "Both are ordinances of the same covenant" or "Both belong to the same covenant" or "Both are signs appointed under the same covenant." Any of these would preserve the point of agreement (both relate to one covenant) without importing the paedobaptist seal-framework that the 1689 explicitly rejects.

## Scripture Analysis
1. **Romans 4:11** — Cited for "seals of the same covenant." The 1689 Appendix engages this passage at length and explicitly argues it does not support the position that baptism is a covenant seal. Using this verse as proof for a claim the Confession itself rejects is particularly problematic.
2. **Colossians 2:11-12** — Cited for the seal claim. This passage discusses spiritual circumcision and baptism, but the 1689's treatment of baptism-circumcision parallels is careful to distinguish the Baptist reading from the paedobaptist one. The passage supports a connection between baptism and Christ's work, not necessarily the seal-framework.
3. **Matthew 26:27-28** — Cited for "seals of the same covenant." This is Jesus' institution of the Lord's Supper with "new covenant" language. It supports the covenantal context of the Lord's Supper but does not itself use "seal" terminology.
4. **Matthew 28:19; 1 Corinthians 11:23** — Appropriate for divine authorship.
5. **Romans 6:3-4; 1 Corinthians 10:16** — Appropriate for Christ and His benefits as the spiritual part.
6. **John 1:33; Hebrews 5:4** — Appropriate for ministerial administration, though these don't specifically support the "ordinarily" qualifier the BLC introduces.
7. **Matthew 28:19-20; 1 Corinthians 11:26** — Appropriate for continuation until Christ's coming.
