# Q61: Can those who have never heard the gospel and so neither know Jesus Christ nor believe in Him be saved by living according to their own natural understanding?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q61 closely follows WLC Q60 in structure and substantially preserves its content regarding the impossibility of salvation apart from knowledge of Christ and faith in Him. However, it drops the WLC's critical concluding clause — "who is the Savior only of his body the church" (Eph. 5:23) — which ties the exclusivity of salvation to Christ's particular redemption of the church. The BLC also omits the 1689's important "much less" (a fortiori) argument distinguishing between those externally called but not elect (who "cannot be saved") and those who have never received the Christian religion at all. The question also replaces "light of nature" with "natural understanding," a weakening of the technical Reformed phrase.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest Shorter Catechism material is the answer on how we may know there is a God:

> "The light of nature in man and the works of God plainly declare there is a God; but his word and Spirit only do it fully and effectually for the salvation of sinners." (Rom. 1:19,20; Ps. 19:1, 2, 3; Acts 17:24; 1 Cor. 2:10; 2 Tim. 3:15,16)

This establishes the insufficiency of the light of nature for salvation, which is the background assumption behind BLC Q61. The Shorter also teaches that the only Redeemer of God's elect is "the Lord Jesus Christ" (Q. "Who is the Redeemer of God's elect?").

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel to compare)
- Structural changes: N/A
- The BLC appropriately addresses a topic the Shorter does not cover directly, filling the gap that the Larger catechism tradition addresses.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 10, Paragraph 4:

> "Others not elected, although they may be called by the Ministry of the word, and may have some common operations of the Spirit, yet not being effectually drawn by the Father, they neither will, nor can truly come to Christ; and therefore cannot be saved: **much less can men that receive not the Christian Religion be saved; be they never so diligent to frame their lives according to the light of nature, and the Law of that Religion they do profess.**"

- Omissions:
  - **The "much less" (a fortiori) argument**: The 1689 structures its teaching as a two-tier argument: (1) even those who hear the gospel and experience common operations of the Spirit cannot be saved without effectual calling; (2) *much less* can those who never received the Christian religion be saved. This a fortiori structure is theologically significant because it shows that the impossibility of salvation for the unevangelized is not simply about lacking information — it is a *stronger* case of an already-established impossibility grounded in election, effectual calling, and total inability. The BLC addresses only the second tier (the unevangelized) without the foundational first tier, losing the deeper theological logic. (Note: the BLC already addressed the first tier in Q60, so this is somewhat mitigated by the catechetical structure, but the 1689 deliberately joins them for theological force.)
  - **"not being effectually drawn by the Father"**: The 1689 explicitly grounds the impossibility of salvation in the Father's sovereign non-drawing. The BLC's framing focuses on the human condition (not hearing, not knowing, not believing) without reference to divine sovereignty in the application of redemption.
  - **"they neither will, nor can truly come to Christ"**: The 1689's language of inability is absent from the BLC's formulation. The BLC states that those who "do not know Jesus Christ nor believe in Him cannot be saved" — which addresses the result without the underlying cause of total inability.
  - **"light of nature"**: The 1689 uses the Reformed technical term "light of nature" (Latin: *lumen naturae*), which has a precise meaning in Reformed theology referring to the innate knowledge of God and moral law available to all persons through general revelation (cf. Rom. 1:19-20, 2:14-15). The BLC replaces this with "natural understanding," which is weaker and less precise — "understanding" focuses on cognitive capacity rather than the theological concept of general revelation.
- Structural changes: The 1689 presents this teaching as a *conclusion* within its discussion of effectual calling — showing that the impossibility of salvation for the unevangelized flows from the doctrine of effectual calling. The BLC presents it as a standalone question about the unevangelized, disconnected from the broader effectual calling framework.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q60:

> "Q. 60. Can they who have never heard the gospel, and so know not Jesus Christ, nor believe in him, be saved by their living according to the light of nature?"
> "A. They who, having never heard the gospel, know not Jesus Christ, and believe not in him, cannot be saved, be they never so diligent to frame their lives according to the light of nature, or the laws of that religion which they profess; neither is there salvation in any other, but in Christ alone, who is the Savior only of his body the church."

- Solidarity: **MODERATE**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected on this question — this is not a baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology question per se.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"who is the Savior only of his body the church" (Eph. 5:23) — DROPPED**: This is the most significant divergence. The WLC's concluding clause ties the exclusivity of Christ's saving work to his role as Savior of his body, the church. This connects the solus Christus principle to particular redemption and ecclesiology — Christ does not merely make salvation possible but actually saves a definite people. The BLC replaces this with the simpler "Salvation comes from no other than Christ alone" (citing Acts 4:12), which affirms exclusivity but drops the particular redemption dimension. This is not an expected Baptist divergence — Particular Baptists affirm particular redemption as strongly as Presbyterians. The omission is unexplained.
  - **"light of nature" → "natural understanding"**: The WLC uses "light of nature," a standard Reformed technical term. The BLC changes this to "natural understanding" in the question and "their own natural understanding" in the answer section. While the meaning is close, the term change loses the connection to the broader Reformed discourse on natural theology and general revelation.
  - **"be they never so diligent to frame their lives"**: The WLC's "be they never so" construction (meaning "no matter how") is preserved in modernized form as "no matter how diligently they frame their lives." This is a faithful modernization.
  - **Question phrasing**: The WLC asks about "living according to the light of nature"; the BLC asks about "living according to their own natural understanding." The addition of "own" subtly shifts the emphasis toward autonomous human reason rather than the objective light of nature that God has given.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"who is the Savior only of his body the church" (Eph. 5:23)**: This is the key phrase dropped without theological reason. It is a critical affirmation connecting solus Christus to particular redemption and ecclesiology. Given that the 1689 Confession affirms particular redemption, this omission is not a Baptist adaptation — it is an unexplained loss.
  - **"neither is there salvation in any other, but in Christ alone"**: The BLC replaces this with "Salvation comes from no other than Christ alone," which is a reasonable paraphrase, so this is not truly dropped — just reformulated.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The missing "Savior of his body the church"**: This is the most consequential omission. WLC Q60's conclusion moves from the general principle (no salvation outside Christ) to the particular principle (Christ is Savior *of his body the church*). This connects three doctrines: (a) the exclusivity of Christ as Savior, (b) particular/definite redemption, and (c) the necessity of belonging to the church (the body of Christ). The BLC's "Salvation comes from no other than Christ alone" affirms (a) but loses (b) and (c). For a catechism intended to align with the 1689 Confession — which strongly affirms particular redemption — this is a significant omission.

2. **"Natural understanding" vs. "light of nature"**: The term "light of nature" appears throughout Reformed confessional literature (1689, 1.1; WCF 1.1; WLC Q2, Q60; etc.) with a specific theological meaning. It refers to what God has made known through creation and conscience (Rom. 1:19-20; 2:14-15), not merely to human cognitive ability. "Natural understanding" lacks this theological precision and could be read as referring merely to human reason or intellect. The Shorter Catechism's own answer on knowing God uses "light of nature" explicitly.

3. **Proof text comparison**: The BLC's proof texts closely mirror those of the WLC:
   - WLC note 253 (Rom. 10:14) = BLC section 1 (Rom. 10:14) — match
   - WLC note 254 (2 Thess. 1:8-9; Eph. 2:12; John 1:10-12) = BLC section 2 (2 Thess. 1:8-9; Eph. 2:12; John 1:10-12) — exact match
   - WLC note 255 (John 8:24; Mark 16:16) = BLC section 3 (John 8:24; Mark 16:16; Acts 4:12; Rom. 1:18-32) — BLC adds Acts 4:12 and Rom. 1:18-32, both appropriate additions
   - WLC note 256 (1 Cor. 1:20-24) = BLC section 4 (1 Cor. 1:20-24; Rom. 1:18-32; Rom. 3:9-19) — BLC adds Romans texts, appropriate
   - WLC note 257 (John 4:22; Rom. 9:31-32; Phil. 3:4-9) = BLC section 5 (John 4:22; Rom. 9:31-32; Phil. 3:4-10) — very close match (BLC extends Phil. reference by one verse)
   - WLC note 258 (Acts 4:12) = BLC section 6 (Acts 4:12) — match
   - **WLC note 259 (Eph. 5:23) — NO BLC EQUIVALENT**: This proof text, supporting the "Savior only of his body the church" clause, has no counterpart in the BLC because the clause itself was dropped.

4. **The BLC's simplification**: By rendering the answer as sectional assertions ("Those who have never heard the gospel / and do not know Jesus Christ / nor believe in Him cannot be saved / no matter how diligently..."), the BLC effectively turns the WLC's flowing theological argument into a series of discrete proof-texted claims. This loses some of the WLC's rhetorical and logical force, though this is partly an artifact of the catechism's sectional format.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Romans 10:14** (section 1): Appropriate. Establishes the necessity of hearing for believing — directly relevant to the question of the unevangelized.
- **2 Thessalonians 1:8-9; Ephesians 2:12; John 1:10-12** (section 2): Strong set. 2 Thess. 1:8-9 teaches judgment on those who "know not God." Eph. 2:12 describes the hopeless condition of those "without Christ." John 1:10-12 establishes the world's failure to know Christ and the necessity of receiving Him. All match WLC proof texts exactly.
- **John 8:24; Mark 16:16; Acts 4:12; Romans 1:18-32** (section 3): John 8:24 and Mark 16:16 directly teach the necessity of faith. Acts 4:12 affirms salvation in no other name. Rom. 1:18-32 establishes that all are without excuse — the light of nature condemns rather than saves. The addition of Acts 4:12 here (used by the WLC for the final clause) works but slightly dilutes its force when it appears again in the final section.
- **1 Corinthians 1:20-24; Romans 1:18-32; Romans 3:9-19** (section 4): 1 Cor. 1:20-24 demonstrates the inability of worldly wisdom to find God. Rom. 1:18-32 (repeated from section 3) shows suppression of natural knowledge. Rom. 3:9-19 establishes universal guilt. Good additions beyond the WLC.
- **John 4:22; Romans 9:31-32; Philippians 3:4-10** (section 5): These address the insufficiency of false religion — even the most sincere religious practice outside of Christ. Very close to WLC proof texts.
- **Acts 4:12** (section 6): The classic solus Christus text. Appropriate, though its appearance here after already being cited in section 3 is slightly redundant. The real loss is the absence of **Ephesians 5:23**, which the WLC cites to connect exclusivity with particular redemption.
