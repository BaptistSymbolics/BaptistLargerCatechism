# Q29: What are the punishments for sin in this world?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q29 is a close modernization of WLC Q28, faithfully preserving the internal/external structure, all six inward punishments, the three categories of outward punishment, and identical proof texts. The minor modernizations ("internal/external" for "inward/outward," "reputations" for "names," "employments" for "employments") are appropriate updates that preserve theological content. The question properly expands the Shorter Catechism's compressed treatment of temporal misery.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** "Q. What is the misery of that estate whereinto man fell? A. All mankind by their fall lost communion with God, are under his wrath and curse, and so made liable to all miseries in this life, to death itself, and to the pains of hell for ever (Lam. 3:39; Rom. 6:23; Mt. 25:41, 46)."

The Shorter Catechism compresses the punishments of sin in this world and the world to come into a single answer. The BLC, following the WLC's pattern, expands this into separate questions. BLC Q29 corresponds to the Shorter's phrase "all miseries in this life, to death itself."

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's compact "all miseries in this life, to death itself" is expanded and specified rather than omitted.
- Structural changes: The BLC follows the WLC in splitting the Shorter's single question into multiple questions (punishments in this world vs. world to come). This is an expansion, not a structural alteration of theological relationships.
- The Shorter's phrase "liable to all miseries" is given concrete content through the detailed internal/external enumeration — a proper function of a larger catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 6, Paragraph 3 — "They being the root, and by Gods appointment, standing in the room, and stead of all mankind; the guilt of the Sin was imputed, and corrupted nature conveyed, to all their posterity descending from them by ordinary generation, being now conceived in Sin, and by nature children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal, unless the Lord Jesus set them free."

- Omissions: None. The 1689's phrase "subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal" is the confession's summary treatment. The BLC's detailed enumeration of internal (spiritual) and external (temporal) punishments, culminating in death, faithfully unpacks this confession language.
- Structural changes: None. The 1689's distinction between "spiritual" and "temporal" miseries maps directly onto the BLC's "internal" and "external" categories.
- The 1689's qualifying phrase "unless the Lord Jesus set them free" is not within the scope of this particular BLC question, which addresses the punishments themselves rather than deliverance from them. This qualification appears elsewhere in the BLC's flow of questions.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q28: "What are the punishments of sin in this world? A. The punishments of sin in this world are either inward, as blindness of mind, a reprobate sense, strong delusions, hardness of heart, horror of conscience, and vile affections; or outward, as the curse of God upon the creatures of our sakes, and all other evils that befall us in our bodies, names, estates, relations, and employments; together with death itself."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this is not a topic where Baptist-Presbyterian differences arise.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped: None. The BLC preserves every element of the WLC answer.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's restructuring from "either inward...or outward" to "both internal and external" with labeled sections is a pedagogical improvement that does not alter theological content. The WLC's "either...or" is a structural marker, not a theological claim of mutual exclusivity.
- The question text changes "punishments of sin" to "punishments for sin" — a trivial preposition change that does not alter meaning.
- The substitution of "reputations" for "names" is a helpful modernization. In 17th-century usage, "names" meant one's public reputation; the BLC makes the intended meaning explicit for modern readers.
- "Whole creation" for "creatures" similarly clarifies the intended meaning (the creation cursed in Genesis 3:17), avoiding potential modern confusion of "creatures" as merely animals.
- All six inward punishments are preserved in identical order with identical proof texts.
- The proof texts match the WLC exactly: Eph. 4:18, Rom. 1:28, 2 Thess. 2:11, Rom. 2:5, Isa. 33:14/Gen. 4:13/Matt. 27:4, Rom. 1:26, Gen. 3:17, Deut. 28:16-68, Rom. 6:21/23.

## Scripture Analysis
- All proof texts are carried over from the WLC without alteration.
- **Ephesians 4:18** (blindness of mind): "Having the understanding darkened, being alienated from the life of God through the ignorance that is in them, because of the blindness of their heart." Appropriate.
- **Romans 1:28** (reprobate sense): "God gave them over to a reprobate mind." Appropriate.
- **2 Thessalonians 2:11** (strong delusions): "God shall send them strong delusion." Appropriate.
- **Romans 2:5** (hardness of heart): "After thy hardness and impenitent heart." Appropriate.
- **Isaiah 33:14; Genesis 4:13; Matthew 27:4** (horror of conscience): All three texts witness to the terror of guilty conscience — Cain's cry, Judas's confession, sinners in Zion afraid. Appropriate.
- **Romans 1:26** (vile affections): "God gave them up unto vile affections." Appropriate.
- **Genesis 3:17** (curse on creation): "Cursed is the ground for thy sake." Appropriate.
- **Deuteronomy 28:16-68** (all other evils): The covenant curses covering every area of life — body, reputation, possessions, relationships, work. Appropriate and comprehensive.
- **Romans 6:21, 23** (death): "The end of those things is death... the wages of sin is death." Appropriate.
