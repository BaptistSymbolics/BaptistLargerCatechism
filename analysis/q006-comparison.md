# Q6: May all men make use of the Holy Scriptures?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
The Larger Catechism's first section faithfully reproduces the Shorter Catechism's answer nearly verbatim. The Larger then expands significantly by adding detailed dispositions for reading Scripture (attentively, reverently, with delight, meekness, obedience, and faith). This expansion is theologically sound and well-supported, but it conflates two distinct theological questions — the *right* to use Scripture and the *manner* of using Scripture — into a single answer, drawing material that the Shorter Catechism addresses separately in its later question on how the word is to be read and heard.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

Direct parallel — "Q. May all men make use of the holy scriptures? A. All men are not only permitted, but commanded and exhorted to read, hear, and understand the holy scriptures (John 5:38; John 17:17,18; Rev. 1:3; Acts 8:30)."

Related question — "Q. How is the word to be read and heard, that it may become effectual to salvation? A. That the word may become effectual to salvation, we must attend thereunto with diligence (Pr. 8:34), preparation (1 Pet. 2:1, 2), and prayer (Ps. 119:18); receive it with faith and love (Heb. 4:2; 2 Thes. 2:10), lay it up in our hearts (Ps. 119:18), and practice it in our lives (Luke 8:15; James 1:25)."

**Key comparison findings:**

1. **Core answer preserved:** The Larger's first section — "All men are not only permitted, but commanded and exhorted to read, to hear, and to be careful to understand the Holy Scriptures" — closely matches the Shorter. The Larger adds "to be careful to" before "understand," which subtly strengthens the exhortation but does not change the meaning.

2. **Scope expansion:** The Larger adds six sub-points on the manner and disposition of reading (attentively, reverently, with delight, meekness, settled resolutions of obedience, and in faith). The Shorter Catechism addresses this topic separately in its question on how the word is to be read and heard. The Larger thus combines two Shorter Catechism questions into one answer.

3. **Proof text differences:**
   - The Shorter cites John 17:17,18 where the Larger has Revelation 17:18-19. The Shorter's reference (Jesus's High Priestly Prayer about sanctification through truth) is more directly relevant to the *use* of Scripture; the Larger's Revelation 17:18-19 reference appears to be either a textual error or a very unusual choice, as that passage concerns the woman/Babylon, not the use of Scripture.
   - The Larger adds numerous proof texts for the expanded dispositions (Luke 10:26; John 10:39; Deut. 17:19; Isaiah 66:2; Mark 12:37; James 1:21; Deut. 32:46; 2 Chron. 20:20), all of which are appropriate.

4. **Missing elements from Shorter's related question:** The Shorter's answer on reading/hearing includes "preparation," "prayer," receiving with "love," laying it up "in our hearts," and practicing "in our lives." The Larger does not include preparation, prayer, love, or the language of laying up and practicing. So while the Larger expands in some directions, it also omits key dispositions that the Shorter emphasizes.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 1, Paragraphs 7 and 8; also Paragraph 6

**Key comparison findings:**

1. **Right to Scripture (1.8):** The Confession explicitly states that God's people "have a right unto, and interest in the Scriptures, and are commanded in the fear of God to read and search them," and therefore Scriptures "are to be translated into the vulgar language of every Nation." The Larger Catechism's affirmation that "all men" are permitted, commanded, and exhorted to use Scripture is consistent with this — indeed, the Catechism may go slightly further by extending the permission to "all men" universally rather than focusing on "the people of God."

2. **Perspicuity of Scripture (1.7):** The Confession teaches that necessary truths are "so clearly propounded... that not only the learned, but the unlearned, in a due use of ordinary means, may attain to a sufficient understanding of them." This undergirds the Catechism's answer — if Scripture is perspicuous, all men may indeed make use of it. The Catechism is consistent here.

3. **Need for the Spirit (1.6):** The Confession acknowledges "the inward illumination of the Spirit of God, to be necessary for the saving understanding of such things as are revealed in the Word." The Larger Catechism does not mention the Spirit's illumination in this answer. While this is not a contradiction, the Confession's qualifier is significant: all men may *read* Scripture, but saving *understanding* requires the Spirit. The Catechism's silence on this point is a notable omission given the Confession's emphasis.

4. **Proof texts:** The Confession cites John 5:39 in paragraph 8, which aligns with the Larger's John 5:38. The overall proof text framework is compatible.

## Detailed Notes

1. **Possible proof text error:** The Larger cites "Revelation 17:18-19" in the first section. Revelation 17 has only 18 verses, so "17:18-19" is problematic. This may be a misprint for "John 17:18-19" (which the Shorter Catechism cites as John 17:17,18) or possibly Revelation 1:3 was intended to stand alone with a different second reference. This warrants editorial review.

2. **Structural choice:** By folding the dispositions for reading into the question about the *right* to Scripture, the Larger conflates access and application. In the Shorter Catechism, these are distinct questions, which is arguably a cleaner pedagogical division. This is a structural concern, not a theological one.

3. **"All men" language:** Both the Shorter and Larger use "all men" for who may use Scripture. The 1689 Confession speaks of "the people of God" having a right to Scripture (1.8) while also affirming its universal perspicuity for "the unlearned" (1.7). The Catechisms' universal language is consistent with and perhaps stronger than the Confession, which is appropriate for a catechetical context emphasizing the Reformation principle against restricting Scripture from laypeople.

4. **Dispositions are sound:** The six dispositions added by the Larger (attentiveness, reverence, delight, meekness, obedience, faith) are all theologically appropriate and well-attested by their proof texts. They reflect good Reformed pastoral theology.

## Scripture Analysis

- **John 5:38** — Common to both catechisms. Jesus telling the Jews to "search the Scriptures." Appropriate.
- **Revelation 17:18-19** — Likely an error in the Larger. Revelation 17 concerns the great city/Babylon. This does not support the doctrine stated. Probable misprint for John 17:18-19 or another reference.
- **Revelation 1:3** — "Blessed is he that readeth..." Excellent proof text for the permission and blessing of Scripture reading. Common to both.
- **Acts 8:30** — Philip and the Ethiopian eunuch. Common to both. Supports the idea that all should read and seek understanding.
- **Luke 10:26** — Jesus asking "What is written in the law? How readest thou?" Supports attentive reading.
- **John 10:39** — Likely intended as John 5:39 ("Search the scriptures") or possibly a reference to Jesus's audience searching. If John 10:39, it concerns the Jews trying to seize Jesus, which is not relevant. Possible error.
- **Deuteronomy 17:19** — The king shall read the law all his days. Good support for diligent reading.
- **Isaiah 66:2** — God looks to the one who trembles at His word. Excellent support for reverence.
- **Mark 12:37** — "The common people heard him gladly." Supports delight in hearing the word.
- **James 1:21** — "Receive with meekness the engrafted word." Directly supports meekness.
- **Deuteronomy 32:46** — Moses commanding Israel to observe all the words of the law. Supports obedience.
- **2 Chronicles 20:20** — "Believe in the LORD your God, so shall ye be established." Supports reading in faith.

**Notable concern:** Two proof texts appear potentially erroneous (Rev. 17:18-19 and possibly John 10:39). These should be verified against the original source text of the Larger Catechism.
