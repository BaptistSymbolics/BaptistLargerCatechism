# Q24: What is sin?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q24 is word-for-word identical to WLC Q24, which itself is an expansion of the Shorter Catechism's definition by adding the qualifying clause "any law of God, given as a rule to the reasonable creature." This expansion is theologically appropriate, specifying that God's law functions as a rule for rational creatures, and is fully consistent with the 1689 Confession's teaching on sin and the law. The proof texts are well chosen and more comprehensive than the Shorter's single citation.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
Q. What is sin?
A. Sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, the law of God (1 John 3:4).

- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves every element of the Shorter's answer.
- Structural changes: None. The theological structure is identical — sin is defined as both a deficiency (want of conformity) and an active violation (transgression) of divine law.
- The BLC changes "the law of God" to "any law of God" — this is a legitimate expansion (from the WLC tradition) that makes explicit what the Shorter implies: that sin pertains to any and every expression of God's law, not just the Decalogue narrowly considered.
- The BLC adds the clause "given as a rule to the reasonable creature," which is a theologically precise qualification identifying the subjects to whom God's law is given as a standard of conduct. This is not a departure from the Shorter but an appropriate elaboration for a Larger Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 6, "Of the fall of Man, of Sin, and of the Punishment thereof":
- 6.1: God "gave him a righteous law, which had been unto life had he kept it, and threatned death upon the breach thereof; yet he did not long abide in this honour... [Adam] did wilfully transgress the Law of their Creation."
- 6.4: "From this original corruption, whereby we are utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all good, and wholly inclined to all evil, do proceed all actual transgressions."
- 6.5: "The corruption of nature, during this Life, doth remain in those that are regenerated... yet both it self, and the first motions thereof, are truely and properly Sin."

Chapter 19, "Of the Law of God":
- 19.1: "God gave to Adam a Law of universal obedience, written in his Heart, and a particular precept of not eating the Fruit of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; by which he bound him, and all his posterity to personal entire exact and perpetual obedience."
- 19.5: "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof."

- Omissions: None. The 1689 does not provide a concise formal definition of sin, so the BLC is not omitting anything; rather, it is providing a definition that is fully consistent with the Confession's teaching.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's definition captures both the Confession's emphasis on transgression of God's law (Ch. 6.1) and the ongoing corruption/want of conformity (Ch. 6.4-5). The phrase "given as a rule to the reasonable creature" aligns with the Confession's teaching that God gave Adam "a Law of universal obedience" and that "reasonable Creatures do owe obedience unto him as their Creator" (Ch. 7.1).

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
WLC Q24: "What is sin?"
A. "Sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, any law of God, given as a rule to the reasonable creature."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC is word-for-word identical to the WLC.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this doctrine has no baptistic implications).
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None — complete textual identity.

## Detailed Notes
This is one of the most straightforward comparisons in the catechism. The definition of sin is a point of complete Reformed consensus across Baptist and Presbyterian traditions. The BLC wisely reproduces the WLC verbatim, as there is no theological reason for Baptist divergence on this fundamental doctrine.

The expansion from the Shorter ("the law of God") to the Larger ("any law of God, given as a rule to the reasonable creature") is a classic example of appropriate catechetical elaboration:
1. "Any law" — makes explicit the universal scope, encompassing the moral law in all its administrations (natural, written, evangelical).
2. "Given as a rule to the reasonable creature" — identifies the subjects of God's law as rational beings (humans and angels), distinguishing moral obligation from the governance of irrational creation by natural providence.

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 John 3:4** — The foundational proof text shared with the Shorter. "Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law." Directly supports the "transgression" element.
- **Leviticus 5:17** — "And if a soul sin, and commit any of these things which are forbidden to be done by the commandments of the LORD; though he wist it not, yet is he guilty." Supports the "want of conformity" element — sin includes even unwitting failures.
- **James 4:17** — "Therefore to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin." Directly supports sins of omission ("want of conformity").
- **Galatians 3:10-12** — Establishes the comprehensive scope of God's law and the curse attending any failure to keep it perfectly. Supports "any law of God" and the absolute standard.

The BLC's proof texts are stronger than the Shorter's single citation, appropriately representing both elements of the definition (omission and commission) with scriptural support. Leviticus 5:17 and James 4:17 are excellent additions that were not in the Shorter but correspond to the WLC's proof text tradition.
