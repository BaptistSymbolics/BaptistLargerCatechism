# Q5: What is the Word of God?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
The Larger Catechism's answer to Q5 is virtually identical to the Shorter Catechism's Q4, with one beneficial addition: the participial clause "being given by divine inspiration." This addition is firmly grounded in the 1689 Confession's teaching (Chapter 1, Paragraphs 1-2, 4) and uses expanded Scripture proof texts to support the same doctrine. No drift from either standard is present.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is the word of God?
> A. The holy scriptures of the Old and New Testament are the word of God, and the only certain rule of faith and obedience (2 Tim. 3:16; Eph. 2:20).

The Larger Catechism answer is nearly word-for-word identical to the Shorter, with one significant expansion: the Larger inserts "being given by divine inspiration" between the identification of the Scriptures and their designation as the Word of God. This is a welcome clarification that makes explicit the ground or reason why the Scriptures are the Word of God—namely, their divine inspiration. The Shorter Catechism leaves the connection between "holy scriptures" and "word of God" as a bare identification; the Larger makes the causal basis explicit.

The core formula "the only certain rule of faith and obedience" is retained verbatim.

**Proof text differences:**
- Shorter cites: 2 Tim. 3:16; Eph. 2:20
- Larger cites: 2 Tim. 3:15-17; 2 Pet. 1:16-21; 2 Pet. 3:2, 15-16 (for divine inspiration); Deut. 4:1-2; Rev. 22:18-19; Isa. 8:19-20; Luke 16:19-31 (for sole rule)

The Larger's proof texts are more extensive and well-chosen. The addition of 2 Peter 1:16-21 is particularly apt for the "divine inspiration" clause. The Larger also adds texts that emphasize the sufficiency and finality of Scripture (Deut. 4:1-2 on adding nothing; Rev. 22:18-19 on adding/subtracting; Luke 16:19-31 on the sufficiency of Moses and the Prophets). The Shorter's Eph. 2:20 (the apostles and prophets as foundation) is not carried over, but this is not a loss since the Larger addresses the same truth with different, equally valid texts.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 1, Paragraphs 1, 2, and 4

**Paragraph 1** teaches that "The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving Knowledge, Faith and Obedience," and that God committed His revelation "wholly unto writing." The Catechism's phrase "the only certain rule of faith and obedience" is drawn directly from this paragraph, using slightly condensed language ("certain" is retained; "sufficient" and "infallible" are not explicitly stated but are implied by "only certain rule").

**Paragraph 2** identifies the books of the Old and New Testaments by name and affirms that "All which are given by the inspiration of God, to be the rule of Faith and Life." The Larger Catechism's inserted clause "being given by divine inspiration" corresponds directly to this confession language. The Confession's phrase "rule of Faith and Life" is paralleled by the Catechism's "rule of faith and obedience"—the substitution of "obedience" for "life" follows the Shorter Catechism's established wording and is theologically equivalent.

**Paragraph 4** states that the authority of Scripture "dependeth not upon the testimony of any man, or Church; but wholly upon God (who is truth it self) the Author thereof; therefore it is to be received, because it is the Word of God." The Catechism's bare identification of the Scriptures as "the Word of God" is consistent with this teaching, though the Catechism does not elaborate on the ground of Scripture's authority as the Confession does—this is appropriate given the catechetical format.

The Catechism faithfully summarizes the Confession's doctrine in compressed form without introducing anything novel or omitting anything essential for a catechetical answer at this point in the sequence.

## Detailed Notes
- The addition of "being given by divine inspiration" is the only material difference from the Shorter Catechism and is a commendable expansion. It makes explicit what the Shorter presupposes and what the Confession teaches at length.
- The Larger Catechism wisely breaks its answer into two sections with separate proof texts: one cluster supporting the divine inspiration of Scripture, and another supporting its status as the sole rule. This is a pedagogically sound approach.
- The phrase "only certain rule" retains the important qualifier "certain," distinguishing the Scriptures from other potential sources of knowledge (nature, tradition) that may contain truth but lack certainty as a rule.
- No theological novelty is introduced. No essential element is omitted.

## Scripture Analysis
The Larger Catechism's proof texts represent a significant improvement over the Shorter's two citations:

**For divine inspiration:** 2 Timothy 3:15-17 is the locus classicus (expanding the Shorter's 3:16 to include the surrounding context). 2 Peter 1:16-21 is the other primary inspiration text, speaking of men "moved by the Holy Spirit." 2 Peter 3:2, 15-16 is well chosen as it treats Paul's epistles as Scripture alongside the Old Testament prophets, supporting the "Old and New Testament" scope.

**For sole rule:** Deuteronomy 4:1-2 ("you shall not add to the word...nor take from it") and Revelation 22:18-19 (the parallel warning at Scripture's close) together bracket the canon with warnings against addition or subtraction. Isaiah 8:19-20 ("To the law and to the testimony!") directs inquirers to Scripture rather than other sources. Luke 16:19-31 ("They have Moses and the Prophets; let them hear them") powerfully teaches the sufficiency of Scripture even against the desire for extraordinary revelation.

All proof texts are appropriate and well-deployed. None are strained or misused.
