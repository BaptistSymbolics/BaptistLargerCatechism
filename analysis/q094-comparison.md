# Q94: What are the guidelines for the proper understanding of the Ten Commandments?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q94 is a close modernization of WLC Q99 that faithfully preserves all eight rules for interpreting the Ten Commandments. The core theology is intact and consistent with the 1689 Confession's teaching on the moral law. However, several precise WLC phrases are weakened in translation: "full conformity in the whole man" becomes a less precise formulation, "gestures" is replaced with "self-expressions," and the crucial phrase "provocations thereunto" is dropped from rule 6. The BLC also splits several rules across multiple sections in ways that slightly fragment the logical flow.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not contain a question on rules for understanding the Ten Commandments. The Shorter moves directly from the summary of the law to the individual commandments. This question belongs to the expanded catechetical treatment appropriate for a Larger Catechism.

- Omissions: N/A (no parallel exists)
- Structural changes: N/A

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God)

Key relevant text from 19.1: "God gave to Adam a Law of universal obedience, written in his Heart, and a particular precept of not eating the Fruit of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; by which he bound him, and all his posterity to personal entire exact and perpetual obedience; promised life upon the fulfilling, and threatned death upon the breach of it."

Key relevant text from 19.2: "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables."

Key relevant text from 19.5: "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation."

Key relevant text from 19.6: "Although true Believers be not under the Law, as a Covenant of Works, to be thereby Justified or condemned; yet it is of great use to them as well as to others: in that, as a Rule of Life, informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty, it directs and binds them, to walk accordingly; discovering also the sinfull pollutions of their Natures, Hearts and Lives..."

- Omissions: None significant. The BLC's eight rules are consistent with and exposit the Confession's teaching on the perpetual binding nature of the moral law (19.1, 19.5), its perfection (19.2), and its spiritual reach (implied in 19.6's discussion of discovering sinful pollutions of "Natures, Hearts and Lives").
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's catechetical format appropriately expands on principles contained more compactly in the Confession.
- The Confession's emphasis on the law binding "personal entire exact and perpetual obedience" (19.1) aligns well with BLC rule 1's teaching on complete and perfect obedience.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q99: "What rules are to be observed for the right understanding of the ten commandments? A. For the right understanding of the ten commandments, these rules are to be observed: 1. That the law is perfect, and bindeth everyone to full conformity in the whole man unto the righteousness thereof, and unto entire obedience forever; so as to require the utmost perfection of every duty, and to forbid the least degree of every sin. 2. That it is spiritual, and so reacheth the understanding, will, affections, and all other powers of the soul; as well as words, works, and gestures. 3. That one and the same thing, in divers respects, is required or forbidden in several commandments. 4. That as, where a duty is commanded, the contrary sin is forbidden; and, where a sin is forbidden, the contrary duty is commanded: so, where a promise is annexed, the contrary threatening is included; and, where a threatening is annexed, the contrary promise is included. 5. That what God forbids, is at no time to be done; what he commands, is always our duty; and yet every particular duty is not to be done at all times. 6. That under one sin or duty, all of the same kind are forbidden or commanded; together with all the causes, means, occasions, and appearances thereof, and provocations thereunto. 7. That what is forbidden or commanded to ourselves, we are bound, according to our places to endeavour that it may be avoided or performed by others, according to the duty of their places. 8. That in what is commanded to others, we are bound, according to our places and callings, to be helpful to them; and to take heed of partaking with others in what is forbidden them."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — All eight rules are preserved in the same order with substantially the same content.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None detected (this is a topic with no Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement).
- Unexpected divergences:
  1. **Rule 1 restructured**: WLC's precise phrase "bindeth everyone to full conformity in the whole man unto the righteousness thereof, and unto entire obedience forever; so as to require the utmost perfection of every duty, and to forbid the least degree of every sin" is rendered as "binds the whole person to observe it completely, and according to its standard, to be completely righteous, and perfectly obey every one of its obligations forever. Moreover, the law forbids even the slightest or partial commission of any sin." The BLC preserves the substance but adds an unnecessary sentence break and loses the elegant compression of "full conformity in the whole man unto the righteousness thereof."
  2. **Rule 2**: WLC's "gestures" is replaced with "self-expressions." While "self-expressions" is a reasonable modernization, "gestures" is more concrete and precise, referring to bodily comportment and outward physical bearing.
  3. **Rule 4 fragmented**: The WLC presents rule 4 as one unified statement covering both the duty/sin and promise/threatening relationships. The BLC splits this into two separate sections, with the second half ("When a warning is added, an opposite promise is included") appearing as its own section. This fragmentation slightly weakens the logical unity of the principle.
  4. **Rule 5 fragmented**: Similarly split across three sections in the BLC versus one unified statement in the WLC. The threefold structure ("never done" / "always obligatory" / "not at all times") is maintained but the splitting reduces the rhetorical force of the single balanced principle.
  5. **Rule 7**: WLC's "we are bound, according to our places to endeavour that it may be avoided or performed by others, according to the duty of their places" becomes "we must try to help others keep those provisions, in the context of our position in life and theirs." The key phrase "that it may be avoided or performed" (specifying both negative and positive duties) is replaced with the less precise "keep those provisions."
  6. **Rule 8**: WLC's "according to our places and callings, to be helpful to them" becomes "we must support others in keeping what the law commands them to do or not to do." The phrase "places and callings" is dropped.

- Key phrases dropped:
  - "full conformity in the whole man unto the righteousness thereof" (rule 1) — weakened to "binds the whole person to observe it completely"
  - "gestures" (rule 2) — replaced with "self-expressions"
  - "provocations thereunto" (rule 6) — the BLC mentions "contributory causes, means, opportunities, and appearances" but drops "provocations," which in the WLC captures the idea of things that provoke or incite toward sin, a distinct concept from mere opportunities or occasions
  - "occasions" (rule 6) — replaced with "opportunities," which is a reasonable modernization
  - "according to our places and callings" (rule 8) — dropped

## Detailed Notes

1. **Structural fragmentation**: The BLC splits WLC rules 4 and 5 each across multiple TOML sections. While this may reflect formatting choices rather than theological decisions, the effect is to break up unified logical principles into discrete statements. The WLC deliberately presents each rule as a single balanced proposition; the BLC's fragmentation reduces this rhetorical and logical unity.

2. **"Provocations thereunto" omission**: This is the most notable content omission. The WLC's rule 6 teaches that the commandments forbid not only sins of a particular kind, but also "all the causes, means, occasions, and appearances thereof, and provocations thereunto." The BLC renders this as "all the contributory causes, means, opportunities, and appearances related to these sins and obligations." The word "provocations" captures the Reformed principle that we must avoid not only what leads to sin (occasions) but also what stirs up sinful desire (provocations) — a subtle but meaningful distinction.

3. **"Self-expressions" vs. "gestures"**: The WLC's "gestures" refers specifically to bodily actions and physical comportment (posture, hand motions, facial expressions in their outward physical dimension). "Self-expressions" is broader and more subjective, potentially collapsing the outward/physical dimension into something more interior. This is a minor concern in a rule that is specifically about the law reaching beyond interior states to outward behavior.

4. **Empty sections**: The BLC has two empty text sections at the end (sections with verses but no text). These appear to be continuation proof texts for rule 8, which is acceptable formatting but unusual.

5. **Rule 6 addition of "typical"**: The BLC adds that "prohibitions...and commandments...are typical and so cover not just those particular sins or obligations but all others of the same kind." The word "typical" is the BLC's rendering of the WLC's concept that specific sins stand "under" a broader category. This is a reasonable clarification.

## Scripture Analysis

The BLC's proof texts largely overlap with traditional Reformed proof texts for this question:

- **Rule 1**: Psalm 19:7; James 2:10; Matthew 5:21-22 — Appropriate and well-chosen. The WLC cites Psalm 19:7, James 2:10, and Romans 7:14 (which the BLC moves to rule 2).
- **Rule 2**: Romans 7:14; Deuteronomy 6:5; Matthew 22:37-39; Matthew 5:21-22, 27-28, 33-34, 37-39, 43-44 — Strong selection. The extended Matthew 5 citations effectively demonstrate the spiritual reach of the law.
- **Rule 3**: Colossians 3:5; Amos 8:5; Proverbs 1:19; 1 Timothy 6:10 — Adequate.
- **Rule 4**: Isaiah 58:13; Deuteronomy 6:13; Matthew 4:9-10; Matthew 15:4-6 and Matthew 5:21-25; Ephesians 4:28 — Adequate, though split across two sections.
- **Rule 5**: Split across three sections with appropriate proof texts. The use of Exodus 20:12 and Proverbs 30:17 for "what God forbids must never be done" is somewhat unusual — these relate to the fifth commandment specifically rather than demonstrating the general principle.
- **Rule 6**: Deuteronomy 4:8-9; Luke 17:10 — Surprisingly sparse for such a substantive rule.
- **Rule 7**: Matthew 12:7 — A single proof text is thin for this principle.
- **Rule 8**: Split across multiple sections with a reasonable selection of texts.
