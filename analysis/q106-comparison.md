# Q106: What is the reason annexed to the third commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q106 faithfully modernizes WLC Q114 with strong structural solidarity, preserving all three theological components: (1) God's identity as LORD and our God grounds the prohibition, (2) His resolute opposition to acquitting transgressors, and (3) the contrast between divine and human judgment. The Shorter Catechism's core content is fully preserved. Scripture citations are virtually identical to the WLC's proof texts.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "What is the reason annexed to the third commandment?"
> "The reason annexed to the third commandment is, that however the breakers of this commandment may escape punishment from men, yet the Lord our God will not suffer them to escape his righteous judgment (1 Sam. 2:12, 17, 22, 29; 3:13; Deut. 28:58, 59)."

- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves and expands every element of the Shorter's answer.
- Structural changes: The Shorter presents the contrast between human and divine judgment in a single compressed sentence ("however... yet..."). The BLC, following the WLC, unpacks this into a fuller three-part structure, which is appropriate expansion for a Larger Catechism. The theological meaning is identical.
- The Shorter's proof texts (1 Sam. 2:12, 17, 22, 29; 3:13; Deut. 28:58-59) are all represented in the BLC, with the BLC adding Leviticus 19:12, Ezekiel 36:21-23, and Zechariah 5:2-4 to match the WLC's fuller citation set.
- The Shorter's key phrase "will not suffer them to escape his righteous judgment" is closely mirrored in the BLC's "will not allow them to escape His righteous punishment."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 23 (Of Lawful Oaths and Vows), Paragraphs 1-3

The 1689 does not contain a direct parallel to the *reason* annexed to the third commandment in catechetical form. However, Chapter 23 addresses the reverence due to God's name in the context of oaths and vows:

> "A lawful Oath is a part of religious worship, wherein the person swearing in Truth, Righteousness, and Judgement, solemnly calleth God to witness what he sweareth; and to judge him according to the Truth or falseness thereof." (23.1)

> "The Name of God only is that by which men ought to swear; and therein it is to be used, with all Holy Fear and reverence, therefore to swear vainly or rashly by that glorious, and dreadful name; or to swear at all by any other thing, is sinful and to be abhorred..." (23.2)

> "...by rash, false, and vain Oaths the Lord is provoked, and for them this Land mournes." (23.3, citing Lev. 19:12)

Chapter 19.6 also speaks to the law's threatenings showing "what even their Sins deserve" for believers, consistent with the catechism's emphasis on God's righteous judgment against transgressors of this commandment.

- Omissions: None. The BLC is consistent with the Confession's teaching on the reverence due to God's name and divine judgment against misuse.
- Structural changes: None of concern. The Confession treats oaths specifically; the BLC treats the broader reason annexed to the commandment. These are complementary, not competing.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q114: "What reasons are annexed to the third commandment?"
> "The reasons annexed to the third commandment, in these words, The Lord thy God, and, For the Lord will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain, are, because he is the Lord and our God, therefore his name is not to be profaned, or any way abused by us; especially because he will be so far from acquitting and sparing the transgressors of this commandment, as that he will not suffer them to escape his righteous judgment; albeit many such escape the censures and punishments of men."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this is a doctrinal area of complete Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance. Minor wording differences are modernization only:
  - WLC "profaned, or any way abused" → BLC "treated as unholy or misused... in any way" (faithful modernization)
  - WLC "so far from acquitting and sparing the transgressors" → BLC "so opposed to acquitting or sparing those who break this commandment" (faithful modernization)
  - WLC "albeit many such escape the censures and punishments of men" → BLC "even though many who do break this commandment escape human condemnation and punishment" (faithful modernization; "censures" modernized to "condemnation")
- Key phrases dropped: None. All three theological components of the WLC answer are preserved:
  1. God's identity as LORD and our God grounds the prohibition against profaning His name
  2. God's resolute refusal to acquit or spare transgressors / righteous judgment
  3. Contrast with human inability to punish all such transgressors

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's three-section structure maps precisely to the WLC's three clauses: (1) "because he is the Lord and our God, therefore his name is not to be profaned" → BLC section 1; (2) "he will be so far from acquitting and sparing... as that he will not suffer them to escape his righteous judgment" → BLC section 2; (3) "albeit many such escape the censures and punishments of men" → BLC section 3.
- The BLC substitutes "righteous punishment" for the WLC/Shorter's "righteous judgment." This is a very minor shift — "punishment" is slightly narrower than "judgment" (which encompasses the judicial process as well as the sentence). However, in context the meaning is clear and the theological import is not materially affected.
- The BLC uses "condemnation and punishment" to render the WLC's "censures and punishments," which is a good modernization — "censures" in its 17th-century ecclesiastical sense (formal church discipline) is well captured by "condemnation."

## Scripture Analysis
- **BLC Section 1** cites Leviticus 19:12 — matches WLC footnote 618 exactly.
- **BLC Section 2** cites Ezekiel 36:21-23, Deuteronomy 28:58-59, and Zechariah 5:2-4 — matches WLC footnote 619 exactly.
- **BLC Section 3** cites 1 Samuel 2:12-24 and 1 Samuel 3:13 — matches WLC footnote 620 (which cites 1 Sam. 2:12, 17, 22, 24 and 1 Sam. 3:13). The BLC uses a range reference (2:12-24) rather than individual verses, but the same passage is in view.
- The Shorter's citations (1 Sam. 2:12, 17, 22, 29; 3:13; Deut. 28:58-59) are fully subsumed by the BLC's broader citation set.
- All proof texts are appropriate and well-matched to their respective theological claims.
