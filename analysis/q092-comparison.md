# Q92: What is the sum of the Ten Commandments?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q92 faithfully mirrors the structure and content of WLC Q102 and Q122, combining them into a single question with sectioned answers. It preserves the Shorter Catechism's core teaching while expanding it with the WLC's two-table division (duty to God / duty to man) and the Golden Rule addition. The theology is fully consistent with the 1689 Confession's treatment of the moral law.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is the sum of the ten commandments?
> A. The sum of the ten commandments is, to love the Lord our God, with all our heart, with all our soul, with all our strength, and with all our mind; and our neighbour as ourselves. (Mt. 22:37-40)

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's content is fully preserved. The Shorter combines both tables into a single sentence; the BLC expands this into sectioned form but retains every element.
- Structural changes: The BLC separates the Shorter's unified answer into three sections: (1) the four commandments summarizing duty to God, (2) the six commandments summarizing duty to man, and (3) the Golden Rule. This is an expansion following the WLC pattern, not a reduction. The Shorter's phrase "and our neighbour as ourselves" appears in BLC section 2 as "to love our neighbor as ourselves."
- The BLC adds the explicit four/six division and the Golden Rule (Matthew 7:12), both drawn from the WLC. These are appropriate expansions for a Larger Catechism.
- Scripture: The Shorter cites Mt. 22:37-40. The BLC cites Luke 10:27 and Mt. 22:37-40 for the first table, Mt. 22:39 for the second table, and Mt. 7:12 for the Golden Rule. The addition of Luke 10:27 provides the parallel Lukan account. All proof texts are appropriate.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraph 2:
> "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables; the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man."

- Omissions: None. The 1689 does not itself provide the summary of the commandments (it leaves that to catechetical instruction), but its two-table division (four commandments for duty to God, six for duty to man) is exactly what the BLC adopts.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's structure perfectly reflects the 1689's two-table framework.
- The BLC's language of "four commandments containing our duty to God" and "six commandments which contain our duty to man" directly echoes the 1689's "the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 102. What is the sum of the four commandments which contain our duty to God?
> A. The sum of the four commandments containing our duty to God is, to love the Lord our God with all our heart, and with all our soul, and with all our strength, and with all our mind.

> Q. 122. What is the sum of the six commandments which contain our duty to man?
> A. The sum of the six commandments which contain our duty to man, is, to love our neighbour as ourselves, and to do to others what we would have them to do to us.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**. The BLC combines WLC Q102 and Q122 into a single question (Q92), but preserves the complete text of both answers verbatim. The only change is combining them under one question rather than two, which is a reasonable editorial choice for a catechism that has fewer total questions.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is an area of complete Baptist-Presbyterian agreement on the moral law.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The BLC reproduces the WLC's answers with only trivial modernization ("neighbour" to "neighbor").
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every phrase from both WLC Q102 and Q122 is preserved in the BLC.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's decision to merge WLC Q102 and Q122 into a single question is structurally sound. The WLC separates them because it treats the two tables in two different sections of the catechism (Q102 appears before the first-table commandments, Q122 before the second-table commandments). The BLC places them together as a single overview question, which is a valid pedagogical choice.
- The BLC's sectioned format (three [[sections]]) naturally maps to: (1) first table summary, (2) second table summary, (3) Golden Rule amplification. This preserves the WLC's logical flow while consolidating the presentation.
- The Shorter Catechism's more concise answer omits the Golden Rule and the explicit four/six division. The BLC's expansion to include these elements is precisely the kind of elaboration expected from a Larger Catechism.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Luke 10:27** (BLC section 1): The Lukan version of the Great Commandment, cited by the WLC in its proof text (footnote 444). Appropriate addition alongside Matthew.
- **Matthew 22:37-40** (BLC section 1): The primary Great Commandment text, shared with the Shorter Catechism. Properly cited.
- **Matthew 22:39** (BLC section 2): The second great commandment specifically. Matches the WLC's proof text (footnote 646 references this verse).
- **Matthew 7:12** (BLC section 3): The Golden Rule. Matches the WLC's proof text for Q122 (footnote 647). An appropriate and well-established summary of our duty to neighbor.
- All proof texts are theologically appropriate and align with those used by the WLC and Shorter Catechism.
