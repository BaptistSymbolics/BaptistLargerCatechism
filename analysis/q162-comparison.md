# Q162: Is everyone saved who hears the gospel and attends church?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q162 faithfully teaches the core Reformed doctrine that not all who hear the gospel and attend a visible church are saved, and that only the elect are true members of the invisible church. This directly parallels WLC Q61. However, the BLC's answer contains a grammatical ambiguity ("The elect are only the true members of the invisible church") that could be read as limiting the invisible church to present elect rather than affirming that *only* the elect constitute it. The WLC's phrasing "but they only who are true members of the church invisible" is clearer. Additionally, the BLC drops the WLC's term "live in the visible church" in favor of "attends church," which weakens the concept from membership and life within the covenant community to mere attendance.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not contain a question specifically asking whether all who hear the gospel and attend church are saved.

The closest related material comes from the Shorter Catechism's treatment of effectual calling and the means of grace:

> Q. What is effectual calling?
> A. Effectual calling is the work of God's Spirit (2 Tim. 1:9; 2 Thess. 2:13, 14), whereby convincing us of our sin and misery (Acts 2:37), enlightening our minds in the knowledge of Christ (Acts 26:18), and renewing our wills (Ez. 36:26, 27), he doth persuade and enable us to embrace Jesus Christ freely offered to us in the gospel (John 6:44, 45; Phil. 2:13).

> Q. What are the outward means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption?
> A. The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption are his ordinances, especially the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer; all which means are made effectual to the elect for salvation (Mt. 28:19, 20; Acts 2:42, 46, 47).

The Shorter Catechism's phrase "made effectual to the elect for salvation" implicitly teaches the same doctrine as BLC Q162 — the means of grace are effectual specifically *to the elect*, not to all who encounter them. The BLC captures this truth but through a distinct question format rather than mirroring the Shorter's approach.

- **Omissions:** No direct parallel to evaluate omissions against. The Shorter's implicit teaching (means effectual "to the elect") is preserved in the BLC's explicit statement about the elect being true members of the invisible church.
- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC addresses this topic through an explicit question (following the WLC's structure) where the Shorter addresses it implicitly through the doctrine of effectual calling.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

**Chapter 26, Paragraph 1:** "The Catholick or universal Church, which (with respect to the internal work of the Spirit, and truth of grace) may be called invisible, consists of the whole number of the Elect, that have been, are, or shall be gathered into one, under Christ the head thereof; and is the spouse, the body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all."

**Chapter 26, Paragraph 3:** "The purest Churches under heaven are subject to mixture, and error; and som have so degenerated as to become no Churches of Christ, but Synagogues of Satan; nevertheless Christ always hath had, and ever shall have a Kingdome in this world, to the end thereof, of such as believe in him, and make profession of his Name."

**Chapter 10, Paragraph 4:** "Others not elected, although they may be called by the Ministry of the word, and may have some common operations of the Spirit, yet not being effectually drawn by the Father, they neither will, nor can truly come to Christ; and therefore cannot be saved: much less can men that receive not the Christian Religion be saved; be they never so diligent to frame their lives according to the light of nature, and the Law of that Religion they do profess."

The BLC faithfully captures the Confession's core teaching: the invisible church consists of the elect (26.1), visible churches are subject to mixture (26.3), and those not elected — even if called by the ministry of the Word — cannot truly come to Christ and cannot be saved (10.4).

- **Omissions:** The Confession's 10.4 provides significantly more doctrinal content than the BLC captures: (1) the non-elect "may have some common operations of the Spirit" — a critical concept about common grace that the BLC omits; (2) they are "not being effectually drawn by the Father" — the reason for their failure to come to Christ, which the BLC omits; (3) they "neither will, nor can truly come to Christ" — the double impossibility (willingness and ability), omitted. However, these details may be addressed in other BLC questions on effectual calling, so the omission here may not be a standalone deficiency.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC simplifies the Confession's nuanced explanation into a bare assertion. The Confession explains *why* not all are saved (lack of effectual drawing, common operations only, inability to come to Christ). The BLC simply states the fact without explanation. This is appropriate for a catechetical format — the WLC Q61 does the same — but the BLC's simplification is even more compressed than the WLC's.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q61: Are all they saved who hear the gospel, and live in the church?
> A. All that hear the gospel, and live in the visible church, are not saved; but they only who are true members of the church invisible.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** The BLC changes "live in the church" to "attends church." This may reflect a Baptist ecclesiological instinct — Baptists emphasize the gathered, regenerate church membership and may be less comfortable with "living in the church" language that could suggest a broader covenantal membership (including children of believers, per WLC Q62). However, the shift from "live in" to "attends" actually *weakens* the point: the WLC makes the stronger claim that even those who genuinely live within the visible church community are not necessarily saved. "Attends" reduces this to mere presence at services, making the contrast less sharp.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"Live in the visible church" changed to "attends a visible church":** "Living in" the church implies a deeper connection — membership, participation, covenant life. "Attends" is weaker and more casual. The WLC's phrasing makes the harder theological point: even deep involvement in the visible church does not guarantee salvation. The BLC's version could be read as merely saying that casual churchgoers may not be saved, which is a less challenging assertion.
  2. **"They only who are true members of the church invisible" restructured to "The elect are only the true members of the invisible church":** The WLC's phrasing clearly answers the question: who is saved? — "they only who are true members of the church invisible." The BLC's phrasing reverses the structure and introduces a grammatical ambiguity. "The elect are only the true members" could be parsed as "the elect are merely the true members" (diminishing) rather than the intended "only the elect are the true members" (exclusive). The WLC's word order is clearer.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  1. "Live in the visible church" — replaced with weaker "attends a visible church"
  2. The WLC's clean exclusive construction "they only who are" — restructured into an ambiguous sentence

## Detailed Notes

### Grammatical Ambiguity
The BLC's second sentence reads: "The elect are only the true members of the invisible church." This is ambiguous:
- **Intended meaning:** Only the elect are true members of the invisible church (exclusivity — matching WLC).
- **Possible misreading:** The elect are merely/only the true members of the invisible church (reducing what the elect are to just this one thing).

A clearer phrasing would be: "Only the elect are true members of the invisible church" or "They only who are true members of the invisible church are saved" (closer to WLC).

### "Attends" vs. "Live in"
The shift from "live in" to "attends" is noteworthy. The WLC's "live in the visible church" encompasses the full range of visible church life — baptism, church membership, participation in ordinances, hearing the Word preached, sitting under church discipline. "Attends church" in modern English suggests showing up for services. This weakens the theological force of the answer, since the point is that even those most thoroughly embedded in the visible church community are not saved apart from being elect members of the invisible church.

### Positive Elements
- The core doctrine is faithfully preserved: not all who hear the gospel and are part of a visible church are saved.
- The visible/invisible church distinction is maintained.
- The identification of the elect as the true members of the invisible church is correct.
- The question is placed appropriately in sequence following Q159 (duty to join a visible church) and preceding Q163 (what is the invisible church?), mirroring the WLC's logical flow from Q61 to Q64.

## Scripture Analysis
- **John 12:38-40** — Isaiah's prophecy about Israel's unbelief despite seeing signs. Strong text for the doctrine that hearing the gospel does not guarantee faith. Not in WLC proof texts for Q61 but theologically appropriate.
- **Romans 9:6** — "For they are not all Israel, which are of Israel." Excellent proof text directly supporting the visible/invisible distinction. Not in WLC Q61's proof texts but powerfully relevant.
- **Matthew 22:14** — "Many are called, but few are chosen." Directly supports the teaching. This text *is* cited in the 1689 Confession 10.4 as a proof text for the non-elect being called by the ministry of the Word.
- **Matthew 7:21** — "Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven." Strong text showing that profession and visible church participation do not guarantee salvation.
- **Matthew 13:41-42** — The parable of the tares, where the Son of Man gathers out of his kingdom "all things that offend." Supports the mixture in the visible church.
- **Romans 11:7** — "The election hath obtained it, and the rest were blinded." Supports the elect/non-elect distinction within the visible covenant community.

The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and arguably stronger than the WLC's sparse footnote for Q61 (which cites John 12:38-40; Romans 9:6; Matthew 22:14 — three of which overlap with the BLC). The additional Matthew 7:21, 13:41-42, and Romans 11:7 are excellent supporting texts.
