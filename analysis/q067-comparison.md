# Q67: What is adoption?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q67 is a close reproduction of WLC Q74 and faithfully exposits the 1689 Confession Chapter XII. It preserves the Shorter Catechism's core content while expanding it with the richer detail found in both the Confession and WLC. However, it omits several significant privileges of adoption detailed in the 1689—most notably the perseverance language ("yet never cast off; but sealed to the day of Redemption"), access to the throne of grace with boldness, and the ability to cry "Abba, Father"—and similarly drops the WLC's "dispensations" from the fatherly care clause.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** Q. What is adoption? A. Adoption is an act of God's free grace (1 John 3:1), whereby we are received into the number and have a right to all the privileges of the sons of God (John 1:12; Rom. 8:14-17).

- Omissions: None of significance. The Shorter's content is fully preserved. The Shorter says "God's free grace"; the BLC says "the free grace of God"—no theological difference. The Shorter's "have a right to all the privileges" is captured by the BLC's "welcomed to all the liberties and privileges."
- Structural changes: The BLC expands the Shorter's compressed formulation by enumerating specific privileges (name, Spirit, fatherly care, heirship) rather than using the Shorter's summary phrase "all the privileges." This is appropriate for a Larger Catechism and mirrors the WLC's approach. The BLC also adds the Christological ground ("in and for His only Son Jesus Christ") and the justification condition ("all those who are justified"), both absent from the Shorter but present in the Confession and WLC.
- The BLC faithfully mirrors and expands the Shorter's content.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter XII, Paragraph 1 — "All those that are justified, God vouchsafed, in, and for the sake of his only Son Jesus Christ, to make partakers of the Grace of Adoption; by which they are taken into the number, and enjoy the Liberties, and Priveledges of Children of God; have his name put upon them, receive the Spirit of Adoption, have access to the throne of Grace with boldness, are enabled to cry Abba, Father, are pitied, protected, provided for, and chastned by him, as by a Father; yet never cast off; but sealed to the day of Redemption, and inherit the promises, as heirs, of everlasting Salvation."

- Omissions:
  - **"access to the throne of Grace with boldness"** — This privilege of adoption is entirely absent from the BLC. The Confession emphasizes the believer's bold access to God in prayer as a consequence of adoption, a significant experiential privilege.
  - **"enabled to cry Abba, Father"** — Related to the above, this phrase captures the intimacy and confidence of the adoptive relationship. The BLC mentions the Spirit being given but does not connect it to the "Abba, Father" cry as both the Confession and Scripture (Gal. 4:6; Rom. 8:15) do.
  - **"yet never cast off; but sealed to the day of Redemption"** — The perseverance/security dimension of adoption is absent. The Confession explicitly teaches that adopted children are never cast off and are sealed, linking adoption to the doctrine of preservation. This is a weighty doctrinal element.
  - **"protected"** and **"provided for"** — The Confession lists five aspects of fatherly care: pitied, protected, provided for, chastened, and never cast off. The BLC reduces this to "under His fatherly care" as a summary phrase. While "fatherly care" could be read to encompass these, the explicit enumeration in the Confession gives greater specificity.
  - **"as heirs, of everlasting Salvation"** — The Confession specifies the inheritance is "everlasting Salvation." The BLC says "heirs of all the promises and fellow-heirs with Christ in glory," which is drawn from the WLC rather than the Confession. Both are orthodox, but the Confession's "everlasting Salvation" is more comprehensive than "glory" alone.
- Structural changes: The Confession begins with justification as the antecedent condition and then lists the privileges sequentially. The BLC follows the same structure (matching the WLC's order). No problematic structural changes.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q74: "Adoption is an act of the free grace of God, in and for his only Son Jesus Christ, whereby all those that are justified are received into the number of his children, have his name put upon them, the Spirit of his Son given to them, are under his fatherly care and dispensations, admitted to all the liberties and privileges of the sons of God, made heirs of all the promises, and fellow-heirs with Christ in glory."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC is very nearly a verbatim reproduction of WLC Q74 in modernized English.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is a doctrine where Baptists and Presbyterians share full agreement.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"dispensations" dropped** — The WLC says "under his fatherly care and dispensations." The BLC says only "under His fatherly care." The word "dispensations" in the WLC refers to God's providential dealings with His children (including discipline and varied providences). While "fatherly care" partially covers this, the WLC's "dispensations" adds the nuance of God's sovereign ordering of circumstances for His children's good. There is no Baptist theological reason to omit this word.
  - **"welcomed to" vs. "admitted to"** — The BLC changes "admitted to all the liberties and privileges" to "welcomed to all the liberties and privileges." This is a modest stylistic modernization. "Welcomed" may slightly soften the legal/forensic connotation of "admitted," but the difference is minor.
- Key phrases dropped: "dispensations" (no theological reason for omission).

## Detailed Notes
1. **The BLC closely follows the WLC** rather than the 1689 Confession for its structure and phrasing. This is appropriate for a catechism, but it means the BLC inherits the WLC's own abridgment of the fuller Confessional treatment.
2. **The 1689 Confession is richer than both the WLC and BLC** on adoption, including: access to the throne of grace with boldness, the Abba Father cry, the five-fold description of fatherly care (pitied, protected, provided for, chastened, never cast off), and the sealing to the day of redemption. A Baptist Larger Catechism might be expected to incorporate more of this distinctly Baptist confessional richness.
3. **The perseverance element** ("never cast off; sealed to the day of Redemption") is perhaps the most significant omission. The 1689 Confession explicitly ties adoption to the security of the believer, a doctrine of great pastoral importance. The BLC's omission here means it does not fully exposit the Confession on this point.
4. **The "Abba, Father" language** is not merely illustrative—it is biblically grounded in two key adoption texts (Gal. 4:6; Rom. 8:15) and represents the subjective, experiential dimension of adoption. The BLC cites Galatians 4:6 for the giving of the Spirit but does not draw out the "Abba, Father" implication that the Confession does.
5. **The BLC's final clause** ("made heirs of all the promises and fellow-heirs with Christ in glory") faithfully reproduces the WLC ending, which is a strong and appropriate conclusion.

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 John 3:1** — Correctly cited for adoption as free grace. Matches Shorter and WLC.
- **Ephesians 1:5; Galatians 4:4-5** — Correctly cited for the Christological ground. Matches WLC prooftexts.
- **John 1:12; Romans 8:15-16** — Cited for reception into the number of God's children. Romans 8:15-16 is appropriate but notably includes the "Abba, Father" cry that the BLC text itself does not mention. This creates a slight tension: the proof text teaches something the catechism answer does not state.
- **Numbers 6:24-27; 2 Corinthians 6:18; Revelation 3:12** — Cited for having God's name put upon them. Numbers 6:24-27 (the Aaronic blessing) is an interesting addition not found in the WLC prooftexts; the WLC cites only 2 Corinthians 6:18 and Revelation 3:12. This is a reasonable expansion.
- **Galatians 4:6** — Cited for the Spirit being given. This verse explicitly mentions the "Abba, Father" cry, further highlighting the omission in the catechism text itself.
- **Psalm 103:13; Matthew 6:32; Hebrews 12:5-11** — Cited for fatherly care. Psalm 103:13 covers the "pitied" aspect, Hebrews 12:5-11 covers chastening, and Matthew 6:32 covers provision. These are appropriate but the catechism text does not enumerate these aspects as the Confession does.
- **Hebrews 6:12; Romans 8:17; 1 Peter 1:3-4** — Cited for the heirship clause. Romans 8:17 is the classic "fellow-heirs with Christ" text. 1 Peter 1:3-4 adds the concept of an inheritance "imperishable, undefiled, and unfading"—a fine addition. Hebrews 6:12 ("imitators of those who through faith and patience inherit the promises") is cited for inheriting promises, matching the WLC's corresponding prooftext (the WLC also cites Heb. 6:12).
