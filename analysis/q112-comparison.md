# Q112: Why does the word Remember begin the fourth commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q112 is a faithful, modernized rendering of WLC Q121. It preserves every substantive theological point from the WLC answer, follows the same two-part structure ("in one respect" / "in another respect" mapping to "partly" / "partly"), and retains all key concepts including preparation, the connection to keeping other commandments, creation and redemption as the essence of religion, the weakness of natural light, restriction of lawful liberty, the danger of worldly engrossment, and Satan's active opposition. The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question. Minor wording variations (e.g., "essence of true religion" for "short abridgment of religion") are legitimate modernizations that preserve meaning. The addition of Hebrews 4:9 is a theologically appropriate expansion.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism addresses the fourth commandment in Q57-62 (what is required, which day, how sanctified, what is forbidden, reasons annexed) but does not have a question specifically addressing why the word "Remember" begins the commandment.

The closest Shorter question is Q62 on the reasons annexed:
> "Q. What are the reasons annexed to the fourth commandment? A. The reasons annexed to the fourth commandment, are God's allowing us six days of the week for our own lawful employments, his challenging a special propriety in a seventh, his own example, and his blessing the Sabbath day."

This addresses the *reasons annexed* to the commandment as a whole, not the specific significance of the word "Remember." BLC Q112 covers different ground — the purpose of the specific word "Remember" — so no omission of Shorter content is at issue here.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraphs 7-8

> Paragraph 7: "As it is of the Law of nature, that in general a proportion of time by Gods appointment, be set a part for the Worship of God; so by his Word in a positive-moral, and perpetual Commandement, binding all men, in all Ages, he hath particularly appointed one day in seven for a Sabbath to be kept holy unto him, which from the beginning of the World to the Resurrection of Christ, was the last day of the week; and from the resurrection of Christ, was changed into the first day of the week which is called the Lords day; and is to be continued to the end of the World, as the Christian Sabbath; the observation of the last day of the week being abolished."

> Paragraph 8: "The Sabbath is then kept holy unto the Lord, when men after a due preparing of their hearts, and ordering their common affairs aforehand, do not only observe an holy rest all the day, from their own works, words, and thoughts, about their worldly employment, and recreations, but also are taken up the whole time in the publick and private exercises of his worship, and in the duties of necessity and mercy."

The 1689 does not address the specific word "Remember" but its Sabbath theology is fully compatible with BLC Q112:
- **"due preparing of their hearts, and ordering their common affairs aforehand"** — corresponds to BLC's point about the word helping "prepare ourselves to keep the commandment."
- **"a positive-moral, and perpetual Commandement"** — aligns with BLC's point that keeping the Sabbath "is not obvious to our natural, human understanding" (i.e., it is positive law, not merely natural law).
- **"holy rest all the day, from their own works, words, and thoughts, about their worldly employment, and recreations"** — aligns with BLC's point about the Sabbath restricting "our freedom from doing things that are lawful on other days."

- Omissions: None. The 1689 does not treat this specific topic, so BLC Q112 is expanding on Confessional Sabbath theology rather than risking omission.
- Structural changes: None.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q121:
> "Q. 121. Why is the word Remember set in the beginning of the fourth commandment? A. The word Remember is set in the beginning of the fourth commandment, partly, because of the great benefit of remembering it, we being thereby helped in our preparation to keep it, and, in keeping it, better to keep all the rest of the commandments, and to continue a thankful remembrance of the two great benefits of creation and redemption, which contain a short abridgment of religion; and partly, because we are very ready to forget it, for that there is less light of nature for it, and yet it restraineth our natural liberty in things at other times lawful; that it cometh but once in seven days, and many worldly businesses come between, and too often take off our minds from thinking of it, either to prepare for it, or to sanctify it; and that Satan with his instruments labours much to blot out the glory, and even the memory of it, to bring in all irreligion and impiety."

- **Solidarity: STRONG**

- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None. This question does not touch on baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology.

- **Unexpected divergences:** None of substance.

- **Key phrases dropped:** None of theological significance. Minor wording changes are all legitimate modernizations:

## Detailed Notes
1. **Structure preserved:** The BLC breaks the answer into multiple sections with proof texts for each point, whereas the WLC presents a single continuous answer. This is a formatting change that aids clarity without altering theology.

2. **Hebrews 4:9 addition:** The BLC adds Hebrews 4:9 ("There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God") to the proof texts for the creation/redemption point. This is a theologically appropriate addition that strengthens the connection between Sabbath observance and redemptive rest — fully consistent with Reformed Sabbatarian theology.

3. **Numbers 15:37-40 usage:** The BLC uses this passage twice (sections 5 and 8), paralleling the WLC's use of this passage at footnote 641 (tendency to forget). The WLC cites it only in connection with Ezekiel 22:26 for the general forgetfulness point, while the BLC additionally applies it to the "worldly affairs" point. This is a minor expansion, not a deviation.

4. **"Essence" vs. "abridgment":** The WLC's "short abridgment of religion" means that creation and redemption summarize or encapsulate the core of true religion. The BLC's "essence of true religion" conveys the same idea — that these two great benefits are the heart of what religion is about. The shift from "abridgment" (summary) to "essence" (core substance) is arguably a slight intensification but not a theological alteration.
