# Q127: What is required in the seventh commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q127 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q138, preserving every substantive element of the WLC's answer in the same theological order. It also faithfully expands the Shorter Catechism's more compact answer without omitting any of the Shorter's core content. The key Shorter phrase — "the preservation of our own and our neighbors chastity, in heart, speech, and behavior" — is fully represented, though with "inclinations" substituted for the WLC's "affections" and the Shorter's implicit heart-level concern. The BLC's rendering of "gift of continency" as "those who cannot control their sexual desires" is less precise but not theologically inaccurate. No doctrinal content from the 1689 Confession's chapter on marriage is contradicted or omitted.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What is required in the seventh commandment? A. The seventh commandment requireth the preservation of our own and our neighbors chastity, in heart, speech, and behavior (1 Cor. 7:2, 3, 5, 34, 36; Col. 4:6; 1 Pet. 3:2)."
- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter's key elements — (1) chastity, (2) in heart, speech, and behavior, (3) preservation in ourselves and others — are all present in the BLC. The BLC uses "sexually pure" rather than "chastity" and "inclinations" rather than "heart," but these are modernization choices that preserve the meaning. The Shorter's word "neighbors" is rendered as "others" in the BLC, which is faithful.
- Structural changes: The BLC expands the Shorter's compact answer into multiple sections (as expected for a Larger Catechism), adding detail about watchfulness, temperance, company, dress, marriage, diligent labor, and resisting temptation. This expansion tracks the WLC's structure precisely and does not restructure the Shorter's theological relationships.
- The BLC's proof texts for the opening sections overlap significantly with the Shorter's citations (1 Cor. 7:2, 34, 36; Col. 4:6; 1 Pet. 3:2), demonstrating intentional alignment.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 25 (Of Marriage):
> "1. Marriage is to be between one Man and one Woman; neither is it lawful for any man to have more then one Wife, nor for any Woman to have more then one Husband at the same time."
> "2. Marriage was ordained for the mutual help of Husband and Wife, for the increase of Man-kind, with a legitimate issue, and for preventing of uncleanness."
> "3. It is lawful for all sorts of people to Marry, who are able with judgment to give their consent; yet it is the duty of Christians to marry in the Lord, and therefore such as profess the true Religion, should not Marry with Infidels, or Idolaters; neither should such as are godly be unequally yoked, by marrying with such as are wicked, in their life, or maintain damnable Heresie."

- Omissions: The BLC does not explicitly address the 1689's teaching that Christians should "marry in the Lord" and avoid marrying infidels, idolaters, or the ungodly (25.3). However, this omission is expected since BLC Q127 addresses the positive duties of the seventh commandment in general, not the specific regulations of marriage. This content more naturally fits a question on marriage itself or the sins forbidden by the seventh commandment.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's statement that "those who cannot control their sexual desires should marry" faithfully reflects the 1689's teaching that marriage was ordained "for preventing of uncleanness" (25.2), drawing on the same underlying theology and proof text (1 Cor. 7:2, 9).
- The BLC's reference to "loving and living together with their spouses" corresponds to the 1689's teaching that marriage was ordained "for the mutual help of Husband and Wife" (25.2). The emphasis on conjugal love and cohabitation aligns with the Confession's positive view of marriage.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 138. What are the duties required in the seventh commandment? A. The duties required in the seventh commandment are, chastity in body, mind, affections, words, and behavior; and the preservation of it in ourselves and others; watchfulness over the eyes and all the senses; temperance, keeping of chaste company, modesty in apparel; marriage by those that have not the gift of continency, conjugal love, and cohabitation; diligent labor in our callings; shunning all occasions of uncleanness, and resisting temptations thereunto."
- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC tracks WLC Q138 point-by-point in the same order, modernizing language while preserving every element.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — the seventh commandment's positive duties are an area of complete Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance. Every element of the WLC is represented:
  - WLC "chastity in body, mind, affections, words, and behavior" → BLC "sexually pure in body, mind, inclinations, words, and actions"
  - WLC "preservation of it in ourselves and others" → BLC "maintain that purity in ourselves and others"
  - WLC "watchfulness over the eyes and all the senses" → BLC "monitor what we look at as well as what we experience with our other senses"
  - WLC "temperance" → BLC "live temperately"
  - WLC "keeping of chaste company" → BLC "keeping pure company"
  - WLC "modesty in apparel" → BLC "dressing modestly"
  - WLC "marriage by those that have not the gift of continency" → BLC "those who cannot control their sexual desires should marry"
  - WLC "conjugal love, and cohabitation" → BLC "loving and living together with their spouses"
  - WLC "diligent labor in our callings" → BLC "work hard at whatever we are called to do"
  - WLC "shunning all occasions of uncleanness, and resisting temptations thereunto" → BLC "avoiding all opportunities for indecency, and resisting any temptation to say, think, or do anything indecent or obscene"
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every element of the WLC's answer is preserved in the BLC.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's substitution of "inclinations" for the WLC's "affections" is a reasonable modernization. In 17th-century usage, "affections" referred to the movements or inclinations of the soul — desires and dispositions. The modern English "inclinations" captures this meaning adequately, though "affections" in its older sense carried a slightly richer connotation of the heart's directional movements. This is a stylistic choice, not a theological departure.
- The BLC's rendering of "gift of continency" as "those who cannot control their sexual desires" shifts from a positive framing (possessing a gift) to a negative one (lacking control). The WLC's phrasing, drawn from 1 Corinthians 7:9, implies that celibacy is a positive divine gift, and those who lack it should marry. The BLC's wording could be read as reducing the matter to a deficiency of self-control rather than an absence of a particular charismatic gift. However, Paul's own language in 1 Cor. 7:9 ("if they cannot exercise self-control, let them marry") supports the BLC's rendering. This is a minor precision issue, not a theological error.
- The BLC's expansion of the final phrase — "resisting any temptation to say, think, or do anything indecent or obscene" — adds specificity beyond the WLC's "resisting temptations thereunto." This is a legitimate clarifying expansion that does not alter the meaning.
- The BLC's "behavior" from the Shorter becomes "actions" in BLC Q127. Both words are adequate translations of the concept.
