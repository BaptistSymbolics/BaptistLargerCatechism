# Q105: What is forbidden in the third commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q105 closely follows the WLC Q113 structure and content, faithfully modernizing its language while preserving nearly all theological substance. It faithfully exposits the Shorter Catechism's condensed statement and aligns with 1689 Chapter XXIII on oaths and vows. The minor concerns are: (1) the WLC's phrase "profane" is dropped from the description of abuse (the WLC has "ignorant, vain, irreverent, profane" while BLC has "ignorance, empty or unholy treatment" — "unholy" may be intended to cover "profane" but is weaker); (2) the WLC's "curious prying into" God's decrees is omitted, leaving only "complaining and quarreling about" and "misapplication"; and (3) "offensive walking" from the WLC is rendered as "in such a way as to offend him," slightly shifting the concept from publicly scandalous conduct to personal offense.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
Q. What is forbidden in the third commandment?
A. The third commandment forbiddeth all profaning and abusing of any thing whereby God makes himself known (Mal. 1:6, 7, 12; 2:2; 3:14).

- Omissions: None significant. The Shorter gives a single summary principle; the BLC properly expands it as a larger catechism should.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC preserves the Shorter's core principle — that the third commandment forbids profaning and abusing anything whereby God makes himself known — and then enumerates the specific sins that fall under this principle.
- The Shorter's proof texts (Malachi 1:6-7, 12; 2:2; 3:14) all appear in BLC Q105's proof texts, showing direct continuity.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XXIII — Of Lawful Oaths and Vows

Chapter XXIII, Paragraph 1: "A lawful Oath is a part of religious worship, wherein the person swearing in Truth, Righteousness, and Judgement, solemnly calleth God to witness what he sweareth; and to judge him according to the Truth or falseness thereof."

Paragraph 2: "The Name of God only is that by which men ought to swear; and therein it is to be used, with all Holy Fear and reverence, therefore to swear vainly or rashly by that glorious, and dreadful name; or to swear at all by any other thing, is sinful and to be abhorred; yet as in matter of weight and moment for confirmation of truth, and ending all strife, an Oath is warranted by the Word of God; so a lawful Oath being imposed, by lawful Authority, in such matters, ought to be taken."

Paragraph 3: "Whosoever taketh an Oath warranted by the Word of God, ought duely to consider the weightiness of so solemn an act; and therein to avouch nothing, but what he knoweth to be the truth; for that by rash, false, and vain Oaths the Lord is provoked, and for them this Land mournes."

Paragraph 4: "An Oath is to be taken in the plain, and common sense of the words; without equivocation, or mental reservation."

Paragraph 5: "A Vow which is not to be made to any Creature, but to God alone, is to be made and performed with all Religious care, and faithfulness: But Popish Monastical Vows, of perpetual single life, professed poverty, and regular obedience, are so far from being degrees of higher perfection, that they are superstitious, and sinful snares, in which no Christian may intangle himself."

- Omissions: The 1689's language about oaths being taken "in the plain, and common sense of the words; without equivocation, or mental reservation" (XXIII.4) is not reflected in the BLC. This is a fairly specific doctrinal point about the nature of oath-taking. The 1689's condemnation of "Popish Monastical Vows" (XXIII.5) is also absent, though this is understandable modernization. The 1689's positive framing that oaths are "a part of religious worship" (XXIII.1) and are "warranted by the Word of God" in matters of "weight and moment" (XXIII.2) is not reflected — the BLC only addresses sinful oaths and vows, not the lawful use being presupposed.
- Structural changes: None significant. The BLC's treatment of perjury, sinful oaths and vows, violating lawful oaths, and fulfilling unlawful ones all correspond to the confession's teaching.
- The 1689's phrase "rash, false, and vain Oaths" (XXIII.3) is echoed in the BLC's treatment of sinful oaths, though less precisely.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q113:
"The sins forbidden in the third commandment are, the not using of God's name as is required; and the abuse of it in an ignorant, vain, irreverent, profane, superstitious or wicked mentioning or otherwise using his titles, attributes, ordinances, or works, by blasphemy, perjury; all sinful cursings, oaths, vows, and lots; violating of our oaths and vows, if lawful and fulfilling them, if of things unlawful; murmuring and quarrelling at, curious prying into, and misapplying of God's decrees and providences; misinterpreting, misapplying, or any way perverting the Word, or any part of it; to profane jests, curious or unprofitable questions, vain janglings, or the maintaining of false doctrines; abusing it, the creatures, or anything contained under the name of God, to charms, or sinful lusts and practices; the maligning, scorning, reviling, or any wise opposing of God's truth, grace, and ways; making profession of religion in hypocrisy, or for sinister ends; being ashamed of it, or a shame to it, by unconformable, unwise, unfruitful, and offensive walking, or backsliding from it."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: The WLC mentions "sacraments" in Q112 (the positive duty question); the BLC does not use this term anywhere, consistent with Baptist preference for "ordinances." However, this is in Q112, not Q113, so it does not affect Q105 directly.
- Unexpected divergences:
  1. **"Profane" dropped**: The WLC lists "ignorant, vain, irreverent, profane, superstitious or wicked mentioning." The BLC renders this as "ignorance, empty or unholy treatment; irreverence; superstition... or any wicked reference." The word "profane" is absent; "unholy" may be intended as its equivalent but is less precise. "Profane" in theological usage specifically means treating holy things as common — a distinct concept from merely "unholy."
  2. **"Curious prying into" God's decrees omitted**: The WLC says "murmuring and quarrelling at, curious prying into, and misapplying of God's decrees and providences." The BLC has "complaining and quarreling about or misapplication of God's decrees and acts of providence" — dropping the "curious prying into" element entirely. This is a meaningful omission: the WLC warns not only against complaining about God's decrees but also against speculative investigation of them beyond what is revealed.
  3. **"Profane jests" weakened**: The WLC's "profane jests" becomes "blasphemous mockery of His word" in the BLC. While "blasphemous mockery" is arguably stronger, it narrows the scope — "profane jests" includes irreverent humor that falls short of outright blasphemy.
  4. **"Offensive walking" shifted**: The WLC's "unconformable, unwise, unfruitful, and offensive walking, or backsliding from it" becomes in the BLC: "stubbornly refusing to obey him and by living unwisely, unfruitfully, or in such a way as to offend him or backslide away from him." The WLC's "unconformable" (not conforming to God's will) becomes "stubbornly refusing to obey" (a shift from passive non-conformity to active refusal). The WLC's "offensive walking" (conduct that scandalizes others) becomes "in such a way as to offend him" (conduct that offends God), subtly shifting from the horizontal (public scandal) to the vertical (divine offense).
  5. **"Or otherwise using" dropped**: The WLC says "wicked mentioning or otherwise using his titles, attributes, ordinances, or works." The BLC has "any wicked reference to his titles, qualities, regulations, or works." The phrase "or otherwise using" extends beyond verbal reference to include non-verbal misuse — the BLC's "reference" may be narrower.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "curious prying into" — a distinct sin of speculative presumption regarding divine decrees
  - "profane" as a descriptor of misuse
  - "or otherwise using" — extending beyond verbal mention
  - "for sinister ends" — the WLC says "hypocrisy, or for sinister ends"; the BLC says "using religion for evil purposes" which captures a similar but slightly different idea

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Qualities" for "attributes"**: The BLC renders the WLC's "attributes" as "qualities." This is a notable vocabulary change. "Attributes" is the standard theological term for God's perfections (omnipotence, omniscience, etc.). "Qualities" is a more generic term that lacks the same theological precision and could be read as implying something less than the classical divine attributes.

2. **"Regulations" for "ordinances"**: The BLC renders "ordinances" as "regulations." In context, the WLC's "ordinances" here likely refers to God's appointed institutions of worship (prayer, preaching, sacraments/ordinances, etc.), not merely rules. "Regulations" flattens this to mere rule-keeping and loses the worship dimension.

3. **"Casting lots" separated**: The WLC includes "lots" in the list "all sinful cursings, oaths, vows, and lots." The BLC separates "casting lots" as its own section with different proof texts (Esther 3:7; 9:24; Psalm 22:18). The WLC's proof text for "lots" points to Esther 3:7 and 9:24 — the BLC matches this. The separation is not theologically problematic but does change the flow.

4. **"Charms" becomes "magic"**: The WLC's "charms" is rendered as "magic" in the BLC. This is a reasonable modernization; "charms" in 17th-century usage referred to magical incantations and spells. The BLC's "the practice of magic" with proof texts Deuteronomy 18:10-14 and Acts 19:13 is faithful.

5. **"Vain janglings" modernized well**: The WLC's "curious or unprofitable questions, vain janglings" becomes "pointless arguing, meaningless talk" — a clear and faithful modernization.

6. **"God's truth, grace, and ways" preserved but altered**: The WLC says "God's truth, grace, and ways." The BLC says "God's truth, grace, and actions." Changing "ways" to "actions" slightly narrows the concept — God's "ways" includes his manner of dealing, his character as displayed in history, and his providential governance, while "actions" is more limited.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts broadly correspond to the WLC's, with some differences in selection.
- The Shorter Catechism's Malachi references (1:6-7, 12; 2:2; 3:14) all appear in BLC Q105.
- Notable additions: The BLC adds Ephesians 5:4 for "blasphemous mockery," which is appropriate (it addresses "crude joking").
- The BLC adds Romans 12:14 for "sinful cursing," which fits (Paul's instruction to bless and not curse).
- The BLC's proof texts for "casting lots" (Esther 3:7; 9:24; Psalm 22:18) are appropriate but narrow — Psalm 22:18 specifically refers to casting lots for Christ's garments, which is more about prophecy than prohibition.
- Deuteronomy 23:18 cited for "oaths and vows" is an unusual choice — it refers to the hire of a prostitute/dog not being brought into the house of the Lord, not directly about oaths. This may be a reference error or may be intended to illustrate vows connected to unlawful things.
