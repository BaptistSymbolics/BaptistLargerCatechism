# Q100: What is required in the second commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q100 is a faithful modernized expansion of the Shorter Catechism's answer and tracks the WLC Q108 very closely, preserving the regulative principle and the full catalog of worship elements. Two minor concerns arise: (1) the BLC replaces "monuments of idolatry" with "all forms of idolatry," which subtly shifts the focus from physical objects to abstract categories, and (2) the 1689's distinctive emphasis on singing (Psalms, Hymns, and Spiritual songs) as a distinct part of religious worship is absent from the BLC's enumeration, despite its explicit mention in 1689 22.5.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is required in the second commandment?
> A. The second commandment requireth the receiving, observing, and keeping pure and entire all such religious worship and ordinances, as God hath appointed in his word (Deut. 32:46; Mt. 23:20; Acts 2:42).

- **Omissions:** None of substance. The Shorter's key phrase "receiving, observing, and keeping pure and entire all such religious worship and ordinances, as God hath appointed in his word" is faithfully rendered in the BLC as "receive, respectfully perform, and preserve completely and purely all the regulations for religion and worship that God has established in His word." The three verbs (receiving/observing/keeping → receive/respectfully perform/preserve) and the two qualifiers (pure and entire → completely and purely) are all preserved.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC follows the Shorter's structure exactly in the opening statement, then expands with specific enumeration (as a Larger Catechism should).
- The BLC's opening line is a clean modernization of the Shorter's answer, maintaining all essential content.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 22 (Of Religious Worship and the Sabbath Day), Paragraph 1:
> "The acceptable way of Worshipping the true God, is instituted by himself; and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be Worshipped according to the imaginations, and devices of Men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representations, or any other way, not prescribed in the Holy Scriptures."

Chapter 22, Paragraph 5:
> "The reading of the Scriptures, Preaching, and hearing the word of God, teaching and admonishing one another in Psalms, Hymns and Spiritual songs, singing with grace in our Hearts to the Lord; as also the Administration of Baptism, and the Lords Supper are all parts of Religious worship of God, to be performed in obedience to him, with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear; moreover solemn humiliation with fastings; and thanksgiving upon special occasions, ought to be used in an holy and religious manner."

Chapter 22, Paragraph 3:
> "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance."

Chapter 23 (Of Lawful Oaths and Vows), Paragraphs 1 and 5:
> "A lawful Oath is a part of religious worship, wherein the person swearing in Truth, Righteousness, and Judgement, solemnly calleth God to witness what he sweareth."
> "A Vow which is not to be made to any Creature, but to God alone, is to be made and performed with all Religious care, and faithfulness."

Chapter 26 (Of the Church), Paragraphs 7-10 (on church government, discipline, ministry, and maintenance).

- **Omissions:**
  - **Singing (Psalms, Hymns, and Spiritual songs):** The 1689 22.5 explicitly lists "teaching and admonishing one another in Psalms, Hymns and Spiritual songs, singing with grace in our Hearts to the Lord" as a distinct part of religious worship alongside reading, preaching, and hearing the Word. The BLC does not include singing in its enumeration of worship elements. This is a notable absence since the 1689 treats it as a coordinate element of worship, not merely a subcategory of another element.
  - **Qualities of worship performance:** The 1689 22.5 specifies that these acts are "to be performed in obedience to him, with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear." The BLC's opening line preserves "respectfully" but does not enumerate these specific dispositions. This is arguably acceptable as a catechetical summary.
  - **Thanksgiving upon special occasions:** The 1689 22.5 mentions "thanksgiving upon special occasions" alongside fasting. The BLC includes "thanksgiving in the name of Christ" as part of prayer but not as a distinct occasional element.

- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC follows the 1689's ordering of worship elements closely.
- **Agreement:** The BLC's regulative principle statement ("all the regulations for religion and worship that God has established in His word") faithfully reflects 1689 22.1. The enumeration of prayer, Word ministry, sacraments, church government, fasting, oaths, and vows all have clear 1689 grounding.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 108. What are the duties required in the second commandment?
> A. The duties required in the second commandment are, the receiving, observing, and keeping pure and entire, all such religious worship and ordinances as God hath instituted in his Word; particularly prayer and thanksgiving in the name of Christ; the reading, preaching, and hearing of the Word; the administration and receiving of the sacraments; church government and discipline; the ministry and maintainance thereof; religious fasting; swearing by the name of God; and vowing unto him; as also the disapproving, detesting, opposing all false worship; and, according to each one's place and calling, removing it, and all monuments of idolatry.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None in this question. The second commandment duties are an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement. The sacraments language is retained (Baptists historically use "ordinances" but both traditions include them here).
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - **"Monuments of idolatry" → "all forms of idolatry":** The WLC specifies "removing it, and all monuments of idolatry" — referring to physical representations, images, and artifacts associated with false worship. The BLC replaces this with "all forms of idolatry," which broadens the reference from concrete objects to abstract categories. While not contradictory, this loses the WLC's specific focus on tangible objects of false worship that Reformed theology has historically applied to images in churches, relics, and physical idols. The WLC's language has specific polemic force against Roman Catholic and Anglo-Catholic practice that the BLC's vaguer phrasing dilutes.
  - **"Detesting" → "denouncing":** The WLC uses the triad "disapproving, detesting, opposing." The BLC uses "disapproving, denouncing, and opposing." "Detesting" is an affective/dispositional term (internal hatred of false worship), while "denouncing" is an active/verbal term (public declaration against false worship). This shifts the emphasis from the heart's response to public action. Both are legitimate duties, but the WLC's inclusion of the dispositional element ("detesting") is arguably more searching.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - **"Monuments of idolatry"** — replaced rather than modernized (see above).
  - **"Detesting"** — replaced with "denouncing" (see above).

## Detailed Notes
1. **Regulative Principle:** The BLC's opening statement is an excellent modernization that preserves the regulative principle — worship must be according to God's Word, not human invention. The language "regulations for religion and worship that God has established in His word" faithfully captures the Reformed and Baptist commitment to the regulative principle of worship.

2. **Enumeration completeness:** The BLC's list of worship elements closely tracks both the WLC and 1689:
   - Prayer and thanksgiving ✓
   - Reading, preaching, hearing the Word ✓
   - Sacraments/ordinances ✓
   - Church government and discipline ✓
   - Ministry and maintenance ✓
   - Religious fasting ✓
   - Oaths and vows ✓
   - Opposing false worship ✓
   - Removing idolatry ✓
   - **Singing** ✗ (present in 1689 22.5, absent from BLC)

3. **The singing omission** is the most notable finding. The 1689 22.5 coordinates singing ("teaching and admonishing one another in Psalms, Hymns and Spiritual songs, singing with grace in our Hearts to the Lord") alongside reading, preaching, and hearing the Word as a distinct part of worship. The WLC Q108 does not separately list singing either, so the BLC is following the WLC's pattern here. However, since the BLC claims to exposit the 1689 as well, and the 1689 explicitly includes singing, its absence is worth noting. This may be defensible on the grounds that the catechism follows the WLC's catechetical pattern for this question rather than the confessional enumeration.

4. **"Respectfully perform"** as a rendering of "observing" is an acceptable modernization that captures the sense of careful, reverent observance.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and appropriate:
- **Deuteronomy 32:46-47; Matthew 28:20; Acts 2:42; 1 Timothy 6:13-14** for the regulative principle — these are standard Reformed proof texts. The Shorter Catechism cites Deut. 32:46, Mt. 23:20 (likely 28:20), and Acts 2:42, showing close alignment.
- **Philippians 4:6; Ephesians 5:20** for prayer and thanksgiving — appropriate texts.
- **Deuteronomy 17:18-19; Acts 15:21; 2 Timothy 4:2; James 1:21-22; Acts 10:33** for Word ministry — a thorough set covering reading, preaching, and hearing.
- **Matthew 28:19; 1 Corinthians 11:23-30** for sacraments — standard baptism and Lord's Supper texts.
- **Matthew 18:15-17; Matthew 16:19; 1 Corinthians 5; 1 Corinthians 12:28; John 20:23** for church government/discipline — comprehensive.
- **Ephesians 4:11-12; 1 Timothy 5:17-18; 1 Corinthians 9:1-15** for ministry maintenance — well-grounded.
- **1 Corinthians 7:5; Joel 2:12-18** for fasting — appropriate.
- **Deuteronomy 6:13** for swearing — standard text.
- **Isaiah 19:21; Psalm 76:11; Psalm 116:14-18** for vows — solid choices.
- **Acts 17:16-17; Psalm 16:4** for opposing false worship — appropriate.
- **Deuteronomy 7:5; Isaiah 30:22** for removing idolatry — good texts that actually do reference physical destruction of idolatrous objects, which is somewhat in tension with the BLC's shift from "monuments" to "forms" of idolatry.
