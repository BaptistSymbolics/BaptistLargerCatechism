# Q88: Of what use is the moral law to all men?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q88 closely follows WLC Q95 in structure and content, preserving all five uses of the moral law common to all men. However, it weakens the WLC's language of the law "binding" men to walk accordingly, replacing it with the softer phrase "informs them of their duty to live by it." Both the WLC and the 1689 Confession (19.6) use the language of binding/directing, not merely informing. The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel, so the BLC is properly functioning as an expansion here.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not address the uses of the moral law. It moves from identifying the moral law as the rule of obedience (Q: "What did God at first reveal to man for the rule of his obedience?" / A: "The rule which God at first revealed to man for his obedience, was the moral law") directly to its summary comprehension in the Ten Commandments.
- Omissions: N/A — no parallel to omit from
- Structural changes: N/A
- The BLC is functioning here as a proper expansion of catechetical teaching, filling a gap the Shorter does not address. This is appropriate for a Larger Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraph 6 — "Although true Believers be not under the Law, as a Covenant of Works, to be thereby Justified or condemned; yet it is of great use to them as well as to others: in that, as a Rule of Life, informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty, it directs and binds them, to walk accordingly; discovering also the sinfull pollutions of their Natures, Hearts and Lives; so as Examining themselves thereby, they may come to further Conviction of, Humiliation for, and Hatred against Sin; together with a clearer sight of the need they have of Christ and the perfection of his Obedience."
- Omissions:
  - **"directs and binds them, to walk accordingly"** — The 1689 uses three verbs for the law's relation to duty: it *informs*, *directs*, and *binds*. The BLC reduces this to "informs them of their duty to live by it," dropping the directive and binding force of the law. This is a weakening of the obligatory character of the moral law.
  - **"Hatred against Sin"** — The 1689 specifies that the law brings "Conviction of, Humiliation for, and Hatred against Sin." The BLC mentions humbling and recognition of sinfulness but omits hatred of sin. However, this element in the 1689 appears in the context of the regenerate specifically, so its absence here (in the uses common to all men) is defensible.
  - **"Examining themselves thereby"** — The 1689's language of self-examination as the mechanism by which the law produces conviction is absent, though this too pertains more to the regenerate use in the 1689's structure.
- Structural changes: The 1689 treats the uses of the law in a single long paragraph covering both general and regenerate-specific uses. The BLC properly separates these (Q88 handles the uses common to all men), following the WLC's tripartite structure (all men / unregenerate / regenerate). This structural choice is sound.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q95 — "Of what use is the moral law to all men? A. The moral law is of use to all men, to inform them of the holy nature and the will of God, and of their duty, binding them to walk accordingly; to convince them of their disability to keep it, and of the sinful pollution of their nature, hearts, and lives: to humble them in the sense of their sin and misery, and thereby help them to a clearer sight of the need they have of Christ, and of the perfection of his obedience."
- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC's structure almost exactly, preserving the same five-point progression: (1) informs of God's nature/will, (2) informs of duty, (3) reveals sinful pollution and inability, (4) humbles in sense of sin/misery, (5) points to need for Christ and His obedience.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected or present on this topic.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"binding them to walk accordingly"** replaced with **"informs them of their duty to live by it"** — The WLC's "binding" conveys the law's authoritative, obligatory character. The BLC's "informs" reduces this to a cognitive function. The law does not merely inform of duty; it binds to duty. This is a subtle but theologically meaningful weakening.
  - **"their disability to keep it"** replaced with **"they are unable to keep it"** — This is a legitimate modernization with no theological loss. "Disability" and "unable" convey the same meaning.
  - **"help them to a clearer sight"** replaced with **"gives them a better awareness"** — Acceptable modernization. Slight loss of the visual metaphor ("sight") but no theological difference.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"binding them to walk accordingly"** — The most significant omission. The binding authority of the law over conscience and conduct is a key Reformed distinctive about the moral law's ongoing relevance.

## Detailed Notes
The overall theological trajectory of Q88 is sound. It faithfully represents the five uses of the moral law common to all men as taught in both the WLC and the 1689 Confession. The progression from revelation of God's nature → duty → conviction of sin → humiliation → need for Christ is preserved intact.

The concern about "binding" vs. "informing" is worth noting because it touches on a recurring debate about the nature of the moral law's authority. The Reformed tradition consistently teaches that the moral law does not merely inform or educate — it *obligates*. The 1689 19.5 states: "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof." This binding character is integral to the law's function. By reducing "binding" to "informing of duty," the BLC slightly weakens this emphasis, even if unintentionally.

That said, the phrase "their duty to live by it" does implicitly contain the notion of obligation — duty is inherently binding. The concern is one of precision and emphasis rather than outright theological error.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and closely track the WLC's references:
- **Romans 1:20; Leviticus 11:44-45; 20:7-8; Romans 7:12** (holy nature and will of God) — Appropriate. Romans 1:20 supports general revelation of God's nature; the Leviticus passages ground holiness in God's character; Romans 7:12 affirms the law is holy.
- **Micah 6:8; James 2:10-11; Romans 1:32** (duty to live by the law) — Good selections. Micah 6:8 summarizes moral duty; James 2:10-11 emphasizes the binding unity of the law; Romans 1:32 shows awareness of moral obligation. Notably, James 2:10-11 is cited in the 1689 (19.5) precisely in the context of the law's *binding* authority — which somewhat compensates for the softer language in the BLC's text.
- **Psalm 19:11-12; Romans 3:20; Romans 7:7** (sinful pollution and inability) — Standard and appropriate proof texts for the convicting function of the law.
- **Romans 3:9-23** (humbling in sin and misery) — Comprehensive passage on universal sinfulness; well-chosen.
- **Galatians 3:21-24; Romans 10:4** (need for Christ and His obedience) — Excellent. Galatians 3:21-24 is the locus classicus for the law as pedagogue leading to Christ; Romans 10:4 ("Christ is the end of the law") directly supports the perfection of His obedience.
