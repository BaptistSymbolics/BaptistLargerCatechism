# Q98: What are we especially taught by these words before me in the first commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q98 faithfully modernizes the core content of the Shorter Catechism and maintains strong solidarity with WLC Q106. However, the BLC adds a second section on mindfulness in God's service that, while drawn from the WLC's final clause, slightly expands the Shorter's scope. The BLC also splits the WLC's single answer into two sections, which slightly weakens the rhetorical connection between God's displeasure at idolatry and the positive duty of serving Him as in His sight. Additionally, the Shorter Catechism's proof text reference appears to contain a transcription error that the BLC corrects.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What are we especially taught by these words before me, in the first commandment?
> A. These words before me, in the first commandment teach us, that God, who seeth all things, taketh notice of and is much displeased with the sin of having any other god (Ex. 8:5, to the end).

- Omissions: None significant. The Shorter's core content — God sees all things, takes notice of, and is much displeased with having any other god — is fully preserved in the BLC's first section.
- Structural changes: The BLC splits the answer into two sections. The first section preserves the Shorter's content and adds an explicit statement about how disobeying the commandment "insolently provokes God" (which elaborates on the Shorter's "much displeased"). The second section adds content about being mindful of God's sight in His service, which is not present in the Shorter but is drawn from the WLC.
- The Shorter's proof text "Ex. 8:5, to the end" appears to be a transcription error for Ezekiel 8:5ff (which is the WLC's proof text). The BLC correctly uses "Ezekiel 8:5-18" and adds Psalm 44:20-21, both matching the WLC's footnotes.
- The BLC's phrase "takes special note of" faithfully modernizes the Shorter's "taketh notice of."
- The BLC's phrase "is very offended by" modernizes "is much displeased with."
- The BLC adds the explanatory phrase "These words emphasize then how important it is to obey this commandment and how disobeying it insolently provokes God," which captures the WLC's language about it being "an argument to dissuade from it, and to aggravate it as a most impudent provocation."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, "Of the Law of God"

The 1689 does not address the specific words "before me" in the first commandment. Chapter 19 treats the moral law generally:

> Paragraph 5: "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation."

> Paragraph 6: "Although true Believers be not under the Law, as a Covenant of Works, to be thereby Justified or condemned; yet it is of great use to them as well as to others: in that, as a Rule of Life, informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty, it directs and binds them, to walk accordingly; discovering also the sinfull pollutions of their Natures, Hearts and Lives..."

- Omissions: N/A — the 1689 does not treat this specific topic at the level of individual commandment phrases, so direct comparison is limited.
- Structural changes: None.
- The BLC's teaching is consistent with the 1689's emphasis on the perpetual binding authority of the moral law and God's authority as Creator. The BLC's stress on God's omniscience and displeasure at idolatry aligns with the 1689's broader theological framework regarding the moral law's binding force.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q106: What are we specially taught by these words before me in the first commandment?
> A. These words before me or before my face, in the first commandment, teach us, that God, who seeth all things, taketh special notice of, and is much displeased with, the sin of having any other God: that so it may be an argument to dissuade from it, and to aggravate it as a most impudent provocation: as also to persuade us to do as in his sight, whatever we do in his service.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this question does not touch baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology).
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance. The BLC faithfully captures the WLC's full content.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "or before my face" — The WLC's alternative rendering of the Hebrew is omitted. This is a minor stylistic simplification that does not alter theological content.
  - "that so it may be an argument to dissuade from it" — This explicit purpose clause is not directly stated in the BLC, though the BLC's "These words emphasize then how important it is to obey this commandment" captures a similar intent in modernized form.
  - "whatever we do in his service" — The BLC's second section says "as we are doing things in His service," which preserves this phrase.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Two-section structure**: The BLC splits the WLC's single continuous answer into two sections. The first section covers God's displeasure at idolatry (the negative/warning aspect). The second section covers doing all things as in God's sight (the positive/duty aspect). This separation is defensible pedagogically but slightly weakens the WLC's rhetorical unity where both points flow from a single consideration of God's omniscience.

2. **"Insolently provokes God"**: The BLC's phrase captures the WLC's "most impudent provocation" well. "Insolently" is an effective modernization of "impudent."

3. **"Just as mindful"**: The BLC's second section urges believers to be "just as mindful of the fact that God sees everything we do as we are doing things in His service." This is a sound paraphrase of the WLC's "to persuade us to do as in his sight, whatever we do in his service," though the WLC's "to persuade us" is slightly more active than the BLC's formulation.

4. **Proof text correction**: The Shorter Catechism's "Ex. 8:5" is almost certainly a transcription error for Ezekiel 8:5, as Exodus 8:5 is about the plague of frogs and has no relevance to the first commandment. The BLC correctly cites Ezekiel 8:5-18, matching the WLC. This is a commendable correction.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Ezekiel 8:5-18** (BLC section 1, WLC footnote 515): Ezekiel's vision of abominations in the temple — images of jealousy, elders worshipping idols, women weeping for Tammuz, men worshipping the sun. Directly relevant to God seeing and being provoked by idolatry. Excellent proof text.
- **Psalm 44:20-21** (BLC section 1, WLC footnote 515): "If we have forgotten the name of our God, or stretched out our hands to a strange god; Shall not God search this out? for he knoweth the secrets of the heart." Directly supports the teaching that God sees all things and takes note of idolatry.
- **1 Chronicles 28:9** (BLC section 2, WLC footnote 516): "Know thou the God of thy father, and serve him with a perfect heart and with a willing mind: for the LORD searcheth all hearts, and understandeth all the imaginations of the thoughts." Supports the positive duty of serving God mindful of His omniscience. Matches WLC's proof text exactly.

All proof texts are well-chosen and align with both the WLC's citations and the theological content of the answer. The BLC distributes them appropriately between its two sections.
