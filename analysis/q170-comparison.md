# Q170: Should the Lord's Supper be withheld from anyone who professes the faith and wants to come to it?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q170 faithfully captures the core substance of WLC Q173 and 1689 Confession 30.8 — that ignorant and scandalous persons must be barred from the table by the church's Christ-given authority until they are instructed and show reformation. However, the BLC introduces language not found in either standard ("spiritual ignorance," "scandalized the church") that subtly narrows the grounds for exclusion, and it omits the 1689's category of "ungodly persons" and its theological grounding of the bar in the unworthiness of partaking "without great sin." The BLC also drops the WLC's important deontic pairing "may and ought," replacing it with "should and must."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel.

The Shorter Catechism does not have a question specifically addressing the withholding of communion. The closest questions are:

> Q. Who are the proper subjects of this ordinance?
> A. They who have been baptized upon a personal profession of their faith in Jesus Christ, and repentance from dead works (Acts 2:41, 42).

> Q. What is required to the worthy receiving of the Lord's supper?
> A. It is required of them that would worthily partake of the Lord's supper, that they examine themselves of their knowledge to discern the Lord's body (1 Cor. 11:28, 29), of their faith to feed upon him (2 Cor. 13:5), of their repentance (1 Cor. 11:31), love (1 Cor. 10:16, 17), and new obedience (1 Cor. 5:7, 8), lest coming unworthily they eat and drink judgment to themselves (1 Cor. 11:28, 29).

The Shorter addresses the individual's responsibility to self-examine — knowledge, faith, repentance, love, and new obedience. BLC Q170 addresses the church's corporate responsibility to bar unworthy communicants. These are complementary rather than overlapping questions, so the BLC is functioning as a genuine Larger Catechism expansion here.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 30, Paragraph 8

> "All ignorant and ungodly persons, as they are unfit to enjoy communion with Christ; so are they unworthy of the Lords Table; and cannot without great sin against him, while they remain such, partake of these holy mysteries, or be admitted thereunto: yea whosoever shall receive unworthily are guilty of the Body and Blood of the Lord, eating and drinking judgement to themselves."

Also relevant: Chapter 26, Paragraphs 7 and 12

> (26.7) "To each of these Churches thus gathered, according to his mind, declared in his word, he hath given all that power and authority, which is any way needfull, for their carrying on that order in worship, and discipline..."

> (26.12) "As all Believers are bound to joyn themselves to particular Churches, when and where they have opportunity so to do; So all that are admitted unto the priviledges of a Church, are also under the Censures and Government thereof, according to the Rule of Christ."

- **Omissions:**
  1. **"Ungodly persons"** — The 1689 bars both "ignorant **and ungodly** persons." The BLC mentions those with "spiritual ignorance" and those whose "lives have scandalized the church," but "ungodly" is a broader category than "scandalous." A person may live an ungodly life inwardly (coldness, prayerlessness, worldliness) without having committed an outwardly scandalous act that shocks the church. The 1689's "ungodly" covers both inward and outward unholiness; the BLC's "scandalized the church" focuses only on the outward.
  2. **"Unfit to enjoy communion with Christ"** — The 1689 grounds the bar theologically: such persons are unfit for spiritual communion with Christ himself, not merely in violation of a church rule. The BLC drops this theological grounding.
  3. **"Cannot without great sin against him"** — The 1689 emphasizes the gravity of unworthy participation as sin against Christ. The BLC omits this warning entirely.
  4. **"Guilty of the Body and Blood of the Lord, eating and drinking judgement to themselves"** — The 1689 concludes with the full Pauline warning (1 Cor. 11:27, 29). The BLC omits this.

- **Structural changes:** The 1689 structures this doctrine in two movements: (1) the unfitness of such persons for communion with Christ, therefore (2) they cannot be admitted. The BLC restructures around the church's authority to exclude, rather than the person's spiritual unfitness. This shifts the emphasis from the theological gravity of the offense to the institutional mechanism of exclusion.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q173

> Q. 173. May any who profess the faith, and desire to come to the Lord's supper, be kept from it?
> A. Such as are found to be ignorant or scandalous, notwithstanding their profession of the faith, and desire to come to the Lord's supper, may and ought to be kept from that sacrament, by the power which Christ hath left in his church, until they receive instruction, and manifest their reformation.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC closely follows the WLC's structure and content. The question formulation, the two categories (ignorant/scandalous), the appeal to Christ's authority in the church, and the twofold resolution (instruction and reformation) are all preserved.

- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected on this topic, as this is not a question touching baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology in a way that would require Baptist modification. Both traditions affirm the church's authority to fence the table.

- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"May and ought" → "should and must"** — The WLC's "may and ought" is a carefully calibrated deontic pairing: "may" affirms the church has the *right* (permission/authority), while "ought" affirms the church has the *duty* (obligation). The BLC's "should and must" collapses this into near-synonyms that both express obligation, losing the WLC's affirmation of legitimate authority. "Should" and "must" differ only in degree of force, not in kind as "may" and "ought" do.
  2. **"Ignorant" → "spiritual ignorance"** — The WLC simply says "ignorant," covering all forms of ignorance about the faith. The BLC qualifies this as "spiritual ignorance," which could be read as narrowing the category. Someone ignorant of basic doctrine (e.g., the Trinity, the gospel) should be barred regardless of whether the ignorance is "spiritual" in some deeper sense.
  3. **"Scandalous" → "scandalized the church"** — The WLC says persons "found to be... scandalous," describing a character quality. The BLC says persons "whose lives have scandalized the church," which shifts from a description of the person to the effect on the community. This subtly raises the bar: one must have actually scandalized the church, not merely live a scandalous life.
  4. **"Profession of faith is based on"** — The BLC rephrases "notwithstanding their profession" as "whose profession of faith is based on spiritual ignorance." The WLC simply notes that these persons profess faith and desire to come, *notwithstanding which* they should be kept away. The BLC's formulation ("based on spiritual ignorance") is awkward — professions of faith are not typically described as "based on" ignorance.

- **Key phrases dropped:**
  1. **"Found to be"** — The WLC's phrase indicates a process of discovery/investigation. The BLC drops this procedural nuance.
  2. **"May and ought"** — See above.
  3. **"Receive instruction"** — The WLC says they must "receive instruction"; the BLC says "properly instructed." The BLC's passive construction is adequate but less precise about who does the instructing and the person's receptivity.
  4. **"Manifest their reformation"** — The BLC says "demonstrate by their behavior that they have reformed their lives." This is a faithful expansion, though wordier.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The question formulation** is slightly different from the WLC. The WLC asks "May any... be kept from it?" (asking about the right to exclude). The BLC asks "Should the Lord's Supper be withheld from anyone...?" (asking about the duty to exclude). The WLC's formulation is more precise because it opens the answer to address both right and duty.

2. **The BLC's proof texts** largely overlap with the WLC's footnoted references:
   - 1 Corinthians 11:27-31 (WLC cites 11:27-34)
   - Matthew 7:6 (shared)
   - 1 Corinthians 5 (shared)
   - Jude 23 (shared)
   - 1 Timothy 5:22 (shared)
   - 2 Corinthians 2:5-8 (WLC cites 2:7)
   - 1 Corinthians 5:4-5 (subset of shared 1 Cor 5)
   The proof text alignment is strong.

3. **The BLC adds "by the authority Christ has left in his church"** — This is a faithful modernization of the WLC's "by the power which Christ hath left in his church." The substitution of "authority" for "power" is acceptable and arguably more precise in modern English, where "power" can connote mere force rather than legitimate authority.

4. **The redemptive purpose of discipline** is well captured in the BLC's second section, which emphasizes the temporary and corrective nature of exclusion ("until they are properly instructed and demonstrate by their behavior that they have reformed their lives"). The proof texts (2 Cor 2:5-8; 1 Cor 5:4-5) appropriately support restoration.

## Scripture Analysis
**BLC Section 1 proof texts:**
- **1 Corinthians 11:27-31** — Core Pauline text on unworthy participation. Appropriate and foundational. The BLC cites a slightly narrower range than the WLC (which goes to v.34), missing the practical instructions about "tarrying for one another."
- **Matthew 7:6** — "Give not that which is holy unto the dogs..." Used by both WLC and the 1689 Confession (30.8n) for the principle of guarding sacred things. Appropriate.
- **1 Corinthians 5** — Paul's instructions on church discipline. Cited as a whole chapter, which is appropriate given the breadth of the passage. Shared with WLC.
- **Jude 23** — "Others save with fear, pulling them out of the fire." Supports the idea of cautious, fearful handling of those in spiritual danger. Shared with WLC.
- **1 Timothy 5:22** — "Lay hands suddenly on no man, neither be partaker of other men's sins." Supports caution in admitting people to church privileges. Shared with WLC.

**BLC Section 2 proof texts:**
- **2 Corinthians 2:5-8** — Paul's instructions on restoring the disciplined person at Corinth. Directly supports the restorative purpose of exclusion. The BLC cites a slightly broader range than the WLC footnote (which cites only 2:7).
- **1 Corinthians 5:4-5** — The specific instructions for the excommunication at Corinth, "for the destruction of the flesh, that the spirit may be saved." Supports the temporary, redemptive nature of discipline.

The proof texts are well-chosen and appropriate to the doctrinal claims. No problematic proof-texting is evident.
