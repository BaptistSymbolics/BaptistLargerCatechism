# Q169: Should those who have doubts about their position in Christ or about whether they are ready to take communion come to the Lord's Supper anyway?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q169 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q172, preserving all key theological elements: the possibility of true interest in Christ without assurance, the threefold condition (grief over lack of assurance, desire to be found in Christ, desire to depart from sin), the parenthetical ground in promises and the sacrament's design for weak Christians, and the dual obligation to bewail unbelief and labor to resolve doubts before coming. The BLC expands the structure into more discrete sections for clarity but does not alter the theological content. No direct Shorter Catechism parallel exists, but the BLC is consistent with its teaching on worthy receiving and the 1689 Confession's doctrine of assurance.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism does not address the specific case of doubting communicants. The closest questions are:

Q: "What is required to the worthy receiving of the Lord's supper?"
A: "It is required of them that would worthily partake of the Lord's supper, that they examine themselves of their knowledge to discern the Lord's body (1 Cor. 11:28, 29), of their faith to feed upon him (2 Cor. 13:5), of their repentance (1 Cor. 11:31), love (1 Cor. 10:16, 17), and new obedience (1 Cor. 5:7, 8), lest coming unworthily they eat and drink judgment to themselves (1 Cor. 11:28, 29)."

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel to evaluate against)
- Structural changes: N/A
- The Shorter's teaching on self-examination of faith is complementary to the BLC's teaching here. The BLC addresses the pastoral situation that arises when self-examination yields doubt rather than assurance — a natural extension of the Shorter's requirements.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 18 (Of the Assurance of Grace and Salvation), Paragraph 3: "This infallible assurance doth not so belong to the essence of faith, but that a true Believer, may wait long and conflict with many difficulties before he be partaker of it; yet being enabled by the Spirit to know the things which are freely given him of God, he may without extraordinary revelation in the right use of means attain thereunto: and therefore it is the duty of every one, to give all diligence to make their Calling and Election sure..."

Chapter 18, Paragraph 4: "True Believers may have the assurance of their Salvation divers ways shaken, diminished, and intermitted; as by negligence in preserving of it, by falling into som special Sin, which woundeth the Conscience, and grieveth the Spirit, by some sudden or vehement temptation, by Gods withdrawing the light of his countenance and suffering even such as fear him to walk in darkness and to have no light; yet are they never destitute of the seed of God, and Life of Faith, that Love of Christ, and the brethren, that sincerity of Heart, and Conscience of duty, out of which by the operation of the Spirit, this Assurance may in due time be revived: and by the which in the mean time they are preserved from utter despair."

Chapter 30 (Of the Lords Supper), Paragraph 7: "Worthy receivers, outwardly partaking of the visible Elements in this Ordinance, do then also inwardly by faith, really and indeed, yet not carnally, and corporally, but spiritually receive, and feed upon Christ crucified & all the benefits of his death..."

Chapter 30, Paragraph 8: "All ignorant and ungodly persons, as they are unfit to enjoy communion with Christ; so are they unworthy of the Lords Table; and cannot without great sin against him, while they remain such, partake of these holy mysteries, or be admitted thereunto..."

- Omissions: None significant. The BLC faithfully applies the 1689's distinction between assurance and faith (Ch. 18.3), its teaching that true believers may lack assurance while retaining true faith (Ch. 18.3-4), and the 1689's distinction between worthy and unworthy receivers (Ch. 30.7-8). The BLC implicitly distinguishes the doubting believer (who may have true interest in Christ) from the "ignorant and ungodly persons" of 30.8.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC correctly positions the doubting communicant as potentially having true faith (consistent with Ch. 18.3's teaching that assurance is not of the essence of faith) and directs them to the Lord's Supper as a means of strengthening that faith — consistent with 30.7's teaching that worthy receivers spiritually feed upon Christ.
- The BLC's use of Isaiah 50:10 and Psalm 88 as proof texts for the opening section directly mirrors the 1689's own proof texts for Ch. 18.3 (which cites the same passages for believers who "walk in darkness and have no light").

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q172: "May one who doubteth of his being in Christ, or of his due preparation, come to the Lord's supper?"
A: "One who doubteth of his being in Christ, or of his due preparation to the sacrament of the Lord's supper, may have true interest in Christ, though he be not yet assured thereof; and in God's account hath it, if he be duly affected with the apprehension of the want of it, and unfeignedly desires to be found in Christ, and to depart from iniquity: in which case (because promises are made, and this sacrament is appointed, for the relief even of weak and doubting Christians) he is to bewail his unbelief, and labor to have his doubts resolved; and, so doing, he may and ought to come to the Lord's supper, that he may be further strengthened."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- The BLC follows the WLC's structure and content very closely, preserving:
  1. The opening affirmation of possible true interest without assurance
  2. The threefold condition ("duly affected with the apprehension of the want," "unfeignedly desires to be found in Christ," "to depart from iniquity")
  3. The parenthetical ground (promises made, sacrament appointed for weak/doubting Christians)
  4. The duty to bewail unbelief and labor to resolve doubts
  5. The conclusion: "may and ought to come" for further strengthening

- Intentional Baptist divergences: The BLC says "communion" and "Lord's Supper" rather than "sacrament of the Lord's supper," consistent with Baptist reluctance toward sacramental language (though the BLC does use "sacrament" in the parenthetical section). This is a minor terminological adjustment, not a theological departure.

- Unexpected divergences: None significant. The BLC's modernization is faithful throughout.

- Key phrases dropped:
  - "in God's account hath it" — The BLC renders this as "In God's view, if such people are aware of and grieved by their lack of assurance," which preserves the theological substance while restructuring the syntax for clarity. The WLC's phrasing is more compressed ("in God's account hath it, if he be duly affected..."), making the conditional nature of the divine reckoning more tightly bound. The BLC separates this into distinct sections but preserves the same conditions.
  - "duly affected with the apprehension of the want of it" — The BLC renders this as "aware of and grieved by their lack of assurance," which captures the core meaning. The WLC's "duly affected" and "apprehension" carry a slightly richer pastoral nuance (being rightly moved by the awareness of lacking assurance), but the BLC's rendering is adequate.
  - "unfeignedly" — The BLC renders the desire to be found in Christ as "sincerely want to be found in Christ," which is a fair modernization of "unfeignedly desires."

## Detailed Notes
1. **Structural expansion:** The BLC breaks the WLC's single flowing answer into eight discrete sections, each with its own proof texts. This is a pedagogical choice that aids clarity without altering content. The WLC's answer is a single complex sentence; the BLC distributes it across sections while maintaining the logical flow.

2. **Proof text alignment:** The BLC uses substantially the same proof texts as the WLC:
   - Isaiah 50:10; 1 John 5:13; Psalm 88; Psalm 77:1-12; Jonah 2:4-7 (WLC: 1102 — Isaiah 50:10; Psalm 88; Psalm 77:1-12; Jonah 2:4)
   - Isaiah 54:7-10; Matthew 5:3-4; Psalm 31:22; Psalm 73:13, 22-23 (WLC: 1103 — same references)
   - Philippians 3:8-9; Psalm 10:17; Psalm 42:1-2, 5, 11 (WLC: 1104 — same references)
   - 2 Timothy 2:19; Isaiah 50:10; Psalm 66:18-20 (WLC: 1105 — same references)
   - Isaiah 40:11, 29, 31; Matthew 11:28; Matthew 12:20; Matthew 26:28 (WLC: 1106 — same references)
   - Mark 9:24 (WLC: 1107 — same)
   - Acts 2:37; Acts 16:30 (WLC: 1108 — same, BLC adds Acts 9:6)
   - Romans 4:11; 1 Corinthians 11:28 (WLC: 1109 — same, BLC adds Matthew 11:28)

3. **Addition of Romans 7:24-25:** The BLC adds this to the "want to get away from sinning" section. This is a fitting addition that illustrates the believer's struggle with remaining sin and desire for deliverance — appropriate to the context and consistent with Reformed theology.

4. **Addition of Acts 9:6:** The BLC adds Paul's conversion response ("Lord, what wilt thou have me to do?") to the "work hard to resolve their doubts" section alongside Acts 2:37 and 16:30. This is a reasonable expansion.

5. **The term "position in Christ":** The BLC modernizes "being in Christ" to "position in Christ." This is adequate, though "union with Christ" might have been a more theologically precise modernization. "Position" could be read in a merely positional/legal sense, while "being in Christ" in the WLC carries a richer union-with-Christ connotation. This is a very minor observation.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are almost entirely drawn from the WLC's own proof texts for Q172, demonstrating strong textual fidelity. The few additions (Romans 7:24-25; Acts 9:6; Matthew 11:28 in the final section) are all appropriate and consistent with the doctrinal point being made. The retention of the WLC's more unusual citations (Psalm 88, Jonah 2:4-7) for the experience of spiritual darkness is commendable. The addition of 1 John 5:13 in the opening section is fitting, as it addresses the purpose of assurance and implicitly acknowledges that not all believers possess it at all times.
