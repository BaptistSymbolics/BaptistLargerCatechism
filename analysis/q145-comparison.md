# Q145: How should the Word of God be read?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q145 is an extremely close modernization of WLC Q157, preserving all its elements faithfully. However, compared to the Shorter Catechism's parallel question, the BLC follows the WLC's structure (which addresses only reading) rather than the Shorter's structure (which addresses both reading and hearing together). The Shorter also contains several key phrases -- "preparation," "faith and love," "lay it up in our hearts," and critically the salvific framing "that it may become effectual to salvation" -- that the BLC omits. The omission of "preparation" and "faith and love" as distinct elements, and especially the loss of the soteriological purpose clause, weakens the Shorter's emphasis.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. How is the word to be read and heard, that it may become effectual to salvation?
> A. That the word may become effectual to salvation, we must attend thereunto with diligence (Pr. 8:34), preparation (1 Pet. 2:1, 2), and prayer (Ps. 119:18); receive it with faith and love (Heb. 4:2; 2 Thes. 2:10), lay it up in our hearts (Ps. 119:18), and practice it in our lives (Luke 8:15; James 1:25).

- **Omissions:**
  - **"that it may become effectual to salvation"** -- The Shorter's entire question is framed teleologically: reading/hearing Scripture has a specific saving purpose. The BLC drops this soteriological framing entirely. The BLC asks simply "How should the Word of God be read?" without reference to salvation as the goal. This is a significant omission of the Shorter's doctrinal emphasis.
  - **"preparation"** -- The Shorter lists preparation as a distinct element alongside diligence and prayer. The BLC has no equivalent. This is a meaningful loss, as preparation (putting away sins, 1 Pet. 2:1-2) is a distinct spiritual discipline from the acts of reading itself.
  - **"faith and love"** -- The Shorter says we must "receive it with faith and love." The BLC's closest parallel is "desire to know, believe, and obey His will," which captures belief but subsumes faith into a list rather than highlighting it as the mode of reception. "Love" as an affective response to the Word is absent.
  - **"lay it up in our hearts"** -- The Shorter's language of treasuring Scripture in the heart (Ps. 119:11) is lost. The BLC has "meditate on it" and "apply it to our lives," which are related but not equivalent to the internal treasuring/hiding of the Word.
  - **"read and heard"** -- The Shorter combines reading and hearing in one question. The BLC (following the WLC pattern) treats reading separately. The hearing of the Word preached is presumably addressed in a separate BLC question, so this is a structural choice rather than an omission, but it does mean this question lacks the Shorter's breadth.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC follows the WLC's more expansive list structure rather than the Shorter's compact formulation. This is understandable for a Larger Catechism but means the Shorter's distinctive emphases (preparation, faith and love, laying up in hearts) get lost in the restructuring rather than being preserved and expanded.
- **Agreement:** Both emphasize diligence/careful attention, obedience/practice, prayer, and the necessity of engaging Scripture actively.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 1, Paragraph 5:
> "We may be moved and induced by the testimony of the Church of God, to an high and reverent esteem of the Holy Scriptures; and the heavenliness of the matter, the efficacy of the Doctrine, and the Majesty of the stile, the consent of all the parts, the scope of the whole (which is to give all glory to God) the full discovery it makes of the only way of mans salvation, and many other incomparable Excellencies, and intire perfections thereof, are arguments whereby it doth abundantly evidence it self to be the Word of God; yet notwithstanding; our full perswasion, and assurance of the infallible truth, and divine authority thereof, is from the inward work of the Holy Spirit, bearing witness by and with the Word in our Hearts."

Chapter 1, Paragraph 6:
> "Nevertheless we acknowledge the inward illumination of the Spirit of God, to be necessary for the saving understanding of such things as are revealed in the Word..."

Chapter 22, Paragraph 5:
> "The reading of the Scriptures, Preaching, and hearing the word of God, teaching and admonishing one another in Psalms, Hymns and Spiritual songs, singing with grace in our Hearts to the Lord; as also the Administration of Baptism, and the Lords Supper are all parts of Religious worship of God, to be performed in obedience to him, with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear..."

- **Omissions:**
  - The 1689 22.5 specifies that reading Scripture is to be performed with "understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear." The BLC captures reverence but does not explicitly name "faith" or "godly fear" as dispositions for reading. The BLC's "absolutely convinced that it is truly God's Word" overlaps with faith but is not identical to reading *with faith* as a receptive posture.
  - The 1689 1.5's language about the Holy Spirit's "inward work... bearing witness by and with the Word in our Hearts" -- the internal testimony of the Spirit -- is richer than the BLC's "only He can enable us to understand it." The BLC captures dependence on God but flattens the Confession's specific doctrine of the Spirit's internal witness.
- **Structural changes:** None of significance. The BLC appropriately draws from multiple Confession paragraphs.
- **Agreement:** Strong alignment on: high and reverent esteem of Scripture (1.5), conviction it is God's Word (1.4-5), necessity of divine illumination (1.6), and reading as part of worship (22.5).

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> Q. 157. How is the Word of God to be read?
> A. The holy Scriptures are to be read with an high and reverent esteem of them; with a firm persuasion that they are the very Word of God, and that he only can enable us to understand them; with desire to know, believe, and obey the will of God revealed in them; with diligence, and attention to the matter and scope of them; with meditation, application, self-denial, and prayer.

- **Solidarity: STRONG**
- The BLC is a very close modernization of WLC Q157, preserving virtually every element:
  - "high and reverent esteem" = "high and reverent esteem" (identical)
  - "firm persuasion that they are the very Word of God" = "absolutely convinced that it is truly God's Word" (faithful modernization)
  - "he only can enable us to understand them" = "only He can enable us to understand it" (nearly identical)
  - "desire to know, believe, and obey the will of God revealed in them" = "desire to know, believe, and obey His will as revealed in the Bible" (faithful)
  - "diligence" = "pay careful attention" (adequate modernization)
  - "attention to the matter and scope of them" = "its content and the extent of its meaning" (faithful; "scope" rendered as "extent of its meaning")
  - "meditation" = "meditate on it" (identical concept)
  - "application" = "apply it to our lives" (faithful)
  - "self-denial" = "deny ourselves under its direction" (faithful, with helpful clarification)
  - "prayer" = "use it as a basis for our prayers" (slightly expanded -- see note below)
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None. This is a topic where Baptist and Presbyterian theology fully agree.
- **Unexpected divergences:** None of substance.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - **"diligence"** as a distinct element -- The WLC lists "diligence" as a separate virtue alongside "attention to the matter and scope." The BLC folds diligence into "pay careful attention," losing the distinction between the discipline of diligent reading (regularity, persistence) and attentiveness to content. This is minor but notable.
- **Minor modification:** The WLC's final element is simply "prayer." The BLC expands this to "use it as a basis for our prayers," which adds the idea of Scripture-shaped prayer. This is a legitimate expansion but subtly shifts from prayer *as a disposition for reading* (praying while/for reading) to prayer *informed by* reading. Both ideas are valid but the WLC's meaning is prayer as a companion to reading (asking God to open our understanding, cf. Ps. 119:18), while the BLC's phrasing emphasizes something slightly different.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Strongest feature:** The BLC's rendering of WLC Q157 is remarkably faithful -- one of the closest modernizations in the catechism. The ten sections break out the WLC's compact answer into digestible pieces while preserving every substantive element.

2. **The Shorter Catechism gap:** The primary concern is not with what the BLC says but with what it doesn't say relative to the Shorter. The Shorter's answer is compact but contains distinctive emphases:
   - The salvific framing ("effectual to salvation") orients the entire practice of reading toward its theological purpose. Without this, reading could appear as mere piety rather than a means of grace.
   - "Preparation" (1 Pet. 2:1-2 -- laying aside malice, guile, hypocrisy, envy, evil speaking) is a precondition the Shorter identifies before the act of reading. The BLC jumps straight to the posture during reading.
   - "Faith and love" as the mode of reception captures both the intellectual (faith) and affective (love) dimensions. The BLC has the intellectual but not the affective.
   - "Lay it up in our hearts" describes an internalization that goes beyond meditation and application.

3. **The BLC follows the WLC rather than the Shorter here.** Since the WLC is the structural model for the BLC, this is understandable. But the Shorter's distinctive content should ideally be preserved somewhere in the Larger Catechism's treatment of this topic, since the BLC is meant to exposit the Shorter's theology.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and largely appropriate for each section.
- Psalm 119:97 is cited for both "high and reverent esteem" and "meditate on it" -- appropriate for both uses.
- The proof texts for "deny ourselves under its direction" (Proverbs 3:5; Deuteronomy 33:3; Galatians 1:15) are somewhat unusual. Proverbs 3:5 ("Trust in the Lord... lean not on your own understanding") supports self-denial well. Deuteronomy 33:3 ("all his saints are in thy hand: and they sat down at thy feet; every one shall receive of thy words") is less directly on point. Galatians 1:15 (Paul's calling) is an unusual choice for self-denial in reading Scripture.
- The proof texts for "use it as a basis for our prayers" (Proverbs 2:1-6; Psalm 119:18; Luke 24:45) mix texts about prayer for understanding (Ps. 119:18) with texts about receiving wisdom (Prov. 2:1-6) and divine illumination (Luke 24:45). The WLC's simpler concept of "prayer" as praying for illumination while reading is better supported by Ps. 119:18 alone; the BLC's expanded concept of Scripture-based prayer would benefit from different proof texts.
- Notable: The Shorter cites 1 Pet. 2:1-2 for "preparation" and Heb. 4:2 for "faith." Neither verse appears in the BLC's proof texts, reflecting the substantive omission of these concepts.
