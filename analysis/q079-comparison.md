# Q79: What shall be done to the wicked at death?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q79 faithfully combines the Baptist Shorter Catechism's structure with the 1689 Confession's richer language on the intermediate state of the wicked. It preserves all key doctrinal elements from both standards — the immediate casting of wicked souls into hell at death, their ongoing torment, the body remaining in the grave, and the anticipation of final resurrection and judgment. The addition of "utter darkness" from the 1689 is a legitimate expansion that enriches the Shorter's baseline.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
Q. "But what shall be done to the wicked at their death?"
A. "The souls of the wicked shall, at their death, be cast into the torments of hell, and their bodies lie in their graves, till the resurrection and judgment of the great day." (Luke 16:23, 24; Acts 2:24; Jude 5, 7; 1 Pet. 3:19; Ps. 49:14)

- Omissions: None. All key elements are preserved: souls cast into hell at death, bodies in graves, awaiting resurrection and judgment.
- Structural changes: The BLC restructures slightly from "cast into the torments of hell" to "cast into hell, where they remain in torment and utter darkness." This shifts from "torments" as the destination to "hell" as the destination with torment as its character — a minor rephrasing that aligns with the 1689's wording and does not alter the theology.
- The Shorter says "torments" (plural); the BLC says "torment" (singular). This is a negligible stylistic difference.
- The BLC adds "where they remain" and "utter darkness," both drawn directly from the 1689 Confession. This is appropriate Larger Catechism expansion of the Shorter's content.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 31, Paragraph 1 — "The Bodies of Men after Death return to dust, and see corruption; but their Souls (which neither die nor sleep) having an immortal subsistence, immediately return to God who gave them: the Souls of the Righteous being then made perfect in holyness, are received into paradise where they are with Christ, and behold the face of God, in light and glory; waiting for the full Redemption of their Bodies; and the souls of the wicked, are cast into hell; where they remain in torment and utter darkness, reserved to the judgement of the great day; besides these two places for Souls separated from their bodies, the Scripture acknowledgeth none."

- Omissions: The BLC omits two elements from this paragraph: (1) the broader anthropological framing that souls "neither die nor sleep" and have "an immortal subsistence," and (2) the concluding clause "besides these two places for Souls separated from their bodies, the Scripture acknowledgeth none." Neither omission is problematic — the first pertains to general anthropology treated elsewhere, and the second is a polemical clarification against purgatory/soul sleep that would be unusual in catechetical form. The 1689's "reserved to" the judgment is softened to "until," losing a slight nuance of judicial custody but not the doctrine itself.
- Structural changes: None of significance. The BLC follows the 1689's order closely.
- The BLC's phrase "remain in torment and utter darkness" is taken almost verbatim from the 1689.
- The BLC adds "resurrection and" before "judgment of the great day," combining the 1689's "judgement of the great day" with the Shorter's "resurrection and judgment of the great day." This is a helpful addition that makes explicit what the Confession implies.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q86 (second half): "Whereas the souls of the wicked are at their death cast into hell, where they remain in torments and utter darkness, and their bodies kept in their graves, as in their prisons, till the resurrection and judgment of the great day."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC closely tracks the WLC's language on the intermediate state of the wicked. The WLC treats the wicked within Q86 (communion in glory with Christ immediately after death), where the fate of the wicked serves as a contrast. The BLC follows the Shorter in giving this its own dedicated question, which is structurally cleaner.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None applicable. This doctrine has no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology dimensions.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance. The only differences are minor stylistic choices.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's "as in their prisons" metaphor is absent from the BLC. However, this metaphor is also absent from both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession, so the BLC is following its primary Baptist standards rather than departing from the WLC. The WLC uses "torments" (plural) where the BLC has "torment" (singular) — negligible. The WLC's "their bodies kept in their graves" becomes "their bodies lie in their graves," matching the Shorter's phrasing.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC achieves an effective synthesis: it takes the Shorter's clean question-and-answer structure (dedicating a separate question to the wicked, rather than appending to the righteous as the WLC does) while enriching it with the 1689's descriptive language ("utter darkness," "where they remain").
- The doctrine of the intermediate state is fully preserved: immediate judgment of wicked souls at death (no soul sleep, no annihilation, no purgatory), conscious torment in hell, bodily resurrection still future.
- The 1689's "reserved to" carries a slight judicial connotation (cf. 2 Pet. 2:9, Jude 6) that "until" lacks, but this is a very minor loss of color rather than a doctrinal shift.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Luke 16:22-24** (BLC) vs **Luke 16:23, 24** (Shorter/1689): Same passage, the BLC adds v.22 for fuller context of the rich man's death and Lazarus — appropriate.
- **Psalm 49:14** — Present in both the BLC and the Shorter. Speaks of the wicked being appointed for Sheol with death as their shepherd.
- The Shorter includes additional texts not in the BLC: Acts 2:24 (Christ's resurrection breaking death's hold — more relevant to Q78 on believers), Jude 5, 7 (judgment on unbelievers), 1 Pet. 3:19 (spirits in prison). These are significant proof texts, particularly Jude 6-7 and 1 Pet. 3:19 which directly support the doctrine of wicked souls in conscious confinement. The BLC's narrower proof text selection is adequate but less comprehensive.
- The 1689 cites Jude 6-7, 1 Pet. 3:19, and Luke 16:23-24 for this doctrine. The BLC retains Luke 16 but omits Jude and 1 Peter — the former is the strongest text for the "reserved" language.
