# Q78: What benefits do believers receive from Christ at death?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q78 is substantially faithful to its standards, closely following WLC Q86 in structure and the 1689 Confession Chapter 31.1 in doctrine. However, it introduces expansive language ("received into the highest heavens," "fruits of their communion in glory with Christ") not present in the Shorter Catechism, and it omits the Shorter's key phrase "immediately pass into glory," replacing it with a more diffuse description. The phrase "rest in the grave as in a bed" follows the WLC verbatim but goes beyond what the 1689 and Shorter state (which say only "rest in their graves"), importing a pastoral metaphor that the Baptist standards do not use.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** Q. What benefits do believers receive from Christ at their death? A. "The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness (Heb. 12:23), and do immediately pass into glory (2 Cor. 5:1, 6, 8; Phil. 1:23; Luke 23:43); and their bodies being still united to Christ (1 Thess. 4:14), do rest in their graves (Is. 57:2) till the resurrection (Job 19:26, 27)."

- **Omissions:** The Shorter's phrase "do immediately pass into glory" is absent from the BLC. The BLC replaces this crisp, definitive statement with "being received into the highest heavens" — which conveys a similar idea but lacks the emphasis on *immediacy* that the Shorter explicitly states. The word "immediately" is a significant theological qualifier affirming the conscious, instant glorification of the soul against any notion of soul sleep or intermediate purgation.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter presents a clean two-part structure: (1) souls made perfect and immediately passing into glory, (2) bodies united to Christ resting in graves till resurrection. The BLC breaks this into seven sections, inserting additional material about "fruits of their communion in glory with Christ," beholding God's face, and the "bed" metaphor. This expansion comes from the WLC rather than the Baptist standards.
- **Agreement:** Both affirm perfection in holiness at death, continued union of the body with Christ, rest of the body in the grave, and reunion of body and soul. The core theology is preserved.
- **Other:** The Shorter says "till the resurrection" while the BLC says "until at the last day body and soul are reunited" — a faithful paraphrase.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 31, Paragraph 1 — "The Bodies of Men after Death return to dust, and see corruption; but their Souls (which neither die nor sleep) having an immortal subsistence, immediately return to God who gave them: the Souls of the Righteous being then made perfect in holyness, are received into paradise where they are with Christ, and behold the face of God, in light and glory; waiting for the full Redemption of their Bodies; and the souls of the wicked, are cast into hell; where they remain in torment and utter darkness, reserved to the judgement of the great day; besides these two places for Souls separated from their bodies, the Scripture acknowledgeth none."

- **Omissions:** (1) The 1689's phrase "immediately return to God who gave them" is not reflected — the BLC drops the concept of immediacy. (2) The 1689's affirmation that souls "neither die nor sleep" — an explicit anti-soul-sleep polemic — is absent from the BLC. (3) The 1689 says souls "are received into paradise where they are with Christ" — the BLC changes "paradise" to "the highest heavens," following the WLC rather than the 1689's own language. (4) The 1689's closing exclusion of any third place ("besides these two places for Souls separated from their bodies, the Scripture acknowledgeth none") is omitted. This is an anti-purgatory/limbus statement that the BLC does not preserve.
- **Structural changes:** The 1689 begins with the body returning to dust before treating the soul; the BLC begins with the soul and treats the body later. This is not doctrinally significant but follows the WLC's ordering rather than the Confession's.
- **Agreement:** The BLC preserves the 1689's affirmations of perfection in holiness, reception to God's presence, beholding the face of God in light and glory, and waiting for the full redemption of the body. The core eschatological teaching is intact.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q86: "What is the communion in glory with Christ, which the members of the invisible church enjoy immediately after death?" A. "The communion in glory with Christ, which the members of the invisible church enjoy immediately after death is, in that their souls are then made perfect in holiness, and received into the highest heavens, where they behold the face of God in light and glory, waiting for the full redemption of their bodies, which even in death continue united to Christ, and rest in their graves as in their beds, till at the last day they be again united to their souls. Whereas the souls of the wicked are at their death cast into hell, where they remain in torments and utter darkness, and their bodies kept in their graves, as in their prisons, till the resurrection and judgment of the great day."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC's structure and language are clearly modeled on WLC Q86. Nearly every phrase of the BLC can be traced to the WLC answer.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** (1) The WLC speaks of "members of the invisible church" — the BLC uses "believers," which is appropriate Baptist usage. (2) The WLC includes the contrasting section on the wicked — the BLC omits this, presumably treating it in a separate question (as the Shorter does).
- **Unexpected divergences:** (1) The WLC's question itself contains the word "immediately" ("enjoy immediately after death") — the BLC drops this significant adverb entirely. This is surprising because the WLC, the 1689, and the Shorter all emphasize immediacy. (2) The WLC's contrast section about the wicked (graves "as in their prisons") is omitted, though this may appear elsewhere in the BLC.
- **Key phrases dropped:** The word "immediately" — present in the WLC question, the 1689 Confession, and implicit in the Shorter's "immediately pass into glory" — is absent from the BLC. This is the most notable omission across all three standards.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The missing "immediately":** All three standards emphasize that the soul's glorification happens immediately at death. The Shorter says "immediately pass into glory," the 1689 says "immediately return to God," and the WLC frames its question around what believers "enjoy immediately after death." The BLC drops this word entirely. While the BLC's description implies immediacy (souls "being made completely holy" and "received into the highest heavens"), the explicit affirmation is lost. This adverb historically serves as a polemic against soul sleep (Anabaptist/psychopannychist errors) and purgatory (Roman Catholic). Its omission weakens the confessional precision.

2. **"Highest heavens" vs. "paradise":** The BLC follows the WLC's "highest heavens" rather than the 1689's "paradise where they are with Christ." Both are defensible, but the 1689's language echoes Christ's own words to the thief on the cross (Luke 23:43) and emphasizes the relational aspect (being "with Christ"). The BLC does cite Luke 23:43 in its proof texts, but its actual text follows the WLC phrasing.

3. **"As in a bed" metaphor:** The BLC includes "rest in the grave as in a bed" from the WLC. The Shorter says only "rest in their graves" and the 1689 does not use this metaphor at all for believers' bodies. This is a WLC addition — not objectionable theologically, but it goes beyond the Baptist standards.

4. **"Communion in glory" framing:** The BLC includes "which are fruits of their communion in glory with Christ," directly importing the WLC Q86's framing concept. This concept does have grounding in 1689 27.1 ("fellowship in his Graces, sufferings, death, resurrection, and glory"), but the specific phrase "communion in glory" as a category for death benefits comes from the WLC's structural framework.

5. **"Completely holy" vs. "perfect in holiness":** The BLC modernizes "made perfect in holiness" to "made completely holy." This is an acceptable modernization, though "perfect in holiness" carries a richer theological resonance (perfection language tied to Hebrews 12:23's "spirits of the righteous made perfect").

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and largely overlap with those of the standards:
- **Hebrews 12:23** — Shared with Shorter and 1689 for perfection in holiness. Appropriate.
- **Acts 7:55-59** — Added by the BLC (Stephen's vision). Not in the Shorter, 1689, or WLC for this question. A reasonable addition showing the saint beholding glory at death.
- **2 Corinthians 5:1-8; Philippians 1:23; Luke 23:43** — Core texts shared across all standards for the intermediate state.
- **Acts 3:21; Ephesians 4:10** — Added by the BLC for "highest heavens." These are not traditional proof texts for this doctrine. Acts 3:21 refers to Christ being received into heaven "until the time of restoring all things" — its application to believers' souls being received into heaven is a stretch. Ephesians 4:10 refers to Christ ascending "far above all the heavens" — again, this is about Christ's ascension, not the intermediate state of believers.
- **1 John 3:2; 1 Corinthians 13:12** — Standard texts for beholding God, shared with the WLC.
- **Romans 8:23; Psalm 16:9** — Appropriate for waiting/redemption of bodies.
- **1 Thessalonians 4:14-16** — Shared with the Shorter for union with Christ in death.
- **Isaiah 57:2** — Shared across standards for rest in the grave.
- **Job 19:26-27** — Shared across standards for bodily resurrection.

The proof texts for "highest heavens" (Acts 3:21; Ephesians 4:10) are the weakest in the set, being Christological texts applied to the believer's intermediate state without clear warrant.
