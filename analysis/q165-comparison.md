# Q165: What is the Lord's Supper?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q165 uses "sacrament" instead of the Shorter Catechism's "ordinance," departing from the primary Baptist standard without clear warrant. More critically, the BLC omits the Shorter's key qualification that receivers partake of Christ's body and blood "not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith" — a phrase the Shorter deliberately includes to guard against Roman Catholic and Lutheran errors, and which the 1689 Confession (30.7) extensively elaborates. The BLC also replaces the Shorter's "according to his appointment" with the weaker "as Christ directed," and changes the Shorter's precise "made partakers of his body and blood, with all his benefits" to "feed on his body and blood," omitting "with all his benefits."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What is the Lord's supper? A. The Lord's supper is an ordinance of the New Testament, instituted by Jesus Christ; wherein by giving and receiving bread and wine, according to his appointment, his death is shown forth, and the worthy receivers are, not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith, made partakers of his body and blood, with all his benefits, to their spiritual nourishment and growth in grace."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith"** — This is the most serious omission. The Shorter explicitly qualifies HOW believers partake of Christ's body and blood: spiritually, by faith, not carnally. The BLC drops this entire anti-sacramentalist qualification. This is a critical guard-phrase in Reformed Baptist theology.
  - **"with all his benefits"** — The Shorter says receivers are made partakers of Christ's body and blood "with all his benefits." The BLC drops "all his benefits," narrowing the scope of what is received.
  - **"worthy receivers"** — The Shorter specifies "worthy receivers" as the ones who are made partakers. The BLC replaces this with "those who receive the Lord's Supper in the right way," which is a reasonable modernization, though slightly less precise.
  - **"instituted by Jesus Christ"** — The Shorter explicitly names Christ as the institutor. The BLC omits this phrase entirely.
- **Structural changes:**
  - The Shorter's "ordinance" becomes the BLC's "sacrament." The Shorter Catechism — the primary Baptist standard — consistently uses "ordinance," not "sacrament." This is a significant terminological departure from the Baptist standard the BLC is meant to exposit.
  - The Shorter's "according to his appointment" (emphasizing Christ's sovereign institution) becomes "as Christ directed" (a weaker phrase suggesting mere instruction rather than authoritative appointment).
  - The Shorter integrates the manner of reception (by faith, not carnally) directly into the definition of the Lord's Supper. The BLC separates the act of proclamation from the act of feeding, and entirely drops the faith/carnal qualification.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 30 (Of the Lords Supper)

**30.1:** "The Supper of the Lord Jesus, was instituted by him, the same night wherein he was betrayed, to be observed in his Churches unto the end of the world, for the perpetual remembrance, and shewing forth the sacrifice of himself in his death; confirmation of the faith of believers in all the benefits thereof, their spiritual nourishment, and growth in him, their further ingagement in, and to, all duties which they owe unto him; and to be a bond and pledge of their communion with him, and with each other."

**30.7:** "Worthy receivers, outwardly partaking of the visible Elements in this Ordinance, do then also inwardly by faith, really and indeed, yet not carnally, and corporally, but spiritually receive, and feed upon Christ crucified & all the benefits of his death: the Body and Blood of Christ, being then not corporally, or carnally, but spiritually present to the faith of Believers, in that Ordinance, as the Elements themselves are to their outward senses."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"by faith, really and indeed, yet not carnally, and corporally, but spiritually"** — The 1689's careful language in 30.7 describing the manner of feeding is entirely absent from the BLC. This is the Confession's most important qualification on the Lord's Supper.
  - **"all the benefits of his death"** — The 1689 specifies that worthy receivers feed upon "Christ crucified & all the benefits of his death." The BLC drops both "crucified" and "all the benefits of his death."
  - **"perpetual remembrance"** — The 1689 names remembrance as a distinct purpose. The BLC subsumes this under "proclaim his death," which captures the showing-forth but loses the memorial emphasis.
  - **"confirmation of the faith of believers"** — The 1689 explicitly names confirmation of faith as a purpose. The BLC's "have their union and communion with Christ confirmed" is related but shifts from confirming faith to confirming union/communion.
  - **"further ingagement in, and to, all duties which they owe unto him"** — The BLC's "commitment to God" is a weak paraphrase that loses the Confession's specificity about duties owed to Christ.
  - **"bond and pledge"** — The 1689 calls the Supper "a bond and pledge of their communion." The BLC drops this covenantal language.
  - **"ordinance"** — The 1689 consistently uses "ordinance" throughout Chapter 30 (appearing 7 times). The BLC uses "sacrament," departing from the Confession's own terminology.
- **Structural changes:**
  - The 1689 separates the outward act (partaking of visible elements) from the inward act (by faith receiving and feeding spiritually). This structure emphasizes the spiritual nature of the feeding. The BLC collapses this distinction.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 168. What is the Lord's supper? A. The Lord's supper is a sacrament of the New Testament, wherein, by giving and receiving bread and wine according to the appointment of Jesus Christ, his death is showed forth; and they that worthily communicate feed upon his body and blood, to their spiritual nourishment and growth in grace; have their union and communion with him confirmed; testify and renew their thankfulness, and engagement to God, and their mutual love and fellowship each with the other, as members of the same mystical body."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC is clearly modeled on WLC Q168, following its structure almost exactly and adopting its "sacrament" terminology and expanded content beyond the Shorter's definition.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent — this is an area of expected agreement between Baptists and Presbyterians.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - **"according to the appointment of Jesus Christ"** → BLC changes to "as Christ directed to proclaim his death." The WLC's phrase "according to the appointment" is stronger and more precise than "as Christ directed." The BLC also inserts "to proclaim his death" into the giving/receiving clause, restructuring the WLC's sentence flow.
  - The WLC says "they that worthily communicate feed upon his body and blood." The BLC replaces "worthily communicate" with "receive the Lord's Supper in the right way," which is a reasonable modernization but loses the technical theological precision of "worthily communicate."
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - **"according to the appointment of Jesus Christ"** — weakened to "as Christ directed"
  - **"worthily communicate"** — replaced with less precise language
  - The WLC's "testify and renew" becomes the BLC's "publicly witness to and repeat anew" — this is a reasonable modernization.

## Detailed Notes

### The "sacrament" vs. "ordinance" problem
The BLC follows the WLC in using "sacrament" rather than the Shorter Catechism's "ordinance." While Reformed Baptists can use "sacrament" (and the 1689 uses both terms in Ch. 28.1), the Shorter Catechism — the BLC's primary Baptist catechetical standard — consistently uses "ordinance." Since the BLC is meant to exposit the Shorter and 1689, following the WLC's terminology over the Shorter's is a notable departure. The WLC naturally uses "sacrament" as a Presbyterian document; the BLC's adoption of this term over its own Baptist standard's "ordinance" is unexpected.

### The critical missing qualification: "by faith, not carnally"
Both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession are explicit that the manner of partaking is spiritual (by faith), not corporal or carnal. This is not a minor theological point — it is the Reformed Baptist distinctive on the Lord's Supper, distinguishing it from:
- Roman Catholic transubstantiation
- Lutheran consubstantiation
- Any physical/corporal presence view

The Shorter's compact formulation ("not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith") and the 1689's extensive treatment (30.7) both make this the central qualification of the doctrine. The BLC's complete omission of this language is a serious gap.

Notably, even the WLC itself does not include this qualification in Q168, instead devoting a separate question (Q170) to the manner of feeding. However, the Shorter Catechism — which the BLC should primarily mirror — integrates this qualification directly into the definition. The BLC should follow its Baptist standard here, not the WLC's separation of the topic.

### "with all his benefits"
Both the Shorter ("with all his benefits") and the 1689 ("all the benefits of his death") specify that what is received includes not just Christ's body and blood but all the benefits flowing from his death. The BLC's "feed on his body and blood and thereby are spiritually nourished and grow in grace" names only nourishment and growth, omitting the comprehensive scope of "all his benefits" (justification, adoption, sanctification, etc.).

## Scripture Analysis
- **Luke 22:20** — Supports "New Testament/covenant" language. Appropriate.
- **Matthew 26:26-28; 1 Corinthians 11:23-27** — Standard institution texts. Appropriate for the giving/receiving and death-proclamation elements. However, the Shorter also cites 1 Cor 10:16 in its proof text, which the BLC reserves for a later section on union/communion.
- **1 Corinthians 10:16** — Used for union and communion with Christ. Appropriate.
- **1 Corinthians 11:24** — Used for thankfulness. Appropriate.
- **1 Corinthians 10:14-16, 21** — Used for commitment to God. Reasonable, though these verses are more about exclusivity of worship than general commitment.
- **1 Corinthians 10:17** — Used for mutual love and fellowship as members of one body. Appropriate.
- Missing: No proof text is offered for the manner of feeding (by faith, not carnally), because the BLC omits this doctrine entirely. The Shorter and 1689 cite 1 Cor 10:16 and 1 Cor 11:23-26 for this purpose.
