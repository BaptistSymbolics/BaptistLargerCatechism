# Q181: For what should we pray?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q181 closely follows WLC Q184, preserving all four positive categories (glory of God, welfare of the church, our own good, others' good) and the negative qualification (nothing unlawful). However, the BLC adds "things that promote" before "the glory of God," subtly narrowing what the WLC and 1689 express more broadly as "all things tending to the glory of God" / "things lawful." The phrase "things that promote" could be read as limiting prayer to things that actively advance God's glory, whereas the standards' language ("tending to") encompasses a broader range including things consistent with or directed toward God's glory. More significantly, the BLC omits the 1689's explicit exclusion of prayer for the dead and for those who have sinned the sin unto death — a qualification the Confession treats as important enough to state explicitly.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism does not have a standalone question on what to pray for. The closest is the definition of prayer:

> "Q. What is prayer? A. Prayer is an offering up our desires to God, by the assistance of the Holy Spirit, for things agreeable to his will, in the name of Christ, believing, with confession of our sins, and thankful acknowledgments of his mercies." (Ps. 62:8; Rom. 8:26; 1 Jn. 5:14; Rom. 8:27; Jn. 16:23; Mt. 21:22; James 1:6; Ps. 32:5, 6; Dan. 9:4; Ph. 4:6)

- Omissions: The Shorter's phrase "things agreeable to his will" is a broader and more theologically precise formulation than BLC's enumerated categories. The BLC's answer breaks this general principle into specific categories (glory of God, welfare of the church, our own/others' good, nothing unlawful), which is appropriate for a Larger Catechism's expository function.
- Structural changes: None concerning. The BLC appropriately expands the Shorter's compressed treatment through specific categories, following the WLC pattern.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraph 4:

> "Prayer is to be made for things lawful, and for all sorts of men living, or that shall live hereafter; but not for the dead, nor for those of whom it may be known that they have sinned the sin unto death."

- Omissions: The 1689 explicitly excludes prayer **for the dead** and prayer **for those who have sinned the sin unto death**. The BLC's "not for anything that is unlawful" technically subsumes these exclusions (since praying for the dead would be unlawful), but the Confession's explicit naming of these categories reflects a deliberate anti-Roman Catholic polemic and a specific biblical warning (1 John 5:16) that the BLC's general language obscures. This is a meaningful loss of specificity, though note that the BLC's question addresses *what things* to pray for while these 1689 exclusions relate more to *for whom* to pray — a topic the WLC treats in Q183 and which may be addressed in a separate BLC question.
- Structural changes: The 1689's structure is "things lawful + for all sorts of men... but not for the dead/sin unto death." The BLC restructures around positive categories (glory of God, welfare of church, our/others' good) with a general negative qualifier. This is not a distortion but follows the WLC rather than the 1689's formulation.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q184:

> "Q. 184. For what things are we to pray? A. We are to pray for all things tending to the glory of God, the welfare of the church, our own or others, good; but not for anything that is unlawful."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected or present in this question (prayer content is not a Baptist-Presbyterian distinctive).
- Unexpected divergences: The BLC changes "all things tending to the glory of God" to "things that promote the glory of God." The WLC's "all things tending to" is broader — it encompasses anything that inclines toward or is consistent with God's glory. "Promote" carries a more active, instrumental sense that could inadvertently narrow the scope. The word "all" is also dropped. This is a subtle shift but may simply be modernization.
- Key phrases dropped: "all things tending to" replaced with "things that promote" — the loss of "all" and the shift from "tending to" to "promote" is a minor precision loss.

Note: WLC Q183 ("For whom are we to pray?") is a separate question that covers the categories of persons (whole church, magistrates, ministers, ourselves, brethren, enemies, all sorts of men living/hereafter, but not the dead nor those who have sinned the sin unto death). If the BLC does not have a parallel to Q183, then the 1689's exclusions regarding the dead and the sin unto death would be entirely unaddressed.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Promote" vs. "tending to"**: The WLC's "tending to" is theologically richer. We pray for daily bread not because it "promotes" God's glory in an active sense, but because it tends toward or is consistent with it. "Promote" could suggest only things that directly advance God's glory qualify, potentially excluding mundane but lawful requests. This is likely unintentional but worth noting.

2. **Loss of "all"**: The WLC says "all things tending to the glory of God." The BLC says "things that promote the glory of God." Dropping "all" slightly weakens the comprehensive scope.

3. **1689 exclusions**: The question of whether prayer for the dead and for those who have sinned the sin unto death is covered elsewhere in the BLC is important. If these exclusions appear in a BLC parallel to WLC Q183 ("For whom are we to pray?"), then the concern about omission from Q181 is minimal. If no such parallel exists, these explicit 1689 teachings would be entirely absent from the catechism.

4. **Overall structure preserved**: The four-part positive framework (God's glory, church's welfare, our good, others' good) plus the negative qualifier (nothing unlawful) faithfully mirrors the WLC structure.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Matthew 6:9** (glory of God): Appropriate — the Lord's Prayer begins with hallowing God's name.
- **Psalm 51:18; Psalm 122:6** (welfare of the church): Good selections — Ps 51:18 ("Do good to Zion") and Ps 122:6 ("Pray for the peace of Jerusalem") directly support praying for the church's welfare.
- **Matthew 7:11** (our own good): Suitable — God gives good things to those who ask.
- **Psalm 125:4; 1 Thessalonians 5:23; 2 Thessalonians 3:16** (others' good): Appropriate selections covering prayer for the righteous and apostolic prayers for others' sanctification and peace.
- **1 John 5:14; James 4:3** (nothing unlawful): Strong pairing — 1 John 5:14 ("according to his will") establishes the positive principle, and James 4:3 ("you ask amiss") warns against improper requests. Notably, 1 John 5:14 is used here for the "nothing unlawful" clause, but 1 John 5:16 (the sin unto death) — which is the proof text for the 1689's explicit exclusion — is not cited, further confirming that exclusion is absent from this question.
