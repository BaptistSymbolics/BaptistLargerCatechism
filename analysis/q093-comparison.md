# Q93: What is the preface to the Ten Commandments?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q93 closely follows the structure and content of WLC Q101 and faithfully exposits the theological substance of the Shorter Catechism's Q44-45. The BLC preserves all major doctrinal elements: God's sovereignty, aseity, covenant relationship, deliverance from bondage, and consequent obligation. However, the BLC omits the Shorter Catechism's explicit title "Redeemer" for God, replacing it with a description of deliverance from "spiritual enslavement." While the concept is retained, the loss of the specific title weakens the precision of the Shorter's compressed formulation.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

Q. What is the preface to the ten commandments?
A. The preface to the ten commandments is in these words; I am the Lord thy God which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage (Ex. 20:2).

Q. What doth the preface to the ten commandments teach us?
A. The preface to the ten commandments teacheth us that because God is the Lord, and our God and redeemer, therefore we are bound to keep all his commandments (Luke 1:74, 75; 1 Pet. 1:15-19).

- **Omissions:** The Shorter explicitly names God as "the Lord, and our God and **redeemer**" — a threefold designation. The BLC drops the title "Redeemer" and instead describes redemptive action ("He delivers us from spiritual enslavement"). The conceptual content is present, but the Shorter's concise title "Redeemer" is a loaded theological term that carries christological and soteriological weight beyond the mere description of deliverance activity.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter gives a compact threefold ground for obedience: (1) God is the Lord, (2) our God, (3) our Redeemer — therefore we keep His commandments. The BLC expands this into an extended exposition that unpacks each element across multiple sections, which is appropriate for a Larger Catechism. No problematic structural rearrangement.
- The BLC's final section ("we are bound to accept only Him as our God and to keep all His commandments") faithfully mirrors the Shorter's conclusion, though "accept only Him as our God" adds specificity not in the Shorter (though consistent with the First Commandment's implications).

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God), Paragraph 5: "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation."

Chapter 7 (Of God's Covenant), Paragraph 1: "The distance between God and the Creature is so great, that although reasonable Creatures do owe obedience unto him as their Creator, yet they could never have attained the reward of Life, but by some voluntary condescension on Gods part, which he hath been pleased to express, by way of Covenant."

Chapter 7, Paragraph 2: "Moreover Man having brought himself under the curse of the Law by his fall, it pleased the Lord to make a Covenant of Grace wherein he freely offereth unto Sinners, Life and Salvation by Jesus Christ, requiring of them Faith in him, that they may be saved..."

Chapter 2 (Of God and of the Holy Trinity), Paragraph 1: "The Lord our God is but one only living, and true God; whose subsistence is in and of himself, infinite in being, and perfection... immutable... eternal... Almighty..."

- **Omissions:** None significant. The BLC's treatment of God's attributes (eternal, unchangeable, almighty, self-existent) aligns with 1689 Chapter 2. The covenantal framing aligns with Chapter 7. The obligation to obedience aligns with Chapter 19.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC appropriately roots the obligation to keep the commandments in both God's sovereign nature and His covenantal-redemptive relationship with His people, which is consistent with the 1689's treatment of the law's binding authority (Ch. 19.5) grounded in "the authority of God the Creator."
- The BLC's language about God's "covenantal relationship with the Israelites in ancient times and with all His people" is consistent with the 1689's understanding of the one covenant of grace progressively revealed.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q101: What is the preface to the ten commandments?
A. The preface to the ten commandments is contained in these words, I am the Lord thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage. Wherein God manifesteth his sovereignty, as being JEHOVAH, the eternal, immutable, and almighty God; having his being in and of himself, and giving being to all his words and works: and that he is a God in covenant, as with Israel of old, so with all his people; who, as he brought them out of their bondage in Egypt, so he delivereth us from our spiritual thraldom; and that therefore we are bound to take him for our God alone, and to keep all his commandments.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None. This question involves no baptismal, ecclesiological, or covenant theology issues that would require Baptist departure.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC says God "manifesteth his sovereignty, as being JEHOVAH" — the BLC renders this as "God reveals His sovereign being as LORD, in and of Himself." The BLC's phrasing subtly shifts from God manifesting His sovereignty *as being JEHOVAH* (the name itself conveys the sovereignty) to God revealing His sovereign *being* as LORD. This is a minor rewording that preserves the substance but slightly alters the emphasis from the divine name to the divine being.
  - The WLC says "giving being to all his words and works" as a single clause. The BLC splits this into two sections: "who gives being to all His words" (Exodus 6:3) and "and works" (Acts 17:24-28). This splitting is merely structural for catechetical purposes and does not alter meaning.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - The WLC's "as being JEHOVAH" — the BLC uses "as LORD" which is a legitimate modernization (JEHOVAH → LORD following modern Bible translation conventions). Not a theological loss.
  - No other substantive WLC phrases are dropped. The BLC tracks the WLC answer very closely, almost clause-by-clause.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Clause-by-clause WLC tracking:** The BLC follows the WLC's answer with remarkable fidelity:
   - WLC: "God manifesteth his sovereignty, as being JEHOVAH" → BLC: "God reveals His sovereign being as LORD"
   - WLC: "the eternal, immutable, and almighty God" → BLC: "eternal, unchangeable, and almighty God"
   - WLC: "having his being in and of himself" → BLC: "in and of Himself"
   - WLC: "giving being to all his words and works" → BLC: "who gives being to all His words... and works"
   - WLC: "a God in covenant, as with Israel of old, so with all his people" → BLC: "His covenantal relationship with the Israelites in ancient times and with all His people"
   - WLC: "he delivereth us from our spiritual thraldom" → BLC: "He delivers us from spiritual enslavement"
   - WLC: "bound to take him for our God alone, and to keep all his commandments" → BLC: "bound to accept only Him as our God and to keep all His commandments"

2. **The "Redeemer" question:** The Shorter Catechism's Q45 explicitly names God as "our God and redeemer." The WLC Q101 does not use the title "Redeemer" either, instead describing the redemptive act ("delivereth us from our spiritual thraldom"). So the BLC's omission of the explicit title "Redeemer" follows the WLC pattern rather than the Shorter. Since the BLC is a *Larger* Catechism, following the WLC's expanded-description approach rather than the Shorter's compressed-title approach is reasonable, but the Shorter's explicit theological title is weightier than a mere description of activity.

3. **"Immutable" vs. "unchangeable":** The BLC modernizes "immutable" to "unchangeable." This is a straightforward vocabulary update with no theological loss.

4. **"Thraldom" vs. "enslavement":** The BLC modernizes "thraldom" to "enslavement." Again, pure vocabulary update.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts closely mirror the WLC's:
- **Exodus 20:2** — Identical citation for the preface text itself.
- **Exodus 3:14** — Both cite for God's aseity/self-existence. The WLC assigns this to "having his being in and of himself"; the BLC to "His sovereign being as LORD, in and of Himself."
- **Isaiah 44:6** — Both cite for divine attributes. WLC uses it for sovereignty; BLC for "eternal, unchangeable, and almighty."
- **Exodus 6:3** — Both cite for "giving being to all his words." Identical usage.
- **Acts 17:24-28** — Both cite for "works." The BLC cites 17:24-28 where the WLC cites 17:24, 28. Minor expansion.
- **Genesis 17:7; Romans 3:29** — Both cite for covenant relationship. Identical.
- **Luke 1:74-75** — Both cite for deliverance from spiritual bondage. Identical.
- **1 Peter 1:15-18; Leviticus 18:30; Leviticus 19:37** — Both cite for the obligation to obedience. The WLC cites 1 Peter 1:15, 17-18 and the same Leviticus passages. Nearly identical.

The proof text alignment is very strong, indicating deliberate and faithful dependence on the WLC tradition.
