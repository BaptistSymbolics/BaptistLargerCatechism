# Q174: In what ways do the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's Supper differ?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q174 closely follows WLC Q177 in structure and content, appropriately removing paedobaptist elements and substituting "repeatedly" for "often." However, the BLC describes baptism as a sign and seal of "our regeneration and engrafting into Christ" while the 1689 Confession's description of baptism is broader—including fellowship in Christ's death and resurrection, engrafting, remission of sins, and giving up unto God to walk in newness of life. The BLC also uses "represent and display" for the Lord's Supper where the WLC uses the stronger "represent and exhibit," and the 1689 speaks of "confirmation of the faith of believers in all the benefits thereof" while the BLC says only "confirm our continuing growth in Him," omitting the phrase "all the benefits thereof."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not include a question comparing how baptism and the Lord's Supper differ. It treats them in separate questions:

- "Q. What is baptism? A. Baptism is an ordinance of the New Testament instituted by Jesus Christ, to be unto the party baptized a sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, burial, and resurrection; of his being ingrafted into him; of remission of sins; and of his giving up himself unto God through Jesus Christ, to live and walk in newness of life (Rom. 6:3, 4, 5; Col. 2:12; Gal. 3:27; Mk. 1:4; Acts 2:38, and 22:16; Rom. 6:3, 4)."

- "Q. What is the Lord's supper? A. The Lord's supper is an ordinance of the New Testament, instituted by Jesus Christ; wherein by giving and receiving bread and wine, according to his appointment, his death is shown forth, and the worthy receivers are, not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith, made partakers of his body and blood, with all his benefits, to their spiritual nourishment and growth in grace (Mt. 26:26, 27, 28; 1 Cor. 11:23-26; 10:16)."

- Omissions: The BLC's description of baptism narrows the Shorter's fuller list ("sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, burial, and resurrection; of his being ingrafted into him; of remission of sins; and of his giving up himself unto God...to live and walk in newness of life") down to just "a sign and seal of our regeneration and engrafting into Christ." While "regeneration" may be intended to summarize some of these elements, it omits explicit mention of fellowship in Christ's death/burial/resurrection, remission of sins, and self-dedication to God. For the Lord's Supper, the Shorter's language about worthy receivers being "made partakers of his body and blood, with all his benefits" is reduced to "represent and display Christ as spiritual food for the soul."
- Structural changes: The BLC consolidates what the Shorter treats in separate questions into a single comparative answer, following the WLC's structure. This is appropriate for a Larger Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 29 (Of Baptism), Paragraph 1: "Baptism is an Ordinance of the New Testament, ordained by Jesus Christ, to be unto the party Baptized, a sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, and resurrection; of his being engrafted into him; of remission of sins; and of his giving up unto God through Jesus Christ to live and walk in newness of Life."

Chapter 30 (Of the Lord's Supper), Paragraph 1: "The Supper of the Lord Jesus, was instituted by him, the same night wherein he was betrayed, to be observed in his Churches unto the end of the world, for the perpetual remembrance, and shewing forth the sacrifice of himself in his death; confirmation of the faith of believers in all the benefits thereof, their spiritual nourishment, and growth in him, their further ingagement in, and to, all duties which they owe unto him; and to be a bond and pledge of their communion with him, and with each other."

- Omissions (Baptism): The 1689 describes baptism as a sign of four things: (1) fellowship in Christ's death and resurrection, (2) engrafting into him, (3) remission of sins, (4) giving up unto God to walk in newness of life. The BLC reduces this to "regeneration and engrafting into Christ." While engrafting is preserved, the other three elements are compressed into the single term "regeneration," which is not the 1689's own framing. The 1689 does not use the word "regeneration" in its baptism chapter.
- Omissions (Lord's Supper): The 1689 lists five purposes: (1) perpetual remembrance and showing forth Christ's death, (2) confirmation of the faith of believers in all the benefits thereof, (3) spiritual nourishment and growth in him, (4) further engagement in duties owed to him, (5) bond and pledge of communion with him and with each other. The BLC captures spiritual nourishment (#3) and partially captures confirmation/growth (#2/#3), but omits remembrance of Christ's death (#1), engagement in duty (#4), and communion bond (#5).
- Structural changes: The BLC uses "sign and seal" for baptism where the 1689 uses only "sign." The term "seal" is imported from the WLC's language. While Reformed Baptists often use this terminology, the 1689 itself does not apply "seal" to baptism.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q177: "Wherein do the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's supper differ? A. The sacraments of baptism and the Lord's supper differ, in that baptism is to be administered but once, with water, to be a sign and seal of our regeneration and ingrafting into Christ, and that even to infants; whereas the Lord's supper is to be administered often, in the elements of bread and wine, to represent and exhibit Christ as spiritual nourishment to the soul, and to confirm our continuance and growth in him, and that only to such as are of years and ability to examine themselves."

- Solidarity: STRONG
- Intentional Baptist divergences:
  - Removal of "and that even to infants" (expected — Baptists reject paedobaptism)
  - Removal of "and that only to such as are of years and ability to examine themselves" (the corresponding restriction). This removal is appropriate since Baptists already restrict baptism to professing believers, making the age qualification on the Supper less necessary as a distinguishing mark.
  - "Repeatedly" substituted for "often" (minor stylistic choice)
- Unexpected divergences:
  - "represent and display" replaces "represent and exhibit." "Exhibit" is a stronger theological term meaning to present or offer for partaking, while "display" has a more passive, visual connotation. This weakens the Reformed understanding of sacramental efficacy.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "continuance and" from "continuance and growth in him" — the BLC says "continuing growth in Him" which may intend to capture the same idea but shifts from two concepts (continuance + growth) to one modified concept (continuing growth). This is minor.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Regeneration" as a summary term:** The BLC's baptism section follows the WLC rather than the 1689 in describing baptism as a sign of "regeneration and engrafting into Christ." The 1689 does not use "regeneration" in its baptism chapter; it instead speaks of fellowship in Christ's death and resurrection, engrafting, remission of sins, and self-dedication. The WLC's use of "regeneration" to summarize is defensible but represents the WLC's theological shorthand rather than the 1689's own language.

2. **"Display" vs. "exhibit":** In Reformed sacramental theology, "exhibit" carries the sense of presenting or offering Christ and his benefits to the faithful recipient, not merely showing them. "Display" loses this participatory dimension, suggesting a visual presentation rather than an effective conveyance. This is a subtle but meaningful weakening.

3. **Missing remembrance dimension:** Neither the BLC nor the WLC Q177 explicitly mentions the Lord's Supper as a "perpetual remembrance" and "shewing forth the sacrifice of himself in his death," which the 1689 30.1 places first among the purposes. The WLC also omits this from Q177 (though it covers it in Q168-170), so the BLC is following its WLC source, but the omission is still notable in light of the 1689's emphasis.

4. **"Sign and seal" terminology:** The BLC follows the WLC in calling baptism a "sign and seal" while the 1689 29.1 says only "sign." This is a point where the BLC follows WLC language rather than the 1689's own formulation. Many Reformed Baptists accept "sign and seal" language, but it is worth noting the 1689 itself does not use it for baptism.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Matthew 3:11; Titus 3:5; Galatians 3:27** (baptism section): Matt 3:11 (water baptism vs. Spirit baptism) is appropriate. Titus 3:5 ("washing of regeneration") supports the "regeneration" language. Gal 3:27 ("baptized into Christ") supports engrafting. The proof texts tilt toward the WLC's "regeneration" framing rather than the 1689's broader description, which cites Rom. 6:3-5, Col. 2:12, Gal. 3:27, Mk. 1:4, Acts 2:38.
- **1 Corinthians 11:23-26; Colossians 2:19** (Lord's Supper as spiritual food): 1 Cor 11:23-26 is standard and appropriate. Colossians 2:19 ("the Head, from which all the body...grows with a growth that is from God") is an interesting choice that supports the "spiritual food for the soul" language but is not typically cited in this context by the 1689 or WLC.
- **1 Corinthians 10:16; John 6:51-53** (continuing growth): 1 Cor 10:16 ("communion of the blood/body of Christ") is standard. John 6:51-53 supports the "spiritual food" imagery well but is debated regarding its direct application to the Lord's Supper.
