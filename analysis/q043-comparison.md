# Q43: How does Christ execute the office of a prophet?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q43 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q43, preserving all essential theological content from the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession. The answer correctly identifies the key elements of Christ's prophetic office: revelation to the Church, through Spirit and Word, across all ages, in various modes, covering the complete will of God, for edification and salvation. Minor simplifications ("various ways" for "divers ways of administration") are stylistic rather than theological.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. How doth Christ execute the office of a prophet?
> A. Christ executeth the office of prophet in revealing to us, by his word and Spirit, the will of God for our salvation. (John 1:18; 1 Pet. 1:10, 11, 12; John 15:15; and 20:31)

- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves all core content from the Shorter.
- Structural changes: None that alter meaning. The BLC appropriately expands the Shorter's compact formulation.
- The Shorter's "to us" becomes "to the Church" — matching the WLC and giving the proper corporate/ecclesial dimension.
- The Shorter's "the will of God" becomes "the complete will of God" — a strengthening, matching the WLC's "the whole will of God."
- The Shorter's "for our salvation" is expanded to "for their edification and salvation" — matching the WLC's fuller purpose clause and adding the important concept of ongoing edification alongside salvation.
- The BLC adds "in various ways" and "throughout all ages" — appropriate expansions for a Larger Catechism, both drawn from the WLC.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 8, Paragraph 8:
> "To all those for whom Christ hath obtained eternal redemption, he doth certainly, and effectually apply, and communicate the same; making intercession for them, uniting them to himself by his spirit, revealing unto them, in and by the word, the mystery of salvation; perswading them to believe, and obey; governing their hearts by his word and spirit, and overcoming all their enemies by his Almighty power, and wisdom; in such manner, and wayes as are most consonant to his wonderful, and unsearchable dispensation."

Chapter 8, Paragraph 10:
> "This number and order of Offices is necessary; for in respect of our ignorance, we stand in need of his prophetical Office; and in respect of our alienation from God, and imperfection of the best of our services, we need his Priestly office, to reconcile us, and present us acceptable unto God: and in respect of our averseness, and utter inability to return to God, and for our rescue, and security from our spiritual adversaries, we need his Kingly office, to convince, subdue, draw, uphold, deliver, and preserve us to his Heavenly Kingdome."

- Omissions: None of substance. The 1689 does not provide a discrete catechetical definition of the prophetic office but rather weaves it into the broader mediatorial work (8.8) and states its necessity (8.10). The BLC's formulation is fully compatible with both.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's structure — revelation through Spirit and Word of the will of God — aligns with 8.8's "revealing unto them, in and by the word, the mystery of salvation."
- The 1689's phrase "the mystery of salvation" (8.8) corresponds well to the BLC's "the complete will of God for their edification and salvation."
- The 1689's emphasis on Christ's work "in such manner, and wayes as are most consonant to his wonderful, and unsearchable dispensation" is reflected in the BLC's "in various ways."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q43. How doth Christ execute the office of a prophet?
> A. Christ executeth the office of a prophet, in his revealing to the church, in all ages, by his Spirit and Word, in divers ways of administration, the whole will of God, in all things concerning their edification and salvation.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question addresses Christology, not ecclesiology, baptism, or covenant theology, so no Baptist divergences are expected or present.
- Unexpected divergences: None of theological significance.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "of administration" — The WLC says "in divers ways of administration" while the BLC simplifies to "in various ways." This is a minor stylistic simplification. The word "administration" (referring to different dispensational modes of revelation — prophets, types, visions, the incarnate Word, apostolic teaching, etc.) adds precision but is not theologically essential since "various ways" still captures the concept.
  - "in all things concerning" — The WLC says "in all things concerning their edification and salvation" while the BLC simplifies to "for their edification and salvation." The WLC's phrasing emphasizes comprehensiveness (all things pertaining to these ends), while the BLC's "for" states the purpose. This is a very slight reduction in emphasis on comprehensiveness, but "the complete will of God" earlier in the answer already carries that comprehensive force.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC follows the WLC's word order "Spirit and Word" rather than the Shorter's "word and Spirit." Both orders are theologically equivalent, but the WLC/BLC order may slightly emphasize the Spirit's role as the agent who makes the Word effectual.
- The BLC's "complete will of God" is a natural modernization of the WLC's "whole will of God," preserving the same meaning.
- The structural flow of the BLC answer is clear and well-ordered: recipient (the Church) → mode (various ways) → temporal scope (throughout all ages) → means (Spirit and Word) → content (complete will of God) → purpose (edification and salvation). This slightly reorders the WLC's sequence but does not alter theological relationships.
- The shift from the Shorter's personal "us"/"our" to the corporate "the Church"/"their" is appropriate for a Larger Catechism treatment and matches the WLC exactly.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts align well with both the Shorter and the WLC:
- **John 15:15; John 1:18** — Christ as the revealer of the Father's will, corresponding to WLC footnotes 166 and 169.
- **Hebrews 1:1-2** — "Various ways" of divine communication, corresponding to WLC footnote 168 ("in divers ways of administration").
- **1 Peter 1:10-12** — Revelation "throughout all ages" by the Spirit, corresponding to WLC footnote 167 and the Shorter's proof text.
- **Acts 20:32; Ephesians 4:11-13; John 20:31** — The purposes of edification and salvation, corresponding to WLC footnote 170.
- The BLC uses essentially the same proof texts as both the Shorter and the WLC, with minor rearrangement to match the modernized text flow. No proof texts are missing or misapplied.
