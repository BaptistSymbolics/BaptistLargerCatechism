# Q20: What are God's works of providence?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q20 faithfully preserves the Shorter Catechism's core definition of providence as God's holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing of all creatures and their actions. The BLC appropriately expands on the Shorter by adding the telic clause "to His own glory" drawn from both the WLC and the 1689 Confession. The only notable difference from the WLC is the omission of the phrase "ordering them," but this is a minor stylistic simplification that does not alter the theological content, since the concept of ordering is subsumed under "governing."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What are God's works of providence?
> A. God's works of providence are his most holy, (Ps. 145:17;) wise (Is. 28:29, Ps. 104:24), and powerful preserving (Heb. 1:3) and governing all his creatures, and all their actions (Ps. 103:19; Mt. 10:29, 30, 31).

- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves every element of the Shorter's answer.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC follows the Shorter's structure exactly.
- The BLC adds "to His own glory" as a telic clause not present in the Shorter. This is a legitimate expansion for a Larger Catechism, drawing on the WLC parallel and the 1689 Confession (Ch. 5.1), which speaks of providence operating "to the praise of the glory of his wisdom, power, justice, infinite goodness and mercy." This addition enriches without contradicting the Shorter.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 5, Paragraph 1:
> "God the good Creator of all things, in his infinite power, and wisdom, doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all Creatures, and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence, to the end for the which they were Created; according unto his infallible foreknowledge, and the free and immutable Councel of his own will; to the praise of the glory of his wisdom, power, justice, infinite goodness and mercy."

- Omissions: The 1689 uses four verbs ("uphold, direct, dispose, and govern") while the BLC uses two activities ("preserving and governing"). This is standard catechetical simplification consistent with both the Shorter and WLC, and is not a doctrinal omission — "preserving" encompasses upholding, and "governing" encompasses directing, disposing, and governing. The 1689's references to "infallible foreknowledge" and "the free and immutable Councel of his own will" are not included, but these are elaborations appropriate to a confession rather than a catechism definition. The Confession's specification "from the greatest even to the least" is also absent, but the BLC's "all His creatures and all their actions" captures the same universality.
- Structural changes: None of theological significance.
- The BLC's "to His own glory" correctly reflects the 1689's "to the praise of the glory of his wisdom, power, justice, infinite goodness and mercy" in summary form.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q18: What are God's works of providence?
> A. God's works of providence are his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures; ordering them, and all their actions, to his own glory.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC closely tracks the WLC's structure, vocabulary, and theological content.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. Providence is not an area of Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's "ordering them" is omitted. In the WLC, "ordering them, and all their actions, to his own glory" has a slightly different syntactic structure from the BLC's "of all His creatures and all their actions to His own glory." The WLC distinguishes between governing the creatures and ordering both them and their actions toward glory; the BLC collapses this into a single governing clause directed toward glory. This is a minor stylistic difference. The concept of "ordering" is semantically contained within "governing," so no theological content is lost.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC question number (Q20) differs from the WLC parallel (Q18), reflecting the BLC's different catechetical structure leading up to this point.
- The BLC's addition of "to His own glory" relative to the Shorter is a textbook example of appropriate Larger Catechism expansion — it draws on confessional teaching to enrich the catechetical answer without contradicting the shorter form.
- The BLC includes Leviticus 21:8 as a proof text for holiness alongside Psalm 145:17, and adds several additional proof texts beyond the Shorter (Genesis 45:7, Psalm 135:6, Psalm 36:6, Nehemiah 9:6, Isaiah 63:14). These expand the scriptural support without introducing foreign theological concepts.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Holiness:** Psalm 145:17 (shared with Shorter/WLC); Leviticus 21:8 (BLC addition — God's holiness in a ceremonial context; appropriate but less directly relevant to providence than Ps. 145:17).
- **Wisdom:** Psalm 104:24 and Isaiah 28:29 (shared with Shorter).
- **Preserving:** Hebrews 1:3 (shared with Shorter/WLC); Psalm 36:6 and Nehemiah 9:6 (BLC additions — both strong texts on God's preservation of creatures).
- **Governing:** Psalm 103:19 (shared with WLC); Psalm 145:14-16 and Job 38-41 (BLC additions — excellent texts on God's sovereign governance).
- **All creatures and actions:** Matthew 10:29-31 (shared with Shorter/1689); Genesis 45:7 (BLC addition — Joseph narrative illustrating providence over human actions); Psalm 135:6 (shared with 1689 proof texts).
- **To His own glory:** Romans 11:36 (shared with WLC proof text); Isaiah 63:14 (BLC addition — God's leading His people for His glorious name).

The proof texts are well-chosen and theologically appropriate, expanding the scriptural foundation without departing from the doctrinal content of the standards.
