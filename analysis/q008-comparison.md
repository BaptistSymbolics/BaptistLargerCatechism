# Q8: What do the Scriptures principally teach?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q8 faithfully reproduces the substance of both the Shorter Catechism and WLC Q5, maintaining the classic twofold division of Scripture's principal content: what man is to believe concerning God, and what duty God requires of man. The answer is virtually identical across all three catechisms and aligns with the 1689 Confession's teaching on the sufficiency and scope of Scripture. The proof texts are appropriate and well-chosen.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What things are chiefly contained in the holy scriptures?
> A. The holy scriptures chiefly contain what man ought to believe concerning God, and what duty God requireth of man. (2 Tim. 1:13; 3:15,16)

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter uses "chiefly contained" while the BLC uses "principally teach" — this matches the WLC's phrasing rather than the Shorter's, but the theological content is identical.
- Structural changes: None. Both maintain the same twofold division: (1) belief concerning God, (2) duty God requires.
- The Shorter uses "ought to believe" where the BLC uses "is to believe" — no material difference.
- The Shorter uses "requireth" where the BLC uses "requires" — simple modernization.
- The BLC splits the answer into two sections with separate proof texts, which actually provides more precision in citation than the Shorter's single answer.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 1, Paragraph 6:
> "The whole Councel of God concerning all things necessary for his own Glory, Mans Salvation, Faith and Life, is either expressely set down or necessarily contained in the Holy Scripture; unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new Revelation of the Spirit, or traditions of men."

Also relevant, Chapter 1, Paragraph 1:
> "The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving Knowledge, Faith and Obedience."

- Omissions: None. The BLC's twofold summary ("believe concerning God" and "duty God requires") is a catechetical distillation of the Confession's fuller statement about "Glory, Mans Salvation, Faith and Life." The catechetical form appropriately summarizes rather than reproducing the Confession's fuller enumeration.
- Structural changes: None. The Confession's pairing of "Faith" and "Life" (or "Knowledge, Faith and Obedience") maps directly onto the catechism's "believe" and "duty" categories.
- The BLC's answer is consistent with the Confession's teaching that Scripture is sufficient for both doctrine (what to believe) and practice (what duty to perform).

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q5:
> Q. 5. What do the Scriptures principally teach?
> A. The Scriptures principally teach, what man is to believe concerning God, and what duty God requires of man.

Proof text: 2 Timothy 1:13.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC answer is virtually word-for-word identical to the WLC. The question wording is exactly the same. The answer wording is exactly the same.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected or present on this topic.
- Unexpected divergences: None. This is a point of complete agreement.
- Key phrases dropped: None. The BLC preserves the WLC answer in its entirety.

## Detailed Notes
- This question represents one of the most straightforward points of agreement across Reformed and Baptist catechetical traditions. The twofold division of Scripture's teaching into faith and practice (doctrine and duty, credenda and agenda) is a common Protestant catechetical framework.
- The BLC's choice to use "principally teach" (matching the WLC) rather than "chiefly contained" (the Shorter's phrasing) represents a deliberate alignment with the Larger Catechism tradition, which is appropriate for a "Larger" catechism.
- The BLC helpfully splits the answer into two sections, allowing separate proof texts for each part of the twofold division.

## Scripture Analysis
**BLC Section 1** ("what man is to believe concerning God"):
- Exodus 34:5-7 — God's self-revelation of His name and attributes; appropriate for "what to believe concerning God"
- John 20:31 — "These are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ"; directly supports the purpose of Scripture as teaching belief
- 2 Timothy 3:15-16 — Scripture's ability to make wise unto salvation; a classic proof text shared with the Shorter Catechism

**BLC Section 2** ("what duty God requires of man"):
- Deuteronomy 10:12-13 — "What does the LORD your God require of you?"; an excellent and direct proof text for duty
- 2 Timothy 3:15-17 — Extended to include v.17 ("that the man of God may be complete, equipped for every good work"); appropriately cited for duty
- Acts 16:30-31 — "What must I do to be saved?"; connects duty with belief
- Ecclesiastes 12:13 — "Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man"; a classic duty text
- 1 Corinthians 10:11 — Scripture written for our instruction; supports the teaching purpose

The proof texts are well-chosen and more extensive than either the Shorter (2 Tim. 1:13; 3:15,16) or the WLC (2 Tim. 1:13), providing stronger biblical support for both halves of the twofold division.
