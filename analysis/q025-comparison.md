# Q25: Did all mankind fall in Adam's transgression?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q25 faithfully teaches the doctrine of the imputation of Adam's sin to all his posterity through the covenant headship. It appropriately combines the Shorter Catechism's parallel question with its subsequent question on the estate of sin and misery, and incorporates the WLC's "public person" language. However, it substitutes "original generation" for "ordinary generation" — a term used consistently across all three standards — which introduces potential ambiguity in a technical phrase.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Did all mankind fall in Adam's first transgression?
> A. The covenant being made with Adam, not only for himself but for his posterity, all mankind descending from him by ordinary generation sinned in him, and fell with him in his first transgression.

> Q. Into what estate did the fall bring mankind?
> A. The fall brought mankind into an estate of sin and misery.

- Omissions: None of substance. The BLC preserves all key doctrinal elements: covenant headship, posterity, descent by generation, sinning and falling in Adam, and the estate of sin and misery.
- Structural changes: The BLC combines these two Shorter questions into a single answer. This is appropriate for a Larger Catechism and does not alter meaning.
- **Wording change — "ordinary generation" to "original generation":** All three standards (Shorter, WLC, 1689) consistently use "ordinary generation," a technical term distinguishing natural human procreation from the supernatural conception of Christ (who thus did not inherit Adam's guilt). "Original generation" is not a recognized Reformed theological term and could be misread as "the first/original act of generation" rather than "the ordinary/natural mode of generation." This is the one point of concern.
- "First transgression" changed to "first disobedience": Minor wording variation. Both terms convey the same concept, though "transgression" is the standard Reformed technical term.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter VI, Paragraphs 2-3

> 2. Our first Parents by this Sin, fell from their original righteousness and communion with God, and we in them, whereby death came upon all; all becoming dead in Sin, and wholly defiled, in all the faculties, and parts, of soul, and body.

> 3. They being the root, and by Gods appointment, standing in the room, and stead of all mankind; the guilt of the Sin was imputed, and corrupted nature conveyed, to all their posterity descending from them by ordinary generation, being now conceived in Sin, and by nature children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal, unless the Lord Jesus set them free.

- Omissions: The 1689's rich language about the *consequences* — "dead in Sin, and wholly defiled, in all the faculties, and parts, of soul, and body" and "children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death and all other miseries" — is not present, but these are appropriately addressed in subsequent BLC questions rather than being omitted entirely. The phrase "the guilt of the Sin was imputed, and corrupted nature conveyed" is implicit but not explicit in the BLC; however, this is detail appropriate for later exposition.
- Structural changes: None of concern. The BLC uses the covenant/public-person framework (drawn from the catechetical tradition) rather than the 1689's "root" and "standing in the room and stead" language, but both express the same federal headship doctrine.
- The 1689's "ordinary generation" is again changed to "original generation" in the BLC.
- Agreement: The core doctrine — that Adam represented all mankind, that his sin is charged to his posterity through natural descent, and that this brought mankind into sin and misery — is faithfully preserved.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q22. Did all mankind fall in that first transgression?
> A. The covenant being made with Adam as a public person, not for himself only, but for his posterity, all mankind descending from him by ordinary generation, sinned in him, and fell with him in that first transgression.

> WLC Q23. Into what estate did the fall bring mankind?
> A. The fall brought mankind into an estate of sin and misery.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC's structure very closely, incorporating both Q22 and Q23 into a single answer.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected in this locus, and none present.
- Unexpected divergences: The substitution of "original generation" for "ordinary generation" is an unexpected departure from the WLC's precise language, as this is not a point of Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's "ordinary generation" is replaced rather than dropped. No other WLC phrases are omitted.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Original" vs. "Ordinary" generation:** This is the primary concern. "Ordinary generation" is a precise theological term with a specific purpose: it distinguishes the normal mode of human reproduction (by which Adam's guilt is transmitted) from Christ's supernatural conception by the Holy Spirit (by which He was exempted from original sin). The term "original generation" lacks this established theological meaning and introduces ambiguity. While the intended meaning is likely the same, the departure from a term used uniformly across the Shorter, WLC, and 1689 is puzzling. This may be an inadvertent error rather than an intentional change.

2. **"First disobedience" vs. "first transgression":** A minor stylistic variation. "Transgression" (parabasin) is the more technical Pauline term (Rom. 5:14), while "disobedience" (parakoe) also has Pauline warrant (Rom. 5:19). Neither is theologically problematic.

3. **Combining two questions:** The BLC appends the Shorter's "estate of sin and misery" answer as a concluding sentence. This is well-executed and provides a natural summary conclusion to the answer.

4. **"Public person" language:** The BLC follows the WLC in using "public person" (a federal theology term), which is not present in the Baptist Shorter Catechism but is theologically sound and consistent with the 1689's "standing in the room, and stead of all mankind." This is appropriate expansion for a Larger Catechism.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Acts 17:26; Romans 3:23** (section 1): Acts 17:26 supports the unity of mankind descending from one man. Romans 3:23 ("all have sinned") supports universal sinfulness. Both are appropriate, though neither directly supports the *covenant* or "public person" language. The WLC cites Acts 17:26 for descent (footnote 89) — good parallel.
- **Genesis 2:16-17; Genesis 3:16-19; Romans 5:12-20; 1 Corinthians 15:21-22** (section 2): These are the standard proof texts for this doctrine, closely matching the WLC footnote 90. Romans 5:12-20 is the primary locus classicus for federal headship. 1 Corinthians 15:21-22 supports the Adam-Christ parallel. Genesis 2:16-17 provides the original command and penalty. Genesis 3:16-19 gives the execution of the curse. Strong and appropriate.
- **Genesis 3:16-19; Romans 5:12; Ephesians 2:1; Romans 3:16-23** (section 3): Appropriate for the "estate of sin and misery" conclusion. Note: "Romans 3:16-23" is an unusual range — Romans 3:16 ("Destruction and misery are in their ways") through 3:23 does cover human sinfulness comprehensively, though the typical citation is Romans 3:23 or Romans 3:10-23. This may be intentional to capture the full indictment.
