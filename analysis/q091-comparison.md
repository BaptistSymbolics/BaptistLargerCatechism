# Q91: Where is the moral law summarized?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q91 faithfully conveys the core teaching that the moral law is summarized in the Ten Commandments, delivered at Sinai, written on two tables, with the first four concerning God and the last six concerning man. However, it omits the WLC's reference to the commandments being "recorded in the twentieth chapter of Exodus," and it subtly alters the language of divine authorship in a way that introduces a minor imprecision regarding how the tablets were written.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- Q. "Where is the moral law summarily comprehended?" A. "The moral law is summarily comprehended in the ten commandments (Deut. 10:4; Mt. 19:17)."

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter's answer is extremely brief; the BLC expands it appropriately.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC preserves the core assertion and adds historical detail about Sinai delivery and the two-table division, which is drawn from the 1689 and WLC rather than being novel.
- The BLC's second section on the four/six division has no direct parallel in the Shorter at this question but corresponds to the Shorter's Q. "What is the sum of the ten commandments?" which addresses the love commandments (Mt. 22:37-40). The BLC appropriately introduces this division here rather than waiting for a separate question.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraph 2:
> "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables; the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man."

- Omissions: The 1689 states the law was "first written in the heart of man" and "continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall" — the BLC omits this entire framework of the law's pre-Sinai existence in the heart. However, this is likely covered in a prior BLC question about God's initial revelation of the moral law (parallel to BSC's Q. "What did God at first reveal to man for the rule of his obedience?"), so this is not a true omission but rather proper topical division.
- Structural changes: The 1689 says the commandments were "delivered by God upon Mount Sinai" and "written in two Tables" — the BLC says they "were delivered by the voice of God on Mount Sinai and written down by Him on two stone tablets." The BLC specifies "the voice of God" for the delivery and "written down by Him" for the tablets. This is acceptable elaboration from the biblical text (Deut. 4:13).
- The four/six division matches the 1689 precisely, though the BLC modernizes "duty towards God" to "obligations to God" and "duty to man" to "obligations to human beings."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q98: "Where is the moral law summarily comprehended?" A. "The moral law is summarily comprehended in the ten commandments, which were delivered by the voice of God upon Mount Sinai, and written by him in two tables of stone; and are recorded in the twentieth chapter of Exodus. The four first commandments containing our duty to God, and the other six our duty to man."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is not a topic where Baptist-Presbyterian theology diverges).
- Unexpected divergences: The BLC drops the clause "and are recorded in the twentieth chapter of Exodus." This is a notable omission — the WLC specifically anchors the Ten Commandments to their scriptural location. While the information is not doctrinally weighty, it serves a pedagogical and confessional purpose (directing students to the text). No theological reason exists for dropping it.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "recorded in the twentieth chapter of Exodus" — dropped without theological reason.
  - "tables of stone" becomes "stone tablets" — acceptable modernization, no concern.
  - "summarily comprehended" becomes "summarized" — acceptable modernization.
  - "duty to God / duty to man" becomes "obligations to God / obligations to human beings" — acceptable modernization.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Written down by Him"**: The BLC says the commandments were "written down by Him on two stone tablets." The biblical account is complex: in Exodus 31:18 and 34:1, God writes on the tablets; in Exodus 34:27-28, there is ambiguity about whether Moses wrote. The WLC says "written by him" (referring to God), which the BLC follows. The addition of "down" is stylistic only.

2. **"Delivered by the voice of God"**: Both the WLC and BLC include this phrase, grounded in Deuteronomy 4:13 ("He declared to you his covenant, which he commanded you to perform, that is, the Ten Commandments, and he wrote them on two tablets of stone"). The BLC's phrasing is faithful.

3. **Four/six division**: The BLC's second section explicitly names the first four commandments as establishing "obligations to God" and the remaining six as "obligations to human beings." This matches both the 1689 and WLC precisely in substance.

4. **Proof texts**: The BLC cites Deuteronomy 4:13; Deuteronomy 10:4; Exodus 34:1-4; Romans 13:8-10; James 2:8-12 for the first section, and Matthew 22:37-40; Matthew 19:17-19 for the second. The WLC cites Deuteronomy 10:4 and Exodus 34:1-4 (footnote 420) and Matthew 22:37-40 (footnote 421). The BLC adds Deuteronomy 4:13, Romans 13:8-10, and James 2:8-12 — all appropriate and strengthening references. Matthew 19:17-19 is shared with the Shorter Catechism.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Deuteronomy 4:13** — Supports "delivered by the voice of God" and "Ten Commandments" and "two stone tablets." Strong and appropriate.
- **Deuteronomy 10:4** — Shared with the Shorter and WLC. Confirms the Ten Commandments on two tables.
- **Exodus 34:1-4** — Shared with WLC. The account of God commanding new tablets and writing on them.
- **Romans 13:8-10** — Supports the moral law being summarized in the commandments, with love as the fulfilling of the law. Good addition not in WLC.
- **James 2:8-12** — Supports the royal law and the binding nature of the whole law. Good addition matching the 1689's proof texts (Jam. 2.8,10,11,12 at 19.5).
- **Matthew 22:37-40** — Shared with WLC and Shorter. The two great commandments summarizing the law, supporting the four/six division.
- **Matthew 19:17-19** — Shared with the Shorter. Jesus directing to the commandments for the path of life.
- The omission of Exodus 20 as a chapter reference (corresponding to the dropped "recorded in the twentieth chapter of Exodus") is consistent — since the BLC dropped the clause, it naturally does not cite the chapter.
