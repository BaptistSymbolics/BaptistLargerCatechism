# Q10: What is God?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q10 closely follows the WLC Q7 structure and achieves strong solidarity with the Westminster tradition. However, it significantly departs from the Shorter Catechism's distinctive structural formulation and omits several important doctrinal elements from the 1689 Confession, including God's simplicity ("without body, parts, or passions"), His aseity in knowledge, His sovereignty ("working all things according to the counsel of his own immutable and most righteous will"), His freedom and absoluteness, and His judicial severity ("terrible in his judgements, hating all sin, and who will by no means clear the guilty").

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is God?
> A. God is a Spirit (John 4:24), infinite (Job 11:7, 8, 9), eternal (Ps. 110:2), and unchangeable (Jas. 1:17) in his being (Ex. 3:14), wisdom (Ps. 147:5), power (Rev. 4:8), holiness (Rev. 15:4), justice, goodness, and truth (Ex. 34:6).

- **Omissions:** The Shorter Catechism has a distinctive and theologically precise structure: God is "infinite, eternal, and unchangeable **in his** being, wisdom, power, holiness, justice, goodness, and truth." This structure teaches that infinity, eternity, and unchangeableness are predicated of each attribute — God is infinite *in* his wisdom, eternal *in* his power, unchangeable *in* his justice, etc. The BLC completely abandons this structure. Instead, it lists attributes serially (all-sufficient, eternal, unchangeable, incomprehensible, omnipresent, omnipotent, omniscient, most wise, most holy...) without the Shorter's cross-predication framework. This is a significant structural change — the Shorter's formulation teaches the doctrine of divine simplicity implicitly (all attributes are mutually infinite, eternal, unchangeable), while the BLC's serial list does not convey this.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC follows the WLC's serial enumeration rather than the Shorter's elegant cross-referencing structure. While the WLC has warrant for its longer enumeration (as a Larger catechism expanding the Shorter), the BLC as a Baptist Larger Catechism should arguably expand *upon* the Shorter's framework rather than replace it with the WLC's.
- The Shorter lists "wisdom" and "power" as divine attributes; the BLC replaces these with "omniscient" and "omnipotent" (modernized equivalents) and adds "most wise" separately. This is acceptable modernization.
- The Shorter's "justice" appears in the BLC as "most holy and just" — combining two attributes. This is acceptable.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 2, Paragraph 1:
> "The Lord our God is but one only living, and true God; whose subsistence is in and of himself, infinite in being, and perfection, whose Essence cannot be comprehended by any but himself; a most pure spirit, invisible, without body, parts, or passions, who only hath immortality, dwelling in the light, which no man can approach unto, who is immutable, immense, eternal, incomprehensible, Almighty, every way infinit, most holy, most wise, most free, most absolute, working all things according to the councel of his own immutable, and most righteous will, for his own glory, most loving, gracious, merciful, long suffering, abundant in goodness and truth, forgiving iniquity, transgression and sin, the rewarder of them that diligently seek him, and withall most just, and terrible in his judgements, hating all sin, and who will by no means clear the guilty."

Chapter 2, Paragraph 2:
> "God having all life, glory, goodness, blessedness, in and of himself: is alone in, and unto himself all-sufficient, not standing in need of any Creature which he hath made, nor deriving any glory from them... in his sight all things are open and manifest, his knowledge is infinite, infallible, and independant upon the Creature, so as nothing is to him contingent, or uncertain; he is most holy in all his Councels, in all his Works, and in all his Commands."

- **Omissions (serious):**
  1. **Divine simplicity language:** "without body, parts, or passions" — this is the classic Reformed formulation of divine simplicity and impassibility, present in the 1689 and central to Reformed theology proper. The BLC omits it entirely.
  2. **"Most free, most absolute"** — God's freedom and absoluteness are omitted. These assert God's sovereign independence and self-determination.
  3. **"Working all things according to the counsel of his own immutable and most righteous will, for his own glory"** — This crucial statement of divine sovereignty and purposeful decree is entirely absent from the BLC.
  4. **"Forgiving iniquity, transgression and sin"** — The 1689 includes God's pardoning nature. The BLC omits this.
  5. **"The rewarder of them that diligently seek him"** — Omitted.
  6. **"Terrible in his judgements, hating all sin, and who will by no means clear the guilty"** — This balancing statement about divine justice and severity is entirely absent. The 1689 deliberately balances mercy with judicial terror; the BLC presents mercy without this counterbalance.
  7. **"Invisible"** and **"who only hath immortality, dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto"** — These descriptions of divine transcendence are omitted.
  8. **"Immense"** — The 1689's "immense" (boundlessness) is replaced by "omnipresent," which is acceptable as modernization.
  9. **Knowledge as "infinite, infallible, and independent upon the Creature, so as nothing is to him contingent or uncertain"** (2.2) — The BLC has "omniscient" but without the Confession's careful elaboration about divine knowledge being independent of creatures and nothing being contingent to God.
  10. **"Most holy in all his Counsels, in all his Works, and in all his Commands"** (2.2) — This comprehensive scope of divine holiness is reduced to simply "most holy and just."

- **Structural changes:** The 1689 presents divine attributes in a theologically ordered flow: from God's unity and aseity → through simplicity and transcendence → to sovereignty and will → to mercy balanced with justice. The BLC flattens this into a simpler serial list.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 7. What is God?
> A. God is a Spirit, in and of himself infinite in being, glory, blessedness, and perfection; all-sufficient, eternal, unchangeable, incomprehensible, every where present, almighty, knowing all things, most wise, most holy, most just, most merciful and gracious, long-suffering, and abundant in goodness and truth.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected or present — the doctrine of God is an area of complete Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - "every where present" → "omnipresent" — acceptable modernization
  - "almighty" → "omnipotent" — acceptable modernization
  - "knowing all things" → "omniscient" — acceptable modernization
  - "most holy, most just" → "most holy and just" — minor combination, but the WLC deliberately separates holiness and justice as distinct attributes receiving individual emphasis. The BLC's combination slightly reduces this emphasis.
  - The BLC adds "glory" and "blessedness" as standalone sections with proof texts, which in the WLC are part of the initial clause "infinite in being, glory, blessedness, and perfection." This is an acceptable structural expansion.
  - "abundant in goodness and truth" → "abundant with goodness and truth" — minor preposition change ("with" vs. "in"), but "in" is the traditional phrasing matching Exodus 34:6.
- **Key phrases dropped:** None of significance — the BLC tracks the WLC very closely. The WLC solidarity is strong.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The core tension:** The BLC achieves excellent WLC solidarity but at the cost of significant 1689 content. Since the 1689 is the primary confessional standard for Baptists, this represents a real deficiency. Many of the omitted elements (simplicity, divine freedom, sovereignty in decree, judicial terror) are doctrines that the 1689 explicitly confesses and that are under contemporary theological pressure.

2. **"Without body, parts, or passions"** is perhaps the most significant omission. This is the historic Reformed statement of divine simplicity and impassibility — doctrines currently debated in evangelical theology. The 1689 deliberately includes this language. A catechism meant to exposit the 1689 should not silently omit it.

3. **The balance of mercy and justice:** The 1689 carefully pairs "most loving, gracious, merciful, long suffering, abundant in goodness and truth, forgiving iniquity, transgression and sin" with "most just, and terrible in his judgements, hating all sin, and who will by no means clear the guilty." The BLC ends with mercy ("most merciful and gracious, long-suffering, and abundant with goodness and truth") without the corresponding judicial severity. This creates an imbalanced portrait of God.

4. **Divine sovereignty in will:** The 1689's "working all things according to the counsel of his own immutable and most righteous will, for his own glory" connects God's nature to His sovereign decree. This is theologically important as it roots God's providential sovereignty in His very nature. The BLC omits this entirely.

5. **It should be noted** that the WLC also lacks some of the 1689's content (simplicity language, judicial severity, etc.), so the BLC's omissions partly flow from tracking the WLC too closely rather than supplementing it with 1689 material.

## Scripture Analysis
- The proof texts generally align well with the attributes they support.
- **Revelation 15:34** (cited for "perfection") appears to be an error — Revelation 15 has only 8 verses. This likely should be **Revelation 15:3-4** (a comma/formatting issue rather than a wrong reference).
- The BLC adds some proof texts not in the WLC (e.g., Hebrews 1:3 for "glory," Matthew 5:48 for "perfection," Colossians 2:3 for "most wise"), which is appropriate expansion.
- For "omnipresent," the BLC cites Psalm 139:1-13, which is a strong and appropriate text. The same reference is reused for "omniscient" — while Psalm 139 does address both themes, distinct proof texts would strengthen the case.
- Hebrews 13:8 is cited for "unchangeable" — this text refers to Jesus Christ specifically ("Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever"), which is appropriate when understood Christologically but is less directly about divine immutability in the classic sense than Malachi 3:6 (also cited) or James 1:17 (also cited).
