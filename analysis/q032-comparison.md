# Q32: How is the grace of God manifested in the Covenant of Grace?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q32 closely follows WLC Q32, modernizing language and preserving the essential structure of God's covenantal provision: freely offering a Mediator, requiring faith, giving the Spirit to the elect to work faith and obedience. However, the addition of "only" to "the only condition" introduces emphasis absent from all three standards, and the 1689's key phrase "to make them willing, and able to believe" — which stresses the Spirit's work on both the will and the capacity — is compressed into the less specific "to work in them that faith." Additionally, "to interest them in him" (WLC) is replaced with "to become united to Him," which subtly shifts the concept from covenantal interest/participation to mystical union, a related but distinct theological idea.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
The Shorter has no standalone parallel to BLC Q32. The relevant content is distributed across two answers:

> Q. Did God leave all mankind to perish in the estate of sin and misery?
> A. God having out of his mere good pleasure, from all eternity, elected some to everlasting life, did enter into a covenant of grace, to deliver them out of the estate of sin and misery, and to bring them into an estate of salvation by a Redeemer. (Eph. 1:4, 5; Rom. 3:20-22; Gal. 3:21, 22)

> Q. How doth the spirit apply to us the redemption purchased by Christ?
> A. The Spirit applieth to us the redemption purchased by Christ, by working faith in us, and thereby uniting us to Christ, in our effectual calling. (Eph. 1:13, 14; John 6:37, 39; Eph. 2:8; Eph. 3:17; 1 Cor. 1:9)

- Omissions: The Shorter's phrase "thereby uniting us to Christ" makes union with Christ the *result* of Spirit-worked faith. The BLC reverses the order by making "united to Him" the purpose of faith as a condition, rather than the fruit of Spirit-applied faith. This is a subtle structural shift in how union with Christ relates to faith.
- Structural changes: The Shorter places the Spirit's work of faith-working in the context of *applying redemption* (effectual calling). The BLC, following the WLC's structure, places it in the context of *manifesting grace in the covenant*. This is an appropriate expansion, not a problem.
- The BLC appropriately expands the Shorter's compact treatment into the fuller WLC-style exposition of how grace is manifested covenantally.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 7, Paragraph 2:
> "Moreover Man having brought himself under the curse of the Law by his fall, it pleased the Lord to make a Covenant of Grace wherein he freely offereth unto Sinners, Life and Salvation by Jesus Christ, requiring of them Faith in him, that they may be saved; and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal Life, his holy Spirit, to make them willing, and able to believe."

- Omissions: The 1689's phrase **"to make them willing, and able to believe"** is a carefully chosen formulation emphasizing that the Spirit's work addresses both the *will* (willingness) and the *capacity* (ability) of the sinner. The BLC's "to work in them that faith" follows the WLC rather than the 1689 here. While theologically compatible, the 1689's distinctive emphasis on the dual aspects of the Spirit's work in overcoming both unwillingness and inability is lost.
- Structural changes: The 1689 says "those that are ordained unto eternal Life" where the BLC says "all His elect" — these are synonymous; no concern.
- The 1689's "requiring of them Faith in him, that they may be saved" specifies the *purpose* of the faith requirement ("that they may be saved"). The BLC's "requires faith as the only condition for sinners to become united to Him" shifts the purpose from salvation broadly to union specifically, and adds "only" as an intensifier. The 1689 does not use the word "only" here (though it clearly teaches faith alone elsewhere).
- Agreement: The core covenantal structure is faithfully preserved — God freely offers life and salvation, requires faith, and gives the Spirit to the elect to enable belief. The BLC's expansion into holy obedience and saving graces goes beyond 1689 7.2 but is consistent with confessional teaching elsewhere (e.g., chapters on sanctification and good works).

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 32. How is the grace of God manifested in the second covenant?
> A. The grace of God is manifested in the second covenant, in that he freely provideth and offereth to sinners a Mediator, and life and salvation by him; and requiring faith as the condition to interest them in him, promiseth and giveth his Holy Spirit to all his elect, to work in them that faith, with all other saving graces; and to enable them unto all holy obedience, as the evidence of the truth of their faith and thankfulness to God, and as the way which he hath appointed them to salvation.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: "Second covenant" (WLC) → "Covenant of Grace" (BLC). The WLC uses "second covenant" throughout Q30-35; the BLC uses the explicit name. This is a minor terminological preference, not a theological divergence. Both the WLC and the 1689 use both terms.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"the only condition"**: The WLC says "the condition"; the BLC adds "only." While Baptists and Presbyterians both affirm that faith is the sole instrument/condition of covenantal interest, the explicit insertion of "only" is an addition not present in any of the three standards. It risks sounding polemical where the original was simply descriptive.
  - **"to become united to Him"** replaces **"to interest them in him"**: The WLC's "interest" is a covenantal/legal term meaning to give a share or stake in Christ and His benefits. The BLC's "united to Him" shifts toward the language of mystical union. These are related but distinct theological concepts — one can have covenantal interest in Christ that includes but is not reducible to union. This narrows the WLC's broader term.
  - **"the truth of"** dropped: The WLC says "the evidence of the truth of their faith"; the BLC says "the evidence of their faith." The WLC's "truth of" distinguishes genuine faith from mere profession — obedience evidences that faith is *true* (not merely claimed). This is a small but meaningful precision lost.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "the truth of" (from "evidence of the truth of their faith") — see above
  - "as the way which he hath appointed them to salvation" is expanded to "the way which He has appointed and established them to salvation" — the addition of "established" is minor and unobjectionable.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's most notable feature is the addition of "only" to "the only condition." Neither the WLC, the 1689, nor the Shorter use this intensifier in this context. While the theological point is defensible (Reformed theology affirms *sola fide*), the placement here is novel. The standards describe faith as "the condition" — defining it as such inherently implies exclusivity without needing "only." The concern is not that the theology is wrong but that the BLC departs from the careful restraint of its standards by adding emphasis where they chose not to.
- The replacement of "to interest them in him" with "to become united to Him" is an interpretive narrowing. The WLC's "interest" is broader: it includes union, but also encompasses justification, adoption, and all covenantal benefits. "United" focuses on one (admittedly central) benefit.
- The separation of the BLC's answer into three distinct sentences (compared to the WLC's single flowing sentence) makes the logical connections slightly less clear, particularly the final clause about "the way...to salvation" which in the WLC clearly connects to both faith and obedience as a unified pathway, while in the BLC it stands more isolated.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Genesis 3:15; 1 Timothy 2:5; John 3:16; John 6:27**: Support the provision of a Mediator. Genesis 3:15 (protoevangelium) is appropriate. 1 Timothy 2:5 directly names Christ as mediator. John 3:16 and 6:27 support God's free gift.
- **1 John 5:11-12**: Supports "life and salvation through Him" — directly states that life is in the Son.
- **John 3:16, 36; John 1:12**: Support faith as the condition. These are standard and appropriate. However, the BLC's proof texts for the "only condition" clause notably omit the classic Reformed proof texts for *sola fide* in its justification sense (e.g., Romans 3:28, Galatians 2:16), relying instead on general "believe and receive" texts. This is adequate but less precise.
- **Isaiah 59:21; Luke 11:13; John 14:16-20; 1 Corinthians 12:13; Romans 8:4, 9, 11, 14-16**: A robust set of proof texts for the giving of the Holy Spirit. The expansion beyond the WLC's citations is appropriate and strengthening.
- **2 Corinthians 4:13; Ephesians 2:8-10; Acts 16:14; 2 Peter 1:1**: Support the Spirit working faith in the elect. Acts 16:14 (Lydia's heart opened) is particularly apt.
- **Galatians 5:22-23**: The fruit of the Spirit as the source for "all other saving graces" — appropriate.
- **Ezekiel 36:27; Ephesians 2:10**: Support enablement to holy obedience — both are strong and appropriate.
- **James 2:18, 22; 2 Corinthians 5:14-15**: Support obedience as evidence of faith and thankfulness — James 2 on faith shown by works is directly on point.
- **Ephesians 2:10**: Cited for the final clause about God's appointed way to salvation — "created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand" supports the concept well.
