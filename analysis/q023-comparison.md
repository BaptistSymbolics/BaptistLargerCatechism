# Q23: Did our first parents continue in the estate wherein they were created?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q23 faithfully combines the Shorter Catechism's structure with the WLC's fuller detail, producing a comprehensive answer that preserves all key doctrinal elements from both Baptist standards and shows strong WLC solidarity. The BLC retains the Shorter's core phrasing while incorporating the WLC's mention of Satan's temptation and the specific sin of eating the forbidden fruit, resulting in a well-balanced modernized answer.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
Q. "Did our first parents continue in the estate wherein they were created?"
A. "Our first parents being left to the freedom of their own will, fell from the estate wherein they were created, by sinning against God." (Gen. 3:6, 7, 8, 13; Eccles. 7:29)

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter's "their own will" becomes "their will" in the BLC—a trivial modernization with no doctrinal impact.
- Structural changes: The BLC rearranges the Shorter's order. The Shorter places the fall first ("fell from the estate") and then states the cause ("by sinning against God"). The BLC places the sin first ("sinned against God in eating the forbidden fruit") and then the consequence ("and thereby, fell from the estate"). This is a minor structural change that, if anything, makes the causal relationship clearer—they sinned and *thereby* fell—rather than altering any theological relationship. Both the sin and the fall are present and properly connected.
- The BLC adds detail from the WLC/1689: "through the temptation of Satan" and "in eating the forbidden fruit." These are legitimate expansions appropriate for a Larger Catechism, and both are present in the Shorter's next question (Q. "What was the sin whereby our first parents fell from the estate wherein they were created?" A. "The sin...was their eating the forbidden fruit"). The BLC consolidates material the Shorter spreads across two questions into one answer.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 6, Paragraph 1:
"Although God created Man upright, and perfect, and gave him a righteous law, which had been unto life had he kept it, and threatned death upon the breach thereof; yet he did not long abide in this honour; Satan using the subtilty of the serpent to seduce Eve, then by her seducing Adam, who without any compulsion, did wilfully transgress the Law of their Creation, and the command given unto them, in eating the forbidden fruit; which God was pleased according to his wise and holy Councel to permit, having purposed to order it, to his own glory."

- Omissions: The 1689 contains several details not in the BLC: (1) Satan's use of "the subtilty of the serpent," (2) the sequential seduction of Eve then Adam, (3) the phrase "without any compulsion" emphasizing voluntariness, (4) the description of the transgression as "wilfully," and (5) God's permissive decree—"which God was pleased according to his wise and holy Councel to permit, having purposed to order it, to his own glory." However, these omissions are appropriate: the BLC is a catechism, not a confession, and the Shorter Catechism parallel does not include these details either. The BLC's phrase "being left to the freedom of their will" implicitly conveys the voluntariness that the 1689 makes explicit with "without any compulsion" and "wilfully." The permissive decree is likely addressed elsewhere in the BLC's treatment of providence and the decrees.
- Structural changes: None of concern. The BLC's answer is consistent with the 1689's narrative flow: freedom of will → Satan's temptation → sin of eating the forbidden fruit → fall from original estate.
- Agreement: The BLC agrees with all core elements: the freedom of will, Satan's role in temptation, the specific sin of eating the forbidden fruit, and the fall from the created estate.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q21:
Q. "Did man continue in that estate wherein God at first created him?"
A. "Our first parents being left to the freedom of their own will, through the temptation of Satan, transgressed the commandment of God in eating the forbidden fruit; and thereby fell from the estate of innocency wherein they were created."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is a topic with no Baptist-Presbyterian distinctives.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - The WLC says "transgressed the commandment of God" while the BLC says "sinned against God." The BLC's phrasing is actually closer to the Shorter Catechism's "sinning against God," which is appropriate for a Baptist catechism that takes the Shorter as its primary baseline. "Sinned against God" is a broader and arguably more personal way of expressing the same reality as "transgressed the commandment of God," and is not theologically weaker.
  - The WLC specifies "the estate of innocency" while the BLC has simply "the estate wherein they were created." The BLC here follows the Shorter Catechism exactly, which also omits "of innocency." This is not a meaningful omission since "the estate wherein they were created" contextually refers to innocency/uprightness, and the Shorter Catechism establishes this as the Baptist baseline.
- Key phrases dropped: "transgressed the commandment of God" (replaced with Shorter's "sinned against God"); "the estate of innocency" (follows Shorter's simpler phrasing). Both choices reflect alignment with the Baptist Shorter Catechism rather than the WLC, which is the correct priority ordering.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC question wording ("Did our first parents continue in the estate wherein they were created?") matches the Shorter Catechism exactly and is very close to the WLC ("Did man continue in that estate wherein God at first created him?"). The BLC's use of "our first parents" rather than the WLC's "man" is a deliberate choice following the Shorter.
- The BLC's answer is essentially a synthesis of the Shorter Catechism's answer with the WLC's additional detail about Satan's temptation and the forbidden fruit. This is precisely what a Baptist Larger Catechism should do—expand the Shorter while maintaining solidarity with the WLC.
- The phrase "being left to the freedom of their will" preserves the Reformed doctrine of the mutability of the will in the state of innocence—Adam and Eve were able to sin and able not to sin (posse peccare et posse non peccare), and God did not coerce the fall but left them to their own will. This is consistent with the 1689's "without any compulsion."

## Scripture Analysis
- **Genesis 3:6-13**: Comprehensive citation covering both the act of eating the forbidden fruit and the subsequent confrontation. The Shorter cites Genesis 3:6, 7, 8, 13 and adds Ecclesiastes 7:29. The BLC drops Ecclesiastes 7:29 ("God made man upright; but they have sought out many inventions"), which provides the contrast between the created estate and the fall. This is a minor loss but the substance is covered by the Genesis citation.
- **2 Corinthians 11:3**: "But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ." This supports the mention of Satan's temptation and is the same proof text used by the 1689 (Chapter 6.1). This is a good addition that the Shorter does not cite.
- The WLC cites Genesis 3:6-8, 13 and Ecclesiastes 7:29 (footnote 88). The BLC's citation of 2 Corinthians 11:3 in addition to Genesis 3 represents an appropriate selection that supports the Satan/temptation element added beyond the Shorter.
