# Q9: What do the Scriptures make known of God?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q9 is virtually identical to WLC Q6, reproducing the same four-fold organizational framework for what the Scriptures reveal about God: what God is, the persons in the Godhead, His decrees, and the execution of His decrees. The only textual change is modernized reverent capitalization ("His decrees" instead of "his decrees"). Though the Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question, the BLC's four categories faithfully map onto the structure of both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest is:
> "Q. What things are chiefly contained in the holy scriptures?
> A. The holy scriptures chiefly contain what man ought to believe concerning God, and what duty God requireth of man."

The Shorter Catechism does not include an intermediate organizational question listing the four topics Scripture makes known about God. Instead, it moves directly from the contents of Scripture to "What is God?" and then treats the persons of the Godhead, the decrees, and the execution of decrees in subsequent questions. However, the Shorter's sequence of questions (What is God? → persons in the Godhead → decrees → execution of decrees) matches exactly the four categories listed in BLC Q9.

- Omissions: None. The BLC's four categories encompass and organize what the Shorter treats sequentially.
- Structural changes: The BLC follows the WLC in adding an explicit organizational question that the Shorter lacks. This is an expansion, not an omission, and provides a helpful summary framework that the Shorter's subsequent questions implicitly follow.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

1689 Chapter 1 (Of the Holy Scriptures), Paragraph 6:
> "The whole Councel of God concerning all things necessary for his own Glory, Mans Salvation, Faith and Life, is either expressely set down or necessarily contained in the Holy Scripture."

1689 Chapter 2 (Of God and of the Holy Trinity):
- Paragraph 1 addresses what God is: "The Lord our God is but one only living, and true God; whose subsistence is in and of himself, infinite in being, and perfection..."
- Paragraph 3 addresses the persons of the Godhead: "In this divine and infinite Being there are three subsistences, the Father the Word (or Son) and Holy Spirit, of one substance, power, and Eternity..."

1689 Chapter 3 (Of Gods Decree):
- Paragraph 1 addresses God's decrees: "God hath Decreed in himself from all Eternity, by the most wise and holy Councel of his own will, freely and unchangeably, all things whatsoever comes to passe..."
- The execution of decrees is addressed through subsequent chapters on creation (Ch. 4) and providence (Ch. 5).

- Omissions: None. All four categories in BLC Q9 have thorough treatment in the 1689.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's four categories map naturally onto the 1689's chapter structure.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> "Q. 6. What do the Scriptures make known of God?
> A. The Scriptures make known what God is, the persons in the Godhead, his decrees, and the execution of his decrees."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC answer is verbatim identical to the WLC, with only the modernized capitalization of "His" in place of "his."
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is a question about the organizational structure of scriptural revelation concerning God, which is not an area of Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every word of the WLC answer is preserved.

## Detailed Notes
- BLC Q9 serves as an organizational or "table of contents" question, setting up the topics that subsequent questions will address in detail. The WLC uses this same approach (Q6 introduces the framework, Q7ff. develops it).
- The change from "his" to "His" is a consistent modernization pattern (reverential capitalization for divine pronouns) that does not alter theological content.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC significantly expands the proof texts compared to the WLC:

**"What God is"**: BLC cites John 4:24; John 20:31; Exodus 34:6-7; Isaiah 40:18-28. The WLC cites only Hebrews 11:6 (note 14). The BLC's references are arguably stronger: John 4:24 (God is Spirit), Exodus 34:6-7 (God's self-revelation of His attributes), and Isaiah 40:18-28 (God's incomparability) directly address what God is. John 20:31 is slightly tangential (written that you may believe Jesus is the Christ), though it does point to Scripture's revelatory purpose.

**"Persons in the Godhead"**: BLC cites Matthew 3:16-17; Deuteronomy 6:4; 1 Corinthians 8:6; Matthew 28:19-20; 2 Corinthians 13:14. The WLC proof text is 1 John 5:7 (note 15, the Comma Johanneum — a contested text). The BLC's references are more robust and less textually disputed, drawing on the baptism of Christ (Matt 3:16-17), the Shema (Deut 6:4), Pauline monotheism (1 Cor 8:6), the Great Commission (Matt 28:19-20), and the apostolic benediction (2 Cor 13:14).

**"His decrees"**: BLC cites Acts 15:14-18; Isaiah 46:9-11; 1 Corinthians 2:6-13. The WLC cites Acts 15:14-15,18 (note 16). The BLC retains the WLC's Acts 15 reference and adds Isaiah 46:9-11 (God declaring the end from the beginning) and 1 Corinthians 2:6-13 (God's hidden wisdom decreed before the ages).

**"Execution of His decrees"**: BLC cites Acts 4:26-28; Acts 2:23; Ephesians 1:11. The WLC cites Acts 4:27-28 (note 17). The BLC retains the WLC's Acts 4 reference (with minor verse range difference) and adds Acts 2:23 (Christ delivered by the determined counsel of God) and Ephesians 1:11 (working all things according to the counsel of His will).

Overall, the BLC's proof texts are a thoughtful expansion that strengthens the scriptural support while retaining the WLC's key references. The avoidance of 1 John 5:7 (Comma Johanneum) for the Trinity section is a prudent textual choice.
