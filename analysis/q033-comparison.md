# Q33: How was the Covenant of Grace revealed through the Scriptures?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q33 closely tracks 1689 Confession 7.3, faithfully reproducing its core content about the Covenant of Grace being revealed in the gospel, founded on the eternal covenant transaction (Covenant of Redemption), first promised in Genesis 3:15, and progressively revealed until completed in the New Testament. However, the BLC introduces a parenthetical gloss on Old Covenant structure ("defined by the Abrahamic, conditioned by the Mosaic, and focused by the Davidic covenants") that, while theologically defensible from a 1689 Federalist perspective, goes beyond the Confession's simpler language of "farther steps" and imports a specific covenantal taxonomy not found in any of the standards. Additionally, the 1689's important concluding affirmation—that salvation has always been by grace alone through this covenant, and that man is "utterly uncapable of acceptance with God upon those terms, on which Adam stood in his state of innocency"—is omitted entirely.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** "Q. Did God leave all mankind to perish in the estate of sin and misery? A. God having out of his mere good pleasure, from all eternity, elected some to everlasting life, did enter into a covenant of grace, to deliver them out of the estate of sin and misery, and to bring them into an estate of salvation by a Redeemer (Rom. 3:20-22; Gal. 3:21, 22)."

This Shorter question addresses the *establishment* of the Covenant of Grace rather than its *progressive revelation*, so the parallel is indirect. The BLC Q33 focuses specifically on the mode of revelation through Scripture—a topic the Shorter does not directly address.

- Omissions: The Shorter's emphasis on God's "mere good pleasure" as the motive for the covenant, and the explicit mention of election ("elected some to everlasting life"), are not reproduced in BLC Q33. However, election is referenced parenthetically ("the redemption of the elect") and the Shorter's question covers different ground, so this is not a direct omission of parallel content.
- Structural changes: None significant. The BLC appropriately treats this as a distinct question about progressive revelation rather than attempting to replicate the Shorter's question about God's salvific initiative.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 7, Paragraph 3 — "This Covenant is revealed in the Gospel; first of all to Adam in the promise of Salvation by the seed of the woman, and afterwards by farther steps, untill the full discovery thereof was compleated in the new Testament; and it is founded in that Eternal Covenant transaction, that was between the Father and the Son, about the Redemption of the Elect; and it is alone by the Grace of this Covenant, that all of the posterity of fallen Adam, that ever were saved, did obtain life and a blessed immortality; Man being now utterly uncapable of acceptance with God upon those terms, on which Adam stood in his state of innocency."

- Omissions:
  1. **"it is alone by the Grace of this Covenant, that all of the posterity of fallen Adam, that ever were saved, did obtain life and a blessed immortality"** — This critical affirmation that OT saints were saved by the same Covenant of Grace is entirely absent from BLC Q33. The 1689 makes explicit that there is one way of salvation across all ages. This is a significant doctrinal point that guards against dispensationalist errors.
  2. **"Man being now utterly uncapable of acceptance with God upon those terms, on which Adam stood in his state of innocency"** — This clause, which reinforces the impossibility of salvation by works after the fall, is also omitted. It serves as the negative corollary establishing why the Covenant of Grace is necessary for all post-fall humanity.
- Structural changes: The BLC reorders the 1689's sequence. The Confession places "revealed in the Gospel" first, then the progressive revelation, then the Covenant of Redemption foundation. The BLC leads with "revealed in the gospel" and immediately introduces the Covenant of Redemption, then returns to progressive revelation. This is a minor rearrangement that does not alter the theological substance.
- Additions: The BLC introduces a parenthetical taxonomy: "which is defined by the Abrahamic, conditioned by the Mosaic, and focused by the Davidic covenants." The 1689 simply says "by farther steps." While this gloss reflects a common 1689 Federalist reading, it introduces a specific covenantal taxonomy not found in the Confession itself and could be seen as importing a particular interpretation into a catechism meant to exposit the standards.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q33: "Was the covenant of grace always administered after one and the same manner? A. The covenant of grace was not always administered after the same manner, but the administrations of it under the Old Testament were different from those under the New."

WLC Q34: "How was the covenant of grace administered under the Old Testament? A. The covenant of grace was administered under the Old Testament, by promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the passover, and other types and ordinances, which did all fore-signify Christ then to come, and were for that time sufficient to build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by whom they then had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation."

WLC Q35: "How is the covenant of grace administered under the New Testament? A. Under the New Testament, when Christ the substance was exhibited, the same covenant of grace was and still is to be administered in the preaching of the Word, and the administration of the sacraments of Baptism and the Lord's Supper; in which grace and salvation are held forth in more fulness, evidence, and efficacy, to all nations."

- Solidarity: MODERATE
- Intentional Baptist divergences:
  - The WLC lists "circumcision, the passover, and other types and ordinances" as OT administrations, and "sacraments of Baptism and the Lord's Supper" as NT administrations. The BLC avoids this sacramental framework entirely, which is expected given Baptist covenant theology's different understanding of the relationship between OT signs and NT ordinances.
  - The WLC's language of covenant "administration" under Old and New Testaments (implying one covenant with two administrations) is replaced by the BLC's distinctively Baptist framework of the "Old Covenant" as a separate covenant entity "defined by the Abrahamic, conditioned by the Mosaic, and focused by the Davidic." This reflects the 1689 Federalist position that the Mosaic covenant is not an administration of the Covenant of Grace but a distinct covenant.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - The WLC Q34 explicitly affirms that OT saints "had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation" through the promised Messiah—the same affirmation found in 1689 7.3 but missing from the BLC. This is an area of normal Baptist-Presbyterian agreement that the BLC drops.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - The WLC's affirmation that OT types and ordinances "were for that time sufficient to build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah" has no parallel in the BLC. While the WLC's specific enumeration of types is expectedly omitted, the affirmation of OT sufficiency is theologically important and shared ground.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The parenthetical taxonomy is novel.** The phrase "which is defined by the Abrahamic, conditioned by the Mosaic, and focused by the Davidic covenants" does not appear in any of the three standards. While it represents a common Reformed Baptist hermeneutical framework, inserting it into a catechism answer introduces interpretive specificity beyond the Confession. The 1689's "by farther steps" is deliberately broad.

2. **The omission of the exclusivity clause is the most significant issue.** The 1689's affirmation that "it is alone by the Grace of this Covenant, that all of the posterity of fallen Adam, that ever were saved, did obtain life and a blessed immortality" is a crucial anti-dispensationalist safeguard. It teaches that there is one way of salvation in all ages—through the Covenant of Grace alone. This is precisely the kind of doctrinal element that a Larger Catechism should expand upon, not omit.

3. **The omission of man's incapacity after the fall** ("Man being now utterly uncapable of acceptance with God upon those terms, on which Adam stood in his state of innocency") removes the negative ground that makes progressive revelation theologically necessary. Without it, the answer describes the *fact* of progressive revelation but loses the *necessity* grounded in the impossibility of works-righteousness after the fall.

4. **The Covenant of Redemption is handled well.** The BLC's parenthetical identification "(commonly called the Covenant of Redemption)" is a helpful addition that names what the 1689 describes as "that Eternal Covenant transaction, that was between the Father and the Son."

## Scripture Analysis
- **2 Timothy 1:9; Titus 1:2** — These are the exact proof texts the 1689 uses (marked with *) for the Covenant of Redemption. Good alignment.
- **Romans 8:28-30; Ephesians 1:3-13** — These are additions not found in the 1689's proof texts for 7.3. They support the election/redemption framework but are more properly associated with the decrees or effectual calling.
- **Genesis 3:15** — Matches the 1689 exactly for the protoevangelium.
- **Hebrews 1:1** — Matches the 1689's proof text (f) for progressive revelation.
- **Hebrews 8:6-13** — A strong addition supporting the new covenant's superiority, not in 1689 7.3's proof texts but theologically appropriate.
- **Hebrews 11:6,13** — Matches the 1689's proof text (g) for OT saints saved by grace through the covenant. Notably, the BLC cites this verse but omits the doctrinal point it was meant to support in the Confession (that all the saved in every age were saved by grace through this covenant alone).
