# Q155: Who should be baptized?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q155 combines the Shorter Catechism's two separate questions on proper subjects (Q97) and infant baptism (Q99) into a single answer. The theology is substantially consistent with the 1689 and Shorter Catechism, faithfully preserving the credobaptist position. However, the BLC drops the term "ordinance" (using "sacrament" instead, diverging from the Shorter's language), omits the Shorter's distinctive phrasing "and to none other" (an exclusive clause), and weakens the argument against infant baptism by replacing the Shorter's threefold "command or example...or certain consequence" with "command nor example...nor necessary consequence," subtly changing "certain consequence" to "necessary consequence"—a shift from epistemic certainty to logical modality.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

Q97: "To whom is baptism to be administered?"
A: "Baptism is to be administered to all those who actually profess repentance towards God, faith in and obedience to our Lord Jesus Christ, and to none other." (Acts 2:38; Mt. 3:6; Acts 8:12, 36, 37, 38; 10:47, 48)

Q99: "Are the infants of such as are professing believers to be baptized?"
A: "The infants of such as are professing believers are not to be baptized, because there is neither command or example in the holy scriptures, or certain consequence from them to baptize such." (Ex. 23:13; Pr. 30:6; Lk. 3:7, 8)

- **Omissions:**
  - "and to none other" — The Shorter's emphatic exclusive clause is absent from the BLC. While the BLC says "the only proper subjects," the Shorter's "and to none other" is a sharper, more direct exclusion that reinforces the restrictive principle. The BLC's phrasing focuses on who qualifies rather than explicitly excluding all others.
  - The Shorter's proof texts for the anti-paedobaptist argument (Ex. 23:13; Pr. 30:6; Lk. 3:7, 8) are entirely absent from the BLC. These texts ground the regulative principle argument (not adding to God's commands) and John the Baptist's rebuke of presumed covenant privilege—both theologically significant.

- **Structural changes:**
  - The BLC merges Q97 and Q99 into one answer, which is a reasonable editorial choice for a larger catechism that can handle longer answers, but it slightly reduces the pedagogical emphasis the Shorter places on the infant baptism question as a distinct matter.
  - "certain consequence" (Shorter) → "necessary consequence" (BLC). The Shorter's "certain consequence" means a conclusion that can be drawn with certainty; "necessary consequence" means a conclusion that logically must follow. While closely related, the shift moves from an epistemic category (what we can know for certain from Scripture) to a logical-modal category (what necessarily follows). This subtly alters the nature of the argument.

- **Terminology:** The BLC uses "sacrament" while the Shorter uses "ordinance" (Q97 context). The Shorter consistently uses "ordinance" in its baptism questions. The BLC's shift to "sacrament" aligns more with WLC vocabulary but departs from the Shorter's Baptist-distinctive terminology.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter XXIX, Paragraph 2: "Those who do actually professe repentance towards God, faith in, and obedience, to our Lord Jesus, are the only proper subjects of this ordinance." (Mar. 16:16; Act. 8:36, 37)

Appendix on Baptism, Section 1: "As to those Christians who consent with us, That Repentance from dead works, and Faith towards God, and our Lord Jesus Christ, is required in persons to be Baptized..."

- **Omissions:** None of significance regarding the positive statement. The BLC closely mirrors 1689 XXIX.2 almost verbatim, adding "Christ" to "our Lord Jesus" (a minor expansion that matches the Shorter's fuller form).

- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC's first sentence is nearly a direct quotation of 1689 XXIX.2.

- **Terminology:** The 1689 uses "ordinance" while the BLC uses "sacrament." The 1689 consistently uses "ordinance" for baptism throughout Chapter XXIX and its appendix. This is a notable terminological departure from the primary confessional standard.

- **Infant baptism argument:** The 1689's appendix provides an extensive, detailed theological argument against infant baptism spanning multiple pages, addressing covenant theology, household baptisms, 1 Cor. 7:14, and the regulative principle. The BLC compresses this into a single sentence. This is appropriate for catechetical format, but the BLC's compressed argument ("neither command nor example...nor necessary consequence") is drawn from the Shorter's phrasing rather than the 1689's more expansive reasoning.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q166: "Unto whom is baptism to be administered?"
A: "Baptism is not to be administered to any that are out of the visible church, and so strangers from the covenant of promise, till they profess their faith in Christ, and obedience to him, but infants descending from parents, either both, or but one of them, professing faith in Christ, and obedience to him, are in that respect within the covenant, and to be baptized."

- **Solidarity:** MODERATE
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:**
  - The WLC affirms infant baptism of children of believing parents; the BLC explicitly denies it. This is the expected and defining Baptist departure.
  - The WLC frames the question through visible church membership and covenant of promise; the BLC frames it through personal profession. This reflects the fundamental Baptist-Presbyterian ecclesiological difference.
  - The WLC's "visible church" and "covenant of promise" framework is replaced by the BLC's focus on individual profession—an expected credobaptist reframing.

- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC mentions "faith in Christ, and obedience to him" as the requirements for adult converts. The BLC adds "repentance towards God" to this pair, making it a triad (repentance, faith, obedience). This addition is not an unexpected divergence from the WLC per se—it reflects the Baptist confessional tradition's emphasis on repentance (drawn from Acts 20:21 and the Shorter Catechism). However, it is worth noting that the WLC does not mention repentance in Q166 while the BLC does.

- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "visible church" and "strangers from the covenant of promise" — dropped, but this is expected Baptist divergence (different ecclesiology and covenant theology).
  - No unexpected WLC phrases are dropped without theological reason. The departures all relate to the expected baptism/ecclesiology/covenant differences.

## Detailed Notes

1. **"Sacrament" vs. "Ordinance"**: The BLC uses "sacrament" where both the 1689 and the Shorter Catechism use "ordinance." This is a significant terminological choice. The Baptist tradition has historically preferred "ordinance" to distance from sacramental theology that implies the rite conveys grace ex opere operato. The 1689 Chapter XXVIII is titled "Of Baptism and the Lords Supper" and consistently uses "ordinance." The Shorter Catechism likewise uses "ordinance." Using "sacrament" here aligns the BLC with WLC vocabulary but departs from both Baptist standards.

2. **"Certain consequence" → "Necessary consequence"**: The Shorter says "certain consequence from them" (i.e., from the Scriptures). The BLC says "necessary consequence from the Scripture." While both deny that infant baptism can be derived from Scripture by good and necessary consequence, the shift in adjective is notable. "Certain" emphasizes that no sure/reliable inference can be drawn; "necessary" emphasizes that no logically required inference exists. These are subtly different epistemic claims.

3. **"Command or example...or certain consequence" → "Command nor example...nor necessary consequence"**: The Shorter uses "or" (disjunctive), the BLC uses "nor" (negative conjunction). Both are grammatically correct in their respective constructions ("neither...nor" vs. "neither...or"), but the Shorter's "or" form ("neither command or example") was standard early modern English and is faithfully rendered in the 1813 Charleston printing.

4. **"to baptism them" → grammatical note**: The BLC text reads "nor necessary consequence from the Scripture to baptism them." This appears to be either an archaic usage (using "baptism" as a verb) or a possible error for "to baptize them."

## Scripture Analysis

**BLC proof texts:** Mark 16:16; Acts 8:36-37; Acts 2:41; Acts 8:12; Acts 18:8; Acts 16:29-34

- Mark 16:16 and Acts 8:36-37 are shared with the 1689 (XXIX.2 cites Mar. 16:16 and Act. 8:36, 37).
- Acts 2:41, Acts 8:12, Acts 18:8, Acts 16:29-34 are additions beyond the 1689's proof texts for XXIX.2, but they are appropriate texts supporting credobaptism.
- **Missing from the Shorter's anti-paedobaptist argument:** Ex. 23:13 (regulative principle—"make no mention of the name of other gods"), Pr. 30:6 ("Add thou not unto his words"), and Lk. 3:7, 8 (John's rebuke against presuming Abrahamic descent for covenant standing). These proof texts ground the *principle* behind rejecting infant baptism (the regulative principle and rejection of covenant presumption), not merely the *practice*. Their absence weakens the theological grounding of the anti-paedobaptist clause.
- Acts 16:29-34 (the Philippian jailer) is an interesting addition, as it is one of the "household baptism" passages that paedobaptists often cite. Its inclusion here presumably intends to show that even in household baptism accounts, faith preceded baptism.
