# Q120: What is required of equals?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q120 closely follows the structure and content of WLC Q131 on the duties of equals, preserving all major elements. Two minor issues are present: (1) the phrase "as if their own" subtly weakens the WLC's stronger "as their own," introducing a hypothetical distance from what should be a direct identification with others' blessings; and (2) "successes" is narrower than the WLC's "advancement," which encompasses broader life progress, not merely successful outcomes.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** The Shorter Catechism does not have a standalone question on the duties of equals. The topic is addressed only in passing within the fifth commandment:
> Q. "What is required in the fifth commandment?" A. "The fifth commandment requireth the preserving the honour and performing the duties belonging to every one in their several places and relations, as superiors, inferiors, or equals." (Rom. 12:10)

- Omissions: N/A — the Shorter only names "equals" as a category without elaborating their duties. The BLC appropriately expands on this.
- Structural changes: None — the BLC's expansion is precisely the kind of elaboration a larger catechism should provide.

The Shorter's brevity here means the BLC is primarily measured against the WLC for this question's content.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XIX (Of the Law of God) — The 1689 does not specifically address the duties of equals. Chapter XIX establishes that the moral law, summarized in the Ten Commandments, remains binding on all believers:
> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation." (XIX.5)

- Omissions: N/A — the Confession does not treat the duties of equals specifically, so there is nothing to omit.
- Structural changes: None.

The BLC is consistent with the Confession's teaching that the moral law binds believers and that the fifth commandment comprehends duties in all human relations.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q131:
> "What are the duties of equals? A. The duties of equals are, to regard the dignity and worth of each other, in giving honour to go one before another; and to rejoice in each others' gifts and advancement, as their own."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected, none found — this is an area of moral theology with no Baptist-Presbyterian distinction.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - "as if their own" (BLC) vs. "as their own" (WLC): The WLC's formulation directly identifies the believer's joy with another's advancement — it IS their own joy. The BLC's "as if" introduces a hypothetical element, subtly suggesting the identification is merely analogical rather than genuine. This weakens the force of Philippians 2:3-4 and Romans 12:15-16.
  - "successes" (BLC) vs. "advancement" (WLC): "Advancement" is broader, encompassing promotion, growth, increase in any form. "Successes" connotes specific achievements or outcomes, which is narrower.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "giving honour to go one before another" replaced with "honoring each other above themselves" — this is a reasonable modernization that captures the same concept from Romans 12:10, though the WLC's phrasing more precisely conveys the idea of deferring precedence.
  - "advancement" replaced with "successes" — a narrowing (see above).

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC restructures the WLC's answer slightly: where the WLC uses "in giving honour to go one before another" as the means of regarding dignity, the BLC uses "by honoring each other above themselves" — this preserves the essential idea but is arguably clearer in modern English.
- The BLC's "pay attention to" for "regard" is a natural modernization, though "regard" carries a stronger connotation of esteem and consideration than "pay attention to," which can suggest mere notice.
- The BLC does not include the companion question on the *sins* of equals (WLC Q132), which names specific violations: undervaluing worth, envying gifts, grieving at advancement, and usurping pre-eminence. This is a structural choice for the catechism as a whole and not a deficiency within Q120 itself.

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 Peter 2:17** ("Honour all men"): Matches WLC footnote 710 for the duty to regard the dignity of others. Well chosen.
- **Romans 12:10** ("in honour preferring one another"): Matches WLC footnote 711. This is the key text for the duty of deferring honor.
- **Romans 12:15-16** ("Rejoice with them that do rejoice... Be of the same mind"): Matches WLC footnote 712. Supports the duty to rejoice in others' blessings.
- **Philippians 2:3-4** ("in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves"): Also from WLC footnote 712. Supports the humility and other-regard required of equals.

The BLC's proof texts are well selected and closely follow the WLC's scriptural support. No proof texts are missing or misapplied.
