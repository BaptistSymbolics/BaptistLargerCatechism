# Q53: How was Christ exalted in His resurrection?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q53 is a close modernization of WLC Q52, faithfully preserving all doctrinal elements of Christ's exaltation in His resurrection. The content aligns well with the 1689 Confession's treatment of the resurrection in Chapter VIII.4 and expands the Shorter Catechism's summary statement on the resurrection as the first element of exaltation. No omissions, contradictions, or problematic structural changes are found. Minor phrasing updates ("vanquished" → "conquered," "quick" → "living") are appropriate modernizations that preserve the theological meaning.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Wherein consisteth Christ's exaltation?
> A. Christ's exaltation consisteth in his rising again from the dead on the third day (1 Cor. 15:4), in ascending up into heaven (Mark 16:19), in sitting at the right hand of God the Father (Eph. 1:20), and in coming to judge the world at the last day (Acts 1:11; 17:31).

The Shorter Catechism treats Christ's exaltation as a single question covering all four states (resurrection, ascension, session, return). BLC Q53 expands the first element — "rising again from the dead on the third day" — into a full answer detailing the nature, declarations, and purposes of the resurrection. This is an appropriate expansion for a Larger Catechism and follows the same pattern as the WLC.

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's core affirmation ("rising again from the dead on the third day") is fully preserved and expanded.
- Structural changes: None problematic. The expansion from summary to detailed exposition follows the expected Shorter-to-Larger relationship.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter VIII, Paragraph 4:
> "This office the Lord Jesus did most willingly undertake, which that he might discharge he was made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it, and underwent the punishment due to us, which we should have born and suffered, being made Sin and a Curse for us: enduring most grievous sorrows in his Soul; and most painful sufferings in his body; was crucified, and died, and remained in the state of the dead; yet saw no corruption: on the third day he arose from the dead, with the same body in which he suffered; with which he also ascended into heaven: and there sitteth at the right hand of his Father, making intercession; and shall return to judge Men and Angels, at the end of the World."

The BLC's treatment is fully consistent with the 1689's affirmations about the resurrection:

| 1689 Element | BLC Coverage |
|---|---|
| "saw no corruption" | ✅ "not having seen corruption in death" |
| "on the third day he arose from the dead" | ✅ "He rose again from the dead on the third day" |
| "with the same body in which he suffered" | ✅ "having the very same body in which He suffered with all its essential properties thereof" |

- Omissions: None from the 1689's resurrection content. The BLC actually adds considerable theological content beyond what the 1689 states about the resurrection itself (the declarations and purposes), which is appropriate catechetical expansion.
- Structural changes: None. The 1689 treats the resurrection within a narrative of Christ's mediatorial work; the BLC isolates and exposits the resurrection as a distinct aspect of exaltation, which is complementary rather than contradictory.
- The BLC's note about the body being "truly united to His soul" and "without mortality and other common infirmities associated with this life" is theologically consonant with the 1689's broader Christological framework and with Chapter XXXI.2's affirmation that the dead "shall be raised up with the self same bodies, and none other; although with different qualities."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q52:
> Q. 52. How was Christ exalted in his resurrection?
> A. Christ was exalted in his resurrection, in that, not having seen corruption in death, (of which it was not possible for him to be held,) and having the very same body in which he suffered, with the essential properties thereof, (but without mortality, and other common infirmities belonging to this life,) really united to his soul, he rose again from the dead the third day by his own power; whereby he declared himself to be the Son of God, to have satisfied divine justice, to have vanquished death, and him that had the power of it, and to be Lord of quick and dead: all which he did as a public person, the head of his church, for their justification, quickening in grace, support against enemies, and to assure them of their resurrection from the dead at the last day.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- The BLC is a very close modernization of WLC Q52, preserving virtually every clause and theological relationship.

- Intentional Baptist divergences: None in this question. The resurrection doctrine is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Sectioning for proof texts**: The BLC breaks the answer into 14 individually cited sections, which is more granular than the WLC's single-paragraph format. This is a structural choice for the BLC's TOML format and does not alter the theology. Each section maps clearly to a portion of the WLC's answer.

2. **"Plainly declared"**: The BLC adds the word "plainly" to "declared himself to be the Son of God." This is a minor interpretive addition that strengthens the force of the resurrection as a declaration but does not alter the theology. It could be seen as reflecting the "with power" of Romans 1:4.

3. **"In order to"**: The BLC introduces "in order to" before the list of purposes (justify, make alive, support, assure), making the purposive/teleological structure more explicit than the WLC's compressed construction. This is a helpful modernization.

4. **"Believers" for "their"**: The BLC makes explicit that the beneficiaries of justification are "believers" rather than using the pronoun "their" (which in the WLC refers back to the church). This is a clarifying expansion consistent with the theology of both standards.
