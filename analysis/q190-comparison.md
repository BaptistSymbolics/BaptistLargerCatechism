# Q190: For what do we pray in the sixth petition?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q190 is a faithful and thorough modernization of WLC Q195 on the sixth petition of the Lord's Prayer. The BLC preserves the WLC's extensive structure covering acknowledgment of God's sovereignty over temptation, the threefold source of temptation, human inability, and the progressive petitions for preservation, support, recovery, and final deliverance. The Shorter Catechism's concise two-part summary is fully encompassed within the BLC's expanded treatment.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

"Q. What do we pray for in the sixth petition? A. In the sixth petition, which is, And lead us not into temptation but deliver us from evil (Mt. 6:13), we pray that God would either keep us from being tempted to sin (Mt. 26:31), or support and deliver us when we are tempted (2 Cor. 12:8)."

- Omissions: None. The Shorter provides a concise two-part summary (keep us from temptation, or support us in it), and the BLC fully encompasses both parts while expanding significantly.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC preserves the Shorter's two-fold structure (prevention or support) and adds a third element from the WLC: recovery after falling. This is faithful expansion, not restructuring.
- The BLC's expanded treatment elaborates the Shorter's compact answer into a detailed theology of temptation that is fully consistent with the Shorter's summary.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** The 1689 does not contain a specific treatment of the Lord's Prayer petitions. The most relevant sections are:

Chapter V (Of Divine Providence), which teaches that God "most wisely and powerfully" governs all creatures and their actions, and that even the sinful actions of creatures proceed "not from his creating influence, but from the free will of the creature being left to itself."

Chapter IX (Of Free Will), which teaches that fallen man is "utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all good, and wholly inclined to all evil."

Chapter XIII (Of Sanctification), which teaches that sanctification is "throughout in the whole man, yet imperfect in this life; there abideth still some remnants of corruption in every part."

- Omissions: N/A -- the Confession does not address the sixth petition directly.
- Structural changes: N/A
- The BLC's teaching on human inability apart from grace, the threefold source of temptation, and the need for divine support in sanctification is fully consistent with the Confession's doctrines of providence, human will, and progressive sanctification.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q195 -- "What do we pray for in the sixth petition?"

The WLC answer covers: (1) acknowledging God's wise, righteous, and gracious ordering of circumstances where we face temptation; (2) the threefold enemy -- Satan, the world, and the flesh; (3) our corruption, weakness, and want of watchfulness even after pardon; (4) our inability to resist or recover from temptation on our own; (5) our desert of being left under temptation's power; (6) petitions that God would overrule the world, subdue the flesh, restrain Satan, order all things, bestow and bless means of grace, and quicken watchfulness; (7) that we be kept from temptation, or if tempted, supported and enabled to stand; (8) or if fallen, raised and recovered with sanctified use of the experience; (9) that sanctification and salvation be perfected, Satan trodden under foot, and we fully freed from sin and temptation and all evil forever.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**. The BLC follows the WLC's structure very closely, covering each element in the same order with modernized language.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. The sixth petition is an area of complete Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None significant.
- Key phrases preserved: The BLC retains the WLC's distinctive progression: acknowledgment of God's sovereignty, threefold enemy, human inability, progressive petitions (kept from temptation / supported in it / recovered from falling), sanctified use of experience, and final deliverance.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's "the flesh" is rendered as "our own sinful natures" -- a faithful modernization. Minor wording differences throughout, but no theological content lost.

## Detailed Notes
This is one of the longest answers in both the WLC and BLC. The BLC's modernization is well-executed, converting the WLC's dense 17th-century prose into clear contemporary English while preserving the theological architecture. The progression from acknowledgment (God's sovereignty over temptation, our weakness) to petition (prevention, support, recovery, final deliverance) is faithfully maintained.

One notable feature: the BLC changes the WLC's "the evil one" framing to match Matthew 6:13 more explicitly ("deliver us from the evil one"), which is a legitimate textual choice. The BLC also adds "temporarily taken captive by them" in the acknowledgment section, which is a helpful clarification that the WLC implies but does not state as explicitly.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts track closely with the WLC's references. Key parallels:
- 2 Chronicles 32:31 and Job 2:6 (God's sovereignty over temptation)
- 1 Chronicles 21:1 and 1 Peter 5:8 (Satan as tempter)
- Luke 21:34 and Mark 4:19 (the world as source of temptation)
- James 1:14 (our own desires as source)
- Galatians 5:17 and Romans 7:18 (remaining corruption)
- Matthew 26:41 (weakness and watchfulness)
- Psalm 51:10,12 (divine renewal and restoration)
- Romans 16:20 (Satan crushed under foot)
- John 17:15 and 1 Thessalonians 5:23 (final deliverance)

The BLC adds several proof texts not in the WLC (e.g., Hebrews 2:18, Ephesians 3:14-17, Hebrews 13:20-21, 1 Peter 1:6-7) that strengthen the scriptural case. No proof texts appear misapplied.
