# Q126: What is the seventh commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q126 faithfully states the seventh commandment, directly paralleling the Shorter Catechism and WLC Q137. The only difference from these standards is the modernized translation ("You shall not commit adultery" for "Thou shalt not commit adultery"), which is the expected and appropriate English update. The proof text (Exodus 20:14) is identical across all three documents. This is a simple, definitional question with no theological content to omit or restructure.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. Which is the seventh commandment? A. The seventh commandment is, Thou shalt not commit adultery (Ex. 20:14)."
- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves the complete content of the Shorter's answer.
- Structural changes: None. Both present the question and commandment quotation in identical structure.
- The translation from "Thou shalt not" to "You shall not" is a standard modernization of the second-person pronoun and verb form, with no change in meaning. Unlike the sixth commandment (where "kill" was updated to "murder" for precision), the key term "adultery" is retained identically.
- Proof texts: Both cite Exodus 20:14 — identical.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraph 2 (Of the Law of God) — "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables; the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man."

Also relevant: Chapter 25 (Of Marriage), which treats the positive institution that the seventh commandment protects:
> "1. Marriage is to be between one Man and one Woman; neither is it lawful for any man to have more then one Wife, nor for any Woman to have more then one Husband at the same time."
> "2. Marriage was ordained for the mutual help of Husband and Wife, for the increase of Man-kind, with a legitimate issue, and for preventing of uncleanness."

- Omissions: N/A — the Confession does not expound individual commandments specifically.
- Structural changes: N/A.
- The BLC's statement of the seventh commandment is fully consistent with the 1689's affirmation that the moral law was "delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments," and with the Confession's teaching on marriage (Chapter 25) which gives the positive institution undergirding this commandment, including that marriage was ordained in part "for preventing of uncleanness."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 137. Which is the seventh commandment? A. The seventh commandment is, Thou shalt not commit adultery."
- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC tracks the WLC exactly in structure, differing only in modernized English ("You shall not" for "Thou shalt not") and question wording ("What is" for "Which is").
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this is an area where no Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement exists.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The only difference is translation modernization, which is expected and appropriate for the BLC's stated purpose.
- Key phrases dropped: None. The WLC answer is a single sentence quoting the commandment, and the BLC preserves this in full.

## Detailed Notes
- This is a purely definitional question — it simply states the commandment text. There is no theological exposition to evaluate for omissions or structural changes. The real theological work happens in the subsequent questions about what the seventh commandment requires and forbids (paralleling WLC Q138-139 and the Shorter Catechism's treatment of the duties required and sins forbidden).
- The WLC's own Q138 expands the positive duties to include "chastity in body, mind, affections, words, and behavior; and the preservation of it in ourselves and others; watchfulness over the eyes and all the senses; temperance, keeping of chaste company, modesty in apparel; marriage by those that have not the gift of continency, conjugal love, and cohabitation; diligent labor in our callings; shunning all occasions of uncleanness, and resisting temptations thereunto." None of this content belongs in Q126, which merely identifies the commandment text.
- The Shorter Catechism's parallel questions on what the seventh commandment requires ("the preservation of our own and our neighbors chastity, in heart, speech, and behavior") and forbids ("all unchaste thoughts, words, and actions") would be evaluated in the BLC's corresponding expansion questions, not here.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Exodus 20:14** — The seventh commandment from the Decalogue. The prohibition of adultery is the summary negative command protecting the marriage covenant. This is the sole proof text cited by the BLC, the Shorter, and the WLC, which is entirely appropriate for a question that simply identifies the commandment text.
