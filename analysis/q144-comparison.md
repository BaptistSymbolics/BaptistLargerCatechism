# Q144: How is the Word made effectual to salvation?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q144 is a close modernization of WLC Q155 and faithfully expands the Shorter Catechism's more compact answer. The theological substance is well preserved. Two minor concerns arise: (1) the BLC says the Spirit "causes" the Word to enlighten rather than "maketh" it "an effectual means of enlightening," subtly shifting the emphasis from the Word-as-means to the Spirit acting alongside it; and (2) the phrase "corrupting influences" replaces "corruptions" (WLC) — "corruptions" in Reformed theology refers specifically to indwelling sin, while "corrupting influences" suggests external pressures, narrowing the scope.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. How is the word made effectual to salvation?
> A. The Spirit of God maketh the reading, but especially the preaching of the word, an effectual means of convincing and converting sinners, and of building them up in holiness and comfort through faith unto salvation. (Neh. 8:8; Acts 26:18; Ps. 19:8; Acts 20:32; Rom. 1:15,16; 10:13-17; 15:4; 1 Cor. 14:24,25; 1 Tim. 3:15-17)

- **Omissions:** The Shorter's phrase "converting sinners" is absent as a distinct term in the BLC. The BLC instead breaks the process into several steps (driving sinners out of themselves, drawing them to Christ, conforming to His image, subduing to His will), which collectively cover conversion but never use the word. This is an expansion from the WLC rather than a loss, but the compact Shorter phrase "converting" is not directly mirrored.
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter presents a compact two-phase structure: (1) convincing and converting sinners, (2) building them up in holiness and comfort through faith unto salvation. The BLC expands this into eight discrete sections. This is appropriate for a "Larger" catechism and follows the WLC's own expansion pattern.
- The Shorter's core elements — Spirit as agent, reading/preaching distinction, convincing, building up in holiness and comfort, through faith, unto salvation — are all present in the BLC.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter X (Of Effectual Calling), Paragraph 1:
> "Those whom God hath predestinated unto Life, he is pleased in his appointed, and accepted time, effectually to call by his word, and Spirit, out of that state of sin, and death, in which they are by nature, to grace and Salvation by Jesus Christ; inlightning their minds, spiritually, and savingly to understand the things of God; taking away their heart of stone, and giving unto them an heart of flesh; renewing their wills, and by his Almighty power determining them to that which is good, and effectually drawing them to Jesus Christ; yet so as they come most freely, being made willing by his Grace."

Chapter XIII (Of Sanctification), Paragraph 1:
> "They who are united to Christ, Effectually called, and regenerated, having a new heart, and a new Spirit created in them, through the vertue of Christ's death, and Resurrection; are also farther sanctified, really, and personally, through the same vertue, by his word and Spirit dwelling in them; the dominion of the whole body of sin is destroyed, and the several lusts thereof, are more and more weakned, and mortified; and they more and more quickened, and strengthned in all saving graces, to the practice of all true holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord."

Chapter XIV (Of Saving Faith), Paragraph 1:
> "The Grace of Faith, whereby the Elect are enabled to beleive to the saving of their souls, is the work of the Spirit of Christ in their hearts; and is ordinarily wrought by the Ministry of the Word; by which also, and by the administration of Baptisme, and the Lords Supper, Prayer and other Means appointed of God, it is increased, and strengthned."

- **Omissions:** None of substance. The BLC's scope is the efficacy of the Word specifically, not effectual calling broadly, so the 1689's language about predestination, heart of stone/flesh, and coming "most freely" are appropriately outside the scope of this question.
- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC aligns with the Confession's teaching that the Spirit works through the Word to enlighten, draw to Christ, sanctify, and strengthen believers.
- The 1689's emphasis on the Word and Spirit working together (Ch. XIII.1: "by his word and Spirit dwelling in them") is reflected in the BLC's opening: "The Spirit of God causes the reading and especially the preaching of the Word."
- The 1689's teaching on sanctification as progressive (Ch. XIII.1: "more and more weakned, and mortified... more and more quickened, and strengthned") is consistent with the BLC's multi-step description of the Spirit's ongoing work through the Word.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q155. How is the Word made effectual to salvation?
> A. The Spirit of God maketh the reading, but especially the preaching of the Word, an effectual means of enlightening, convincing, and humbling sinners; of driving them out of themselves, and drawing them unto Christ; of conforming them to his image, and subduing them to his will; of strengthening them against temptations and corruptions; of building them up in grace, and establishing their hearts in holiness and comfort through faith unto salvation.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC follows the WLC's structure almost exactly, preserving all eight elements in the same order.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected for this question (no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology at stake), and none found.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"causes" vs. "maketh...an effectual means"**: The WLC says the Spirit "maketh the reading...an effectual means of enlightening." The BLC says the Spirit "causes the reading...to enlighten." The WLC phrasing emphasizes that the Word itself is the means (instrumentality), while the BLC phrasing could be read as the Spirit acting to produce enlightenment with the Word as occasion rather than instrument. This is a subtle but real shift in emphasis away from the means-of-grace framework.
  2. **"corrupting influences" vs. "corruptions"**: The WLC reads "strengthening them against temptations and corruptions." The BLC reads "strengthens them against temptations and corrupting influences." In Reformed theology, "corruptions" (Latin: *corruptiones*) refers specifically to the remaining indwelling sin in the believer (cf. 1689 XIII.2: "there abideth still some remnants of corruption in every part"). "Corrupting influences" shifts the reference toward external pressures, narrowing the scope and losing the internal dimension that the WLC intends.
  3. **"God's grace" vs. "grace"**: The WLC says "building them up in grace." The BLC says "builds them up in God's grace." This is a minor clarifying addition, theologically unobjectionable.
- **Key phrases dropped:** None entirely dropped. The full WLC structure is preserved.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "causes...to enlighten" formulation**: The WLC/Shorter deliberately use the phrase "maketh...an effectual means" because this upholds the Reformed doctrine that God ordinarily works *through* means, not merely *alongside* them. The Word is not merely the occasion of the Spirit's work but the instrument. The BLC's "causes...to enlighten" is not wrong but is less precise on this point. The Shorter Catechism's "maketh the reading...an effectual means of convincing and converting" makes the instrumentality of the Word explicit.

2. **"Corrupting influences" vs. "corruptions"**: This is the most notable change. The WLC proof text (footnote 998) cites Matthew 4:4,7,10 (Christ resisting temptation by Scripture), Ephesians 6:16-17 (armor of God), Psalm 19:11 (keeping God's Word), and 1 Corinthians 10:11 (written for our admonition). The BLC uses these same texts. But "corruptions" in the WLC encompasses both external temptations (covered by "temptations") and internal remaining sin. By changing "corruptions" to "corrupting influences," the BLC creates near-redundancy with "temptations" (both now point outward) and loses the internal reference to indwelling sin that the WLC intends as a distinct category.

3. **Addition of Acts 17:11-12**: The BLC adds this reference (the Bereans searching Scripture) to the first section on enlightenment. This is a helpful and appropriate addition not found in the WLC proof texts.

4. **Addition of Colossians 1:27 and 1:28**: These are good additions to the conforming/strengthening sections, emphasizing "Christ in you, the hope of glory" and the goal of presenting everyone mature in Christ.

5. **The eight-section structure**: The BLC breaks the WLC's single flowing answer into eight discrete sections, each with its own proof texts. This is pedagogically useful and preserves the WLC's logical order faithfully.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts substantially overlap with the WLC's while adding several helpful references:
- **Section 1** (enlightening): Nehemiah 8:8, Acts 26:18, Psalm 19:8 match WLC footnote 993. Acts 17:11-12 is a good addition (Berean example of Scripture's enlightening power).
- **Section 2** (convincing/humbling): 1 Corinthians 14:24-25, 2 Chronicles 34:18-19,26-28 match WLC footnote 994. Jeremiah 23:28-29 and Hebrews 4:12 are strong additions on the convicting power of the Word.
- **Section 3** (driving out/drawing to Christ): Acts 2:37-41 matches WLC footnote 995. Acts 8:27-39 matches as well.
- **Section 4** (conforming to image): 2 Corinthians 3:18 matches WLC footnote 996. Colossians 1:27 is added.
- **Section 5** (subduing to will): 2 Corinthians 10:4-6, Romans 6:17 match WLC footnote 997.
- **Section 6** (strengthening against temptation): Matthew 4:4,7,10; Ephesians 6:16-17; Psalm 19:11; 1 Corinthians 10:11 match WLC footnote 998. Colossians 1:28 is added.
- **Section 7** (building in grace): Acts 20:32, 2 Timothy 3:15-16 match WLC footnote 999. Ephesians 4:11-12 and 1 Corinthians 3:9-11 are added.
- **Section 8** (establishing in holiness): Romans 16:25, 1 Thessalonians 3:2,10-13, Romans 15:4, Romans 10:13-17, Romans 1:16 match WLC footnote 1000.

The proof text work is thorough and well-aligned with the WLC, with several valuable additions. No proof texts are misused or misapplied.
