# Q117: What are the particular sins against those in authority by those under them?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q117 is a close modernization of WLC Q128, preserving the essential structure and theological content. However, two small changes weaken precision: the shift from "counsels" to "advice" loses some of the deliberative weight of the original, and the phrase "as reflected in" subtly reframes the grammatical relationship between the sins and "their lawful counsels, commands, and corrections," potentially narrowing the scope. The replacement of "government" with "authority" is defensible but loses a nuance present in both the WLC and the Shorter Catechism's broader fifth commandment framework.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** The Shorter Catechism has no specific question on sins of inferiors. The relevant broader question is:

> Q. What is forbidden in the fifth commandment?
> A. The fifth commandment forbiddeth the neglect of, or doing any thing against the honour and duty which belongeth to every one in their several places and relations (Mt. 15:4-6; Ez. 34:24; Rom. 13:8).

- Omissions: None significant. The BLC expands upon the Shorter's general principle with appropriate specificity, as a larger catechism should.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC correctly treats this as an expansion of the Shorter's general prohibition, detailing the particular sins of those in the "inferior" relation.
- The Shorter's phrase "honour and duty which belongeth to every one in their several places and relations" establishes the broad framework within which BLC Q117 operates. The BLC faithfully works within this framework.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God), Paragraph 5:
> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation."

Chapter 24 (Of the Civil Magistrate), Paragraph 3:
> "Civil Magistrates being set up by God, for the ends aforesaid; subjection in all lawful things commanded by them, ought to be yeilded by us, in the Lord; not only for wrath but for Conscience sake; and we ought to make supplications and prayers for Kings, and all that are in Authority, that under them we may live a quiet and peaceable life, in all godliness and honesty."

- Omissions: None. The 1689 does not provide a specific exposition of the fifth commandment's particular sins; it addresses the moral law generally and civil authority specifically. The BLC appropriately fills this catechetical role.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's treatment is consistent with the 1689's teaching that lawful authority derives from God and that subjection to it is a matter of conscience, not merely compulsion.
- The 1689's qualifier "in all lawful things" (Ch. 24.3) is echoed in the BLC's "lawful advice, commands, and correction," preserving the important limitation on the scope of obedience owed.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> Q. 128. What are the sins of inferiors against their superiors?
> A. The sins of inferiors against their superiors are, all neglect of the duties required toward them; envying at, contempt of, and rebellion against, their persons and places, in their lawful counsels, commands, and corrections; cursing, mocking and all such refractory and scandalous carriage, as proves a shame and dishonour to them and their government.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected or present. This question concerns the moral law's application to human authority relations, which is common ground between Baptists and Presbyterians.
- Unexpected divergences: None that rise to the level of theological concern.
- Key phrases dropped or altered:
  1. **"counsels" → "advice"**: "Counsels" carries a weightier, more deliberative connotation than "advice." While "advice" is a valid modernization, it slightly weakens the gravity of the original. This is a minor precision loss.
  2. **"places" → "position"**: Acceptable modernization. "Position" adequately captures the WLC's meaning of one's station or office.
  3. **"refractory and scandalous carriage" → "stubborn resistance and disgraceful display"**: Acceptable modernization. "Refractory" maps well to "stubborn resistance" and "scandalous carriage" to "disgraceful display."
  4. **"government" → "authority"**: The WLC's "government" refers to the broader exercise of rule and governance — how superiors administer their charge. "Authority" is narrower, focusing on the right to rule rather than the exercise of it. This is a minor precision loss.
  5. **"as reflected in their lawful advice, commands, and correction"**: The WLC's grammar links "their persons and places" to "in their lawful counsels, commands, and corrections" — meaning that the rebellion is against persons and places *as exercised in* their lawful counsels, commands, and corrections. The BLC's "as reflected in" is a reasonable rendering of this grammatical relationship, though it could be read as suggesting the sins are against persons/position only insofar as those are "reflected in" the counsels — potentially narrowing the scope.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC maintains the WLC's careful structure: (1) general neglect of duty, (2) specific sins of envy/contempt/rebellion directed at persons and their offices, (3) these sins manifested in resistance to lawful directives, (4) cursing and mocking, (5) a general catch-all for shameful behavior.
- The BLC's Scripture references largely parallel the WLC's traditional proof texts, with some variation. The BLC provides verse-by-verse references for each clause rather than footnoted references to the whole answer.
- The singular "correction" (BLC) vs. plural "corrections" (WLC) is insignificant.
- The question wording itself differs: BLC asks about "particular sins against those in authority by those under them" while WLC asks about "sins of inferiors against their superiors." The BLC's phrasing avoids the older terminology of "inferiors/superiors" while preserving the relational concept. This is appropriate modernization.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Matthew 15:4-6** (failure to perform duties): Matches WLC proof text for neglect of duties. Jesus rebukes the Pharisees for using Corban to avoid honoring parents — an appropriate text.
- **Numbers 11:28-29; Psalm 106:16** (envying): Strong texts. Numbers 11 shows Joshua's envy regarding the Spirit resting on Eldad and Medad; Psalm 106 records Israel's envy of Moses and Aaron.
- **1 Samuel 8:7; Isaiah 3:5** (contempt): Appropriate. 1 Samuel 8:7 shows contempt for God's appointed governance; Isaiah 3:5 describes social inversion and contempt for elders.
- **2 Samuel 15:1-12** (rebellion): Absalom's rebellion against David — a paradigmatic text for rebellion against lawful authority.
- **Exodus 21:15; 1 Samuel 10:27** (against person/position): Exodus 21:15 addresses striking a parent; 1 Samuel 10:27 shows "sons of Belial" despising Saul's kingship. Both apt.
- **1 Samuel 2:25** (lawful advice): The sons of Eli refusing to heed their father's counsel — directly appropriate.
- **Deuteronomy 21:18-21** (commands and correction): The stubborn and rebellious son statute — the classic OT text for this sin.
- **Proverbs 30:11, 17** (cursing/mocking): Directly relevant proverbs about cursing parents and mocking authority.
- **Proverbs 19:26** (shameful behavior): "He who mistreats his father and chases away his mother is a son who brings shame and disgrace" — fitting conclusion text.
- Overall, the proof texts are well-chosen and appropriate. They cover familial, ecclesiastical, and civil authority, consistent with the fifth commandment's broad scope.
