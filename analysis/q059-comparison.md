# Q59: What is effectual calling?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
The BLC's answer on effectual calling draws heavily from both the WLC and the 1689 Confession but omits a critical element from its primary standard, the Shorter Catechism: "convincing us of our sin and misery." This is a significant doctrinal omission because conviction of sin is the necessary precondition for the sinner's reception of Christ — without it, enlightenment and will-renewal have no intelligible object. Additionally, the BLC drops the WLC's "powerfully determining their wills" and the 1689's parallel language about God "by his Almighty power determining them to that which is good," weakening the confession's emphasis on divine monergism in the renewal of the will. The BLC also omits the 1689's explicit teaching that "the Creature being wholly passive therein" and that the effectual call operates by "no less power than that which raised up Christ from the dead."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is effectual calling?
> A. Effectual calling is the work of God's Spirit, whereby convincing us of our sin and misery, enlightening our minds in the knowledge of Christ, and renewing our wills, he doth persuade and enable us to embrace Jesus Christ freely offered to us in the gospel (2 Tim. 1:9; 2 Thess. 2:13, 14; Acts 2:37; Acts 26:18; Ez. 36:26, 27; John 6:44, 45; Phil. 2:13).

**Agreement:**
- Both identify effectual calling as a divine work (the Shorter attributes it to "God's Spirit"; the BLC to "God's almighty power and grace")
- Both include enlightening minds in the knowledge of Christ
- Both include renewing wills
- Both describe the result as the sinner being enabled/made willing to respond

**Omissions:**
- **"Convincing us of our sin and misery"** — This is the most significant omission. The Shorter Catechism presents a three-fold work: (1) convincing of sin and misery, (2) enlightening the mind in knowledge of Christ, (3) renewing the will. The BLC drops the first element entirely. This is not a minor omission — conviction of sin is the subjective preparation that gives the enlightenment and will-renewal their proper context. Without it, the order of salvation loses its experiential and logical coherence. The proof text Acts 2:37 (the pricking of conscience at Pentecost) that the Shorter cites for this element has no counterpart in the BLC's scripture references.
- **"Freely offered to us in the gospel"** — The Shorter specifies that Christ is "freely offered to us in the gospel," tying the offer of Christ to the instrumentality of the gospel. The BLC changes this to "the grace offered and conveyed," which retains the concept of offer but shifts from Christ being offered in the gospel to grace being offered and conveyed more generically. This is a subtle but notable structural change.

**Structural Changes:**
- The Shorter attributes effectual calling simply to "God's Spirit." The BLC, following the WLC, expands this to "God's almighty power and grace" and adds the entire parenthetical about God's "free and special love to His elect and not from anything inherently in them." This is an expansion, not a structural change, and is drawn from the 1689 Confession Chapter 10.2 and the WLC. This expansion is appropriate for a Larger Catechism.
- The Shorter's verb is "persuade and enable us to embrace Jesus Christ." The BLC changes this to "willing and freely able to answer His call voluntarily and to gladly accept the grace offered and conveyed." The Shorter's "persuade and enable" language is dropped. While the BLC's language conveys a similar result, the Shorter's dual verb ("persuade and enable") more precisely captures the Reformed understanding that God both changes the disposition (persuade) and grants the capacity (enable).

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 10, Paragraphs 1-2

**Chapter 10.1:**
> "Those whom God hath predestinated unto Life, he is pleased in his appointed, and accepted time, effectually to call by his word, and Spirit, out of that state of sin, and death, in which they are by nature, to grace and Salvation by Jesus Christ; inlightning their minds, spiritually, and savingly to understand the things of God; taking away their heart of stone, and giving unto them an heart of flesh; renewing their wills, and by his Almighty power determining them to that which is good, and effectually drawing them to Jesus Christ; yet so as they come most freely, being made willing by his Grace."

**Chapter 10.2:**
> "This Effectual Call is of God's free, and special grace alone, not from any thing at all foreseen in man, nor from any power, or agency in the Creature, coworking with his special Grace, the Creature being wholly passive therein, being dead in sins and trespasses, until being quickned & renewed by the holy Spirit, he is thereby enabled to answer this call, and to embrace the Grace offered and conveyed in it; and that by no less power, then that which raised up Christ from the dead."

**Agreement:**
- The BLC follows the Confession's structure of calling by Word and Spirit, enlightening minds, renewing wills, and the result of free willing response
- The BLC's "out of His free and special love to His elect and not from anything inherently in them" faithfully captures 10.2's "of God's free, and special grace alone, not from any thing at all foreseen in man"
- The BLC's "even though they are dead in sin" reflects the Confession's "being dead in sins and trespasses"
- The BLC's "to gladly accept the grace offered and conveyed" echoes the Confession's "to embrace the Grace offered and conveyed in it"

**Omissions:**
- **"Out of that state of sin, and death, in which they are by nature"** — The Confession explicitly names the state from which the elect are called. The BLC mentions being "dead in sin" parenthetically but does not describe calling *out of* that state as a transition from one condition to another.
- **"Taking away their heart of stone, and giving unto them an heart of flesh"** — This vivid Ezekiel 36:26 imagery is in the Confession but absent from the BLC. The BLC's "renews and transforms their wills" is thinner than the Confession's fuller description of heart-replacement followed by will-renewal and powerful determination.
- **"By his Almighty power determining them to that which is good"** — The Confession's language of God "determining" the will to good is a strong monergistic statement that the BLC omits. The BLC says only "renews and transforms their wills" without the language of divine determination. This weakens the confession's emphasis on the irresistibility of grace in will-renewal.
- **"The Creature being wholly passive therein"** — The Confession explicitly teaches the creature's total passivity in effectual calling until quickened. The BLC omits this entirely. While the BLC does teach that God makes the sinner willing (implying prior inability), the Confession's explicit denial of creaturely cooperation is a stronger and more precise formulation that guards against synergism.
- **"Nor from any power, or agency in the Creature, coworking with his special Grace"** — The Confession's explicit anti-synergistic formula is absent. The BLC's "not from anything inherently in them" addresses the *cause* of election but not the *process* of calling — it doesn't explicitly deny creaturely cooperation in the act of being called.
- **"By no less power than that which raised up Christ from the dead"** — The Confession's dramatic comparison of effectual calling to resurrection power is absent from the BLC's answer, though Ephesians 1:18-20 is cited in the proof texts. This comparison powerfully reinforces the monergistic character of calling.

**Structural Changes:**
- The Confession places "to grace and Salvation by Jesus Christ" as the goal of effectual calling. The BLC instead describes the calling as inviting and drawing "to Jesus Christ" — which is close but lacks the explicit mention of "salvation" as the destination.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

**WLC Q67:**
> Q. What is effectual calling?
> A. Effectual calling is the work of God's almighty power and grace, whereby (out of his free and special love to his elect, and from nothing in them moving him thereunto) he doth, in his accepted time, invite and draw them to Jesus Christ, by his Word and Spirit; savingly enlightening their minds, renewing and powerfully determining their wills, so as they (although in themselves dead in sin) are hereby made willing and able freely to answer his call, and to accept and embrace the grace offered and conveyed therein.

- **Solidarity: STRONG** — The BLC is clearly modeled on the WLC's answer, following its structure almost exactly. The BLC is essentially a modernized paraphrase of the WLC.

**Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent. This is a question where Baptist and Presbyterian theology fully agree. There is no baptismal, ecclesiological, or covenant-theological content to diverge on.

**Unexpected divergences:**
- **"Powerfully determining their wills"** → BLC has only "renews and transforms their wills." The WLC's "powerfully determining" is a significantly stronger statement about God's sovereign action on the will than the BLC's "transforms." This language is drawn from the 1689 Confession itself ("by his Almighty power determining them to that which is good"), so its absence in the BLC cannot be attributed to a Baptist-Presbyterian difference. This is the BLC departing from *both* its Baptist and Presbyterian standards simultaneously.
- **"And from nothing in them moving him thereunto"** → BLC has "not from anything inherently in them." The WLC's phrase ("nothing in them moving him") is more dynamic, denying that anything in the creature *motivates* God's calling. The BLC's "inherently in them" could be read more narrowly as denying only inherent qualities, potentially leaving room for foreseen responses. This is likely an artifact of modernization rather than an intentional theological shift, but the precision is weakened.
- **"Able freely to answer his call"** → BLC has "freely able to answer His call voluntarily." The BLC adds "voluntarily" and "gladly" which are not in the WLC. While these additions are theologically unobjectionable (the Confession says "most freely" and "made willing"), they shift the emphasis from God's enabling to the quality of the human response.

**Key phrases dropped:**
- **"Powerfully determining their wills"** — Dropped without theological reason (see above)
- **"From nothing in them moving him thereunto"** — Replaced with weaker formulation
- The WLC's "hereby" (connecting God's sovereign work to the result) is absent, slightly weakening the causal link

## Detailed Notes
1. **The omission of conviction of sin is the most serious issue.** The Shorter Catechism's three-fold pattern (conviction → enlightenment → will-renewal) represents a careful theological ordering. Conviction of sin creates the felt need that enlightenment in Christ addresses, and will-renewal enables the sinner to act on that knowledge. By dropping conviction, the BLC presents enlightenment and will-renewal without the experiential ground that makes the gospel intelligible as good news. This is not merely a stylistic choice — it omits a distinct work of the Spirit that all three standards (Shorter, Confession implicitly, and the broader Reformed tradition) affirm.

2. **The weakening of "determining" to "transforms" is doctrinally significant.** The 1689 Confession (10.1) says God "by his Almighty power determining them to that which is good," and the WLC says "powerfully determining their wills." The BLC's "renews and transforms" does not carry the same weight of divine sovereignty over the will. "Transform" suggests a change of character; "determine" asserts God's sovereign direction of the will to its proper object. The difference matters for the doctrine of irresistible grace.

3. **The omission of creaturely passivity.** The 1689's explicit "the Creature being wholly passive therein" is a polemical statement against Arminian synergism. Its absence from the BLC is notable, particularly given that the BLC is supposed to exposit the 1689. While the BLC does not contradict this teaching, it fails to confess it.

4. **The BLC adds elements not in the Shorter or WLC.** The phrase "at the right time" captures the Confession's "appointed and accepted time" and the WLC's "accepted time" — this is an appropriate expansion. The addition of "by His Word and Spirit" to the Shorter's simpler "God's Spirit" is drawn from the Confession and WLC and is a legitimate Larger Catechism expansion. The phrase "invites and draws" nicely captures the WLC's "invite and draw."

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are generally appropriate and overlap significantly with both the Shorter and WLC:

- **Ezekiel 37:9-14; John 5:25; Ephesians 1:18-20; 2 Timothy 1:8-9** — For the opening clause on power and grace. Eph 1:18-20 is notable because it contains the resurrection-power comparison that the BLC's *text* omits but the *proof texts* retain. This suggests the author was aware of the Confession's teaching but chose not to include it in the catechism answer itself.
- **Titus 3:4-5; Ephesians 2:4-9; Romans 9:11; Deuteronomy 9:5** — For God's free love, not from anything in the elect. Good selections.
- **John 3:5; Titus 3:5; 2 Corinthians 5:20; 6:1-2; John 6:44-45; Acts 16:14; 2 Thessalonians 2:13-14** — For calling by Word and Spirit. John 6:44-45 is shared with the Shorter (for "persuade and enable"). 2 Cor 5:20 picks up the gospel-offer dimension.
- **Acts 26:18; 1 Corinthians 2:10-12; 2 Corinthians 4:6; Ephesians 1:17-18** — For enlightening minds. Acts 26:18 is shared with the Shorter.
- **Ezekiel 11:19; 36:26-27; John 6:45** — For renewing wills. Ez 36:26-27 is shared with the Shorter and the Confession.
- **Ephesians 2:5; Philippians 2:13; Deuteronomy 30:6; Isaiah 45:22; Matthew 11:28-30; Revelation 22:17** — For the final clause on being made willing. Phil 2:13 is shared with the Shorter.

**Missing proof text:** The Shorter cites **Acts 2:37** for conviction of sin and misery ("they were pricked in their heart"). This text is entirely absent from the BLC, confirming that the omission of conviction is not merely a wording change but a deliberate exclusion of the concept.

**Notable addition:** The BLC adds **Matthew 11:28-30** ("Come unto me, all ye that labour") and **Revelation 22:17** ("whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely") as proof texts for the final clause. These emphasize the invitation and willingness aspects, which is consistent with the BLC's tendency to emphasize the human response side of effectual calling. While these texts are not inappropriate, their selection — combined with the omission of conviction language — subtly shifts the emphasis from God's sovereign, monergistic work to the experiential quality of the human response.
