# Q27: How is original sin conveyed from our first parents unto their posterity?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q27 is a verbatim reproduction of WLC Q26, addressing the specific mechanism by which original sin passes from Adam to his descendants: natural generation. The answer is fully consistent with the 1689 Confession's teaching (Chapter 6.3) and does not omit any doctrinal content within its proper scope. The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question on conveyance, though related content appears in its question on mankind falling in Adam.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest is:
> "Q. Did all mankind fall in Adam's first transgression?
> A. The covenant being made with Adam, not only for himself but for his posterity, all mankind descending from him by ordinary generation sinned in him, and fell with him in his first transgression." (Gen. 2:16, 17; Rom. 5:12; 1 Cor. 15:21, 22)

- Omissions: None applicable (no direct parallel question exists). The Shorter's related answer addresses the *basis* of the fall (covenant headship, ordinary generation, sinning "in him"), while the BLC question specifically addresses the *mechanism of conveyance* of original sin as a distinct corruption. These are complementary rather than overlapping concerns.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC appropriately treats the conveyance of original sin as a separate question from federal headship and imputation, consistent with the WLC's more detailed structure.
- The Shorter's phrase "ordinary generation" is echoed in the BLC's "natural generation" — these are synonymous.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 6, Paragraph 3:
> "They being the root, and by Gods appointment, standing in the room, and stead of all mankind; the guilt of the Sin was imputed, and corrupted nature conveyed, to all their posterity descending from them by ordinary generation, being now conceived in Sin, and by nature children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal, unless the Lord Jesus set them free."

- Omissions: None within the scope of this question. The 1689's paragraph 3 addresses multiple doctrines in a single passage: (1) federal headship ("root...standing in the room and stead"), (2) imputation of guilt, (3) conveyance of corrupted nature, and (4) resulting consequences. BLC Q27 properly isolates element (3) — the conveyance mechanism — which is the narrow scope of the question. The other elements are covered by surrounding BLC questions (e.g., imputation and guilt in Q25-26, consequences in Q28ff).
- Structural changes: None. The BLC faithfully extracts the conveyance doctrine.
- The BLC's "natural generation" corresponds to the 1689's "ordinary generation." The BLC's "conceived and born in sin" directly reflects the 1689's "conceived in Sin."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q26:
> "Q. 26. How is original sin conveyed from our first parents unto their posterity?
> A. Original sin is conveyed from our first parents unto their posterity by natural generation, so as all that proceed from them in that way are conceived and born in sin."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC answer is verbatim identical to the WLC Q26 answer. Even the question wording is identical (with only the numbering shifted from Q26 to Q27).
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This doctrine involves no baptismal, ecclesiological, or covenant theology distinctions between Baptists and Presbyterians.
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None — perfect reproduction.

## Detailed Notes
- This is one of the clearest cases of direct WLC adoption in the BLC. The question and answer are word-for-word identical.
- The doctrine of original sin's conveyance through natural generation is a point of complete agreement between Baptist and Presbyterian confessional traditions. The 1689 and Westminster Confessions use virtually identical language on this point.
- The BLC's proof texts (Psalm 51:5; Job 14:4; Job 15:14; John 3:6) overlap significantly with the 1689's citations for this section (Ps. 51:5; Job 14:4) while adding Job 15:14 and John 3:6 for additional scriptural support.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Psalm 51:5** ("Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me") — Classic proof text for original sin from conception; cited by both BLC and 1689.
- **Job 14:4** ("Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? not one.") — Supports the impossibility of sinless offspring from sinful parents; cited by both BLC and 1689.
- **Job 15:14** ("What is man, that he should be clean? and he which is born of a woman, that he should be righteous?") — Additional support for universal sinfulness from birth; BLC addition beyond the 1689's citations.
- **John 3:6** ("That which is born of the flesh is flesh") — Supports the doctrine that natural generation produces sinful nature; BLC addition providing New Testament support for the doctrine.
- The proof texts are appropriate and well-chosen, covering both Old and New Testaments and supporting the specific claim about natural generation as the means of conveyance.
