# Q188: For what do we pray in the fourth petition?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q188 faithfully follows the WLC Q193 structure and content, preserving all major theological elements of the fourth petition's exposition. The Shorter Catechism's core content is fully preserved and expanded. Two minor concerns exist: (1) the WLC's phrase "in the use of lawful means" is replaced with a weaker formulation, and (2) the WLC's "holy and comfortable use" becomes "sanctified by us," which slightly shifts the theological emphasis.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What do we pray for in the fourth petition?
> A. In the fourth petition, which is, Give us this day our daily bread, we pray that of God's free gift we may receive a competent portion of the good things of this life, and enjoy his blessing with them.

- Omissions: None. All core elements of the Shorter are preserved and expanded as expected for a larger catechism.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC properly expands the Shorter's compact answer into a fuller exposition while preserving the same theological architecture.
- The Shorter's key phrase "of God's free gift" is preserved in the BLC ("his free gift").
- The Shorter's "competent portion of the good things of this life" is preserved as "what is sufficient for us" and "sufficient worldly goods" -- a reasonable modernization of "competent portion."
- The Shorter's "enjoy his blessing with them" is expanded into multiple clauses about continued blessing, sanctification, contentment, and protection.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 5 (Of Divine Providence), Paragraph 1:
> "God the good Creator of all things, in his infinite power, and wisdom, doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all Creatures, and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence, to the end for the which they were Created; according unto his infallible foreknowledge, and the free and immutable Councel of his own will; to the praise of the glory of his wisdom, power, justice, infinite goodness and mercy."

Chapter 5, Paragraph 3:
> "God in his ordinary Providence maketh use of means; yet is free to work, without, above, and against them at his pleasure."

Chapter 5, Paragraph 7:
> "As the Providence of God doth in general reach to all Creatures, so after a most special manner it taketh care of his Church, and disposeth of all things to the good thereof."

Chapter 6 (Of the Fall), Paragraph 3:
> "They being the root, and by Gods appointment, standing in the room, and stead of all mankind; the guilt of the Sin was imputed, and corrupted nature conveyed, to all their posterity descending from them by ordinary generation, being now conceived in Sin, and by nature children of wrath, the servants of Sin, the subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal, unless the Lord Jesus set them free."

- Omissions: None of substance. The BLC's teaching on forfeiture of blessings through Adam's sin and our own sin aligns with the 1689's doctrine of the fall and its temporal consequences (Ch. 6.3).
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's framework of providential dependence aligns with the 1689's doctrine of providence (Ch. 5), and the acknowledgment of human inability and sinful tendencies aligns with the 1689's doctrine of total depravity (Ch. 6.3-4).
- The BLC's "God's providential allowance" and "his fatherly wisdom" reflects the 1689's teaching that providence is governed by God's "wise and holy" counsel (Ch. 5.1).
- The 1689's emphasis on God using means in providence (Ch. 5.3) provides the confessional backdrop for the BLC's teaching on lawful enjoyment of temporal blessings.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q193:
> "In the fourth petition, (which is, Give us this day our daily bread,) acknowledging, that in Adam, and by our own sin, we have forfeited our right to all the outward blessings of this life, and deserve to be wholly deprived of them by God, and to have them cursed to us in the use of them; and that neither they of themselves are able to sustain us, nor we to merit, or by our own industry to procure them; but prone to desire, get, and use them unlawfully: we pray for ourselves and others, that both they and we, waiting upon the providence of God from day to day in the use of lawful means, may, of his free gift, and as to his fatherly wisdom shall seem best, enjoy a competent portion of them; and have the same continued and blessed unto us in our holy and comfortable use of them, and contentment in them; and be kept from all things that are contrary to our temporal support and comfort."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology distinctives.
- Unexpected divergences: Two minor points noted below.
- Key phrases dropped:
  1. **"in the use of lawful means"** -- The WLC says we pray "waiting upon the providence of God from day to day **in the use of lawful means**." The BLC renders this as "wait daily on God's providential allowance of the outward blessings of this life and that...we may **lawfully enjoy** his free gift." The WLC's phrase teaches that we actively employ lawful means while waiting on providence; the BLC's phrasing shifts toward lawfully *enjoying* what God provides. This subtly weakens the WLC's emphasis on the Christian's duty to use legitimate industry as the ordinary means through which God provides. The concept is not entirely absent (Eph 4:28 and 2 Thess 3:11-12 in the proof texts address industrious labor), but the catechetical text itself does not state it as clearly.
  2. **"holy and comfortable use"** -- The WLC prays that blessings would be "continued and blessed unto us in our **holy and comfortable use** of them." The BLC renders this as blessings that "would sustain our needs and be **sanctified by us**." The WLC's "holy and comfortable" balances sanctification with legitimate enjoyment; the BLC's "sanctified by us" focuses only on the holiness dimension. However, the proof text (1 Tim 4:3-5) preserves the fuller meaning, and the BLC's "that we would be satisfied with them" partially recovers the comfort/enjoyment aspect.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Structural fidelity**: The BLC follows the WLC's exact two-part structure: (a) acknowledgment of human unworthiness and sinful tendencies, followed by (b) positive petitions. This is well-preserved.
2. **Modernization quality**: The BLC successfully modernizes the WLC's 17th-century English while preserving theological content. "Competent portion" becomes "what is sufficient for us" -- an appropriate update.
3. **Expanded negative section**: The BLC slightly expands the WLC's "prone to desire, get, and use them unlawfully" into three separate clauses ("lust after," "acquire," and "use them in unlawful ways"), each with individual proof texts. This is a helpful pedagogical expansion.
4. **"Temporal support and comfort"**: The WLC's closing phrase "kept from all things that are contrary to our temporal support and comfort" becomes "protect us from anything that undermines our support and sustenance in this world." The BLC drops "comfort" in favor of "sustenance," slightly narrowing the scope. However, this is a very minor point.
5. **Proof text alignment**: The BLC uses largely the same proof texts as the WLC. Notably, Proverbs 10:22 appears twice (once for "nor actually obtain them by our own efforts" and once for continued blessing), which mirrors and expands the WLC's usage. Psalm 90:17 and Psalm 144:12-15 are added, reinforcing the prayer for God's blessing on lawful labor.
6. **"For ourselves and others"**: The WLC's "we pray for ourselves and others, that both they and we" is preserved verbatim in substance in the BLC.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC preserves all major WLC proof text categories and adds a few supplementary references.
- **Shared proof texts**: Matt 6:11, Gen 2:17, Gen 3:17, Rom 8:20-22, Jer 5:25 (WLC has 5:25, BLC has 5:24-25), Deut 28:15ff, Deut 8:3, Gen 32:10, Deut 8:17-18, Jer 6:13, Mark 7:21-22, Hos 12:7, James 4:3, Gen 43:12-14, Gen 28:20-21, Eph 4:28, 2 Thess 3:11-12, Phil 4:6, 1 Tim 4:3-5, 1 Tim 6:6-8, Prov 30:8-9.
- **Added by BLC**: Luke 12:15 (covetousness), James 4:13-15 (submission to God's will), Psalm 90:17 (God establishing the work of our hands), Psalm 144:12-15 (prayer for temporal prosperity), Proverbs 10:22 (added for the blessing section), Lamentations 3:22 (God's mercies). These additions are all theologically appropriate and strengthen the proof text apparatus.
- **WLC proof text not in BLC**: The WLC has no unique proof texts that the BLC drops; rather, the BLC expands the proof text base.
