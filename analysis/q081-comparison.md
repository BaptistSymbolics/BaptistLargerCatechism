# Q81: What shall immediately follow after the resurrection?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q81 closely tracks WLC Q88 in its first section, faithfully affirming the general and final judgment of angels and men immediately after the resurrection. However, the second section diverges from the WLC by replacing its concise eschatological watchfulness language ("that all may watch and pray, and be ever ready for the coming of the Lord") with a looser paraphrase ("so that all may anticipate, pray for, and be ever ready for the coming of the Lord"). The substitution of "anticipate" for "watch" subtly weakens the vigilance motif, and "pray for" (the coming) differs from the WLC's "pray" (a general posture of prayerfulness). Additionally, the 1689 Confession's rich purpose language — that the unknown day is meant both "to deter all men from sin" and for "the greater consolation of the godly" — is entirely omitted.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The nearest questions are:

> Q. What benefits do believers receive from Christ at the resurrection?
> A. At the resurrection believers, being raised up in glory, shall be openly acknowledged, and acquitted in the day of judgment, and made perfectly blessed, both in soul and body, in the full enjoyment of God to all eternity. (1 Cor. 15:43; Mt. 25:23; Mt. 10:32; 1 John 3:2; 1 Cor. 13:12; 1 Thess. 4:17, 18)

> Q. What shall be done to the wicked, at the day of judgment?
> A. At the day of judgment the bodies of the wicked, being raised out of their graves, shall be sentenced, together with their souls, to unspeakable torments with the devil and his angels for ever. (John 5:28, 29; Mt. 25:41, 46; 2 Thes. 1:8, 9)

- Omissions: The Shorter does not address the timing question (what "immediately follows" the resurrection) as a distinct topic, so the BLC is filling a gap rather than condensing the Shorter. No content from these Shorter questions is omitted — they are addressed by other BLC questions.
- Structural changes: None applicable — the BLC is expanding coverage beyond the Shorter's scope by adopting the WLC's Q88 framework.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 32, Paragraph 1:
> "God hath appointed a Day wherein he will judge the world in Righteousness, by Jesus Christ; to whom all power and judgement is given of the Father; in which Day not only the Apostate Angels shall be judged; but likewise all persons that have lived upon the Earth, shall appear before the Tribunal of Christ; to give an account of their Thoughts, Words, and Deeds, and to receive according to what they have done in the body, whether good or evil."

Chapter 32, Paragraph 3:
> "As Christ would have us to be certainly perswaded that there shall be a Day of judgement, both to deter all men from sin, and for the greater consolation of the godly, in their adversity; so will he have that day unknown to Men, that they may shake off all carnal security, and be always watchful, because they know not at what hour, the Lord will come; and may ever be prepared to say, Come Lord Jesus, Come quickly, Amen."

- Omissions:
  - **Christ as Judge**: The 1689 explicitly identifies Jesus Christ as the one to whom "all power and judgement is given of the Father." The BLC makes no mention of Christ as judge.
  - **Accountability language**: The 1689's "to give an account of their Thoughts, Words, and Deeds, and to receive according to what they have done in the body, whether good or evil" is absent. This detailed accountability motif is a significant doctrinal element.
  - **Dual purpose of the unknown day**: The 1689 states the day is unknown both "to deter all men from sin" and for "the greater consolation of the godly in their adversity." The BLC drops both purposes, replacing them with a vaguer "so that all may anticipate, pray for, and be ever ready."
  - **"Shake off all carnal security"**: This pointed language about spiritual complacency is lost.
  - **"Come Lord Jesus, Come quickly, Amen"**: The 1689's climactic expression of longing (from Rev. 22:20) is absent.
- Structural changes: The 1689 presents the unknown timing as serving two theological purposes (deterrence and consolation) that produce a response (watchfulness and longing). The BLC collapses this into a single undifferentiated response clause.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> WLC Q88. What shall immediately follow after the resurrection?
> A. Immediately after the resurrection shall follow the general and final judgment of angels and men; the day and hour whereof no man knoweth, that all may watch and pray, and be ever ready for the coming of the Lord.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC's first section is verbatim from the WLC. The second section closely follows the WLC's structure.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement).
- Unexpected divergences:
  - "the day and hour whereof no man knoweth" (WLC) → "of which no one knows the day or hour" (BLC): Acceptable modernization.
  - "that all may watch and pray" (WLC) → "so that all may anticipate, pray for" (BLC): "Anticipate" replaces "watch," losing the active vigilance connotation. "Pray for" (the coming) changes the meaning from a general posture of prayerfulness to praying specifically for the Lord's return — a different emphasis.
- Key phrases dropped: None entirely dropped, but "watch" is replaced rather than modernized. A more faithful modernization would have been "that all may be watchful and pray" or similar.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Watch" vs. "Anticipate"**: The Greek γρηγορέω (grēgoreō) in Matthew 24:42 carries connotations of alertness, vigilance, and staying awake — a warning against spiritual complacency. "Anticipate" connotes expectation or looking forward to, which is theologically positive but lacks the urgency and warning note. The WLC's "watch" is more faithful to the biblical vocabulary and to the 1689's "shake off all carnal security, and be always watchful."

2. **"Pray" vs. "Pray for"**: The WLC says "watch and pray" — a general spiritual posture (cf. Matt. 26:41, "Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation"). The BLC's "pray for" implies praying for the coming of the Lord, which, while biblically grounded (cf. "Come Lord Jesus," Rev. 22:20; "Thy kingdom come," Matt. 6:10), changes the emphasis from prayerful vigilance to eschatological longing. This is not wrong but is a departure from the WLC's and the 1689's emphasis.

3. **Omission of Christ as Judge**: Both the 1689 (Ch. 32.1) and the WLC's surrounding questions identify Christ as the appointed judge. The BLC's passive construction ("shall follow the general and final judgment") matches the WLC Q88 answer precisely — neither the WLC Q88 nor the BLC names Christ as judge in this particular answer. This is not a BLC-specific omission but is worth noting since the BLC is meant to "exposit" the 1689, where Christ's judicial role is prominent.

4. **The BLC expands scope beyond the Shorter**: Since the Baptist Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel to this question, the BLC is adopting WLC Q88's framework. This is appropriate given the BLC's stated goal of solidarity with the WLC.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Ecclesiastes 12:14** ("God shall bring every work into judgment"): Supports the general judgment. Also used in the 1689's proof texts for Ch. 32.1.
- **2 Peter 2:4-15**: Supports judgment of angels. The range is unusually broad (through v.15 includes Balaam); 2 Pet. 2:4 and 2:9 are the most directly relevant verses.
- **Matthew 25:46**: Supports eternal consequences — everlasting punishment and eternal life. Used in both the 1689 and the Shorter.
- **2 Corinthians 5:10**: Supports appearing before the judgment seat of Christ. Also in the 1689's proof texts.
- **Matthew 24:36-44**: Supports the unknown day and hour, and the call to watchfulness. Aligns with the 1689's Ch. 32.3.
- **Mark 13:35-37**: Supports watchfulness. Also cited in the 1689's proof texts for Ch. 32.3.

The proof texts are well-chosen and align with the confessional standards. The BLC adds Ecclesiastes 12:14, 2 Peter 2:4-15, and 2 Corinthians 5:10 beyond what the WLC Q88 cites, drawing appropriately from the 1689's proof-text apparatus.
