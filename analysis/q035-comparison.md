# Q35: How is the Old Covenant related to the Covenant of Grace?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q35 addresses the Old Covenant's historical and typological relationship to the Covenant of Grace — a topic the 1689 treats under Chapter 7.3 and that the WLC handles at Q33-34. The BLC correctly teaches progressive revelation through types and shadows and that salvation has always been by the Covenant of Grace alone. However, the BLC's framing introduces a terminological tension: the 1689 does not use the term "Old Covenant" in Chapter 7 at all, and importantly, the 1689 does not treat the Mosaic covenant as a separate "Old Covenant" alongside the Covenant of Grace, but rather speaks of the one Covenant of Grace being "revealed in the Gospel; first of all to Adam in the promise of Salvation by the seed of the woman, and afterwards by farther steps." Additionally, the BLC omits the 1689's crucial statement that the Covenant of Grace is "founded in that Eternal Covenant transaction, that was between the Father and the Son, about the Redemption of the Elect" — a key distinctively Baptist confessional element that grounds the entire covenant theology. The BLC also omits the 1689's statement that man is "utterly uncapable of acceptance with God upon those terms, on which Adam stood in his state of innocency."

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest is:

> "Q. Did God leave all mankind to perish in the estate of sin and misery?"
> "A. God having out of his mere good pleasure, from all eternity, elected some to everlasting life (Eph. 1:4, 5), did enter into a covenant of grace, to deliver them out of the estate of sin and misery, and to bring them into an estate of salvation by a Redeemer (Rom. 3:20-22; Gal. 3:21, 22)."

The Shorter Catechism does not have a question specifically addressing the Old Covenant's relationship to the Covenant of Grace or the administration of the covenant under the Old Testament. This topic is properly the domain of a Larger Catechism expanding on confessional content.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 7, Paragraphs 2-3

Chapter 7.2:
> "Moreover Man having brought himself under the curse of the Law by his fall, it pleased the Lord to make a Covenant of Grace wherein he freely offereth unto Sinners, Life and Salvation by Jesus Christ, requiring of them Faith in him, that they may be saved; and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal Life, his holy Spirit, to make them willing, and able to believe."

Chapter 7.3:
> "This Covenant is revealed in the Gospel; first of all to Adam in the promise of Salvation by the seed of the woman, and afterwards by farther steps, untill the full discovery thereof was compleated in the new Testament; and it is founded in that Eternal Covenant transaction, that was between the Father and the Son, about the Redemption of the Elect; and it is alone by the Grace of this Covenant, that all of the posterity of fallen Adam, that ever were saved, did obtain life and a blessed immortality; Man being now utterly uncapable of acceptance with God upon those terms, on which Adam stood in his state of innocency."

- **Omissions:**
  1. **Eternal Covenant transaction (pactum salutis):** The 1689 explicitly grounds the Covenant of Grace in "that Eternal Covenant transaction, that was between the Father and the Son, about the Redemption of the Elect." The BLC omits this entirely. This is a significant omission because it is a distinctive element of 1689 covenant theology that distinguishes it from the Westminster standards (which place this in WLC Q31 differently). The pactum salutis is the theological foundation upon which the entire covenant framework rests.
  2. **Man's utter incapacity:** The 1689 states that "Man being now utterly uncapable of acceptance with God upon those terms, on which Adam stood in his state of innocency." This negative statement reinforces the necessity of the Covenant of Grace. The BLC omits this.
  3. **"First of all to Adam" language:** The 1689 traces the Covenant of Grace back to "the promise of Salvation by the seed of the woman" (Gen. 3:15 — the protoevangelium), then "by farther steps." The BLC jumps directly to Abraham (Gen. 12:1-3) without tracing the covenant back to the Adamic promise. This is a notable structural gap.
  4. **"Freely offereth unto Sinners, Life and Salvation by Jesus Christ":** The 1689's language of free offer is absent from the BLC.
  5. **"Requiring of them Faith in him":** The 1689 explicitly names faith as a requirement. BLC Q35 mentions faith only in the final section about Galatians 3, not as a confessional requirement of the covenant.

- **Structural changes:**
  1. The 1689 speaks of ONE Covenant of Grace "revealed in the Gospel" that progresses "by farther steps." The BLC introduces the term "Old Covenant" as a distinct entity that is "historically and typologically related to the Covenant of Grace." This is a significant structural reframing. The 1689's framework is one covenant progressively revealed; the BLC's framework presents two covenants in relation to each other. While the BLC may intend the "Old Covenant" to refer to the Mosaic administration specifically (distinct from the Covenant of Grace itself), the phrasing creates ambiguity about whether the Old Covenant is a distinct covenant or an administration of the one Covenant of Grace.
  2. The 1689 follows a pattern: Covenant of Works broken → Covenant of Grace given → progressively revealed → grounded in pactum salutis → sole means of salvation. The BLC restructures this as: Old Covenant has historical promises → Old Covenant has types/shadows → only the Covenant of Grace saves → Old Covenant imprisoned under sin. The logical movement is different: the 1689 moves from grace to its progressive revelation; the BLC moves from the Old Covenant to grace.

- **Agreement:** The BLC correctly affirms that "it is only by the Covenant of Grace that all descendants of Adam that ever were saved did obtain eternal life" — this closely mirrors the 1689's "it is alone by the Grace of this Covenant, that all of the posterity of fallen Adam, that ever were saved, did obtain life and a blessed immortality." The BLC's final section on Galatians 3:19-22 (the law imprisoning under sin so that the promise comes through faith) also finds warrant in Reformed Baptist covenant theology.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q33-34

WLC Q33:
> "Was the covenant of grace always administered after one and the same manner?"
> "A. The covenant of grace was not always administered after the same manner, but the administrations of it under the Old Testament were different from those under the New."

WLC Q34:
> "How was the covenant of grace administered under the Old Testament?"
> "A. The covenant of grace was administered under the Old Testament, by promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the passover, and other types and ordinances, which did all fore-signify Christ then to come, and were for that time sufficient to build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by whom they then had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation."

- **Solidarity:** MODERATE — The BLC shares the WLC's core conviction that the OT pointed to Christ through types and shadows and that the elect under the OT were saved by faith in the promised Messiah. However, the structural framework differs substantially.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:**
  1. The WLC speaks of "administrations" of the one covenant of grace under Old and New Testaments — classic Presbyterian covenant theology. The BLC's framing of "Old Covenant" as a separate entity "related to" the Covenant of Grace departs from this and is consistent with 1689 Baptist covenant theology, which distinguishes the Old (Mosaic) Covenant from the Covenant of Grace more sharply. This is an expected and acceptable divergence.
  2. The WLC includes "circumcision" and "the passover" as covenant administrations — ordinances Baptists would treat differently. The BLC avoids this. Expected divergence.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. The WLC says OT types "were for that time sufficient to build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah." The BLC does not affirm the sufficiency of OT revelation for OT saints' faith — this is a significant omission of a point where Baptists and Presbyterians agree.
  2. The WLC says the elect "had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation" through the Messiah. The BLC's closest parallel ("all descendants of Adam that ever were saved did obtain eternal life") captures "eternal life" but omits "full remission of sin" — a minor but notable omission of forensic/justification language.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  1. "Sufficient to build up the elect in faith" — dropped without theological reason
  2. "Full remission of sin" — dropped without theological reason
  3. "Promises, prophecies, sacrifices" — the WLC's enumeration of specific OT means of covenant administration is replaced by the BLC with a more general description

## Detailed Notes

1. **Terminological precision — "Old Covenant":** The most significant concern is the BLC's use of "Old Covenant" without careful definition. In 1689 Baptist covenant theology, the "Old Covenant" (Mosaic covenant) is typically understood as a distinct covenant from the Covenant of Grace — not an administration of it (contra Westminster). The BLC appears to use this framework ("the Old Covenant is historically and typologically related to the Covenant of Grace"), which is confessionally Baptist. However, by beginning the Old Covenant promises with Abraham (Gen. 12), the BLC conflates Abrahamic and Mosaic elements under "Old Covenant." In the 1689 framework, the Abrahamic promise is more closely identified with the Covenant of Grace itself (as a "farther step" of its revelation), not with the Old/Mosaic Covenant.

2. **Missing protoevangelium (Gen. 3:15):** The 1689 explicitly begins with "the promise of Salvation by the seed of the woman" as the first revelation of the Covenant of Grace. The BLC skips this and begins with Abraham. This is a notable omission for a catechism that claims to exposit the 1689.

3. **Missing pactum salutis:** The 1689's statement that the Covenant of Grace "is founded in that Eternal Covenant transaction, that was between the Father and the Son, about the Redemption of the Elect" is a theologically significant element that the BLC omits. While this might be addressed elsewhere in the catechism, its absence in a question specifically about how covenants relate is notable.

4. **Galatians 3 framing:** The BLC's final section — "the Old Covenant imprisoned everything under sin in order that the only means to obtain the promised inheritance was through faith in Christ" — is good Pauline theology from Galatians 3:19-22. However, the language "imprisoned everything under sin" could be misread as suggesting the Old Covenant itself was sinful or purely negative. The 1689 and WLC both preserve the positive function of OT revelation (types and shadows pointing to Christ, sufficient for building up the elect in faith). The BLC's exclusively negative concluding note about the Old Covenant creates an imbalanced picture.

5. **Positive role of OT types:** While the BLC does affirm types and shadows "pointed to Christ and the Covenant of Grace," it does not affirm that these were *effective* or *sufficient* for OT saints. The WLC explicitly says they "were for that time sufficient to build up the elect in faith." This is a point of agreement between Baptists and Presbyterians that the BLC drops.

## Scripture Analysis

The BLC's proof texts are generally well-chosen:
- **Genesis 12:1-3; 18:18-19; 22:17-18** — Appropriate for Abrahamic promises, though these are more naturally associated with the Covenant of Grace's progressive revelation than with a distinct "Old Covenant"
- **Luke 1:54-55, 72-73; Galatians 3:16** — Strong support for Messiah as seed of Abraham
- **Isaiah 9:6-7; Jeremiah 23:5-6; Revelation 19:15-16** — Good Messianic kingship texts
- **Hebrews 8:5; Colossians 2:16-17; Hebrews 9:9-12, 24; 10:1** — Excellent types and shadows support
- **Galatians 4:21-31; Romans 9-11; Hebrews 8:5-13; 9:15-24** — Strong, though Romans 9-11 is very broad as a citation
- **Hebrews 11:6, 13; Romans 4; Acts 4:12; John 8:56** — Good support for salvation always through grace/faith; mirrors 1689 7.3 proof texts closely
- **Galatians 3:19-22; 2 Corinthians 3:6-9; Romans 8:3; 10:4** — Strong Pauline support for law's inability to save

Notable absence: **Genesis 3:15** — the protoevangelium, which the 1689 explicitly cites as the first revelation of the Covenant of Grace, is entirely absent from the BLC's proof texts here. This confirms the structural gap noted above.
