# Q49: How did Christ humble Himself in His life?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q49 closely follows WLC Q48 in structure and content, modernizing language while preserving the core theological framework. The Shorter Catechism's parallel (Q27) treats Christ's humiliation as a whole rather than dividing it into stages, so the BLC follows the WLC's structure of isolating the life-stage of humiliation. One notable wording change — "disgraces" for WLC's "indignities" and "frailty" for WLC's "infirmities" — subtly narrows the semantic range, and the phrase "struggling with" replaces the WLC's stronger "conflicting with," potentially weakening the sense of active combat with suffering.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Wherein did Christ's humiliation consist?
> A. Christ's humiliation consisted in his being born, and that in a low condition, made under the law, undergoing the miseries of this life, the wrath of God, and the cursed death of the cross; in being buried, and continuing under the power of death for a time. (Gal. 4:4; Heb. 12:2,3; Is. 53:2,3; Luke 22:44; Mt. 27:46; Phil. 2:8; 1 Cor. 15:3,4; Acts 2:24-27,31; Mt. 12:40)

- Omissions: The Shorter speaks of "undergoing the miseries of this life" — a comprehensive phrase. The BLC narrows this to "struggling with the disgraces of this world," which captures reproach and shame but potentially omits the broader category of suffering and misery (poverty, hunger, weariness, grief) that "miseries" encompasses. The Shorter's phrase "miseries of this life" is more comprehensive than "disgraces of this world."
- Structural changes: The Shorter treats all of Christ's humiliation in one answer; the BLC (following the WLC) isolates the life-stage. This is an acceptable structural expansion, not a concern.
- The BLC appropriately includes subjection to the law, perfect fulfillment, temptations of Satan, and bodily infirmities — all elements implied or stated in the Shorter's compressed answer.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8, Paragraph 4:
> "This office the Lord Jesus did most willingly undertake, which that he might discharge he was made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it, and underwent the punishment due to us, which we should have born and suffered, being made Sin and a Curse for us: enduring most grievous sorrows in his Soul; and most painful sufferings in his body; was crucified, and died, and remained in the state of the dead; yet saw no corruption."

Also relevant, Chapter 8, Paragraph 2:
> "...did when the fullness of time was come take unto him mans nature, with all the Essential properties, and common infirmities thereof, yet without sin."

- Omissions: The 1689 emphasizes Christ's *willing* undertaking of His office ("most willingly undertake") — a voluntariness that the BLC does not explicitly state in this question. However, this may be addressed elsewhere in the BLC's treatment. The 1689's reference to "common infirmities" (8.2) is well paralleled by the BLC's "frailty of His body, whether common to the nature of man."
- Structural changes: None of significance. The BLC's content on law-keeping and bodily infirmity aligns with 1689 8.2 and 8.4.
- The 1689's phrase "made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it" is directly mirrored in the BLC's "subjecting Himself to the Law, which He perfectly fulfilled."
- Agreement is strong on the core doctrinal content of Christ's active obedience and assumption of human weakness.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q48:
> Q. How did Christ humble himself in his life?
> A. Christ humbled himself in his life, by subjecting himself to the law, which he perfectly fulfilled; and by conflicting with the indignities of the world, temptations of Satan, and infirmities in his flesh, whether common to the nature of man, or particularly accompanying that his low condition.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None apparent — this question involves no baptismal, ecclesiological, or covenant theology differences.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - "struggling with" replaces "conflicting with" — the WLC's "conflicting" carries a stronger military/combative connotation (Latin *conflictus* = striking together) that better captures the theological weight of Christ's active warfare against the world's opposition, Satan's attacks, and bodily weakness. "Struggling" is weaker, suggesting difficulty or effort rather than combat.
  - "disgraces of this world" replaces "indignities of the world" — "indignities" is broader, encompassing not only shameful treatment (disgrace) but also affronts to dignity, undeserved lowliness, and contemptuous treatment. "Disgraces" narrows primarily to shame and dishonor.
  - "frailty of His body" replaces "infirmities in his flesh" — "infirmities in his flesh" is the more traditional theological language, echoing the biblical term (cf. Heb. 4:15 "infirmities"). "Frailty" suggests weakness or fragility, while "infirmities" captures the full range of human limitation including hunger, weariness, suffering, and susceptibility to pain. The shift from "flesh" to "body" also slightly narrows — "flesh" in theological usage encompasses the full scope of human nature in its weakness, while "body" is more narrowly physical.
- Key phrases dropped: No key theological phrases are dropped outright. The substitutions noted above are modernizations, but they consistently narrow the semantic range rather than preserve it.

## Detailed Notes
1. The BLC's structure follows WLC Q48 almost exactly, confirming strong solidarity in the division of Christ's humiliation into stages (conception/birth, life, death, after death).
2. The proof texts are nearly identical to the WLC's, with appropriate overlap: Galatians 4:4 (law), Matthew 5:17 and Romans 5:19 (fulfillment), Psalm 22:6 / Isaiah 53:2-3 / Hebrews 12:2-3 (indignities/disgraces), Matthew 4:1-11 / Luke 4:13 (temptations), and Hebrews 2:17-18 / 4:15 / Isaiah 52:13-14 (infirmities/frailty).
3. The BLC adds "Matthew 4:1-11" explicitly where the WLC has "Matthew 4:1-12" — a trivial verse-range difference.
4. The word choices ("struggling," "disgraces," "frailty," "body") consistently modernize toward a slightly narrower and less theologically precise vocabulary. Individually each change is minor; cumulatively they represent a slight weakening of the original's precision and theological weight.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Galatians 4:4** — Properly cited for subjection to the law. Matches both WLC and Shorter.
- **Matthew 5:17; Romans 5:19** — Properly cited for perfect fulfillment of the law. Romans 5:19 ("by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous") is particularly important for grounding active obedience.
- **Psalm 22:6; Isaiah 53:2-3; Hebrews 12:2-3** — These support the "disgraces/indignities" clause. Psalm 22:6 ("a reproach of men, and despised of the people") and Isaiah 53:2-3 ("despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows") actually better support the WLC's broader "indignities" than the BLC's narrower "disgraces."
- **Matthew 4:1-11; Luke 4:13** — Standard proof texts for Christ's temptation by Satan. Well chosen.
- **Hebrews 2:17-18; 4:15; Isaiah 52:13-14** — These support the infirmities/frailty clause. Hebrews 4:15 uses "infirmities" (Greek *astheneiais*), which is broader than "frailty" and supports the WLC's word choice over the BLC's. Isaiah 52:14 ("his visage was so marred more than any man") speaks to physical suffering beyond mere frailty.
