# Q76: Shall all men die?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q76 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q84, preserving all three theological elements: death as wages of sin, the universal appointment to die, and universal sinfulness as the cause. The question and answer align with both the 1689 Confession's teaching on the punishment of sin and the Shorter Catechism's treatment of death as part of the misery of the fall. Only the most minor stylistic changes distinguish the BLC from the WLC.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct standalone parallel. The Shorter addresses death within its treatment of the misery of the fall:
- Q. "What is the misery of that estate whereinto man fell?" A. "All mankind by their fall lost communion with God, are under his wrath and curse, and so made liable to all miseries in this life, to death itself, and to the pains of hell for ever" (Lam. 3:39; Rom. 6:23; Mt. 25:41, 46).

- Omissions: None. The BLC expands on what the Shorter treats compactly. The Shorter's mention of "death itself" as part of the broader misery of the fall is consistent with the BLC's focused treatment of the universality of death.
- Structural changes: None of concern. The BLC separates out the question of universal death into its own question (following the WLC pattern), which is a natural expansion for a larger catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**
- Chapter 6, Paragraph 1: God "created Man upright, and perfect, and gave him a righteous law, which had been unto life had he kept it, and threatned death upon the breach thereof."
- Chapter 6, Paragraph 2: "Our first Parents by this Sin, fell from their original righteousness and communion with God, and we in them, whereby death came upon all; all becoming dead in Sin."
- Chapter 6, Paragraph 3: Mankind's posterity are "the subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal, unless the Lord Jesus set them free."
- Chapter 31, Paragraph 1: "The Bodies of Men after Death return to dust, and see corruption."

- Omissions: None. The BLC's three-part answer (death as wages of sin, universal appointment to die, universal sinfulness) is fully consistent with the 1689's teaching that death was threatened as the penalty for sin (6.1), came upon all through the fall (6.2), and that all posterity are subjects of death (6.3). The 1689's qualification "unless the Lord Jesus set them free" (6.3) is not included, but this is appropriately handled in subsequent BLC questions about the state of believers at death rather than being required here.
- Structural changes: None. The causal chain (sin → death → all have sinned) matches the Confession's logic precisely.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q84: "Shall all men die?" A. "Death being threatened as the wages of sin, it is appointed unto all men once to die; for that all have sinned."
- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this doctrine involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology distinctives).
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC says "Death being threatened as the wages of sin" while the BLC says "Since death is the wages of sin." The WLC's "threatened" adds the nuance that death was a threatened penalty (pointing back to the covenant of works), which the BLC replaces with a simpler causal statement. This is an extremely minor stylistic difference — the substance is identical, and the BLC's phrasing still correctly identifies death as the wages of sin per Romans 6:23.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC restructures the WLC's participial opening ("Death being threatened as the wages of sin") into a causal clause ("Since death is the wages of sin"), which is a natural modernization that preserves the logical structure.
- The word "once" is preserved from the WLC, faithfully reflecting Hebrews 9:27.
- The causal conclusion "because all have sinned" (BLC) vs. "for that all have sinned" (WLC) is a straightforward modernization of the archaic "for that."
- All three proof texts (Rom. 6:23, Heb. 9:27, Rom. 5:12) are identical between the BLC and WLC.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Romans 6:23** ("the wages of sin is death"): Correctly cited for the opening clause establishing death as sin's penalty.
- **Hebrews 9:27** ("it is appointed unto men once to die"): Correctly cited for the universality and singularity of death.
- **Romans 5:12** ("by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned"): Correctly cited for the universality of sin as the cause of universal death. This verse also connects to Adam's federal headship (1689 6.2-3), grounding the universal scope.
- The proof texts match the WLC's footnotes exactly (364, 365, 366), confirming strong structural solidarity.
