# Q176: Should we pray only to God?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q176 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q179, preserving the same theological structure, logical argument, and conclusion. The question follows the WLC's approach of establishing God's exclusive attributes as the ground for exclusive prayer, treating the mediator question separately (BLC Q177-178, paralleling WLC Q180-181). The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question, as this is WLC-expansion territory, but the content aligns with the 1689 Confession's teaching on worship and prayer.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism does not include a dedicated question about praying to God only. The closest content is embedded in the definition of prayer itself:

> "Q. What is prayer? A. Prayer is an offering up our desires to God (Ps. 62:8), by the assistance of the Holy Spirit (Rom. 8:26), for things agreeable to his will (1 Jn. 5:14; Rom. 8:27), in the name of Christ (Jn. 16:23), believing (Mt. 21:22; James 1:6), with confession of our sins (Ps. 32:5, 6; Dan. 9:4), and thankful acknowledgments of his mercies (Ph. 4:6)."

The Shorter's definition implicitly teaches prayer is directed to God ("offering up our desires to God") but does not develop the argument for exclusivity. BLC Q176 legitimately expands on this as a Larger Catechism question should.

- Omissions: None applicable (no parallel to mirror)
- Structural changes: None applicable

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 22, Paragraphs 2-3

> "2. Religious Worship is to be given to God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and to him alone; not to Angels, Saints, or any other Creatures; and since the fall, not without a Mediator, nor in the Mediation of any other but Christ alone." (Matt. 4:9-10; John 6:23; Matt. 28:19; Rom. 1:25; Col. 2:18; Rev. 19:10; John 14:6; 1 Tim. 2:5)

> "3. Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men." (Ps. 95:1-7; Ps. 65:2)

- Omissions: The 1689 specifies "God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit" as the Trinitarian recipients of worship — BLC Q176 uses simply "God" without Trinitarian specification. However, this follows the WLC Q179's approach, which also simply says "God." The 1689's explicit exclusion of "Angels, Saints, or any other Creatures" is generalized in the BLC to "no one else," which captures the substance. The mediator clause ("since the fall, not without a Mediator, nor in the Mediation of any other but Christ alone") is appropriately handled in BLC Q177-178, following the WLC's structural separation of these topics.
- Structural changes: None. The 1689's identification of prayer as "one special part of natural worship" is faithfully reflected in BLC Q176's section "Since prayer is an integral part of religious worship."
- The 1689 calls prayer part of "natural worship" while BLC says "religious worship" — this follows the WLC's wording ("special part thereof," referring to religious worship). The 1689's point is that prayer is natural (i.e., required of all men by nature), while the WLC/BLC emphasize its role within religious worship. Both are true and complementary.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q179:

> "Q. 179. Are we to pray unto God only? A. God only being able to search the hearts, hear the requests, pardon the sins, and fulfill the desires of all; and only to be believed in, and worshipped with religious worship; prayer, which is a special part thereof, is to be made by all to him alone, and to none other."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "religious worship" → BLC has "truly worshiped" — drops "religious" as a qualifier but the concept is preserved in the later section "Since prayer is an integral part of religious worship." The WLC connects the two ideas in a single clause; the BLC separates them but preserves both.
  - WLC's "fulfill the desires of all" → BLC has "fulfills the desires of everyone" — equivalent modernization.
  - WLC's compact single-sentence answer structure is expanded into sectioned proof-text format, which is the BLC's standard approach and not a theological change.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Question wording**: WLC asks "Are we to pray unto God only?" while BLC asks "Should we pray only to God?" — a natural modernization with no theological difference.
2. **Logical structure preserved**: Both the WLC and BLC follow the same argumentative structure: (a) God alone has certain incommunicable prerogatives (searching hearts, hearing prayers, pardoning sins, fulfilling desires); (b) God alone is to be believed in and worshiped; (c) therefore prayer, as part of worship, belongs to God alone; (d) and to no one else.
3. **BLC adds 2 Samuel 22:32 and John 14:1** to the proof texts for "He is the only one to believe in," supplementing the WLC's Romans 10:14. These are appropriate additions that strengthen the point.
4. **BLC adds Isaiah 42:8 and Jeremiah 3:23** to the final "to no one else" section, supplementing the WLC's Romans 10:14. Isaiah 42:8 ("I will not give my glory to another") and Jeremiah 3:23 ("Truly in vain is salvation hoped for from the hills") are strong additions.
5. **"By everyone"**: BLC includes "by everyone" in the section "it is to be made by everyone only to Him," which corresponds to WLC's "by all" — preserving the universal obligation of prayer to God alone.
