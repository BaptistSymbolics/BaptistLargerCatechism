# Q116: What kind of honor is owed to those over us?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q116 is a faithful modernized expansion of the Shorter Catechism's answer on the duties of the fifth commandment toward superiors, and it tracks WLC Q127 with near-exact structural solidarity. Every element of the WLC answer is preserved in modernized form with appropriate proof texts. The Shorter's broad framework ("preserving the honour and performing the duties belonging to every one in their several places and relations, as superiors, inferiors, or equals") is expanded in the BLC exactly as the WLC expands it — focusing specifically on the duties owed by inferiors to superiors. No omissions from the standards are identified.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is required in the fifth commandment?
> A. The fifth commandment requireth the preserving the honour and performing the duties belonging to every one in their several places and relations, as superiors (Eph. 5:21), inferiors (1 Pet. 2:17), or equals (Rom. 12:10).

- **Omissions:** None. The Shorter's answer is a general statement covering all three relational categories (superiors, inferiors, equals). BLC Q116 is the specific expansion of the "inferiors" duty toward "superiors" — which is the expected function of a Larger Catechism. The Shorter's key concept of "preserving the honour and performing the duties belonging to every one in their several places and relations" is fully embodied in the BLC's detailed enumeration of those duties.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC follows the expected catechetical pattern of expanding the Shorter's general statement into specific duties. The Shorter's tripartite framework (superiors/inferiors/equals) would be handled across multiple BLC questions, with Q116 addressing inferiors' duties toward superiors specifically.
- The BLC's concluding phrase "so that we will be an honor to them and to their authority" directly echoes the Shorter's emphasis on "preserving the honour" belonging to each relation.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 24 (Of the Civil Magistrate), Paragraph 3:
> "Civil Magistrates being set up by God, for the ends aforesaid; subjection in all lawful things commanded by them, ought to be yeilded by us, in the Lord; not only for wrath but for Conscience sake; and we ought to make supplications and prayers for Kings, and all that are in Authority, that under them we may live a quiet and peaceable life, in all godliness and honesty."

Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God), Paragraph 5:
> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation."

- **Omissions:** None. The 1689 does not provide an exhaustive treatment of the fifth commandment duties at the confessional level (it treats the moral law generally and then specifically addresses the civil magistrate). The BLC's content on subjection to lawful commands, prayer for those in authority, and obedience "in the Lord" all align with 1689 24.3. The 1689's phrase "subjection in all lawful things" is captured by the BLC's "willingly heed and obey their lawful commands and advice." The 1689's "not only for wrath but for Conscience sake" is a theological grounding that the BLC addresses through its proof texts (Romans 13:1-6) rather than explicit statement, which is acceptable in a catechetical format.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC appropriately expands beyond the civil magistrate to cover all forms of authority (family, church, commonwealth) as the fifth commandment demands, which is consistent with the 1689's treatment of the moral law as a perpetual standard (19.5).
- **Agreement:** The BLC's emphasis on praying for those in authority (citing 1 Tim. 2:1-2), obeying lawful commands (citing Rom. 13:1-6; 1 Pet. 2:13-14), and supporting their persons financially (citing Rom. 13:6-7; 1 Tim. 5:17-18) all have direct grounding in 1689 24.3 and its proof texts.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 127. What is the honour that inferiors owe to their superiors?
> A. The honour which inferiors owe to their superiors is, all due reverence in heart, word, and behaviour; prayer and thanksgiving for them; imitation of their virtues and graces; willing obedience to their lawful commands and counsels; due submission to their corrections; fidelity to, defence, and maintenance of their persons and authority, according to their several ranks, and the nature of their places; bearing with their infirmities, and covering them in love, that so they may be an honour to them and to their government.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None. The fifth commandment duties toward superiors are an area of complete Baptist-Presbyterian agreement. There are no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues at stake in this question.
- **Unexpected divergences:** None. The BLC tracks WLC Q127 with remarkable precision. Every clause of the WLC answer is present in the BLC in modernized form:

  | WLC Element | BLC Rendering |
  |---|---|
  | "all due reverence in heart, word, and behaviour" | "respect in our hearts, our words, and our actions" |
  | "prayer and thanksgiving for them" | "pray and give thanks for them" |
  | "imitation of their virtues and graces" | "emulate their virtues and gifts" |
  | "willing obedience to their lawful commands and counsels" | "willingly heed and obey their lawful commands and advice" |
  | "due submission to their corrections" | "submit to their correction" |
  | "fidelity to" | "be faithful to them" |
  | "defence" | "defend" |
  | "maintenance of their persons and authority" | "support their persons and authority" |
  | "according to their several ranks, and the nature of their places" | "as is appropriate to their rank and position" |
  | "bearing with their infirmities, and covering them in love" | "tolerate their imperfections and infirmities and cover them with our love" |
  | "that so they may be an honour to them and to their government" | "so that we will be an honor to them and to their authority" |

- **Key phrases dropped:** None of substance.
  - "graces" → "gifts" — a minor lexical substitution; the WLC says "virtues and graces," the BLC says "virtues and gifts." Both terms capture the idea of divinely-bestowed qualities worthy of imitation. This is an acceptable modernization.
  - "government" → "authority" — another minor lexical modernization in the final clause. "Government" in the WLC carries the sense of their ruling/governing authority, which "authority" captures adequately.
  - "due" is dropped from "due reverence" and "due submission" — the BLC simply says "respect" and "submit." The qualifier "due" in the WLC signals proportionality (reverence and submission appropriate to the relation). The BLC captures this proportionality concept through the later phrase "as is appropriate to their rank and position," so the omission of "due" from the individual clauses is not a loss of content.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Structural fidelity:** The BLC breaks the WLC's single flowing sentence into discrete sections, each with its own proof texts. This is a pedagogical improvement that makes the catechetical answer easier to teach and memorize while preserving every element of the original.

2. **Question wording:** The BLC asks "What kind of honor is owed to those over us?" while the WLC asks "What is the honour that inferiors owe to their superiors?" The BLC's phrasing is a clean modernization — "those over us" replaces "superiors" and "those over us" implicitly identifies the catechist as an "inferior" in the relational sense. The WLC's more formal terminology ("inferiors"/"superiors") is historic Reformed convention; the BLC's rendering is accessible without losing meaning.

3. **"Graces" vs. "gifts":** The WLC Q127 cites Heb. 13:7 and Phil. 3:17 for "imitation of their virtues and graces." The BLC cites the same two texts for "emulate their virtues and gifts." The word "graces" in the WLC carries a theological richness (graces as God-wrought spiritual qualities), while "gifts" is broader but still captures God-given endowments. This is a defensible modernization, though "graces" has a specifically theological resonance that "gifts" does not fully replicate.

4. **Expanded proof texts:** The BLC significantly expands the proof text citations for "willingly heed and obey their lawful commands and advice" (Eph. 6:1-7; 1 Pet. 2:13-14; Rom. 13:1-6; Heb. 13:17; Prov. 4:3-4; Prov. 23:22; Ex. 18:19-24) compared to the WLC's single footnote reference. This is appropriate for a Baptist catechism that emphasizes Scripture saturation.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and appropriate throughout:
- **Malachi 1:6; Leviticus 19:3** for heart reverence — both are standard texts on honoring authority figures. Mal. 1:6 ("A son honoureth his father") is the same text used in WLC footnote 658.
- **Proverbs 31:28; 1 Peter 3:6** for word-honor — Prov. 31:28 matches WLC footnote 659. 1 Pet. 3:6 (Sarah calling Abraham "lord") is an apt addition.
- **Leviticus 19:32; 1 Kings 2:19** for behavioral honor — Lev. 19:32 matches WLC footnote 660 (rising before the aged).
- **1 Timothy 2:1-2** for prayer — matches WLC footnote 661 and 1689 24.3.
- **Hebrews 13:7; Philippians 3:17** for imitation — matches WLC footnote 662.
- **Ephesians 6:1-7; 1 Peter 2:13-14; Romans 13:1-6; Hebrews 13:17; Proverbs 4:3-4; Proverbs 23:22; Exodus 18:19-24** for obedience — a comprehensive set covering family (Eph. 6, Prov. 4, Prov. 23), civil (1 Pet. 2, Rom. 13), and church (Heb. 13:17) authority, plus Moses' wise acceptance of counsel (Ex. 18). This expands the WLC's references significantly.
- **Hebrews 12:9; 1 Peter 2:18-20** for submission to correction — Heb. 12:9 matches WLC footnote 664.
- **Titus 2:9-10** for fidelity — matches WLC footnote 665.
- **1 Samuel 26:15-16; 2 Samuel 18:3; Esther 6:2** for defense — 1 Sam. 26:15-16 matches WLC footnote 666. The additions from 2 Samuel and Esther enrich the point.
- **Matthew 22:21; Romans 13:6-7; 1 Timothy 5:17-18; Galatians 6:6; Genesis 45:11; Genesis 47:12** for maintenance/support — Matt. 22:21 and Rom. 13:6-7 match WLC footnote 667. The additions cover church support (1 Tim. 5:17-18; Gal. 6:6) and filial support (Gen. 45, 47).
- **1 Peter 2:18; Proverbs 23:22; Genesis 9:23** for bearing with infirmities — 1 Pet. 2:18 matches WLC footnote 668. Gen. 9:23 (Shem and Japheth covering Noah's nakedness) is a vivid OT illustration of covering a superior's shame.
- **Psalm 127:3-5; Proverbs 31:23** for being an honor to them — Ps. 127:3-5 matches WLC footnote 669. Prov. 31:23 (her husband is known in the gates) adds the theme of a wife bringing honor to her husband's standing.
