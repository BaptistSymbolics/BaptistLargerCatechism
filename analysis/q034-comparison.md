# Q34: How does the Covenant of Grace differ from the Old Covenant?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q34 correctly presents the 1689 Baptist distinctive that the Old Covenant differs from the Covenant of Grace "not merely in administration, but also in substance." This is the key Baptist departure from Westminster's covenant theology. However, the BLC omits crucial 1689 teachings about the progressive revelation of the Covenant of Grace (Ch. 7.3), the foundational Eternal Covenant transaction between Father and Son (Ch. 7.3), and the fact that all the saved from fallen Adam onward were saved by the same Covenant of Grace (Ch. 7.3). The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel to this question, so the BLC is largely breaking new ground — but in doing so, it should still preserve the 1689's own framework for understanding how the Old Covenant relates to redemptive history, which it does not adequately do. The question also structurally risks implying that Old Testament saints were not saved by the Covenant of Grace, since it contrasts the two covenants without affirming their relationship to the one gospel promise.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism does not have a question specifically contrasting the Old Covenant with the Covenant of Grace.

The closest related question is:

> "Q. Did God leave all mankind to perish in the estate of sin and misery?
> A. God having out of his mere good pleasure, from all eternity, elected some to everlasting life (Eph. 1:4, 5), did enter into a covenant of grace, to deliver them out of the estate of sin and misery, and to bring them into an estate of salvation by a Redeemer (Rom. 3:20-22; Gal. 3:21, 22)."

And the questions on the outward means of grace:

> "Q. What are the outward means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption?
> A. The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption are his ordinances, especially the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer; all which means are made effectual to the elect for salvation (Mt. 28:19, 20; Acts 2:42, 46, 47)."

- Omissions: Since there is no direct parallel, omissions from the Shorter are not the primary concern. However, the Shorter's formulation of the Covenant of Grace emphasizes election, God's "mere good pleasure," and the Redeemer as the means of deliverance — none of which appear in BLC Q34.
- Structural changes: The BLC introduces a question format that has no Shorter Catechism precedent. The Shorter does not contrast Old Covenant vs. Covenant of Grace at all. This is new catechetical territory, which is acceptable for a Larger Catechism expanding on the Shorter — but it increases the importance of fidelity to the 1689 in this expansion.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 7, Paragraph 2:
> "Moreover Man having brought himself under the curse of the Law by his fall, it pleased the Lord to make a Covenant of Grace wherein he freely offereth unto Sinners, Life and Salvation by Jesus Christ, requiring of them Faith in him, that they may be saved; and promising to give unto all those that are ordained unto eternal Life, his holy Spirit, to make them willing, and able to believe."

Chapter 7, Paragraph 3:
> "This Covenant is revealed in the Gospel; first of all to Adam in the promise of Salvation by the seed of the woman, and afterwards by farther steps, untill the full discovery thereof was compleated in the new Testament; and it is founded in that Eternal Covenant transaction, that was between the Father and the Son, about the Redemption of the Elect; and it is alone by the Grace of this Covenant, that all of the posterity of fallen Adam, that ever were saved, did obtain life and a blessed immortality; Man being now utterly uncapable of acceptance with God upon those terms, on which Adam stood in his state of innocency."

Chapter 19, Paragraph 3:
> "Besides this Law commonly called moral, God was pleased to give to the people of Israel Ceremonial Laws, containing several typical ordinances, partly of worship, prefiguring Christ, his graces, actions, sufferings, and benefits; and partly holding forth divers instructions of moral duties, all which Ceremonial Laws being appointed only to the time of reformation, are by Jesus Christ the true Messiah and only Law-giver who was furnished with power from the Father, for that end, abrogated and taken away."

Chapter 20, Paragraph 1:
> "The Covenant of Works being broken by Sin, and made unprofitable unto Life; God was pleased to give forth the promise of Christ, the Seed of the Woman, as the means of calling the Elect, and begetting in them Faith and Repentance; in this Promise, the Gospel, as to the substance of it, was revealed, and therein Effectual, for the Conversion and Salvation of Sinners."

- **Omissions (significant):**
  1. **The Eternal Covenant transaction between Father and Son** (Ch. 7.3): The 1689 explicitly grounds the Covenant of Grace in the pactum salutis — the eternal covenant between Father and Son about the redemption of the elect. BLC Q34 makes no mention of this foundational doctrine.
  2. **Progressive revelation of the Covenant of Grace** (Ch. 7.3): The 1689 teaches that the Covenant of Grace was "first of all to Adam in the promise of Salvation by the seed of the woman, and afterwards by farther steps, untill the full discovery thereof was compleated in the new Testament." This progressive unfolding is absent from BLC Q34, which treats the Old Covenant and Covenant of Grace as if they were two entirely parallel, contemporaneous arrangements rather than showing how the gospel promise ran through and was revealed within the Old Testament era.
  3. **All the saved from fallen Adam onward were saved by the Covenant of Grace** (Ch. 7.3): The 1689 explicitly teaches that "it is alone by the Grace of this Covenant, that all of the posterity of fallen Adam, that ever were saved, did obtain life and a blessed immortality." BLC Q34 fails to affirm this critical point, which means it risks implying that Old Testament saints were saved under a fundamentally different arrangement than New Testament saints.
  4. **The ceremonial laws as prefiguring Christ** (Ch. 19.3): The 1689 teaches that the ceremonial laws were "partly of worship, prefiguring Christ, his graces, actions, sufferings, and benefits." BLC Q34 mentions the temporary priesthood and blood of bulls and goats but does not explicitly state their typological function pointing to Christ.
  5. **Faith as the requirement** (Ch. 7.2): The 1689 specifies that the Covenant of Grace requires "Faith in him, that they may be saved." BLC Q34 does not mention faith at all in the context of the Covenant of Grace.

- **Structural changes:**
  The BLC structures its answer as a point-by-point contrast (Old Covenant priesthood vs. eternal priesthood; blood of bulls and goats vs. blood of the Redeemer; national/temporal promises vs. eternal salvation; circumcision/Passover vs. Word/baptism/Lord's Supper). While this is pedagogically useful, it restructures the 1689's framework. The 1689 does not set up a simple binary Old Covenant vs. Covenant of Grace contrast. Instead, the 1689 presents one Covenant of Grace progressively revealed, with the Old Covenant (Mosaic) being a distinct, temporary covenant that served alongside the gospel promise. The BLC's binary contrast, without the qualifying language of Ch. 7.3, could be read to imply a redemptive-historical disjunction that the 1689 itself carefully avoids.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> "Q. 33. Was the covenant of grace always administered after one and the same manner?
> A. The covenant of grace was not always administered after the same manner, but the administrations of it under the Old Testament were different from those under the New."

> "Q. 34. How was the covenant of grace administered under the Old Testament?
> A. The covenant of grace was administered under the Old Testament, by promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the passover, and other types and ordinances, which did all fore-signify Christ then to come, and were for that time sufficient to build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by whom they then had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation."

> "Q. 35. How is the covenant of grace administered under the New Testament?
> A. Under the New Testament, when Christ the substance was exhibited, the same covenant of grace was and still is to be administered in the preaching of the Word, and the administration of the sacraments of Baptism and the Lord's Supper; in which grace and salvation are held forth in more fulness, evidence, and efficacy, to all nations."

- **Solidarity:** WEAK — This is an intentional and expected departure. The BLC deliberately rejects the WLC framework of one covenant with two administrations.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:**
  1. The WLC speaks of the Covenant of Grace being "administered under the Old Testament" — the 1689 Baptist position rejects this, holding the Old (Mosaic) Covenant to be substantially different from the Covenant of Grace. The BLC's opening line ("not merely in administration, but also in substance") is a direct and appropriate rejection of the Westminster framework.
  2. The WLC says Old Testament types and ordinances "did all fore-signify Christ then to come" and were sufficient to "build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by whom they then had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation." The BLC does not preserve this affirmation that Old Testament believers had full remission and eternal salvation through the promised Messiah — which is a concern, because even the 1689 affirms this (Ch. 7.3).
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. The WLC's phrase "which did all fore-signify Christ then to come" has no equivalent in the BLC. While the BLC's substance/administration distinction is intentionally Baptist, the typological function of Old Covenant ordinances pointing to Christ is affirmed by both traditions. The BLC should have included this.
  2. The WLC affirms that Old Testament believers "had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation" through the Messiah. The 1689 agrees (Ch. 7.3: "all of the posterity of fallen Adam, that ever were saved, did obtain life and a blessed immortality" by the Covenant of Grace). The BLC drops this entirely, which is not a Baptist distinctive but a simple omission of shared Reformed theology.
  3. The WLC says grace and salvation under the New Testament are "held forth in more fulness, evidence, and efficacy, to all nations." This language of greater fulness/efficacy is absent from the BLC, though it would be appropriate even within a 1689 framework.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "fore-signify Christ then to come" — the typological purpose of Old Testament ordinances
  - "sufficient to build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah"
  - "by whom they then had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation"
  - "grace and salvation are held forth in more fulness, evidence, and efficacy, to all nations"

## Detailed Notes
1. **The substance/administration distinction is correct and important.** The BLC's opening assertion that the Old Covenant differs from the Covenant of Grace "not merely in administration, but also in substance" is the key 1689 distinctive (contrasting with WCF 7.5-6 which treats the Old and New as two administrations of one covenant). This is faithful to the 1689's omission of WCF 7.5-6 and its replacement with Ch. 7.3's distinct framework.

2. **But the BLC goes further than the 1689 in separating the Old Covenant from the gospel.** The 1689 carefully balances its substance distinction with the affirmation that the Covenant of Grace was progressively revealed from Adam onward and that all the saved were saved by it. The BLC's point-by-point contrast, without this balancing affirmation, could be read as implying a hard dispensational-like disjunction between the Testaments that the 1689 does not teach.

3. **National/temporal promises language needs qualification.** The BLC states that the Old Covenant "contained national, temporal promises" and cites Genesis 15:18, 17:8, etc. However, these are Abrahamic promises, not specifically Mosaic/Sinaitic promises. The 1689 does not characterize the Abrahamic promises as merely "national, temporal." The conflation of Abrahamic and Mosaic under "Old Covenant" is potentially confusing, as many 1689 Baptists distinguish between the Abrahamic promise (which is related to the Covenant of Grace) and the Mosaic covenant (which is the Old Covenant proper).

4. **The absence of faith.** The 1689 Ch. 7.2 specifies that the Covenant of Grace requires "Faith in him, that they may be saved." BLC Q34 describes the Covenant of Grace in terms of its priesthood, sacrifice, and means of administration, but never mentions faith. This is a notable omission for a catechism that should be training believers.

5. **The "single, complete sacrifice" language is good.** The BLC's description of "the very blood of the Redeemer as the single, complete sacrifice for sins" is a faithful and clear presentation of Hebrews 10:10, 14.

6. **Administration section is solid.** The contrast between circumcision/Passover and Word/baptism/Lord's Supper is clear and appropriate.

## Scripture Analysis
- **2 Corinthians 3:6-9; Hebrews 8:7-13:** Appropriate for the substance distinction between Old and New Covenants.
- **Hebrews 5:1-4; 7:11, 23:** Correctly cited for the temporary priesthood.
- **Hebrews 10:1-4:** Strong support for the insufficiency of animal sacrifices.
- **Leviticus 1-7; Hebrews 9:18-19:** Appropriate for Old Covenant sacrificial system.
- **Genesis 15:18; 17:8; 12:6-7; 13:14-17; 15:5:** These are Abrahamic, not Mosaic texts. While they contain national/temporal elements, using them to characterize "the Old Covenant" is potentially misleading, as many 1689 Baptists see the Abrahamic covenant as distinct from the Mosaic/Old Covenant. This proof text selection could inadvertently conflate the two.
- **Hebrews 7:24; 8:1, 6; 9:11-15:** Excellent citations for the eternal priesthood of Christ.
- **Hebrews 10:10, 14:** Perfect support for the single, complete sacrifice.
- **Galatians 3:7-14; Hebrews 9:11-12:** Appropriate for eternal salvation promise.
- **Romans 4:11; Genesis 17:9-14:** Appropriate for circumcision, though Romans 4:11 notably calls circumcision "a sign of the righteousness of faith" — which complicates the BLC's simple Old Covenant framing.
- **1 Corinthians 5:7; Exodus 12:** Appropriate for the Passover.
- **Matthew 28:19-20; Luke 24:47-48:** Appropriate for New Covenant administration.
- **Matthew 26:28; 1 Corinthians 11:23-25:** Appropriate for the Lord's Supper.
