# Q146: Who should preach the Word of God?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
The BLC closely mirrors WLC Q158 and is broadly consistent with the 1689 Confession's teaching on who may preach. However, two issues deserve attention: (1) the BLC omits the 1689's explicit mention of the Holy Spirit's role in gifting and fitting preachers, which is a notable omission of a key pneumatological qualification; and (2) the BLC substitutes "called to do it" for the WLC's "called to that office," which is actually an appropriate Baptist adjustment aligning with 1689 26.11's broader allowance for non-ordained preachers, but the BLC doesn't make this Baptist distinctive explicit.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel.

The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not contain a question specifically addressing who should preach the Word. The closest question is:

> Q. How is the word made effectual to salvation?
> A. The Spirit of God maketh the reading, but especially the preaching of the word, an effectual means of convincing and converting sinners, and of building them up in holiness and comfort through faith unto salvation.

This question addresses the *efficacy* of preaching rather than the *qualifications* of preachers, making it an indirect parallel at best. The BLC Q146 therefore fills a gap in the Shorter Catechism rather than expanding on an existing question, taking its lead from the WLC.

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel to omit from)
- Structural changes: N/A

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 26, Paragraphs 9 and 11

**Paragraph 9:**
> "The way appointed by Christ for the Calling of any person, fitted, and gifted by the Holy Spirit, unto the Office of Bishop, or Elder, in a Church, is, that he be chosen thereunto by the common suffrage of the Church it self; and Solemnly set apart by Fasting and Prayer, with imposition of hands of the Eldership of the Church, if there be any before Constituted therein."

**Paragraph 11:**
> "Although it be incumbent on the Bishops or Pastors of the Churches to be instant in Preaching the Word, by way of Office; yet the work of Preaching the Word, is not so peculiarly confined to them; but that others also gifted, and fitted by the Holy Spirit for it, and approved, and called by the Church, may and ought to perform it."

Key observations:

- **Agreement on gifting and calling:** The BLC's requirement that preachers be "sufficiently gifted" and "properly approved and called" aligns with the 1689's teaching that preachers must be gifted, approved, and called.

- **Omission — the Holy Spirit:** The 1689 twice specifies that the gifting and fitting is *by the Holy Spirit* (26.9: "fitted, and gifted by the Holy Spirit"; 26.11: "gifted, and fitted by the Holy Spirit for it"). The BLC says only "sufficiently gifted" without identifying the source of the gifts. This is a notable omission. The pneumatological qualification is theologically significant: it grounds the preacher's ability in divine empowerment rather than mere natural talent or academic training. The Confession deliberately attributes the gifting to the Spirit, and the BLC should preserve this.

- **Omission — "fitted":** The 1689 consistently pairs "gifted" with "fitted" (26.9, 26.11), suggesting two distinguishable qualifications — spiritual gifts and personal suitability/preparation. The BLC collapses these into "sufficiently gifted," losing the nuance.

- **Omission — "by the Church":** The 1689 specifies that non-ordained preachers must be "approved, and called by the Church" (26.11). The BLC says "properly approved and called to do it" without specifying the Church as the approving and calling body. This is a significant ecclesiological detail in Baptist polity — the local congregation's role in recognizing and authorizing preachers.

- **Agreement on breadth of preaching ministry:** The BLC's phrasing "called to do it" (rather than "called to that office") is actually well-suited to the 1689's distinctive Baptist teaching that preaching is not confined to the office of elder. This is a legitimate Baptist adjustment from the WLC.

- Omissions: Holy Spirit as source of gifting; "fitted" alongside "gifted"; "by the Church" as the approving body
- Structural changes: None significant

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q158

> Q. 158. By whom is the Word of God to be preached?
> A. The Word of God is to be preached only by such as are sufficiently gifted, and also duly approved and called to that office.

- **Solidarity: STRONG** — The BLC closely follows the WLC's structure and language. Both use "sufficiently gifted" and the combined requirement of approval and calling. The sentence structure is nearly identical.

- Intentional Baptist divergences:
  - **"called to do it" vs. "called to that office":** The WLC ties preaching to the ministerial *office*, consistent with Presbyterian polity where preaching is a mark of the ordained ministry. The BLC changes "that office" to "do it," which aligns with the 1689's explicit teaching (26.11) that preaching is not confined to the office of elder/bishop. This is a legitimate and expected Baptist ecclesiological adjustment, though one the BLC makes subtly rather than explicitly.

- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"properly" vs. "duly":** The BLC substitutes "properly approved" for "duly approved." This is stylistic modernization with no theological significance.

- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"to that office":** Dropped and replaced with "to do it." As noted above, this is a theologically motivated Baptist adjustment, not a careless omission. However, the BLC could have been more explicit about the Baptist broadening of preaching beyond the office.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The pneumatological gap is the primary concern.** Both the WLC and 1689 ground the preacher's gifting in God's sovereign provision (the WLC implicitly through its proof texts citing Eph 4:8-11; the 1689 explicitly through "gifted by the Holy Spirit"). The BLC's bare "sufficiently gifted" could be read as referring to natural abilities or training rather than Spirit-given gifts. A catechism that teaches *who* should preach ought to make clear *whence* the gifts come.

2. **The office/activity distinction is well-handled.** The BLC's shift from "office" to "do it" is a defensible adjustment that reflects the 1689's distinctive Baptist position. The WLC's Presbyterian ecclesiology ties preaching strictly to the ministerial office; Baptists have historically recognized a broader preaching ministry while still requiring church approval.

3. **The proof texts are appropriate.** The BLC cites 1 Timothy 3:2-7 and Titus 1:7-9 for the gifting requirement (elder qualifications), Malachi 2:6-7 (the priest's lips should keep knowledge), and 2 Corinthians 3:5-6 (sufficiency from God — which partially addresses the pneumatological concern at the level of proof texts, even though the answer text itself doesn't make this explicit). For the calling requirement, the BLC cites Jeremiah 14:14-15, Romans 10:14-15, Hebrews 5:1-4, 1 Timothy 4:14-16, and 1 Timothy 5:22. These are well-chosen and overlap substantially with the WLC's proof texts.

4. **Note on "2 Timothy 2:1-2" in the proof texts:** The BLC cites "Timothy 2:1-2" which appears to be a reference to 2 Timothy 2:1-2 ("the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also"). This is an excellent text for the topic but the reference appears to be missing the book number "2" before "Timothy."

## Scripture Analysis
**BLC Proof Texts for Section 1 (sufficiently gifted):**
- 1 Timothy 3:2-7 — Elder qualifications including "apt to teach." Shared ground with WLC proof text 1015.
- Titus 1:7-9 — Elder qualifications. Appropriate parallel to 1 Tim 3.
- "Timothy 2:1-2" — Likely 2 Timothy 2:1-2, on entrusting teaching to faithful men. Strong and relevant, but the reference may have a typo.
- Malachi 2:6-7 — The priest's lips should keep knowledge. Shared with WLC proof text 1015.
- 2 Corinthians 3:5-6 — "Our sufficiency is of God; who also hath made us able ministers." Shared with WLC proof text 1015. This text implicitly supports the pneumatological point the answer text fails to make explicit.

**BLC Proof Texts for Section 2 (approved and called):**
- Jeremiah 14:14-15 — False prophets whom God did not send. Parallel to WLC proof text 1016.
- Romans 10:14-15 — "How shall they preach, except they be sent?" Shared with WLC proof text 1016.
- Hebrews 5:1-4 — No one takes the priestly honor upon himself. Broader than WLC's citation of Heb 5:4 alone.
- 1 Timothy 4:14-16 — The gift given by prophecy with laying on of hands. Shared with WLC proof text 1016.
- 1 Timothy 5:22 — "Lay hands suddenly on no man." Shared with WLC proof text 1016.

The proof text selection is strong and well-aligned with both the WLC and 1689 traditions. The absence of Acts 11:19-21 and 1 Peter 4:10-11 (cited by the 1689 at 26.11 for non-ordained preaching) is notable but understandable given the catechism's concise format.
