# Q114: To whom does father and mother refer in the fifth commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q114 faithfully modernizes WLC Q124-125 in a single combined question, preserving all essential theological content: natural parents, superiors in age and gifts, and those in authority in family, church, and civil government. The reciprocal teaching about why superiors are styled "father and mother" (both as instruction to superiors and encouragement to inferiors) is fully retained. The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question on this specific topic, and the 1689 Confession does not address the meaning of "father and mother" in the fifth commandment directly but provides consistent teaching on civil authority.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism addresses the fifth commandment in terms of duty rather than defining who is meant by "father and mother":

> Q. Which is the fifth commandment?
> A. The fifth commandment is, Honor thy father and thy mother; that thy days may be long in the land which the Lord thy God giveth thee (Ex. 20:12).
>
> Q. What is required in the fifth commandment?
> A. The fifth commandment requireth the preserving the honour and performing the duties belonging to every one in their several places and relations, as superiors (Eph. 5:21), inferiors (1 Pet. 2:17), or equals (Rom. 12:10).
>
> Q. What is forbidden in the fifth commandment?
> A. The fifth commandment forbiddeth the neglect of, or doing any thing against the honour and duty which belongeth to every one in their several places and relations (Mt. 15:4-6; Ez. 34:24; Rom. 13:8).
>
> Q. What is the reason annexed to the fifth commandment?
> A. The reason annexed to the fifth commandment is a promise of long life and prosperity (as far as it shall serve for God's glory, and their own good) to all such as keep this commandment (Deut. 5:16; Eph. 6:2, 3).

- Omissions: None. The Shorter addresses the fifth commandment from a different angle (duties required/forbidden) and does not attempt to define who "father and mother" means. The BLC appropriately expands into this definitional question as a larger catechism should.
- Structural changes: None. The Shorter's language of "superiors, inferiors, or equals" in the required duties question is consistent with the BLC's framework of superiors in age, gifts, and authority. The BLC's separate question defining "father and mother" properly complements the Shorter's treatment rather than replacing it.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 24 — Of the Civil Magistrate:

> "1. God the supream Lord, and King of all the World, hath ordained Civil Magistrates to be under him, over the people for his own glory, and the publick good; and to this end hath armed them with the power of the Sword, for defence and encouragement of them that do good, and for the punishment of evil doers."
>
> "3. Civil Magistrates being set up by God, for the ends aforesaid; subjection in all lawful things commanded by them, ought to be yeilded by us, in the Lord; not only for wrath but for Conscience sake; and we ought to make supplications and prayers for Kings, and all that are in Authority, that under them we may live a quiet and peaceable life, in all godliness and honesty."

The 1689 does not directly address the meaning of "father and mother" in the fifth commandment, as confessions typically do not provide catechetical exposition of individual commandments at this level of detail. However, the Confession's teaching on civil magistrates being ordained by God and owed subjection is fully consistent with the BLC's inclusion of civil government among those encompassed by "father and mother."

- Omissions: None applicable — the Confession does not treat this specific question.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's three spheres of authority (family, church, civil government) align with the 1689's treatment of church authority (Ch. 26) and civil authority (Ch. 24), and its presupposition of family authority throughout.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q124: "Who are meant by father and mother in the fifth commandment?"
> "By father and mother, in the fifth commandment, are meant, not only natural parents, but all superiors in age and gifts; and especially such as, by God's ordinance, are over us in place of authority, whether in family, church, or commonwealth."

WLC Q125: "Why are superiors styled father and mother?"
> "Superiors are styled father and mother, both to teach them in all duties toward their inferiors, like natural parents, to express love and tenderness to them, according to their several relations; and to work inferiors to a greater willingness and cheerfulness in performing their duties to their superiors, as to their parents."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology distinctions.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - The BLC uses "civil government" where the WLC uses "commonwealth." This is a simple modernization with no theological significance.
  - The BLC combines WLC Q124 and Q125 into a single question. This is a structural choice but no content is lost — both the definition (Q124 material) and the reason for the terminology (Q125 material) are fully preserved.
  - The WLC says superiors are styled father and mother "both to teach them in all duties toward their inferiors" — the BLC rephrases this as reminding those in authority that "they are responsible for and should act in a loving and tender way." The BLC's "responsible for" captures the duty concept, and "loving and tender" preserves the WLC's "love and tenderness."
  - The WLC says this is "to work inferiors to a greater willingness and cheerfulness in performing their duties to their superiors, as to their parents." The BLC rephrases as "those under them are also encouraged to accept their authority more willingly and cheerfully, as if they were their natural parents." This is faithful modernization.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - The WLC's "especially such as, by God's ordinance" becomes "specifically to those whom God has ordained to be over us in positions of authority." The word "especially" (WLC) versus "specifically" (BLC) is a very minor shift — "especially" implies the category of those in authority is the most important subset within the broader group of superiors in age and gifts, while "specifically" functions similarly in context. No theological loss.
  - The WLC's "in all duties toward their inferiors" is simplified in the BLC but the concept of comprehensive duty is retained through "responsible for."

## Detailed Notes
1. **Combination of WLC Q124-125**: The BLC merges two WLC questions into one. This is an effective approach for a modernized catechism — the definitional question and the rationale for the terminology flow naturally together. No theological content is sacrificed.

2. **Three spheres of authority preserved**: Family, church, and civil government are all retained as the three domains of authority encompassed by the fifth commandment. This is a key structural element of the Reformed understanding of the fifth commandment, and it is fully intact.

3. **Reciprocal teaching preserved**: The BLC maintains both directions of the teaching — (a) that the parental language instructs superiors in their duty of care, and (b) that it encourages inferiors in willing submission. This two-directional application is theologically important and faithfully reproduced.

4. **Language of "inferiors/superiors"**: The BLC uses "those under them" rather than "inferiors," which is a reasonable modernization. The WLC's technical vocabulary ("inferiors," "superiors") is partially retained ("superiors in age") and partially replaced with more accessible language.
