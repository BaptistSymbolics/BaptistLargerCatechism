# Q45: How does Christ make intercession?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q45 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q55 with near-verbatim structural correspondence. It preserves all the key elements of Christ's intercessory work found in the 1689 Confession and the Shorter Catechism, while appropriately expanding on the Shorter's brief reference to "continual intercession." No omissions, contradictions, or structural distortions are present.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. How doth Christ execute the office of a priest?
> A. Christ executeth the office of priest in his once offering up himself a sacrifice to satisfy divine justice (Heb. 9:14, 28) and reconcile us to God (Heb. 2:17), and in making continual intercession for us (Heb. 7:24, 25).

- Omissions: None. The Shorter mentions intercession only briefly ("making continual intercession for us") as one half of the priestly office. The BLC expands on this by describing the *manner* of that intercession, which is the proper function of a Larger Catechism question.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC treats the content of intercession as a separate question (as the WLC does), while the Shorter combines sacrifice and intercession under the single priestly office question. This is an appropriate expansion, not a structural alteration.
- The BLC's phrase "continually appearing" preserves the Shorter's "continual intercession" language.
- The Shorter's two aspects of priesthood (sacrifice to satisfy justice/reconcile, and intercession) are both presupposed in BLC Q45, which focuses specifically on the "how" of intercession, mentioning "the merit of His obedience and sacrifice on earth" as the ground.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8 (Of Christ the Mediator)

Paragraph 4:
> "...ascended into heaven: and there sitteth at the right hand of his Father, making intercession; and shall return to judge Men and Angels, at the end of the World."

Paragraph 8:
> "To all those for whom Christ hath obtained eternal redemption, he doth certainly, and effectually apply, and communicate the same; making intercession for them, uniting them to himself by his spirit, revealing unto them, in and by the word, the mystery of salvation; perswading them to believe, and obey; governing their hearts by his word and spirit, and overcoming all their enemies by his Almighty power, and wisdom..."

- Omissions: None. The 1689 mentions intercession in two places: briefly in 8.4 (the narrative of Christ's work) and in 8.8 (its effectual application). The BLC faithfully captures the intercession theme while expanding on *how* it is carried out, consistent with its catechetical purpose.
- Structural changes: None. The 1689 places intercession within the broader context of Christ's effectual application of redemption (8.8). The BLC appropriately isolates the "how" of intercession as a distinct question while maintaining the connection to Christ's heavenly appearance, sacrifice, and advocacy.
- The 1689's emphasis on Christ interceding "for them" (those for whom redemption was obtained) is reflected in the BLC's consistent "for them" / "their" language and the proof texts from John 17:9, 20, 24.
- The 1689's emphasis on "all of free, and absolute Grace, without any condition foreseen in them" (8.8) is not explicitly stated in BLC Q45 but is also not contradicted; the BLC's focus is narrower (the manner of intercession specifically), and this broader gracious framework is addressed elsewhere in the catechism.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q55: How doeth Christ make intercession?
> A. Christ maketh intercession, by his appearing in our nature continually before the Father in heaven, in the merit of his obedience and sacrifice on earth, declaring his will to have it applied to all believers; answering all accusations against them, and procuring for them quiet of conscience, notwithstanding daily failings, access with boldness to the throne of grace, and acceptance of their persons and services.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- The BLC follows the WLC almost verbatim in structure and content, with only minor modernizing adjustments:
  - "his appearing in our nature continually" → "continually appearing in our human nature" (word order modernized, "human" added for clarity)
  - "declaring his will to have it applied to all believers" → "declaring His will to be applied to all believers" (simplified syntax)
  - "quiet of conscience, notwithstanding daily failings" → "peace of conscience, in spite of their daily failures" ("peace" for "quiet," "in spite of" for "notwithstanding," "failures" for "failings")
  - "acceptance of their persons and services" → "full acceptance of their persons and services" ("full" added)
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this doctrine is not a point of Baptist-Presbyterian divergence).
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every element of the WLC answer is preserved in the BLC.
- The addition of "full" before "acceptance of their persons and services" is a minor intensification consistent with the theology of both traditions, not a divergence.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's proof texts are almost identical to the WLC's, with slight variation:
  - BLC adds Isaiah 53:12 alongside Hebrews 1:3 for the merit of obedience/sacrifice section. This is a sound addition, as Isaiah 53:12 explicitly states that the Servant "made intercession for the transgressors."
  - The proof text alignment is otherwise virtually identical between BLC and WLC.
- The BLC's section structure cleanly separates each aspect of Christ's intercession, making the theological progression clear: (1) heavenly appearance in human nature, (2) ground in obedience/sacrifice, (3) declaration of His will for believers, (4) advocacy against accusations, (5) procuring peace of conscience, (6) boldness of access, (7) full acceptance.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Hebrews 9:12, 24** — Christ's entrance into the heavenly holy place, appearing before God for us. Directly supports "continually appearing in our human nature before the Father in heaven."
- **Isaiah 53:12** — "He ... made intercession for the transgressors." Added by BLC (not in WLC proof texts) as additional support for the sacrificial ground of intercession. Appropriate addition.
- **Hebrews 1:3** — Christ's purging of sins and sitting at God's right hand. Supports "the merit of His obedience and sacrifice on earth."
- **John 3:16; John 17:9, 20, 24** — Supports "declaring His will to be applied to all believers." John 17 is particularly apt as the high priestly prayer. John 3:16 (shared with WLC) highlights the divine will for believers.
- **Romans 8:33-34** — The forensic advocacy of Christ against accusations. Directly supports "answering all accusations against them."
- **Romans 5:1-2; 1 John 2:1-2** — Justification producing peace, and Christ as advocate when believers sin. Supports "procuring for them peace of conscience, in spite of their daily failures."
- **Hebrews 4:16** — "Come boldly unto the throne of grace." Directly supports "access with boldness to the throne of grace."
- **Ephesians 1:6** — Accepted in the Beloved. Supports "full acceptance of their persons."
- **1 Peter 2:5** — Spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ. Supports "and services."

All proof texts are well-chosen and doctrinally appropriate. No questionable or strained uses of Scripture.
