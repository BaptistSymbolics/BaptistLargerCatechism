# Q157: Did not baptism come to replace circumcision and thus, should be administered as it was?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q157 addresses the paedobaptist argument that baptism replaces circumcision and should therefore be administered on the same terms (including to infants). The BLC's answer — that spiritual circumcision (regeneration by the Spirit), not baptism, replaces physical circumcision as the seal of the covenant of grace, and that baptism is connected with faith rather than following circumcision's administration rules — is well-grounded in the 1689 Confession's Appendix and Chapter 29. There is no direct Shorter Catechism parallel for this polemical question, and the intentional divergence from the WLC's covenant administration framework (Q34) is an expected and appropriate Baptist departure. The answer is theologically precise and faithful to Particular Baptist covenant theology.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel

The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not address the circumcision-baptism replacement argument directly. The closest questions are:

> Q. What is baptism?
> A. Baptism is an ordinance of the New Testament instituted by Jesus Christ, to be unto the party baptized a sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, burial, and resurrection; of his being ingrafted into him (Rom. 6:3, 4, 5; Col. 2:12; Gal. 3:27); of remission of sins (Mk. 1:4; Acts 2:38, and 22:16); and of his giving up himself unto God through Jesus Christ, to live and walk in newness of life (Rom. 6:3, 4).

> Q. To whom is baptism to be administered?
> A. Baptism is to be administered to all those who actually profess repentance towards God (Acts 2:38; Mt. 3:6), faith in and obedience to our Lord Jesus Christ, and to none other (Acts 8:12, 36, 37, 38; 10:47, 48).

> Q. Are the infants of such as are professing believers to be baptized?
> A. The infants of such as are professing believers are not to be baptized, because there is neither command or example in the holy scriptures, or certain consequence from them to baptize such (Ex. 23:13; Pr. 30:6; Lk. 3:7, 8).

- Omissions: Not applicable (no direct parallel to compare).
- Structural changes: Not applicable.

The BLC's Q157 serves as supplementary polemical material expanding on the Shorter's denial of infant baptism, providing the theological rationale for *why* the circumcision argument fails. This is appropriate "Larger Catechism" material that deepens the Shorter's basic assertion.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 7, Paragraphs 2-3 (Of God's Covenant); Chapter 29 (Of Baptism); Appendix on Baptism

The most relevant text is from the 1689 Appendix on Baptism:

> "yet we conceive the seal of that Covenant is the indwelling of the Spirit of Christ in the particular and individual persons in whom he resides, and nothing else, neither do they or we suppose that baptism is in any such manner substituted in the place of circumcision, as to have the same (and no other) latitude, extent, or terms, then circumcision had; for that was suited only for the Male children, baptism is an ordinance suited for every beleiver, whether male, or femal."

And:

> "If our brethren do suppose baptism to be the seal of the Covenant which God makes with every beleiver (of which the Scriptures are altogether silent) it is not our concern to contend with them herein; yet we conceive the seal of that Covenant is the indwelling of the Spirit of Christ in the particular and individual persons in whom he resides, and nothing else"

Also relevant, 1689 Chapter 7.3:

> "This Covenant is revealed in the Gospel; first of all to Adam in the promise of Salvation by the seed of the woman, and afterwards by farther steps, untill the full discovery thereof was compleated in the new Testament; and it is founded in that Eternal Covenant transaction, that was between the Father and the Son, about the Redemption of the Elect"

And 1689 Chapter 29.1-2:

> "Baptism is an Ordinance of the New Testament, ordained by Jesus Christ, to be unto the party Baptized, a sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, and resurrection; of his being engrafted into him; of remission of sins; and of his giving up unto God through Jesus Christ to live and walk in newness of Life."

> "Those who do actually professe repentance towards God, faith in, and obedience, to our Lord Jesus, are the only proper subjects of this ordinance."

- Omissions: None significant. The BLC faithfully captures the Appendix's two key arguments: (1) the seal of the covenant is the Spirit's indwelling (spiritual circumcision), not baptism, and (2) baptism should not be administered according to circumcision's rules.
- Structural changes: None problematic. The BLC condenses the Appendix's lengthy argument into a catechetical form while preserving the core theological logic.

**Agreement:** The BLC's claim that "Baptism does not replace circumcision, but rather regeneration by the Spirit, the circumcision made without hands (commonly called spiritual circumcision), replaces physical circumcision as the seal of the covenant of grace" directly mirrors the Appendix's argument that the seal of the covenant is "the indwelling of the Spirit of Christ." The BLC's conclusion that "we should not administer baptism according to the rules of administration for circumcision" directly echoes the Appendix's denial that baptism is "substituted in the place of circumcision, as to have the same (and no other) latitude, extent, or terms."

**Expansion:** The BLC adds the reference to Colossians 2:11-12 as the bridge text connecting baptism with faith (rather than with circumcision's administration). This is a sound exegetical move that distills the Appendix's more diffuse argument into a focused catechetical point.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct WLC parallel (the WLC does not raise or address this question)

The most relevant WLC questions are:

> Q. 34. How was the covenant of grace administered under the Old Testament?
> A. The covenant of grace was administered under the Old Testament, by promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the passover, and other types and ordinances, which did all fore-signify Christ then to come, and were for that time sufficient to build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by whom they then had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation.

> Q. 35. How is the covenant of grace administered under the New Testament?
> A. Under the New Testament, when Christ the substance was exhibited, the same covenant of grace was and still is to be administered in the preaching of the Word, and the administration of the sacraments of Baptism and the Lord's Supper; in which grace and salvation are held forth in more fulness, evidence, and efficacy, to all nations.

> Q. 166. Unto whom is baptism to be administered?
> A. Baptism is not to be administered to any that are out of the visible church, and so strangers from the covenant of promise, till they profess their faith in Christ, and obedience to him, but infants descending from parents, either both, or but one of them, professing faith in Christ, and obedience to him, are in that respect within the covenant, and to be baptized.

- Solidarity: **WEAK** (intentionally so — this is a core Baptist-Presbyterian divergence)
- Intentional Baptist divergences: The WLC's Q34-35 framework implicitly supports the circumcision-to-baptism continuity argument that the BLC explicitly rejects. In the WLC's covenantal framework, circumcision and baptism are both seals of the same covenant of grace under different administrations, naturally supporting paedobaptism (Q166). The BLC's entire Q157 is a direct rebuttal of this framework. This is an expected and necessary divergence for a Baptist catechism.
- Unexpected divergences: None. All divergences flow from the fundamental Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement on covenant theology and the subjects of baptism.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's language of "the same covenant of grace" being "administered" differently under Old and New Testaments (implying circumcision and baptism are parallel signs) is intentionally rejected rather than dropped. The 1689 Confession deliberately omits the Westminster Confession's Chapter 7.5-6 distinction between Old and New Testament *administrations* of one covenant, instead presenting the covenant of grace as revealed progressively but not administered through circumcision in the same covenantal sense.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Catechetical function:** Q157 is one of a series of polemical questions (Q155-158) that address paedobaptist arguments. This is appropriate "Larger Catechism" material — the Shorter Catechism states the positive doctrine, while the Larger addresses objections. This parallels how the WLC itself handles objections in other doctrinal areas.

2. **The "spiritual circumcision" argument:** The BLC's identification of spiritual circumcision (regeneration by the Spirit) as the replacement for physical circumcision is theologically precise. The Appendix to the 1689 makes exactly this argument: the seal of the covenant is "the indwelling of the Spirit of Christ," not baptism. The BLC's use of "circumcision made without hands" terminology comes directly from Colossians 2:11.

3. **Colossians 2:11-12 as the linchpin:** The BLC's third section uses Colossians 2:11-12 to argue that baptism is "mentioned with faith as the means whereby we are in Christ, and complete in Him." This is a faithful reading of the passage, where Paul connects circumcision made without hands (spiritual regeneration) with baptism "through the faith of the operation of God." The WLC actually uses this same passage (in its proof texts for Q167) but reads it differently — as supporting the continuity of circumcision and baptism as covenant signs. The BLC's reading emphasizes faith as the connecting element, consistent with Baptist hermeneutics.

4. **Relationship to Q158:** The BLC follows Q157 with Q158, which addresses the Abrahamic covenant argument more directly (distinguishing the national covenant from the covenant of grace). Together, Q157-158 form a comprehensive Baptist response to the two main paedobaptist arguments from the Abrahamic covenant.

## Scripture Analysis
**Section 1 — Philippians 3:2-3; Romans 2:28-29; Deuteronomy 30:6; Deuteronomy 10:16:**
These texts are well-chosen to support the claim that spiritual circumcision replaces physical circumcision:
- **Philippians 3:2-3**: "For we are the circumcision, which worship God in the spirit, and rejoice in Christ Jesus, and have no confidence in the flesh." Directly identifies believers as "the circumcision" based on spiritual reality, not physical rite.
- **Romans 2:28-29**: "circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter." Explicitly teaches that true circumcision is spiritual, not physical. This text is also cited in the WLC's proof texts for Q165 (footnote 1056), showing both traditions recognize its importance but draw different conclusions.
- **Deuteronomy 30:6; 10:16**: Old Testament roots of the spiritual circumcision concept — God will "circumcise thine heart." These establish that even under the Old Covenant, the physical rite pointed to a spiritual reality.

**Section 2 — Ephesians 1:13-14; 2 Corinthians 1:21-22; Jeremiah 31:31-34:**
These texts support the claim that the Spirit (not baptism) is the seal of the covenant:
- **Ephesians 1:13-14**: "ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise, Which is the earnest of our inheritance." The Spirit is explicitly called the "seal" — directly supporting the BLC's (and the 1689 Appendix's) argument.
- **2 Corinthians 1:21-22**: "Who hath also sealed us, and given the earnest of the Spirit in our hearts." Reinforces the Spirit as seal.
- **Jeremiah 31:31-34**: The New Covenant passage, in which the law is written on hearts. This supports the discontinuity between Old and New Covenant administration.

**Section 3 — Colossians 2:11-12:**
The pivotal text for the entire argument:
- "In whom also ye are circumcised with the circumcision made without hands, in putting off the body of the sins of the flesh by the circumcision of Christ: Buried with him in baptism, wherein also ye are risen with him through the faith of the operation of God, who hath raised him from the dead."
- The BLC reads this as: spiritual circumcision → baptism → faith → completeness in Christ. Baptism is connected to faith ("through the faith of the operation of God"), not to the administration rules of physical circumcision. This is a defensible exegetical reading and aligns with the 1689's emphasis on faith as prerequisite for baptism (Chapter 29.2).
- Notably, this is the same text the WLC cites to support improving baptism (Q167, footnote 1068) and that appears in the WLC's infant baptism proof texts (Q166, footnote 1067). The different conclusions drawn from the same text reflect the underlying hermeneutical difference between the traditions.
