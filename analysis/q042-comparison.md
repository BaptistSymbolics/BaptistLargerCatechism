# Q42: What offices does Christ execute as our Redeemer?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q42 faithfully preserves the threefold office (prophet, priest, king) and the dual estate framework (humiliation and exaltation) from both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession. However, the BLC drops the WLC's significant theological content about the *reason* Christ is called "Christ" — namely, that He was "anointed with the Holy Ghost above measure" and "set apart, and fully furnished with all authority and ability" to execute these offices. Since the Shorter Catechism also lacks this anointing language (the BSC parallels BLC closely), this is not an omission from the primary standards but represents a missed opportunity for the "Larger" catechism to expand on this important theme as the WLC does.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What offices doth Christ execute as our Redeemer?
> A. Christ as our Redeemer executeth the offices of a prophet, of a priest, and of king, both in his estate of humiliation and exaltation (Acts 3:22; Heb. 12:25; 2 Cor. 13:3; Heb. 5:5, 6, 7; 7:25; Ps. 2:6; Is. 9:6, 7; Mt. 21:5; Ps. 2:8-11).

- Omissions: None of substance. The BLC preserves the threefold office and the dual estate exactly as in the Shorter.
- Structural changes: The BLC adds "of His church" to the kingly office ("king of His church"), which the Shorter simply states as "king." This addition is not an alteration but a clarification consistent with the 1689 Confession (Ch. 8.1: "King; Head and Saviour of his Church") and the WLC ("king of his church"). This is a positive expansion.
- The BLC modernizes "doth...execute" to "does...execute" — appropriate stylistic update.
- The BLC adds section-level proof texts rather than listing them all at the end, which provides more granular Scripture support for each office.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8 (Of Christ the Mediator)

Paragraph 1:
> "It pleased God in his eternal purpose, to chuse and ordain the Lord Jesus his only begotten Son, according to the Covenant made between them both, to be the Mediator between God and Man; the Prophet, Priest and King; Head and Saviour of his Church, the heir of all things, and judge of the world."

Paragraph 9:
> "This office of Mediator between God and Man, is proper onely to Christ, who is the Prophet, Priest, and King of the Church of God; and may not be either in whole, or any part thereof transfer'd from him to any other."

Paragraph 10:
> "This number and order of Offices is necessary; for in respect of our ignorance, we stand in need of his prophetical Office; and in respect of our alienation from God, and imperfection of the best of our services, we need his Priestly office, to reconcile us, and present us acceptable unto God: and in respect of our averseness, and utter inability to return to God, and for our rescue, and security from our spiritual adversaries, we need his Kingly office, to convince, subdue, draw, uphold, deliver, and preserve us to his Heavenly Kingdome."

- Omissions: The BLC does not address the *necessity* of each office (Ch. 8.10) — why we need each one (ignorance→prophet, alienation→priest, averseness→king). However, this is appropriate for a question that simply identifies the offices; the necessity of each office is a distinct doctrinal point that would belong in a separate question.
- Structural changes: None. The threefold office and dual estate are preserved faithfully.
- The BLC's phrase "king of His church" aligns well with 1689 Ch. 8.9's "King of the Church of God."
- The 1689 also adds "Head and Saviour of his Church, the heir of all things, and judge of the world" (Ch. 8.1), but these are additional descriptors of the Mediator's role beyond the scope of this question.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q42:
> Q. 42. Why was our Mediator called Christ?
> A. Our Mediator was called Christ, because he was anointed with the Holy Ghost above measure, and so set apart, and fully furnished with all authority and ability, to execute the offices of prophet, priest, and king of his church, in the estate both of his humiliation and exaltation.

- Solidarity: **MODERATE**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is pure Christology with no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology distinctions at stake.
- Unexpected divergences: The WLC Q42 addresses a *different question* — not "what offices" but "*why* was our Mediator called Christ?" The WLC roots the threefold office in Christ's anointing by the Holy Spirit ("anointed with the Holy Ghost above measure") and His being "set apart, and fully furnished with all authority and ability." The BLC Q42 instead parallels the BSC (and WLC Q43-45 territory) by simply asking what offices Christ executes. This is a legitimate structural choice — the BLC follows the Shorter Catechism's question rather than the WLC's — but it means the BLC lacks a question corresponding to the WLC's important teaching on the anointing as the basis of the offices.
- Key phrases dropped: "anointed with the Holy Ghost above measure," "set apart, and fully furnished with all authority and ability." These phrases are theologically significant because they ground the munus triplex in Christ's messianic anointing (the meaning of "Christ/Messiah"). Whether the BLC addresses this elsewhere would determine if this is a gap or simply a structural redistribution.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Question framing**: The BLC asks "What offices does Christ execute as our Redeemer?" which closely mirrors the BSC's "What offices doth Christ execute as our Redeemer?" The WLC asks the distinct question "Why was our Mediator called Christ?" — a deeper question that explains the *ground* of the offices in the messianic anointing. The BLC's choice to follow the BSC rather than the WLC is defensible but means the "Larger" catechism does not expand beyond the Shorter on this point.

2. **"King of His church"**: The BLC adds this qualifier (absent in the BSC, present in both the WLC and 1689). This is a good addition that makes explicit what the standards teach.

3. **Empty proof text**: The final section ("both in His estate of humiliation and exaltation") has no proof texts assigned. The BSC provides proof texts for the whole answer collectively (Acts 3:22; Heb. 12:25; 2 Cor. 13:3; Heb. 5:5, 6, 7; 7:25; Ps. 2:6; Is. 9:6, 7; Mt. 21:5; Ps. 2:8-11). This is likely not a theological issue but a formatting gap — the dual estate is supported by the proof texts already given for each individual office.

4. **No expansion beyond the Shorter**: As a "Larger" catechism, one might expect Q42 to expand on the BSC's answer — perhaps incorporating the WLC's anointing language or the 1689's language about the necessity of each office. Instead, BLC Q42 is nearly identical to the BSC answer with only the addition of "of His church." The expansion presumably comes in subsequent questions about each individual office.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Prophet proof texts**: Acts 3:21-22 (Moses' prophecy of a prophet); Luke 4:18-21 (Jesus reads Isaiah in the synagogue); Hebrews 1:1-2 (God speaks through His Son); Deuteronomy 18:18 (the prophet like Moses). These are solid and overlap with both the BSC (Acts 3:22) and the 1689 (Acts 3.22).
- **Priest proof texts**: Hebrews 5:5-7 (Christ's priestly appointment and suffering); Hebrews 4:14-15 (great high priest). These align with the BSC (Heb. 5:5, 6, 7) and the 1689 (Heb. 5.5,6).
- **King proof texts**: Psalm 2:6 (installed as King on Zion); Luke 1:32-34 (throne of David); John 18:37 (born to be king); Matthew 21:5 (king comes riding on a donkey); Isaiah 9:6-7 (government on His shoulders); Philippians 2:8-11 (exalted and given the name above every name). These are strong and overlap with the BSC (Ps. 2:6; Is. 9:6, 7; Mt. 21:5) and the 1689 (Ps. 2.6; Luk. 1.33).
- **Luke 1:32-34**: The reference is slightly unusual — v. 34 is Mary's question about the conception, not directly about kingship. Luke 1:32-33 would be the more precise reference for Christ's kingly office. This is a minor proof text imprecision.
- **Empty final section**: No proof texts for "both in His estate of humiliation and exaltation." The BSC provides texts that cover both estates (Heb. 12:25; 2 Cor. 13:3; Heb. 7:25; Ps. 2:8-11). Consider adding representative texts here.
