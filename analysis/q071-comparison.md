# Q71: Why are believers not completely sanctified?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q71 is a close modernization of WLC Q78 and faithfully reflects 1689 Chapter XIII, paragraphs 2-3. The answer preserves all the key theological elements: remnants of sin in every part, the flesh-Spirit warfare, defeat by temptation, commission of many sins, hindrance in spiritual duties, and the imperfection/defilement of best works. However, the BLC omits the 1689's critical paragraph 3 teaching that despite corruption's temporary prevalence, the regenerate part ultimately overcomes through the Spirit — a significant qualification that balances the otherwise bleak picture of ongoing sin.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism has no separate question on the imperfection of sanctification. The closest is the definition of sanctification: "Sanctification is the work of God's free grace, whereby we are renewed in the whole man after the image of God, and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness." (2 Thess. 2:13; Eph. 4:23, 24; Rom. 6:4, 6; 8:1)
- Omissions: N/A — no direct parallel to omit from.
- Structural changes: N/A.
- The BLC is expanding beyond the Shorter here, following the WLC's more detailed treatment. This is appropriate for a "Larger" catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XIII, Paragraphs 2-3.

Paragraph 2: "This Sanctification is throughout, in the whole man, yet imperfect in this life; there abideth still some remnants of corruption in every part, whence ariseth a continual, and irreconcilable war; the Flesh lusting against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the Flesh."

Paragraph 3: "In which war, although the remaining corruption for a time may much prevail; yet through the continual supply of strength from the sanctifying Spirit of Christ the regenerate part doth overcome; and so the Saints grow in Grace, perfecting holiness in the fear of God, pressing after an heavenly life, in Evangelical Obedience to all the commands which Christ as Head and King, in his Word hath prescribed to them."

- Omissions: The BLC covers paragraph 2 well but **omits the hopeful resolution from paragraph 3** — that "the regenerate part doth overcome" through "the continual supply of strength from the sanctifying Spirit of Christ," and that "the Saints grow in Grace, perfecting holiness in the fear of God." The 1689 deliberately pairs the problem (para. 2) with the assurance of ultimate victory (para. 3). The BLC presents only the problem side.
- Structural changes: The 1689 describes the war as "irreconcilable" — emphasizing the absolute opposition between flesh and Spirit. The BLC renders this as "desires of their old sinful nature that are contrary to the Spirit," which captures the opposition but loses the "irreconcilable" quality that rules out any compromise or peace treaty with sin.
- The BLC's phrase "through their whole being" nicely modernizes the Confession's "in every part."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q78: "Whence ariseth the imperfection of sanctification in believers? A. The imperfection of sanctification in believers ariseth from the remnants of sin abiding in every part of them, and the perpetual lustings of the flesh against the spirit; whereby they are often foiled with temptations, and fall into many sins, are hindered in all their spiritual services, and their best works are imperfect and defiled in the sight of God." (Rom. 7:18, 23; Mark 14:66-72; Gal. 2:11-12; Heb. 12:1; Isa. 64:6; Exod. 28:38)
- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC is a very close modernization of the WLC answer. Nearly every clause has a direct counterpart.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None in this question (sanctification is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement).
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's "perpetual" lustings is rendered as "continually plagued," which is adequate. The WLC's "in all their spiritual services" becomes "from performing their spiritual obligations" — a slight narrowing from hindrance *in* services to hindrance *from* services, but the verse (Heb. 12:1) supports the BLC's rendering. The WLC's precise "in the sight of God" is preserved as "in God's eyes."

## Detailed Notes
1. **Question framing:** The WLC asks "whence ariseth" (what is the source), while the BLC asks "why" — effectively the same question in modernized form.
2. **"Spiritual services" vs. "spiritual obligations":** The WLC's "hindered in all their spiritual services" suggests believers perform spiritual duties but imperfectly. The BLC's "hindered from performing their spiritual obligations" could be read as suggesting believers are prevented from performing duties at all. This is a subtle but noteworthy shift in emphasis, though not a theological distortion.
3. **Missing eschatological hope:** The most significant observation is that BLC Q71 presents the ongoing struggle without any resolution. The 1689 (XIII.3) and the WLC's following Q79 both provide the hopeful counterbalance. If the BLC addresses this in a subsequent question, this concern is mitigated. The 1689's deliberate pairing of the problem with assured victory is a pastoral and theological feature worth preserving.
4. **Galatians 5:16-18 as proof text:** The BLC adds Gal. 5:16-18 to the final section on imperfect works. This text speaks to the flesh-Spirit warfare more broadly and is better suited to the earlier section on the ongoing conflict. Isaiah 64:6 and Exodus 28:38 (both from the WLC) properly support the "best works are imperfect and defiled" claim.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Romans 7:18-23** — Retained from WLC; properly supports remnants of sin and the internal war.
- **Mark 14:66-72** — Retained from WLC (Peter's denial); properly illustrates believers defeated by temptation.
- **Galatians 2:11-12** — Retained from WLC (Peter's hypocrisy at Antioch); properly illustrates believers falling into sin.
- **Hebrews 12:1** — Retained from WLC; supports hindrance in spiritual duties, though the verse's emphasis on "laying aside" sin and "running with patience" has a more active/hopeful tone than the BLC's use suggests.
- **Isaiah 64:6** — Retained from WLC; properly supports best works being defiled.
- **Exodus 28:38** — Retained from WLC; Aaron bearing the iniquity of holy things — powerfully supports the idea that even holy offerings need Christ's mediation.
- **Galatians 5:16-18** — Added by BLC (not in WLC for this question). This is a valid text on the flesh-Spirit conflict but is slightly misplaced as a proof for "best works are imperfect and defiled." It better supports the earlier clause about desires contrary to the Spirit.
