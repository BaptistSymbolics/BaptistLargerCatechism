# Q103: What is the third commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q103 is a straightforward statement of the third commandment text (Exodus 20:7) in modern English. The Shorter Catechism's parallel question is identically scoped — both simply quote the commandment. The modernization from "take...in vain" to "misuse" is a standard translation update (following NIV phrasing) that preserves the meaning. No doctrinal content is omitted, added, or restructured.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
Q: "Which is the third commandment?"
A: "The third commandment is, Thou shalt not take the name of the Lord thy God in vain; for the Lord will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain (Ex. 20:7)."

- Omissions: None. Both quote the full commandment text with the annexed reason.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC preserves the two-clause structure: prohibition + annexed warning.
- The BLC modernizes the language ("You shall not misuse" for "Thou shalt not take...in vain"; "anyone guiltless who misuses" for "him guiltless that taketh...in vain") but preserves full content.
- Both cite Exodus 20:7 as the sole proof text.

Note: The Shorter also has separate questions on what is required (Q. "What is required in the third commandment?"), what is forbidden (Q. "What is forbidden in the third commandment?"), and the reason annexed (Q. "What is the reason annexed to the third commandment?"). BLC Q103 corresponds only to the statement question, not these expositions; presumably the BLC addresses these in subsequent questions.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 23 (Of Lawful Oaths and Vows), Paragraphs 1-3

The 1689 does not quote the third commandment as a standalone statement but applies it in the context of oaths and vows:

Chapter 23.1: "A lawful Oath is a part of religious worship, wherein the person swearing in Truth, Righteousness, and Judgement, solemnly calleth God to witness what he sweareth; and to judge him according to the Truth or falseness thereof." (citing Ex. 20:7)

Chapter 23.2: "The Name of God only is that by which men ought to swear; and therein it is to be used, with all Holy Fear and reverence, therefore to swear vainly or rashly by that glorious, and dreadful name; or to swear at all by any other thing, is sinful and to be abhorred."

- Omissions: Not applicable — the BLC question merely states the commandment; the 1689 applies it to a specific area (oaths/vows). No conflict exists.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's quotation of the commandment text is consistent with the 1689's citation of the same verse.
- The 1689's application of the third commandment specifically to oaths, vows, and the reverent use of God's name in swearing is consonant with the BLC's broader statement of the commandment.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
WLC Q111: "Which is the third commandment?"
A: "The third commandment is, Thou shalt not take the name of the Lord thy God in vain: for the Lord will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC is a direct modern-English rendering of the identical WLC answer.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is a simple commandment quotation with no ecclesiological or covenantal content).
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None. The full commandment text and annexed warning are preserved.

The WLC also has extensive subsequent questions (Q112-114) on what is required, forbidden, and the reasons annexed. BLC Q103 corresponds only to Q111 (the statement), maintaining perfect structural solidarity at this level.

## Detailed Notes
- The translation choice of "misuse" for the Hebrew *nasa' lashav'* (literally "lift up to emptiness/vanity") is functionally equivalent to "take in vain." The NIV uses "misuse" while the KJV/ESV use "take in vain." Both convey the prohibition against empty, frivolous, false, or irreverent use of God's name.
- The BLC capitalizes "LORD" (representing the divine name YHWH), which is appropriate and consistent with standard Bible translation conventions.
- The BLC uses "His" (capitalized) for the pronoun referring to God, a common evangelical convention.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Exodus 20:7** — The sole proof text is the commandment itself, which is entirely appropriate for a question that simply states the commandment. This matches both the Shorter Catechism and WLC usage.
- No proof texts are missing or added compared to the standards.
