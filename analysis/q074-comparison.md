# Q74: Can true believers be infallibly assured that they will remain in a state of grace?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q74 covers the doctrine of assurance of grace and salvation but introduces two significant problems: (1) it replaces the 1689's specific Christological ground of assurance — "the Blood and Righteousness of Christ revealed in the Gospel" — with the vaguer phrase "the truth of God's promises," thereby weakening the objective, Christ-centered foundation of assurance; and (2) it omits the 1689's qualification "in the right use of means," which guards against passive presumption. Additionally, the BLC drops several qualifying phrases from the 1689 and WLC without theological justification.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** The Shorter Catechism has no dedicated question on the mechanics of assurance. The closest reference is embedded in the answer about the benefits of justification, adoption, and sanctification:

> "Q. What are the benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification? A. The benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification, are assurance of God's love, peace of conscience, joy in the Holy Spirit, increase of grace, and perseverance therein to the end."

- Omissions: The Shorter treats assurance as "assurance of God's love" — a warm, personal, relational phrase. The BLC speaks of assurance of being "in a state of grace" and "persevering to salvation" but does not mention assurance of God's *love*. This is a subtle but meaningful tonal shift from personal relationship to forensic status.
- Structural changes: The Shorter locates assurance as one benefit among several flowing from the application of redemption. The BLC dedicates an entire question to the mechanics of assurance, which is appropriate expansion for a Larger Catechism, following the WLC pattern.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 18, "Of the Assurance of Grace and Salvation"

Chapter 18, Paragraph 1:
> "such as truely believe in the Lord Jesus, and love him in sincerity, endeavouring to walk in all good Conscience before him, may in this life be certainly assured that they are in the state of Grace; and may rejoyce in the hope of the glory of God which hope shall never make them ashamed."

Chapter 18, Paragraph 2:
> "This certainty is not a bare conjectural, and probable perswasion, grounded upon a fallible hope; but an infallible assurance of faith founded on the Blood and Righteousness of Christ revealed in the Gospel; and also upon the inward evidence of those graces of the Spirit unto which promises are made, and on the testimony of the Spirit of adoption, witnessing with our Spirits that we are the children of God; and as a fruit thereof keeping the heart both humble and holy."

Chapter 18, Paragraph 3:
> "This infallible assurance doth not so belong to the essence of faith, but that a true Believer, may wait long and conflict with many difficulties before he be partaker of it; yet being enabled by the Spirit to know the things which are freely given him of God, he may without extraordinary revelation in the right use of means attain thereunto: and therefore it is the duty of every one, to give all diligence to make their Calling and Election sure..."

- **Omissions:**
  1. **"love him in sincerity"** (18.1): The 1689 qualifies true believers as those who "truely believe in the Lord Jesus, and love him in sincerity, endeavouring to walk in all good Conscience before him." The BLC reduces this to "endeavor to walk in good conscience before Him," dropping the love qualification entirely.
  2. **"the Blood and Righteousness of Christ revealed in the Gospel"** (18.2): This is the most significant omission. The 1689 grounds assurance specifically in the objective work of Christ — His blood (atonement) and righteousness (imputation) as revealed in the Gospel. The BLC replaces this with "the truth of God's promises," which is far more generic. The 1689's formulation ties assurance directly to the material content of justification; the BLC's generic phrasing could refer to any divine promise.
  3. **"in the right use of means"** (18.3): The 1689 says assurance may be attained "without extraordinary revelation in the right use of means." The BLC says "without extraordinary revelation" but drops "in the right use of means." This qualification is important because it teaches that assurance ordinarily comes through the appointed means of grace (Word, sacraments, prayer), not passively. Its omission opens the door to a more subjective, feeling-based view of assurance.
  4. **"the inward evidence of those graces of the Spirit unto which promises are made"** (18.2): The 1689 and WLC both speak of discerning graces "unto which promises are made" or "to which the promises of life are made." The BLC renders this as "spiritual insight into their own hearts, to which these promises are directed." The shift from discerning specific *graces* to general "insight into their own hearts" weakens the Reformed emphasis on evidencing specific fruits as signs of grace.
  5. **"keeping the heart both humble and holy"** (18.2): The 1689 names this as a fruit of true assurance, guarding against presumption. The BLC drops this entirely.
  6. **"rejoyce in the hope of the glory of God"** (18.1): The 1689's language of eschatological joy is absent from the BLC.
  7. **"it is the duty of every one, to give all diligence to make their Calling and Election sure"** (18.3): This important exhortation is absent.
- **Structural changes:** The 1689 presents a clear three-fold ground of assurance in an ordered sequence: (a) the Blood and Righteousness of Christ in the Gospel [objective], (b) the inward evidence of graces to which promises are made [reflective], (c) the testimony of the Spirit of adoption [experiential]. The BLC rearranges these into separate sections and, critically, replaces the first (Christological) ground with a weaker formulation, making the structure: (a) faith grounded on truth of God's promises [generic], (b) Spirit giving insight into hearts to which promises are directed [somewhat reflective], (c) Spirit testifying they are children of God [experiential]. The loss of the explicit Christological ground is structurally significant.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q80:

> "Q. 80. Can true believers be infallibly assured that they are in the estate of grace, and that they shall persevere therein unto salvation? A. Such as truly believe in Christ, and endeavour to walk in all good conscience before him, may, without extraordinary revelation, by faith grounded upon the truth of God's promises, and by the Spirit enabling them to discern in themselves those graces to which the promises of life are made, and bearing witness with their spirits that they are the children of God, be infallibly assured that they are in the estate of grace, and shall persevere therein unto salvation."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC is clearly modeled on WLC Q80, following its question structure and much of its answer closely.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent; there are no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues in this question.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. The WLC says "by faith grounded upon the truth of God's promises" as a unified phrase where faith is the instrument grounded on God's promises. The BLC says "faith grounded on the truth of God's promises and from the Spirit, who gives believers spiritual insight into their own hearts." The BLC breaks the WLC's tightly integrated single sentence into separate sections, which weakens the unified structure.
  2. The WLC says "the Spirit enabling them to discern in themselves those graces to which the promises of life are made." The BLC renders this as "the Spirit, who gives believers spiritual insight into their own hearts, to which these promises are directed." The WLC's "discern in themselves those graces" is more precise than "spiritual insight into their own hearts" — the former directs attention to specific evidences of grace, the latter could be read more subjectively.
  3. The WLC's "promises of life" becomes merely "these promises" in the BLC, losing the specific reference to eternal life.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  1. **"truly believe in Christ"**: The WLC specifies faith *in Christ*; the BLC's section 1 says only "True believers who endeavor to walk in good conscience before Him" without specifying the object of belief.
  2. **"those graces to which the promises of life are made"**: Replaced with the less precise "spiritual insight into their own hearts, to which these promises are directed."

## Detailed Notes
1. **The critical Christological omission**: Both the 1689 (18.2) and WLC Q80 ground assurance in Christ — the 1689 explicitly in "the Blood and Righteousness of Christ revealed in the Gospel," and the WLC in "faith grounded upon the truth of God's promises" (with faith understood as faith *in Christ* per the question's opening clause). The BLC, by separating the opening clause ("True believers who endeavor to walk in good conscience") from the ground of assurance ("faith grounded on the truth of God's promises"), loses the WLC's integration where faith-in-Christ is the instrument. The 1689's more explicit Christological language is entirely absent.

2. **"In the right use of means"**: This phrase from 1689 18.3 is doctrinally important. It teaches that assurance is ordinarily obtained through the means of grace — hearing the Word preached, receiving the ordinances, prayer, and fellowship. The BLC's omission of this phrase is particularly notable because the 1689 is the primary standard the BLC is meant to exposit. The WLC also lacks this specific phrase (it is a distinctive emphasis of the Westminster/1689 Confessions rather than the catechisms), but since the BLC claims to exposit the 1689, this omission matters.

3. **Sectional fragmentation**: The WLC achieves theological elegance by integrating all three grounds of assurance into a single flowing sentence, showing their organic unity. The BLC's four-section structure artificially separates these grounds, potentially suggesting they are independent rather than interconnected aspects of one unified assurance.

## Scripture Analysis
- **1 John 2:3; Hebrews 10:19-23** (Section 1): Appropriate for the qualifying condition of walking in good conscience. Hebrews 10:19-23 is a strong addition not in the 1689's proofs for 18.1, connecting assurance to Christ's blood and the new and living way — ironically, the proof text points to the Christological grounding that the BLC's text drops.
- **1 John 5:13; Hebrews 6:19-20; 2 Peter 1:5-11** (Section 2): Good selection. 1 John 5:13 maps to 1689 18.1's proof. Hebrews 6:19-20 connects to the anchor of the soul. 2 Peter 1:5-11 aligns with the 1689's exhortation to "make their Calling and Election sure" — notably the text of this exhortation is absent from the BLC even though the proof text is cited.
- **1 Corinthians 2:12; 1 John 3:14-24; 1 John 4:13-16; Hebrews 6:11-12** (Section 3): Appropriate for the Spirit's role in illuminating the believer's heart. 1 John 4:13-16 is a strong addition emphasizing love as evidence. Hebrews 6:11-12 maps to the 1689's proof for 18.3.
- **Romans 8:15-16** (Section 4): The standard proof text for the Spirit's testimony, matching both the 1689 (18.2) and WLC Q80.
