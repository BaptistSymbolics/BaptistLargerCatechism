# Q4: What is the chief and highest end of man?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
The Larger Catechism's answer — "Man's chief and highest end is to glorify God fully and to enjoy Him completely forever" — faithfully synthesizes theology present throughout both the 1689 Confession and the Shorter Catechism. The Baptist Shorter Catechism lacks a direct parallel question, but the doctrine of God's glory as the end of all things and the believer's full enjoyment of God in eternity are well attested in both standards. The additions of "fully" and "completely" are appropriate expansions that intensify without distorting the theology.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism (Charleston 1813) does not include a "chief end of man" question, unlike the Westminster Shorter Catechism from which it otherwise draws heavily. The closest parallels are distributed across several questions:

- **Decrees of God:** "The decrees of God are his eternal purpose according to the counsel of his will, whereby, **for his own glory**, he hath foreordained whatsoever comes to pass" (Eph. 1:4, 11; Rom. 9:22-23; Is. 46:10)
- **First Commandment:** "The first commandment requireth us to know and acknowledge God to be the only true God and our God, and to **worship and glorify him** accordingly" (Mt. 4:10; Ps. 29:2)
- **Benefits at the Resurrection (Q84):** "At the resurrection believers, being raised up in glory, shall be openly acknowledged, and acquitted in the day of judgment, and **made perfectly blessed, both in soul and body, in the full enjoyment of God to all eternity**" (1 John 3:2; 1 Cor. 13:12; 1 Thess. 4:17-18)

The Larger Catechism helpfully supplies the "chief end" question that the Baptist Shorter Catechism omitted from the Westminster tradition. The two-fold structure — glorifying God and enjoying Him — is consistent with the Shorter Catechism's teaching on God's glory as the purpose of His decrees and the believer's full enjoyment of God at the resurrection. The Larger Catechism's adverbs "fully" and "completely" correspond well to the Shorter's "full enjoyment of God" and the theme of being "perfectly blessed."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter II.1-2 (Of God and of the Holy Trinity), Chapter III.3 (Of God's Decree), Chapter IV.1-2 (Of Creation), Chapter XXXI.1 (Of the State of Man after Death), Chapter XXXII.2 (Of the Last Judgement)

**Glorifying God:** The Confession repeatedly affirms that God's glory is the ultimate end of all things:
- Ch. II.1: God works all things "for his own glory" (Prov. 16:4; Rom. 11:36)
- Ch. II.2: God does not derive glory from creatures but "onely manifesting his own glory in, by, unto, and upon them"; "of whom, through whom, and to whom are all things" (Rom. 11:34-36)
- Ch. III.3: Predestination is "for the manifestation of his glory" and "to the praise of his glorious grace"
- Ch. IV.1: Creation was "for the manifestation of the glory of his eternal power, wisdom, and goodness"

**Enjoying God:** The Confession teaches the believer's enjoyment of God in both the intermediate and final states:
- Ch. IV.2: Man was created with "reasonable and immortal souls, rendring them fit unto that life to God; for which they were Created"
- Ch. XXXI.1: The righteous after death "behold the face of God, in light and glory"
- Ch. XXXII.2: The righteous shall "receive that fulness of Joy, and Glory, with everlasting reward, in the presence of the Lord"

The Larger Catechism's answer is a faithful distillation of these Confession themes. The phrase "enjoy Him completely forever" aligns with the Confession's "fulness of Joy, and Glory" and "everlasting reward, in the presence of the Lord." The word "completely" appropriately captures what the Confession describes as the difference between the intermediate state (where saints "behold the face of God") and the final state (where they receive "fulness" of joy).

## Detailed Notes
1. **The "chief end" question as a valuable addition:** The Baptist Shorter Catechism's omission of a "chief end" question is a notable gap compared to the Westminster tradition. The Larger Catechism rightly fills this gap, providing a foundational question that orients all subsequent catechetical instruction toward God's glory and man's enjoyment of Him.

2. **"Fully" and "completely" as intensifiers:** The Larger Catechism adds "fully" (to glorify God) and "completely" (to enjoy Him) beyond what is found in the Westminster Larger Catechism's "wholly." These are appropriate expansions that emphasize the eschatological consummation — the totality of glorification and enjoyment that will characterize the final state. This corresponds to the Confession's language of "fulness of Joy" (Ch. XXXII.2).

3. **Two-section structure:** The Larger Catechism divides the answer into two sections with distinct proof texts — one for glorifying God and one for enjoying Him. This is pedagogically helpful and theologically sound, reflecting the two inseparable but distinguishable aspects of man's chief end.

4. **No tension with the standards:** The Larger Catechism neither narrows nor contradicts the Confession or Shorter Catechism on this point. It synthesizes a doctrine that both standards teach throughout their contents into a single, memorable formulation.

## Scripture Analysis
**Section 1 — Glorifying God:** Romans 11:36; 1 Corinthians 6:20; 1 Corinthians 10:31; Psalm 86:9, 12
- Romans 11:36 ("For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever") is directly cited in the 1689 Confession Ch. II.1 and II.2 as a proof text for God's glory as the end of all things. Strong and appropriate.
- 1 Corinthians 10:31 ("Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God") is a classic proof text for the duty to glorify God in all of life. Not cited in the Confession or Shorter at this point but wholly consistent.
- 1 Corinthians 6:20 ("For ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body") adds the redemptive motivation for glorifying God. Appropriate.
- Psalm 86:9, 12 ("All nations whom thou hast made shall come and worship before thee, O Lord; and shall glorify thy name... I will glorify thy name for evermore") — strong and fitting.

**Section 2 — Enjoying God:** Psalm 73:24-28; John 17:20-23; Psalm 16:5-11; Revelation 21:3-4; Revelation 19:7-9
- Psalm 73:24-28 ("Thou shalt guide me with thy counsel, and afterward receive me to glory... God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever") is an excellent proof text for the enjoyment of God as man's ultimate end.
- John 17:20-23 (Christ's high-priestly prayer for believers' unity with the Father and Son) connects enjoyment of God to trinitarian communion. Rich and appropriate.
- Psalm 16:5-11 ("In thy presence is fulness of joy; at thy right hand there are pleasures for evermore") is perhaps the single best proof text for the "enjoy Him completely forever" clause.
- Revelation 21:3-4 ("God himself shall be with them, and be their God") and Revelation 19:7-9 (the marriage supper of the Lamb) point to the eschatological consummation of this enjoyment.

The proof texts are well chosen, covering both the duty of glorification and the promise of eternal enjoyment. Several (Rom. 11:36, Ps. 16:11) are classic Reformed proof texts for the chief end doctrine. The inclusion of Revelation 21:3-4 and 19:7-9 for the "enjoy" clause is a thoughtful addition that roots the doctrine in the biblical narrative of consummation.
