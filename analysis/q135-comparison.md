# Q135: What is the tenth commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q135 simply states the text of the tenth commandment from Exodus 20:17 in modernized English. It faithfully mirrors the Shorter Catechism's parallel question, preserves the complete commandment text without omission, and maintains strong solidarity with the WLC. As a question that merely recites Scripture, there is minimal theological room for divergence, and the BLC handles it well.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- BSC: "Which is the tenth commandment?" / "The tenth commandment is Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house, thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's wife, nor his man-servant, nor his maid-servant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor anything that is thy neighbour's." (Ex. 20:17)

- Omissions: None. The BLC preserves the full text of the commandment, including the listing of house, wife, manservant, maidservant, ox, donkey, and "anything that belongs to your neighbor."
- Structural changes: None. The order of items in the commandment is preserved exactly as in the Shorter Catechism and the biblical text.
- The only difference is modernization of language ("You shall not" for "Thou shalt not," "donkey" for "ass," "anything that belongs to your neighbor" for "anything that is thy neighbour's"). These are appropriate and expected updates that do not alter meaning.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God)

The 1689 Confession does not recite individual commandments but addresses the moral law generally:

> "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments" (19.2)

> "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation." (19.5)

- Omissions: N/A — the 1689 does not recite individual commandment texts, so no comparison of commandment wording is possible.
- Structural changes: None.
- The BLC's recitation of the tenth commandment is consistent with the 1689's affirmation of the perpetual binding authority of the moral law as summarized in the Ten Commandments.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- WLC Q146: "Which is the tenth commandment?" / "The tenth commandment is, Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house, thou shall not covet they neighbour's wife, nor his man-servant, nor his maid-servant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour's." (Footnote 910: Exodus 20:17)

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None (this is a simple commandment recitation with no ecclesiological or covenantal content).
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None. The BLC preserves every element of the WLC's answer in modernized form. The question wording shifts from "Which is" to "What is," which is a trivial stylistic update with no theological significance.

## Detailed Notes
- This is a straightforward commandment-recitation question. The BLC faithfully reproduces Exodus 20:17 in modern English.
- The two-clause structure of the commandment is preserved: first the prohibition against coveting a neighbor's house, then the expanded prohibition covering wife, servants, livestock, and all possessions.
- The BLC correctly uses "donkey" instead of "ass," which is a standard modern Bible translation rendering (cf. ESV, NIV, CSB).
- The companion questions BLC Q136 (duties required) and Q137 (sins forbidden) appropriately expand on this commandment text, paralleling WLC Q147-148 and BSC's treatment.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Exodus 20:17** — The sole proof text, and the correct one. This is the standard Reformed citation for the tenth commandment text.
- The BLC's rendering is consistent with modern English translations of this verse and preserves the full enumeration of items forbidden to covet.
