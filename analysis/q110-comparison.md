# Q110: What is forbidden in the fourth commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q110 faithfully modernizes the Shorter Catechism's answer on sins forbidden in the fourth commandment, preserving all key elements in recognizable form. The structure closely mirrors both the Shorter and WLC Q119, with all core categories of Sabbath violation retained. The BLC's modernized language ("loafing" for "idleness," "useless" for "unnecessary/needless") maintains the theological substance without loss.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What is forbidden in the fourth commandment? A. The fourth commandment forbiddeth the omission or careless performance of the duties required (Ez. 22:26; Amos 8:5; Mal. 1:13), and the profaning the day by idleness (Acts 20:7, 9), or doing that which is in itself sinful (Ez. 23:38), or by unnecessary thoughts, words, or works, about worldly employments or recreations (Jer 17:24-27; Is. 58:13)."

- Omissions: None of substance. All four categories of sin are preserved: (1) omission/careless performance, (2) profaning by idleness, (3) doing what is sinful in itself, (4) unnecessary worldly thoughts/words/works.
- Structural changes: The BLC splits the Shorter's compact answer into four sections with individual proof texts, which improves clarity without altering theological relationships. The phrase "profaning the day" is rendered as "treating the day as unholy," which is an accurate modernization.
- The Shorter's "omission" becomes "failing to do anything required by the commandment" — a faithful expansion. "Careless performance" becomes "doing it carelessly, negligently, or in such a way as not to benefit from it" — which adds the WLC's "unprofitable" concept. "Being tired of keeping it" preserves "being weary of them" from the WLC (not in the Shorter, but a legitimate expansion from the Larger tradition).
- The Shorter's "unnecessary" is rendered as "useless" in the BLC. These are close but not identical: "unnecessary" (the Shorter's term) emphasizes that the works need not be done on the Sabbath, while "useless" could imply only futile works are forbidden. However, in context ("useless works, words, and thoughts about our worldly affairs and recreations"), the meaning is clear — these are works that serve no holy purpose on the Sabbath day. This is a minor terminological choice that does not alter the doctrine.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 22, Paragraph 8 — "The Sabbath is then kept holy unto the Lord, when men after a due preparing of their hearts, and ordering their common affairs aforehand, do not only observe an holy rest all the day, from their own works, words, and thoughts, about their worldly employment, and recreations, but also are taken up the whole time in the publick and private exercises of his worship, and in the duties of necessity and mercy."

- Omissions: None. The 1689 states the positive duty (Ch. 22.8); the BLC's Q110 addresses the negative (what is forbidden). The Confession's language about rest "from their own works, words, and thoughts, about their worldly employment, and recreations" is directly reflected in the BLC's final section: "all useless works, words, and thoughts about our worldly affairs and recreations."
- Structural changes: None. The BLC appropriately addresses the forbidden side of what the Confession states positively, which is the standard catechetical approach (the "required" question covers the positive side).
- The 1689's emphasis on "holy rest all the day" from worldly matters is preserved by the BLC's prohibition of worldly works, words, and thoughts. The Confession's pairing of "employment and recreations" maps to the BLC's "worldly affairs and recreations."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q119: "What are the sins forbidden in the fourth commandment? A. The sins forbidden in the fourth commandment are, all omissions of the duties required, all careless, negligent, and unprofitable performing of them, and being weary of them; all profaning the day by idleness, and doing that which is in itself sinful; and by all needless works, words, and thoughts, about our worldly employments and recreations."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**. The BLC follows the WLC's structure and content very closely, modernizing language while preserving all theological categories.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. The fourth commandment is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance. The BLC uses "useless" where the WLC has "needless" and the Shorter has "unnecessary." All three terms function similarly in context.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC's "all omissions of the duties required" is rendered as "failing to do anything required by the commandment" — not dropped, just modernized. The WLC's "unprofitable performing" is captured in "in such a way as not to benefit from it." The WLC's phrase "all profaning the day" is rendered as "treating the day as unholy" — equivalent. No key WLC phrases are dropped without representation.

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's four-section structure maps cleanly to the WLC's four categories of Sabbath violation: (1) omission of duty, (2) careless/wearied performance, (3) profaning by idleness and inherently sinful acts, (4) needless worldly works/words/thoughts. The BLC separates category 3 into its own section ("treating the day as unholy by loafing, by doing anything in itself sinful") and category 4 into another ("all useless works, words, and thoughts about our worldly affairs and recreations"), which is a clean organizational choice.
- "Loafing" as a modernization of "idleness" is colloquial but theologically adequate — both describe wasting the Sabbath in indolence rather than applying it to worship.
- The BLC adds "and being tired of keeping it" to the second section, following the WLC's "being weary of them" — a legitimate expansion beyond the Shorter that the Larger Catechism tradition warrants.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts are drawn from both the Shorter and WLC traditions: Ezekiel 22:26 (Shorter/WLC), Acts 20:7-9 (WLC), Ezekiel 33:30-32 (WLC), Amos 8:5 (Shorter/WLC), Malachi 1:13 (Shorter/WLC), Ezekiel 23:38 (Shorter/WLC), Jeremiah 17:24-27 (Shorter/WLC), Isaiah 58:13-14 (Shorter/WLC with the addition of v.14).
- The BLC expands Isaiah 58:13 to 58:13-14, including the promise attached to Sabbath-keeping — a helpful addition.
- The assignment of proof texts to individual sections is well-matched: Ezekiel 22:26 for omission of duties, the Acts/Ezekiel 33/Amos/Malachi cluster for careless/weary performance, Ezekiel 23:38 for profanation by sin, and Jeremiah/Isaiah for worldly thoughts and works.
