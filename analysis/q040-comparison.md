# Q40: Why was it necessary that the Mediator should be God and man in one person?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q40 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q40, preserving the theological content with only minor stylistic updates. The question addresses the necessity of the hypostatic union for the mediatorial work, affirming that the proper works of each nature must be accepted by God and relied upon by us as the works of the whole person. The proof texts are identical to the WLC. No doctrinal content is omitted or restructured.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The closest is BSC Q21:
> "Q. Who is the Redeemer of God's elect? A. The only Redeemer of God's elect is the Lord Jesus Christ (1 Tim. 2:5, 6); who, being the eternal Son of God, became man (John 1:14; Gal. 4:4), and so was and continueth to be God and man in two distinct natures, and one person for ever (Rom. 9:5; Lk. 1:35; Col. 2:9; Heb. 7:24, 25)."

- Omissions: N/A — the Shorter affirms the *fact* of the two natures in one person but does not have a question addressing *why* this was necessary. BLC Q40 is thus a Larger Catechism expansion, not a Shorter Catechism parallel.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC appropriately goes beyond the Shorter's scope here, which is the purpose of a Larger Catechism.
- The Shorter's affirmation that Christ is "God and man in two distinct natures, and one person for ever" is presupposed by BLC Q40, which asks why this union was necessary.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 8, Paragraph 2:
> "So that two whole, perfect, and distinct natures, were inseparably joined together in one Person: without conversion, composition, or confusion: which Person is very God, and very Man; yet one Christ, the only Mediator between God and Man."

Chapter 8, Paragraph 7:
> "Christ in the work of Mediation acteth according to both natures, by each nature doing that which is proper to it self; yet by reason of the Unity of the Person, that which is proper to one nature, is sometimes in Scripture attributed to the Person denominated by the other nature."

- Omissions: None that rise to concern. The 1689 8.7 elaborates on the *communicatio idiomatum* (the attribution of properties of one nature to the person denominated by the other), which is a more technical statement than the BLC provides. However, the BLC's answer — that the works of each nature are accepted by God and relied on by us "as the works of the whole person" — implicitly teaches the same principle: the unity of the person means the acts of each nature belong to the whole person.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's framing aligns with the 1689's teaching that each nature does "that which is proper to it self" while the unity of the person ensures the works are attributed to the whole Christ.
- The 1689's Chalcedonian language ("without conversion, composition, or confusion") is not repeated here, but that is addressed in the BLC's Christological questions on the person of Christ (the parallel to Q36/37), not in this question about the necessity of the union for mediatorial work.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q40:
> "Q. 40. Why was it requisite that the Mediator should be God and man in one person? A. It was requisite that the Mediator, who was to reconcile God and man, should himself be both God and man, and this in one person, that the proper works of each nature might be accepted of God for us, and relied on by us as the works of the whole person."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This is a Christological question with no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology implications.
- Unexpected divergences: None.
- Key phrases dropped: None of substance. All theological content is preserved:
  - "requisite" → "necessary" (stylistic modernization)
  - "and this in one person" → "in one person" (the BLC integrates this into the clause "should Himself be both God and man in one person" rather than setting it off separately; meaning identical)
  - "for us" → "on our behalf" (stylistic modernization, same meaning)
  - The BLC adds "Himself" for emphasis, which is already present in the WLC ("should himself be")

## Detailed Notes
- BLC Q40 is one of the closest BLC-to-WLC correspondences in the catechism. The answer is essentially a word-for-word modernization.
- The theological logic is preserved exactly: (1) the Mediator must reconcile God and man, (2) therefore He must be both God and man in one person, (3) so that each nature's proper works are accepted by God and (4) relied on by us as works of the whole person.
- The division into two sections in the BLC's TOML format correctly splits at the theological joint: the first section addresses God's acceptance of the works, the second addresses our reliance on them.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Matthew 1:21, 23** — Supports the union of divine and human: "Jesus" (human name, saving work) and "Emmanuel" (God with us, divine nature). Identical to WLC proof text 158.
- **Matthew 3:17** — The Father's acceptance of the Son's works ("in whom I am well pleased"). Supports the claim that the works of each nature are "accepted of God on our behalf." Identical to WLC proof text 158.
- **Hebrews 9:14** — Christ "through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God" — the divine nature giving worth to the human nature's sacrifice. Supports the unity of the person in mediatorial work. Identical to WLC proof text 158.
- **1 Peter 2:6** — "He that believeth on him shall not be confounded" — supports our reliance on Christ as a whole person. Identical to WLC proof text 159.
- All four proof texts are identical to the WLC's references for this question.
