# Q37: How did Christ, being the Son of God, become man?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q37 faithfully reproduces the content of the Shorter Catechism and WLC Q37, preserving all key theological elements: the true body, reasonable soul, virginal conception by the Holy Spirit, birth from Mary "of her substance," and sinlessness. The BLC's sectional formatting breaks the answer into logical clauses but introduces no theological alteration. The phrase "of her substance" — present in the WLC but absent from the Shorter — is rightly included, strengthening the anti-Docetic/anti-Gnostic affirmation.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. How did Christ, being the Son of God become man? A. Christ the Son of God became man by taking to himself a true body (Heb. 2:14, 17; 10:5), and a reasonable soul (Mt. 26:38); being conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit in the womb of the Virgin Mary, and born of her (Luke 1:27, 31, 34, 35, 42; Gal. 4:4), yet without sin (Heb. 4:15; 7:26)."

- Omissions: None. All core elements of the Shorter's answer are preserved: true body, reasonable soul, conception by the Holy Spirit, in the womb of the virgin Mary, born of her, yet without sin.
- Structural changes: The BLC splits the single-sentence answer into three sections, but the theological relationships remain identical. "Taking to himself a true body and a reasonable soul" is presented as the first section, the virginal conception as the second, and sinlessness as the third — this follows the Shorter's own logical flow.
- Additions: The BLC adds "of her substance" (from the WLC), which is a legitimate expansion consistent with the 1689's teaching that Christ took "mans nature, with all the Essential properties." It also adds additional proof texts (John 1:14; Phil. 2:7; Luke 2:40, 52; John 11:33; 2 Cor. 5:21; 1 John 3:5) beyond those in the Shorter, all of which are appropriate.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 8, Paragraph 2 — "The Son of God, the second Person in the Holy Trinity, being very and eternal God, the brightness of the Fathers glory, of one substance and equal with him: who made the World, who upholdeth and governeth all things he hath made: did when the fullness of time was come take unto him mans nature, with all the Essential properties, and common infirmities thereof, yet without sin: being conceived by the Holy Spirit in the Womb of the Virgin Mary, the Holy Spirit coming down upon her, and the power of the most High overshadowing her, and so was made of a Woman, of the Tribe of Judah, of the Seed of Abraham, and David according to the Scriptures: So that two whole, perfect, and distinct natures, were inseparably joined together in one Person: without conversion, composition, or confusion: which Person is very God, and very Man; yet one Christ, the only Mediator between God and Man."

- Omissions: The BLC does not reproduce all of 8.2's content, but this is expected — Q37 addresses only the *manner* of the incarnation, not the full Christological exposition. The Confession's language about "all the Essential properties, and common infirmities thereof," the genealogical details (Tribe of Judah, Seed of Abraham and David), the Chalcedonian formulation ("two whole, perfect, and distinct natures... without conversion, composition, or confusion"), and the identification as "the only Mediator" are handled by other BLC questions in the sequence.
- Structural changes: None that alter meaning. The BLC uses "true body and a reasonable soul" (following the catechetical tradition) rather than the Confession's "mans nature, with all the Essential properties." These are complementary expressions of the same doctrine — the catechism's language specifies the two components (body and soul) while the Confession speaks of the whole nature.
- Agreement: The BLC correctly captures the Confession's teaching on virginal conception, the Holy Spirit's role, Mary's substance contributing to Christ's humanity, and the sinlessness of the incarnate Son.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q37: "How did Christ, being the Son of God, become man? A. Christ the Son of God became man, by taking to himself a true body, and a reasonable soul, being conceived by the power of the Holy Ghost in the womb of the virgin Mary, of her substance, and born of her, yet without sin."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC Q37 is virtually identical to WLC Q37 in content. The question wording is the same, and every theological element of the WLC answer is preserved.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None — this doctrine has no baptistic distinctives.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The BLC faithfully mirrors the WLC's answer.
- Key phrases dropped: None. All key WLC phrases are retained: "true body," "reasonable soul," "power of the Holy Spirit" (modernized from "Holy Ghost"), "womb of the virgin Mary," "of her substance," "born of her," "yet without sin."

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's section-based formatting helpfully isolates three theological affirmations: (1) the reality of the assumed human nature (body and soul), (2) the mode of incarnation (virginal conception), and (3) the sinlessness qualification. This aids catechetical instruction without altering content.
- The phrase "of her substance" is theologically significant: it guards against any suggestion that Christ's body passed through Mary as through a channel (as some Anabaptist groups taught), affirming instead that he received genuine human nature from her. The BLC rightly includes this WLC phrase even though the Shorter omits it.
- The BLC modernizes "Holy Ghost" to "Holy Spirit" — an appropriate and expected update.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts for section 1 (John 1:14; Matt. 26:38; Phil. 2:7; Heb. 2:14-17; Luke 2:40, 52; John 11:33) expand substantially beyond the Shorter's citations (Heb. 2:14, 17; 10:5; Matt. 26:38) and the WLC's (John 1:14; Matt. 26:38). The additions are appropriate: Phil. 2:7 addresses the incarnation itself; Heb. 2:14-17 addresses sharing in flesh and blood; Luke 2:40, 52 evidences the true humanity (growth in wisdom and stature); John 11:33 evidences the reasonable soul (emotional response). Notably, Heb. 10:5 from the Shorter ("a body hast thou prepared me") is not cited, though Heb. 2:14-17 covers similar ground.
- Section 2 proof texts (Luke 1:27, 31, 35; Gal. 4:4) closely match the WLC's citations (Luke 1:27, 31, 35, 42; Gal. 4:4). Luke 1:42 (Elizabeth's blessing) is omitted from the BLC — a minor difference with no theological significance.
- Section 3 proof texts (Heb. 4:15; 7:26; 2 Cor. 5:21; 1 John 3:5) expand the WLC's citations (Heb. 4:15; 7:26) with two additional texts affirming sinlessness. Both additions are strong and appropriate.
