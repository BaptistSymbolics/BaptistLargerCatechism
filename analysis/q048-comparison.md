# Q48: How did Christ humble Himself in His conception and birth?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q48 closely follows WLC Q47 in structure and content, faithfully modernizing its language. The Shorter Catechism's parallel (Q27) covers humiliation broadly rather than specifically addressing conception and birth, but the BLC preserves its key elements. The one notable issue is the BLC's rendering of "divers circumstances of more than ordinary abasement" (WLC) as "conditions that were even worse than ordinary" — a weakening that loses the WLC's emphasis on the multiplicity and variety of humiliating circumstances, and slightly shifts the focus from the nature of the circumstances to a comparative judgment about conditions.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Wherein did Christ's humiliation consist?
> A. Christ's humiliation consisted in his being born, and that in a low condition (Luke 2:7), made under the law (Gal. 4:4), undergoing the miseries of this life (Heb. 12:2, 3; Is. 53:2, 3), the wrath of God (Luke 22:44; Mt. 27:46), and the cursed death of the cross (Phil. 2:8); in being buried (1 Cor. 15:3,4), and continuing under the power of death for a time (Acts 2:24, 25, 26, 27, 31; Mt. 12:40).

- Omissions: The Shorter's phrase "made under the law" is not present in BLC Q48, but this is expected since the BLC distributes the humiliation across multiple questions (Q48 covers conception and birth only; "made under the law" pertains more to Christ's life). The Shorter's "born, and that in a low condition" is preserved in the BLC's "born of a woman of low estate."
- Structural changes: None of concern. The BLC appropriately expands the Shorter's summary treatment into a more detailed question focused specifically on conception and birth, which is the proper function of a larger catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8, Paragraph 2:
> "The Son of God, the second Person in the Holy Trinity, being very and eternal God, the brightness of the Fathers glory, of one substance and equal with him: who made the World, who upholdeth and governeth all things he hath made: did when the fullness of time was come take unto him mans nature, with all the Essential properties, and common infirmities thereof, yet without sin: being conceived by the Holy Spirit in the Womb of the Virgin Mary, the Holy Spirit coming down upon her, and the power of the most High overshadowing her, and so was made of a Woman, of the Tribe of Judah, of the Seed of Abraham, and David according to the Scriptures."

Also Chapter 8, Paragraph 4:
> "This office the Lord Jesus did most willingly undertake, which that he might discharge he was made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it..."

- Omissions: The 1689's emphasis on Christ being "very and eternal God, the brightness of the Fathers glory, of one substance and equal with him" is condensed in the BLC to "from all eternity the Son of God in the bosom of the Father." While shorter, the BLC still captures the eternal divine Sonship. The 1689's mention of conception "by the Holy Spirit in the Womb of the Virgin Mary" is absent from the BLC — though this is covered in BLC Q37 (on the incarnation) rather than here. The 1689's genealogical specificity ("of the Tribe of Judah, of the Seed of Abraham, and David") is not present, though this level of detail is not expected in a catechetical treatment of humiliation.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC's theological structure — eternal Son condescending to become man, born of a woman in lowly circumstances — faithfully reflects the 1689's Christological framework.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q47:
> Q. 47. How did Christ humble himself in his conception and birth?
> A. Christ humbled himself in his conception and birth, in that, being from all eternity the Son of God, in the bosom of the Father, he was pleased in the fulness of time to become the son of man, made of a woman of low estate, and to be born of her; with divers circumstances of more than ordinary abasement.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC is clearly modeled directly on WLC Q47, preserving its structure nearly verbatim in modernized form.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None apparent. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology issues.
- Unexpected divergences: None of substance.
- Key phrases dropped/altered:
  1. **"he was pleased"** (WLC) → **"it pleased Him"** (BLC): Functionally equivalent; the BLC preserves the emphasis on Christ's voluntary condescension.
  2. **"made of a woman"** (WLC) → **"born of a woman"** (BLC): A minor alteration. "Made of a woman" echoes Galatians 4:4 more precisely (γενόμενον ἐκ γυναικός), but "born of a woman" is a reasonable modernization that preserves the essential meaning.
  3. **"and to be born of her"** (WLC) → absent in BLC: The WLC has a two-part structure: "made of a woman of low estate, **and to be born of her**." The BLC collapses this into "born of a woman of low estate," combining the two clauses. This loses the WLC's subtle distinction between the incarnation itself (made of a woman) and the actual birth event (born of her), though the theological content is substantially preserved.
  4. **"with divers circumstances of more than ordinary abasement"** (WLC) → **"and into conditions that were even worse than ordinary"** (BLC): This is the most significant change. The WLC's "divers circumstances" emphasizes the multiplicity and variety of the humiliating conditions (the manger, the swaddling clothes, no room at the inn, etc.). The BLC's "conditions" is more generic. Additionally, "abasement" carries stronger theological weight than "worse than ordinary" — abasement connotes humiliation and degradation specifically, while "worse than ordinary" is a comparative judgment that lacks the same theological precision.

## Detailed Notes
The BLC answer is recognizably a modernization of WLC Q47. The core theological content is intact: Christ's eternal divine Sonship, His voluntary condescension, the fullness of time, His being born of a woman of low estate, and the extraordinary lowliness of His birth circumstances.

The key question for assessment is whether the final clause adequately preserves the WLC's meaning. The WLC's "divers circumstances of more than ordinary abasement" is rich and precise:
- "divers" = multiple, varied
- "circumstances" = the specific details surrounding the birth
- "more than ordinary" = exceeding what was typical even for the low estate
- "abasement" = humiliation, degradation

The BLC's "conditions that were even worse than ordinary" captures the comparative element but loses:
- The multiplicity ("divers")
- The specificity ("circumstances" vs. generic "conditions")
- The theological weight ("abasement" vs. "worse")

This does not rise to a significant concern because the essential point — that Christ's birth circumstances involved extraordinary humiliation beyond what His low-estate mother's situation would normally entail — is preserved. But it represents a weakening of precision.

## Scripture Analysis
**BLC proof texts:** John 1:14,18; Galatians 4:4; Luke 2:7; Philippians 2:6-8; 2 Corinthians 8:9

**WLC proof texts (footnote 187):** John 1:14,18; Galatians 4:4; Luke 2:7

The BLC adds Philippians 2:6-8 and 2 Corinthians 8:9 beyond the WLC's proof texts for Q47. Both are excellent additions:
- Philippians 2:6-8 directly addresses Christ's self-emptying and taking the form of a servant — highly relevant to the humiliation in conception and birth.
- 2 Corinthians 8:9 ("though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor") captures the voluntary condescension from glory to lowliness.

Notably, the WLC cited these same texts under Q46 (the general question on the estate of humiliation), so the BLC is not introducing novel proof texts but rather redistributing them to the more specific question where they also apply. This is a sound editorial choice.

Luke 2:7 supports "born... of low estate and into conditions that were even worse than ordinary" by referencing the manger and the lack of room at the inn. The proof text selection is solid throughout.
