# Q47: What was the estate of Christ's humiliation?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q47 provides a general overview of Christ's humiliation that closely follows WLC Q46 in structure but omits critical content from the Shorter Catechism. The Shorter specifies that Christ was "made under the law," endured "the wrath of God," and suffered "the cursed death of the cross" — all of which the BLC drops entirely. These are not peripheral details but core soteriological elements that the 1689 Confession also affirms. The BLC reduces the humiliation to a bare chronological outline (conception, birth, life, death, burial) without the theological substance that makes Christ's humiliation salvifically significant.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Wherein did Christ's humiliation consist?
> A. Christ's humiliation consisted in his being born, and that in a low condition, made under the law, undergoing the miseries of this life, the wrath of God, and the cursed death of the cross; in being buried, and continuing under the power of death for a time.

- **Omissions:**
  - **"made under the law"** — This is a critical theological qualification. Christ's subjection to the law is central to his active obedience and his role as covenant surety. The Shorter includes it; the BLC drops it entirely.
  - **"undergoing the miseries of this life"** — The Shorter specifies that Christ's life involved suffering the miseries common to fallen existence. The BLC simply says "life" with no theological content.
  - **"the wrath of God"** — This is arguably the most serious omission. The Shorter explicitly names the bearing of divine wrath as a constituent element of Christ's humiliation. This is the heart of penal substitutionary atonement. The BLC drops it completely.
  - **"the cursed death of the cross"** — The Shorter specifies not merely death but the cursed, shameful nature of crucifixion (cf. Gal 3:13, Deut 21:23). The BLC says only "death."
  - **"in being buried"** — The Shorter explicitly mentions burial as part of the humiliation. The BLC says "after His death until His resurrection" but does not name burial.
  - **"low condition"** — The Shorter says Christ was born "in a low condition." The BLC uses "low condition" to describe the overall estate but does not apply it specifically to Christ's birth circumstances.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC restructures the Shorter's theologically loaded description into a bare chronological list. The Shorter weaves together the stages of humiliation with their theological significance (law-keeping, wrath-bearing, cursed death). The BLC strips this to a timeline: conception → birth → life → death → post-death. This transforms a soteriologically rich statement into a biographical outline.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8, Paragraph 4:
> "This office the Lord Jesus did most willingly undertake, which that he might discharge he was made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it, and underwent the punishment due to us, which we should have born and suffered, being made Sin and a Curse for us: enduring most grievous sorrows in his Soul; and most painful sufferings in his body; was crucified, and died, and remained in the state of the dead; yet saw no corruption: on the third day he arose from the dead."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"made under the Law, and did perfectly fulfill it"** — The Confession emphasizes both Christ's subjection to the law and his perfect fulfillment of it (active obedience). The BLC omits both.
  - **"underwent the punishment due to us, which we should have born and suffered"** — The Confession's explicit penal substitutionary language is absent from the BLC.
  - **"being made Sin and a Curse for us"** — This imputation language (2 Cor 5:21, Gal 3:13) is omitted.
  - **"enduring most grievous sorrows in his Soul; and most painful sufferings in his body"** — The Confession specifies both soul-suffering and bodily suffering. The BLC has no such distinction.
  - **"was crucified"** — The specific mode of death is omitted.
  - **"remained in the state of the dead; yet saw no corruption"** — The Confession affirms Christ remained dead but did not see corruption (Acts 13:37). The BLC omits the no-corruption detail.
- **Structural changes:** The 1689 presents the humiliation as an integral part of Christ's mediatorial work, emphasizing willingness, law-fulfillment, penal substitution, and the nature of his sufferings. The BLC presents a detached chronological summary that could describe any human life.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q46:
> Q. 46. What was the estate of Christ's humiliation?
> A. The estate of Christ's humiliation was that low condition, wherein he for our sakes, emptying himself of his glory, took upon him the form of a servant, in his conception and birth, life, death, and after his death, until his resurrection.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC Q47 is nearly verbatim from WLC Q46, with only minor modernization ("emptied Himself of His glory by taking" vs. "emptying himself of his glory, took").
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None applicable at this question.
- **Unexpected divergences:** None from the WLC itself. However, the WLC follows Q46 with four detailed questions (Q47-50) that unpack each stage of humiliation with rich theological content — Christ's eternal Sonship, subjection to the law, bearing God's wrath, the cursed death of the cross, burial, and continuing under the power of death. The BLC appears to compress all of this into a single question without carrying forward the substantive content from the WLC's follow-up questions or from its own primary standards (the Shorter and 1689).
- **Key phrases dropped:** The WLC Q46 itself is faithfully reproduced. The concern is not with Q46 specifically but with whether the BLC covers the content of WLC Q47-50 elsewhere. If the BLC has subsequent questions expanding on each stage of humiliation (as the WLC does), this concern may be mitigated.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The core problem is theological thinning.** BLC Q47 reproduces the WLC's overview question (Q46) almost verbatim, which in the WLC serves as a summary heading before four detailed expansion questions. If the BLC similarly expands on each stage in subsequent questions, the omissions noted here are not omissions at all but simply content yet to come. However, if BLC Q47 stands alone as the only question on Christ's humiliation, then it fails to preserve the substantive content of both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession.

2. **The Shorter Catechism packs soteriological substance into a single answer.** Because the Shorter has only one question on humiliation, it includes the key theological content directly: law, wrath, cursed cross, burial. A Larger Catechism that only gives the overview without the expansion actually says *less* than the Shorter on this doctrine.

3. **The proof texts partially compensate.** The BLC cites Philippians 2:6-8, Galatians 4:4 (made under the law), Isaiah 53:10, 1 John 2:2 (propitiation), Psalm 22:1, and Matthew 27:46 (cry of dereliction). These texts point toward the theological content that the answer text omits. However, proof texts cannot substitute for explicit doctrinal formulation in a catechism answer.

4. **The "for our sakes" language is preserved** — this maintains the substitutionary framework at the overview level, which is good. But the Shorter and 1689 go much further in specifying *what* Christ endured for our sakes.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Philippians 2:6-8** — Appropriate for the kenosis/servant form language. The BLC stops at v.8 which includes "death, even the death of the cross" and "obedient" — yet the answer text does not mention the cross or obedience.
- **Luke 1:31; Luke 2:7** — Appropriate for conception and birth.
- **Galatians 4:4** — This verse says Christ was "made under the law" — a phrase the Shorter includes but the BLC answer text omits despite citing the verse.
- **2 Corinthians 8:9** — Speaks of Christ becoming poor for our sakes. Appropriate.
- **Isaiah 52:3; Hebrews 2:18; Luke 9:58** — Appropriate for the sufferings and lowliness of Christ's life.
- **Psalm 22:1; Matthew 27:46** — The cry of dereliction ("My God, why have you forsaken me?") — points to the bearing of God's wrath, which the answer text does not articulate.
- **Isaiah 53:10** — "It pleased the LORD to crush him" — points to penal substitution, not articulated in the text.
- **1 John 2:2** — "He is the propitiation for our sins" — points to wrath-bearing, not articulated in the text.
- **Matthew 12:40; 1 Corinthians 15:3-4; Acts 2:24-31** — Appropriate for the post-death/pre-resurrection state.

**Pattern:** The proof texts contain the theology that the answer text omits. This is a structural weakness — a catechism answer should teach the doctrine explicitly, with proof texts providing Scriptural warrant for what is taught. Here, the proof texts do theological work that the answer text should be doing.
