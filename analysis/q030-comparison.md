# Q30: What are the punishments for sin in the world to come?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q30 closely follows WLC Q29 with only minor modernizations ("interruption" for "intermission"). However, the BLC uses "include" rather than the WLC's definitive "are," subtly suggesting a non-exhaustive list rather than a complete statement. More notably, the Shorter Catechism's parallel content specifies torment "with the devil and his angels" (from Matt. 25:41), a phrase both the BLC and WLC omit, though the BLC does cite Matt. 25:41 in its proof texts.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
- "Q. What is the misery of that estate whereinto man fell? A. All mankind by their fall lost communion with God, are under his wrath and curse, and so made liable to all miseries in this life, to death itself, and to the pains of hell for ever." (Matt. 25:41, 46; Rom. 6:23)
- "Q. What shall be done to the wicked, at the day of judgment? A. At the day of judgment the bodies of the wicked, being raised out of their graves, shall be sentenced, together with their souls, to unspeakable torments with the devil and his angels for ever." (John 5:28, 29; Matt. 25:41, 46; 2 Thess. 1:8, 9)

- **Omissions:** The Shorter specifies that the wicked are sentenced to torment "with the devil and his angels" — a detail drawn from Matt. 25:41 that the BLC omits from its answer text, though it does cite the verse. The Shorter also says "unspeakable torments" where the BLC has "most grievous torments" (following the WLC).
- **Structural changes:** None significant. The BLC's framing as "punishments of sin in the world to come" properly narrows the Shorter's broader eschatological statements to their eternal component.
- The Shorter's language of being "sentenced, together with their souls" emphasizes the unity of body-soul punishment at the final judgment, which the BLC captures with "torments in soul and body."

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**
- Chapter VI.3: "...the subjects of death and all other miseries, spiritual, temporal and eternal, unless the Lord Jesus set them free."
- Chapter XXXI.1 (Of the State of Man after Death): "...the souls of the wicked, are cast into hell; where they remain in torment and utter darkness, reserved to the judgement of the great day."
- Chapter XXXII.2 (Of the Last Judgement): "The end of Gods appointing this Day, is for the manifestation of the glory of his Mercy, in the Eternal Salvation of the Elect; and of his Justice in the Eternal damnation of the Reprobate, who are wicked and disobedient; for then shall the Righteous go into Everlasting Life, and receive that fulness of Joy, and Glory, with everlasting reward, in the presence of the Lord: but the wicked who know not God, and obey not the Gospel of Jesus Christ, shall be cast into Eternal torments, and punished with everlasting destruction, from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power."

- **Omissions:** The 1689's Ch. 32.2 specifies "punished with everlasting destruction, from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power" — language directly echoing 2 Thess. 1:9. The BLC renders this as "everlasting separation from the comfortable presence of God," which captures the concept but softens the 1689's punitive language of "destruction" and "from the glory of his power." The 1689 also explicitly identifies those punished as "the wicked who know not God, and obey not the Gospel of Jesus Christ," though this is contextually understood in the BLC's catechetical flow. The 1689's Ch. 31.1 mentions "utter darkness" as a feature of hell, which the BLC omits.
- **Structural changes:** The 1689 frames the Last Judgment as manifesting both "the glory of his Mercy" and "his Justice" — a doxological framework absent from the BLC's more descriptive answer.
- The BLC faithfully preserves the core substance: eternal torments, separation from God's presence, soul-and-body punishment, and the everlasting duration.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** "Q. 29. What are the punishments of sin in the world to come? A. The punishments of sin in the world to come, are everlasting separation from the comfortable presence of God, and most grievous torments in soul and body, without intermission, in hell-fire forever."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None (this doctrine has no Baptist-Presbyterian distinctives).
- **Unexpected divergences:** The BLC uses "include" rather than "are," which introduces a subtle non-exhaustiveness absent from the WLC's definitive formulation.
- **Key phrases dropped:** None. The BLC preserves all key WLC phrases with only vocabulary modernizations: "interruption" for "intermission," "hellfire" for "hell-fire."

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Include" vs. "are":** The WLC says the punishments "are" these things — a definitive, exhaustive description. The BLC says they "include" these things, implying additional unstated punishments may exist. While theologically defensible (one could argue there are aspects of eternal punishment beyond human comprehension), this wording represents a departure from the WLC's confident completeness. It is a minor shift but worth noting since the BLC's purpose is to show solidarity with the WLC.

2. **"Without interruption" vs. "without intermission":** This is a faithful modernization. Both convey the same meaning — the torments are unceasing and without pause.

3. **The devil and his angels:** The Shorter's mention of torment "with the devil and his angels" (Matt. 25:41) is theologically significant — it emphasizes the company of the damned and echoes Christ's own words about the destination "prepared for the devil and his angels." The BLC's omission follows the WLC pattern, but given the BLC's stated role of expositing the Shorter, this is a notable gap. However, the BLC does cite Matt. 25:41 as a proof text, which partially mitigates this concern.

4. **"Utter darkness":** The 1689 (Ch. 31.1) includes "utter darkness" alongside "torment" in describing the intermediate state of the wicked. The BLC omits this biblical image (cf. Matt. 25:30), though this is not a doctrinal omission so much as a descriptive one.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts substantially overlap with the WLC and 1689:
- **2 Thessalonians 1:9** — Shared with WLC (footnote 110) and 1689 (Ch. 32.2). Core verse for "everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord."
- **Luke 16:24-26** — Shared with WLC (footnote 110, which cites v. 24). The BLC extends to vv. 25-26, capturing the fixed gulf between Abraham's bosom and Hades.
- **John 3:36** — Not in the WLC proof texts. A BLC addition emphasizing that God's wrath "remains" on the unbeliever. Good supplementary text.
- **Mark 9:43-48** — Shared with WLC (footnote 110, which cites vv. 43-44, 46, 48). Supports "hellfire" and unquenchable nature.
- **Matthew 25:41, 46** — Shared with both Shorter and 1689. V. 41 ("prepared for the devil and his angels") supports the Shorter's language the BLC omits from its answer text but includes here.
- **Revelation 14:11** — Not in WLC proof texts. A BLC addition emphasizing "the smoke of their torment ascends forever and ever, and they have no rest day or night." Strong support for "without interruption."

The BLC's scripture citations are well-chosen and slightly more extensive than the WLC's. The inclusion of Matt. 25:41 in the proof texts partially compensates for the omission of "with the devil and his angels" from the answer text.
