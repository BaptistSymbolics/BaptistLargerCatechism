# Q156: Were not infants baptized when whole households were baptized?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q156 faithfully expands on the household baptism argument that the 1689 Confession's appendix (section 4) treats at length, and which the Shorter Catechism addresses summarily in Q199. The BLC distills the 1689 appendix's detailed reasoning into a catechetical format: that the households described in Acts are said to have heard, believed, and rejoiced — activities infants cannot perform — and therefore the apostles give no command, example, or inference for infant baptism. This is a natural expansion of the Shorter's terse "neither command or example in the holy scriptures, or certain consequence from them" into the specific household-baptism sub-question, and it faithfully preserves the 1689's core reasoning.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. Are the infants of such as are professing believers to be baptized?
> A. The infants of such as are professing believers are not to be baptized, because there is neither command or example in the holy scriptures, or certain consequence from them to baptize such (Ex. 23:13; Pr. 30:6; Lk. 3:7, 8).

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter gives its denial of infant baptism as a single comprehensive statement; Q156 addresses one particular sub-argument (household baptisms) that falls under the Shorter's general categories of "no command or example...or certain consequence." The Shorter's threefold framework (command, example, consequence) is echoed in Q156's concluding clause: "the apostles give no command, example, or inference to baptize the infants of believers."
- Structural changes: None. Q156 functions as a subsidiary question elaborating the "example" and "consequence" categories of the Shorter's answer, which is appropriate for a Larger Catechism format.
- The BLC Q155 already handles the Shorter Q199 directly. Q156 is a follow-up question exploring one of the main paedobaptist counter-arguments. This is a natural catechetical expansion.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 29, Paragraph 2:
> "Those who do actually professe repentance towards God, faith in, and obedience, to our Lord Jesus, are the only proper subjects of this ordinance."

Appendix, Section 4 (on household baptisms):
> "The arguments and inferences that are usually brought for, or against Infant baptism from those few instances which the Scriptures afford us of whole families being baptised; are only conjectural; and therefore cannot of themselves, be conclusive on either hand... Cornelius worshipped God with all his house, the Jaylor, and Crispus the chief ruler of the Synagogue, believed God with each of their houses. The houshold of Stephanus addicted themselves to the Ministry of the Saints: so that thus far Worshipping, and Believing runs parallel with Baptism."

- Omissions: The 1689 appendix concedes that the household arguments are "only conjectural" and "cannot of themselves, be conclusive on either hand" — a notably irenic and measured posture. The BLC takes a somewhat more definitive tone ("it does not appear that there were infants"), though it arrives at the same conclusion. The 1689's acknowledgment that the argument is inconclusive on both sides is softened but not contradicted, since the BLC rightly notes the positive evidence (the whole household believing, rejoicing, etc.) that the 1689 itself marshals. This is a minor tonal difference, not a substantive omission.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC follows the same logical structure as the 1689 appendix: examine the household cases, note that the households did things infants cannot do, and conclude there is no warrant for infant baptism from these texts.
- The BLC's reference to Lydia (Acts 16:14-15), the Philippian jailer (Acts 16:30-34), Crispus (Acts 18:8), Cornelius (Acts 10:2), Stephanas (1 Corinthians 16:15; 1 Corinthians 1:16), and the royal official (John 4:53) closely tracks the cases the 1689 appendix discusses: Cornelius, the Jailer, Crispus, and Stephanas.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q166:
> Q. Unto whom is baptism to be administered?
> A. Baptism is not to be administered to any that are out of the visible church, and so strangers from the covenant of promise, till they profess their faith in Christ, and obedience to him, but infants descending from parents, either both, or but one of them, professing faith in Christ, and obedience to him, are in that respect within the covenant, and to be baptized.

- Solidarity: WEAK (as expected on this topic)
- Intentional Baptist divergences: This is a question with no WLC parallel, as the WLC would never pose this question. The WLC's Q166 positively affirms infant baptism based on covenant membership. The BLC's Q156 specifically argues against a key paedobaptist proof — the household baptism texts — which the WLC never addresses in catechetical form. This divergence is entirely expected and is the core of the Baptist-Presbyterian difference on baptism.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The areas where Baptists and Presbyterians diverge on baptism are fully expected. The BLC does not depart from the WLC in any area where there should be agreement.
- Key phrases dropped: Not applicable. The WLC has no parallel question about household baptisms, so there are no phrases to preserve or drop.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Catechetical expansion is well-judged.** The household baptism argument is one of the most common paedobaptist appeals. The Shorter Catechism's summary denial ("neither command or example...or certain consequence") invites the kind of specific rebuttal that the Larger Catechism format allows. BLC Q155 gives the general denial (paralleling the Shorter), and Q156-157 address the two most common counter-arguments (household baptisms and circumcision).

2. **Logical structure is sound.** The BLC moves from (a) the household cases cited, to (b) the observation that the gospel was spoken to the entire household, to (c) the observation that the whole household believed and rejoiced, to (d) the conclusion that infants could not have done these things. This mirrors the 1689 appendix's reasoning.

3. **Tonal difference from 1689.** The 1689 appendix is notably irenic, acknowledging that household arguments are "only conjectural" on both sides. The BLC is somewhat more assertive ("it does not appear that there were infants"), which is appropriate for a catechism's didactic format rather than the appendix's apologetic format. This is a difference of genre, not of theology.

4. **Concluding formula preserves the Shorter's threefold structure.** The BLC's conclusion — "the apostles give no command, example, or inference to baptize the infants of believers" — closely mirrors the Shorter's "neither command or example...or certain consequence." The substitution of "inference" for "certain consequence" is a natural modernization.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and track the 1689 appendix:
- **Acts 16:14-15** (Lydia's household): The 1689 notes that if Lydia "had been a married person...her husband would also have been named."
- **Acts 16:30-34** (Philippian jailer): The BLC specifically cites v. 32 ("the word of the gospel was spoken to the entire household") and v. 34 ("the whole household rejoiced, believing in God"). The 1689 names "the Jaylor...believed God with...his house."
- **Acts 18:8** (Crispus): The 1689 names "Crispus the chief ruler of the Synagogue, believed God with...his house."
- **Acts 10:2** (Cornelius): The 1689 names "Cornelius worshipped God with all his house."
- **1 Corinthians 16:15** (Stephanas): The 1689 notes "The houshold of Stephanus addicted themselves to the Ministry of the Saints."
- **1 Corinthians 1:16** (Stephanas): Additional reference to Paul's baptism of this household.
- **John 4:53** (Royal official): "He himself believed and his whole household." This text is not explicitly discussed in the 1689 appendix but fits the same pattern — the whole household believed, an activity infants cannot perform. This is an appropriate addition.

The Scripture citations comprehensively cover the major household baptism passages and are well-aligned with the confessional standards.
