# Q115: What is the general scope of the fifth commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
The BLC closely follows the WLC's structure for describing the general scope of the fifth commandment, modernizing the language appropriately. However, it omits "mutually" from the WLC's "mutually owe" (reducing the emphasis on reciprocity), and more significantly, it drops the Shorter Catechism's phrase "preserving the honour" — reducing the fifth commandment's scope from both honour-preservation and duty-performance to duty-performance alone.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is required in the fifth commandment?
> A. The fifth commandment requireth the preserving the honour and performing the duties belonging to every one in their several places and relations, as superiors (Eph. 5:21), inferiors (1 Pet. 2:17), or equals (Rom. 12:10).

- Omissions: The Shorter says "preserving the honour **and** performing the duties" — a twofold requirement. The BLC retains only "the performance of those duties," dropping the distinct concept of honour-preservation. The Shorter's "several places and relations" is compressed to "particular relationship" in the BLC, losing the language of "places" (stations/offices) as distinct from "relations" (personal connections).
- Structural changes: The BLC reorders the categories from the Shorter's "superiors, inferiors, or equals" to "over, under, or equal to them." This inversion is not theologically significant but departs from the traditional ordering without apparent reason.
- The BLC uses the identical three proof texts (Eph. 5:21; 1 Pet. 2:17; Rom. 12:10), maintaining strong continuity.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God), Paragraph 2; Chapter 24 (Of the Civil Magistrate), Paragraph 3

> "The same Law that was first written in the heart of man, continued to be a perfect rule of Righteousness after the fall; & was delivered by God upon Mount Sinai, in Ten Commandments and written in two Tables; the four first containing our duty towards God, and the other six our duty to man." (19.2)

> "Civil Magistrates being set up by God, for the ends aforesaid; subjection in all lawful things commanded by them, ought to be yielded by us, in the Lord; not only for wrath but for Conscience sake." (24.3)

- The 1689 does not treat the fifth commandment individually, so there is no direct omission to flag. The Confession's treatment of the moral law (19.5) affirms that the moral law "doth for ever bind all," which the BLC's exposition of particular commandment duties is consistent with.
- The Confession's chapter on civil magistrates (24.3) elaborates the duty of subjection to superiors in civil matters, citing 1 Pet. 2:17 — the same text the BLC uses. The BLC's general statement is consistent with, and introductory to, the kind of relational duty the Confession elaborates.
- No contradictions or structural changes relative to the 1689.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> WLC Q126. What is the general scope of the fifth commandment?
> A. The general scope of the fifth commandment is, the performance of those duties which we mutually owe in our several relations, as inferiors, superiors, or equals.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC directly follows the WLC's question and structure, using the same question wording ("general scope of the fifth commandment") and substantially the same answer framework.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected at this level of generality; none found.
- Unexpected divergences: The BLC drops "mutually" from "mutually owe." The WLC's "mutually" emphasizes that the duties of the fifth commandment run in both directions — superiors owe duties to inferiors just as inferiors owe duties to superiors. The BLC's "we owe to others" is one-directional in its phrasing, though the inclusion of all three categories (over, under, equal) implicitly preserves some sense of reciprocity.
- Key phrases dropped: "mutually" — a theologically meaningful qualifier that emphasizes the reciprocal nature of fifth-commandment duties. Also "in our several relations" is replaced with "depending upon our particular relationship to them," which shifts from the classic language of "several relations" (multiple distinct social relations/stations) to a more individualistic framing ("our particular relationship").

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Mutually" omission**: The WLC's "mutually" is doing important theological work. The fifth commandment is not merely about inferiors obeying superiors; it encompasses the obligations superiors have toward inferiors (cf. WLC Q129 on the sins of superiors). Dropping "mutually" risks implying a one-directional flow of duty. The BLC's phrasing "duties which we owe to others" partially compensates by including all three categories, but the explicit mutuality is lost.

2. **"Preserving the honour" omission from the Shorter**: The Shorter Catechism distinguishes between preserving honour and performing duties as two aspects of what the fifth commandment requires. The BLC collapses this into duty-performance alone. Since this is the "general scope" question (not "what is required"), some compression is understandable, but as the BLC is supposed to be the *larger* catechism expanding on the Shorter, dropping a concept the Shorter includes is notable.

3. **"Places and relations" vs. "particular relationship"**: The Shorter's "several places and relations" captures the Reformed teaching that God has established distinct stations (places) and connections (relations) in society. The BLC's "particular relationship to them" modernizes this but slightly narrows it — "places" (offices, stations) is a broader concept than "relationship."

4. **Category reordering**: The WLC lists "inferiors, superiors, or equals," and the Shorter lists "superiors, inferiors, or equals." The BLC reorders to "over, under, or equal." Neither the WLC nor Shorter ordering is theologically motivated, so this is cosmetic.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC cites Ephesians 5:21, 1 Peter 2:17, and Romans 12:10 — identical to both the Shorter Catechism and WLC Q126 (footnote 657). This is a strength: the proof texts are perfectly aligned across all three standards.

- **Ephesians 5:21** ("Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God") — notably supports the *mutual* dimension that the BLC's text weakens by dropping "mutually."
- **1 Peter 2:17** ("Honour all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honour the king.") — supports the honour-preservation concept the Shorter includes but the BLC drops.
- **Romans 12:10** ("Be kindly affectioned one to another with brotherly love; in honour preferring one another") — again emphasizes mutuality and honour.

The proof texts actually support the fuller formulations of the WLC and Shorter better than the BLC's trimmed answer, since two of the three texts emphasize mutuality (Eph. 5:21, Rom. 12:10) and one emphasizes honour (1 Pet. 2:17).
