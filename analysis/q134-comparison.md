# Q134: What is forbidden in the ninth commandment?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q134 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q145, expanding the Shorter Catechism's concise summary into a detailed enumeration of sins forbidden by the ninth commandment. The BLC preserves the core theological content -- everything prejudicial to truth or injurious to the good name of ourselves or our neighbors -- while providing the extensive practical application that the WLC format demands.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** "Q. What is forbidden in the ninth commandment? A. The ninth commandment forbiddeth whatsoever is prejudicial to the truth, or injurious to our own or our neighbour's good name (1 Sam. 17:28; Lev. 19:16; Ps. 15:2, 3)."

- Omissions: None. The Shorter gives a broad summary principle; the BLC expands it into specific applications, which is the entire purpose of a Larger Catechism.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC opens with the same framing -- "forbids everything detrimental to the truth and the good reputation of others as well as our own" -- directly mirroring the Shorter's "forbiddeth whatsoever is prejudicial to the truth, or injurious to our own or our neighbour's good name." The remainder is detailed exposition of that principle.
- The BLC faithfully preserves the Shorter's two-part structure: (1) prejudice to truth, and (2) injury to good name, both our own and our neighbor's.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XIX - Of the Law of God, Paragraphs 1-5

The 1689 does not enumerate the individual commandments or their specific applications. It treats the moral law generally, affirming that it "doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof" (XIX.5). The BLC's detailed treatment of the ninth commandment is an exposition that the Confession's framework supports but does not itself provide.

- Omissions: N/A -- the Confession does not address the ninth commandment specifically.
- Structural changes: N/A
- The BLC's exposition is consistent with the Confession's affirmation of the perpetual obligation of the moral law.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q145 - "What are the sins forbidden in the ninth commandment?"

The WLC Q145 answer is an extensive catalogue of sins against truth and good name, including: prejudicing truth, giving false evidence, suborning false witnesses, pleading for evil causes, overbearing truth, passing unjust sentence, calling evil good and good evil, forgery, concealing truth, undue silence, speaking truth unseasonably or maliciously, perverting truth, equivocation, lying, slandering, backbiting, detracting, tale-bearing, whispering, scoffing, reviling, rash/harsh/partial censuring, misconstructing intentions, flattering, vain-glorious boasting, thinking too highly or meanly of ourselves or others, denying God's gifts and graces, aggravating smaller faults, hiding/excusing sins, unnecessary discovering of infirmities, raising false rumors, evil suspicion, envying deserved credit, scornful contempt, fond admiration, breach of lawful promises, and neglecting things of good report.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**. The BLC follows the WLC's structure very closely, covering the same categories in essentially the same order with modernized language.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected or found on this topic. The ninth commandment is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: None significant. The BLC modernizes the language throughout (e.g., "fond admiration" becomes "foolish admiration," "misconstructing" becomes "misconstruing") but preserves the substance.
- Key phrases preserved: The BLC retains the WLC's progression from courtroom sins through speech sins to sins of thought and disposition, concluding with the positive duty to promote good name.
- Key phrases dropped: Minor condensation in some areas (the WLC's "suborning false witnesses" is not explicitly separated from "giving false evidence" in the BLC), but no theological content is lost.

## Detailed Notes
This is one of the longest answers in both the WLC and BLC. The BLC successfully modernizes the archaic language while preserving the comprehensive scope. The structure -- moving from judicial sins, to speech sins, to sins of thought and disposition, to the positive duty -- is faithfully maintained.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC uses an extensive set of proof texts that largely overlaps with the WLC's citations. Some references are updated or supplemented (e.g., the BLC adds specific Proverbs references for various categories). The proof text coverage is thorough and appropriate to the claims made.
