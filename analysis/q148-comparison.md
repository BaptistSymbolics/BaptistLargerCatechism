# Q148: What is required of those who hear the word preached?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q148 closely tracks WLC Q160 in structure and content, faithfully modernizing its language. It preserves the Shorter Catechism's core requirements (diligence, preparation, prayer, faith, love, laying it up, practicing it). However, the BLC expands the Shorter's concise "attend thereunto with diligence" into a more elaborate "pay careful attention" plus a separate "prepare themselves" plus "pray for understanding," and the Shorter's critical phrase "effectual to salvation" — which frames the entire answer in soteriological terms — is absent from the BLC, weakening the salvific urgency of the duties described. Additionally, the proof text for "lovingly" (2 Thessalonians 2:9-10) is problematic, as this passage speaks of those who perish because they "refused to love the truth," functioning more as a warning than a positive proof of receiving the word with love.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. How is the word to be read and heard, that it may become effectual to salvation?
> A. That the word may become effectual to salvation, we must attend thereunto with diligence, preparation, and prayer; receive it with faith and love, lay it up in our hearts, and practice it in our lives.

- **Omissions:**
  - The Shorter's framing "that it may become effectual to salvation" is entirely absent from BLC Q148. This is the most significant omission: the Shorter explicitly ties the duties of hearing to the soteriological purpose of the Word's effectualness. The BLC lists the same duties but without this framing, reducing the answer from "what makes hearing salvifically effective" to merely "what listeners should do."
  - The Shorter's phrase "receive it with faith" is present in the BLC but reworded as "accept the truths in it faithfully" — shifting from the instrument of faith receiving the Word to a more generic notion of faithful acceptance.

- **Structural changes:**
  - The Shorter treats "diligence, preparation, and prayer" as a unified trio of pre-conditions. The BLC splits these into three separate sections with distinct proof texts, which is an expansion rather than a structural alteration.
  - The Shorter's compact "receive it with faith and love" becomes a longer enumeration in the BLC: "faithfully, lovingly, humbly, and with a ready mind" — following the WLC's expanded list. This is appropriate given the BLC's Larger Catechism purpose.

- **Other findings:** The BLC adds "talk about it" (conferring) and "meditate on it," which are absent from the Shorter but present in the WLC. This is appropriate expansion for a Larger Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XXII, Paragraph 5
> "The reading of the Scriptures, Preaching, and hearing the word of God, teaching and admonishing one another in Psalms, Hymns and Spiritual songs, singing with grace in our Hearts to the Lord; as also the Administration of Baptism, and the Lords Supper are all parts of Religious worship of God, to be performed in obedience to him, with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear."

- **Omissions:**
  - The 1689 specifies hearing the word should be "with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear." The BLC captures faith (as "faithfully") and humility (comparable to reverence), but "understanding" and "godly fear" are not explicitly present. The BLC does include "pray for understanding" (Psalm 119:18), which addresses understanding indirectly but shifts it from a manner of hearing to a preparatory activity.

- **Structural changes:** None of theological significance. The 1689 treats hearing the word as one element within a broader catalog of worship activities; the BLC appropriately focuses a dedicated question on the duties of hearers, which is the catechism's proper function.

- **Other findings:** The 1689's treatment is relatively brief on this specific topic, as a confession of faith rather than a catechism. The BLC's expansion is warranted and consistent with the Confession's theology.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q160
> Q. 160. What is required of those that hear the Word preached?
> A. It is required of those that hear the Word preached, that they attend upon it with diligence, preparation, and prayer; examine what they hear by the Scriptures; receive the truth with faith, love, meekness, and readiness of mind, as the Word of God; meditate, and confer of it; hide it in their hearts, and bring forth the fruit of it in their lives.

- **Solidarity: STRONG**
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent. This question deals with hearing the Word, not with sacraments, ecclesiology, or covenant theology. No Baptist-Presbyterian divergence is expected or present.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC's "attend upon it with diligence" becomes "pay careful attention to it" — a reasonable modernization but slightly less robust in connotation (diligence implies sustained effort over time; careful attention could be momentary).
  - The WLC's "confer of it" becomes "talk about it" — again a reasonable modernization, though "confer" has a more deliberate, theological connotation of mutual edification than casual "talking."
- **Key phrases dropped:** No key WLC phrases are dropped. The BLC preserves all major elements: diligence/attention, preparation, prayer, examination by Scripture, receiving with faith/love/meekness/readiness, treating it as the Word of God, meditation, conferring, hiding in hearts, and bringing forth fruit. The structural correspondence is nearly point-for-point.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Effectual to salvation" omission:** The most notable theological difference is with the Shorter Catechism. The Shorter's question explicitly asks how the Word becomes "effectual to salvation," framing all the duties as means toward a salvific end. The BLC's question ("What is required of those who hear the word preached?") and answer lack this soteriological framing entirely. This follows the WLC's pattern (Q160 also lacks this phrase), so it may be a deliberate structural choice to address effectualness elsewhere. However, since the Shorter is a primary standard, the absence of this salvific framing is worth noting.

2. **"Faithfully" vs. "with faith":** The BLC renders "receive the truth with faith" as "accept the truths in it faithfully." This is a subtle but meaningful shift. "With faith" identifies the instrument (faith) by which the truth is received. "Faithfully" is an adverb modifying the manner of acceptance, which could mean merely "reliably" or "consistently." The Shorter and WLC both use the substantive "faith" — a theologically loaded term pointing to saving faith as the instrument of reception. The BLC's adverbial form weakens this precision.

3. **Proof text concern — 2 Thessalonians 2:9-10 for "lovingly":** This passage reads: "The coming of the lawless one is by the activity of Satan with all power and false signs and wonders, and with all wicked deception for those who are perishing, because they refused to love the truth and so be saved." While the passage does connect loving the truth with salvation, it does so negatively (as a warning about those who fail to love it). The WLC's footnote for "love" cites the same passage (2 Thess. 2:10), so the BLC is following the WLC's proof text tradition here, but it remains an unusual choice for a positive duty.

4. **"Review carefully" vs. "examine":** The BLC's "review carefully what they hear through the Bible" modernizes the WLC's "examine what they hear by the Scriptures." Both convey the Berean principle (Acts 17:11). "Review" is slightly weaker than "examine" — review suggests going over material, while examine suggests testing and scrutinizing — but this is a minor stylistic point.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Proverbs 8:32-34; Psalm 84:1-4; Psalm 27:4** for "pay careful attention": Appropriate texts on attentiveness and delight in God's dwelling/instruction.
- **1 Peter 2:1-2; Luke 8:18; James 1:21** for "prepare themselves": Strong choices — putting away sin, being careful how you hear, receiving with meekness.
- **Psalm 119:18; Ephesians 6:18-19** for "pray for understanding": Psalm 119:18 is strong. Ephesians 6:18-19 is about praying for boldness in proclamation, which is more about the preacher than the hearer — a slightly unusual choice here.
- **Acts 17:11** for "review carefully through the Bible": Classic Berean text, perfectly apt.
- **Hebrews 4:2** for "accept the truths in it faithfully": Apt — the word must be "mixed with faith" in hearers.
- **2 Thessalonians 2:9-10** for "lovingly": As noted above, this is a negative/warning text. Follows WLC tradition but is an unusual proof for a positive duty.
- **James 1:21; Psalm 25:9** for "humbly": Strong — meekness in receiving the word, God guiding the humble.
- **Acts 17:11; Acts 2:41** for "with a ready mind": Apt — Berean readiness and the willingness of those at Pentecost.
- **1 Thessalonians 2:13** for "treating it as the word of God": Precisely on point.
- **Hebrews 2:1** for "meditate on it": This text is about "paying much closer attention" lest we drift — more about attentiveness than meditation per se. The WLC cites this same passage for "meditate."
- **Deuteronomy 6:6-7; Luke 24:13-33** for "talk about it": Deuteronomy is about teaching/talking of God's words. Luke 24 (road to Emmaus) is a creative and effective choice for conferring about the Word.
- **Proverbs 2:1-5; Psalm 119:11** for "hide it in their hearts": Classic and apt texts.
- **Luke 8:15; James 1:25** for "bring forth the fruit of it in their lives": Strong choices — the good soil bearing fruit, and the doer of the word being blessed.
