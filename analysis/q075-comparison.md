# Q75: Are all true believers at all times assured of their salvation?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q75 significantly rewrites the doctrine of assurance by replacing the 1689's carefully enumerated causes of diminished assurance with the psychologized phrase "all kinds of psychological problems," omits the 1689's rich description of what believers retain even when assurance is interrupted (the seed of God, life of faith, love of Christ and the brethren, sincerity of heart, conscience of duty), and omits the 1689's teaching that assurance "may in due time be revived" through the Spirit's operation. The final section introduces language ("inner sense of His presence and support") that diverges from both the 1689 and WLC, which speak of being preserved from "utter despair" rather than maintaining a continuous "inner sense" of the Spirit's presence.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Shorter does not have a dedicated question on whether all believers are at all times assured. The closest is:

> Q. What are the benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification?
> A. The benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification, are assurance of God's love, peace of conscience (Rom. 5:1, 2, 5), joy in the Holy Spirit (Rom. 5:5, 17), increase of grace (Pr. 4:18), and perseverance therein to the end (1 John 5:13; 1 Pet. 1:5).

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel to serve as baseline)
- Structural changes: N/A
- The Shorter lists "assurance of God's love" as a benefit but does not address the question of whether it is always enjoyed. BLC Q75 expands into territory covered by the 1689 Confession ch. 18 and WLC Q81.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XVIII, "Of the Assurance of Grace and Salvation," paragraphs 3-4.

Paragraph 3:
> "This infallible assurance doth not so belong to the essence of faith, but that a true Believer, may wait long and conflict with many difficulties before he be partaker of it; yet being enabled by the Spirit to know the things which are freely given him of God, he may without extraordinary revelation in the right use of means attain thereunto: and therefore it is the duty of every one, to give all diligence to make their Calling and Election sure, that thereby his heart may be enlarged in peace and joy in the holy Spirit, in love and thankfulness to God, and in strength and chearfulness in the duties of obedience, the proper fruits of this Assurance; so far is it from inclining men to looseness."

Paragraph 4:
> "True Believers may have the assurance of their Salvation divers ways shaken, diminished, and intermitted; as by negligence in preserving of it, by falling into som special Sin, which woundeth the Conscience, and grieveth the Spirit, by some sudden or vehement temptation, by Gods withdrawing the light of his countenance and suffering even such as fear him to walk in darkness and to have no light; yet are they never destitute of the seed of God, and Life of Faith, that Love of Christ, and the brethren, that sincerity of Heart, and Conscience of duty, out of which by the operation of the Spirit, this Assurance may in due time be revived: and by the which in the mean time they are preserved from utter despair."

- **Omissions (Section 1 of BLC vs. 1689 §3):**
  - Omits "conflict with many difficulties" — the 1689 notes believers not only wait but actively struggle
  - Omits the entire teaching that assurance can be attained "without extraordinary revelation in the right use of means"
  - Omits the duty "to give all diligence to make their Calling and Election sure"
  - Omits the proper fruits of assurance: "peace and joy in the holy Spirit, in love and thankfulness to God, and in strength and chearfulness in the duties of obedience"
  - Omits that assurance is "so far from inclining men to looseness"

- **Omissions (Section 2 of BLC vs. 1689 §4):**
  - The 1689 enumerates four specific causes of diminished assurance: (1) negligence in preserving it, (2) falling into special sin which wounds conscience and grieves the Spirit, (3) sudden or vehement temptation, (4) God's withdrawing the light of his countenance. The BLC replaces all four with "being afflicted by all kinds of psychological problems, sins, temptations, and from losing the sense of God's favor." This collapses the careful distinctions and introduces the modernized phrase "psychological problems" which has no confessional precedent and reframes spiritual conditions in therapeutic categories.
  - The 1689 specifies that sin "woundeth the Conscience, and grieveth the Spirit" — the BLC drops this precise language about sin's effect on conscience and the Holy Spirit.
  - The 1689's language "shaken, diminished, and intermitted" is more precise than the BLC's "weakened and interrupted."

- **Omissions (Section 3 of BLC vs. 1689 §4):**
  - The 1689 describes what believers retain: "the seed of God, and Life of Faith, that Love of Christ, and the brethren, that sincerity of Heart, and Conscience of duty." The BLC omits this entire catalogue.
  - The 1689 teaches assurance "may in due time be revived" through "the operation of the Spirit." The BLC omits the revivability of assurance entirely.
  - The 1689 says believers are "preserved from utter despair." The BLC rephrases this as the Holy Spirit "keeps believers from complete despair by never allowing them to be without an inner sense of His presence and support." The added phrase "inner sense of His presence and support" goes beyond the 1689, which does not promise a continuous subjective sense of the Spirit's presence. The 1689 grounds preservation from despair in the objective realities retained (seed of God, life of faith, love, sincerity, conscience of duty), not in a subjective inner sense.

- **Structural changes:**
  - The 1689 moves from objective causes → objective graces retained → future revival of assurance → present preservation from despair. The BLC collapses this into a simpler: assurance not essential → can be lost → Spirit prevents complete despair. The rich middle section about what believers objectively retain is entirely absent.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q81:
> Q. 81. Are all true believers at all times assured of their present being in the estate of grace, and that they shall be saved?
> A. Assurance of grace and salvation not being of the essence of faith, true believers may wait long before they obtain it; and, after the enjoyment thereof, may have it weakened and intermitted, through manifold distempers, sins, temptations, and desertions; yet they are never left without such a presence and support of the Spirit of God as keeps them from sinking into utter despair.

- **Solidarity:** MODERATE
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected on this topic; assurance doctrine is shared ground between Baptists and Presbyterians.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC uses "manifold distempers" — a broad term covering spiritual, physical, and emotional maladies. The BLC replaces this with "all kinds of psychological problems," which narrows the scope to a modern therapeutic category and loses the older, broader meaning.
  - The WLC includes "desertions" (God withdrawing the sense of his presence) as a distinct cause. The BLC renders this as "losing the sense of God's favor," which is similar but less precise — "desertions" carries stronger theological weight in Reformed theology as a recognized category of divine testing.
  - The WLC says believers are never left "without such a presence and support of the Spirit of God as keeps them from sinking into utter despair." The BLC rephrases as "never allowing them to be without an inner sense of His presence and support." The WLC says the Spirit's presence and support *keeps them from despair* — the function is preservation from despair. The BLC says they are never without an "inner sense" of the Spirit — this shifts from objective divine action to subjective human experience, and could be read as promising a continuous felt awareness of the Spirit, which contradicts the very experiences described in the preceding section (losing the sense of God's favor).
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "present being in the estate of grace, and that they shall be saved" — the WLC distinguishes between assurance of present grace and future salvation. The BLC simply says "their salvation," collapsing this distinction.
  - "sinking into utter despair" — the vivid verb "sinking" is lost.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Psychological problems" is a significant departure.** The 1689 uses "negligence," "special Sin," "vehement temptation," and "God's withdrawing the light of his countenance." The WLC uses "distempers, sins, temptations, and desertions." Both frameworks treat these as spiritual-theological categories. Replacing them with "psychological problems" imports a modern therapeutic framework that redefines spiritual affliction in clinical terms. A believer experiencing God's desertion (Psalm 22:1) is undergoing something fundamentally different from a "psychological problem."

2. **The omission of retained graces is the most serious problem.** The 1689's paragraph 4 is structured around a crucial theological claim: even when assurance is interrupted, believers are "never destitute of the seed of God, and Life of Faith, that Love of Christ, and the brethren, that sincerity of Heart, and Conscience of duty." This is the confessional ground for perseverance through dark seasons. The BLC replaces this entire structure with a single claim about the Spirit's "inner sense," which is both less theologically robust and arguably contradicts the experience described.

3. **The "inner sense" language creates a tension.** If believers can lose "the sense of God's favor" (section 2), how can they simultaneously always have "an inner sense of His presence and support" (section 3)? The 1689 avoids this tension by grounding preservation not in a subjective sense but in objective spiritual realities that persist even when the believer cannot feel them.

4. **Omission of the duty to pursue assurance.** The 1689 §3 teaches it is "the duty of every one, to give all diligence to make their Calling and Election sure." This pastoral exhortation is entirely absent from the BLC, which presents assurance more passively.

5. **Omission of assurance's revival.** The 1689 teaches assurance "may in due time be revived" by "the operation of the Spirit." This hopeful pastoral note — that the dark night does not last forever — is missing from the BLC.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Ephesians 1:13** (BLC section 1): Used by the WLC as proof text 353 for assurance not being of the essence of faith. Appropriate usage, matching WLC.
- **Isaiah 50:10; Psalm 88:1-15** (BLC section 1): Both used by WLC (proof texts 354) for believers waiting long. Appropriate usage, matching WLC.
- **Psalm 77:1-12; Psalm 51:8-12; Psalm 31:22; Psalm 22:1** (BLC section 2): These match WLC proof text 355. Appropriate usage.
- **Ephesians 4:30** (BLC section 2): Not in WLC Q81 proof texts but is relevant to grieving the Spirit. The 1689 ch.18 §4 references falling into sin "which woundeth the Conscience, and grieveth the Spirit," so this is an appropriate addition.
- **Luke 22:31-34** (BLC section 2): Peter's denial — appropriate illustration of sin weakening assurance.
- **1 John 3:9** (BLC section 3): Matches WLC proof text 356 and 1689 §4 reference. Appropriate.
- **Isaiah 54:7-10** (BLC section 3): Matches WLC proof text 356 context. Appropriate.
- **1 Peter 4:12-14** (BLC section 3): Not in WLC Q81 proof texts. Addresses suffering and the Spirit of glory resting on believers — loosely relevant but not directly about preservation from despair.
- Notable proof text omission: **Psalm 73:15, 23** (WLC proof text 356) — "Nevertheless I am continually with thee: thou hast holden me by my right hand" — a key text grounding the objective nature of divine preservation even when subjective assurance is absent.
