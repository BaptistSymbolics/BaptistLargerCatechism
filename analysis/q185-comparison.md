# Q185: What do we pray for in the first petition?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q185 closely tracks WLC Q190 and faithfully expands the Shorter Catechism's compressed answer on the first petition. All major theological elements are preserved: human inability to honor God, prayer for grace to know and glorify Him in all His self-revelation, prayer against atheism and idolatry, and prayer for providential ordering to God's glory. Two minor concerns emerge: (1) the BLC substitutes "spiritual ignorance" for the WLC's bare "ignorance," which narrows the scope; and (2) the BLC uses "invincible providence" where the WLC has "overruling providence"—a defensible but non-standard term that introduces vocabulary not found in any of the standards.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What do we pray for in the first petition?
> A. In the first petition, which is, Hallowed be thy name (Mt. 6:9), we pray that God would enable us and others to glorify him in all that whereby he maketh himself known (Ps. 67:2, 3), and that he would dispose all things to his own glory (Ps. 83 throughout; Rom. 11:36).

- **Omissions:** None. The Shorter has two compressed elements: (1) prayer that God would enable glorification of Himself in all His self-revelation, and (2) prayer that He would dispose all things to His own glory. Both are preserved and expanded in the BLC—the first across sections 3–9, the second in the final section.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC faithfully expands the Shorter's two-part structure into the fuller catechetical treatment expected of a Larger Catechism. The Shorter's "glorify him in all that whereby he maketh himself known" is unpacked into specific categories (titles, attributes, regulations, word, works) and specific modes (thought, word, deed), following the WLC's pattern. The Shorter's "dispose all things to his own glory" is rendered as "by His invincible providence, He would direct and regulate everything to His own glory."
- The Shorter's proof text Rom. 11:36 ("For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever") is not cited in the BLC, though Ps. 83 is retained.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 2, Paragraph 2:
> "God having all life, glory, goodness, blessedness, in and of himself: is alone in, and unto himself all-sufficient, not standing in need of any Creature which he hath made, nor deriving any glory from them, but onely manifesting his own glory in, by, unto, and upon them, he is the alone fountain of all Being, of whom, through whom, and to whom are all things, and he hath most soveraign dominion over all creatures, to do by them, for them, or upon them, whatsoever himself pleaseth."

Chapter 5, Paragraph 1 (Of Divine Providence):
> "God the good Creator of all things, in his infinite power, and wisdom, doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all Creatures, and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence, to the end for the which they were Created; according unto his infallible foreknowledge, and the free and immutable Councel of his own will; to the praise of the glory of his wisdom, power, justice, infinite goodness and mercy."

Chapter 22, Paragraph 3 (Prayer):
> "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance."

- **Omissions:** None of significance. The 1689 does not directly address the content of the Lord's Prayer petitions, but its teaching on God's glory (Ch. 2), providence (Ch. 5), and prayer (Ch. 22) undergird the BLC's answer.
- **Structural changes:** None. The BLC's teaching aligns well with the Confession's framework: God manifests His glory through creation and providence (Ch. 2.2, 5.1), and believers are called to honor Him in prayer (Ch. 22.3). The BLC's opening acknowledgment of human "incapability" and "disinclination" to honor God aligns with the Confession's pervasive teaching on human inability apart from grace.
- The BLC's final section on God directing "everything to His own glory" by "invincible providence" is consistent with 1689 Ch. 5.1's teaching that God "doth uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all Creatures" by His "most wise and holy providence."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q190:
> Q. 190. What do we pray for in the first petition?
> A. In the first petition, (which is, Hallowed by thy name,) acknowledging the utter inability and indisposition that is in ourselves and all men to honor God aright, we pray, that God would by his grace enable and incline us and others to know, to acknowledge, and highly to esteem him, his titles, attributes, ordinances, Word, works, and whatsoever he is pleased to make himself known by; and to glorify him in thought, word, and deed: that he would prevent and remove atheism, ignorance, idolatry, profaneness, and whatsoever is dishonorable to him; and, by his overruling providence, direct and dispose of all things to his own glory.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC follows the WLC's structure almost verbatim, preserving the same three-part progression: (1) acknowledgment of inability/indisposition, (2) prayer for grace to know and glorify God, (3) prayer against specific evils and for providential ordering to God's glory.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None expected or found. This question does not touch on baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - The WLC's "ordinances" is replaced with "regulations" in the BLC. This is a modernization choice—"ordinances" in the WLC context refers broadly to God's appointed institutions and commands, while "regulations" is a reasonable synonym, though it lacks the liturgical/ecclesiastical connotation of "ordinances."
  - The WLC's "ignorance" becomes "spiritual ignorance" in the BLC. This narrows the scope: the WLC prays broadly against all ignorance of God (whether intellectual, moral, or spiritual), while the BLC specifies only "spiritual" ignorance. The WLC's broader term encompasses natural ignorance, willful blindness, and spiritual darkness; the qualifier "spiritual" may inadvertently exclude prayer against mere intellectual or cultural ignorance of God.
  - The WLC's "profaneness" becomes "any kind of desecration" in the BLC. This is a reasonable modernization—"profaneness" and "desecration" cover similar ground, though "desecration" may carry a slightly narrower connotation (violating something sacred) vs. "profaneness" (treating the sacred as common).
  - The WLC's "overruling providence" becomes "invincible providence" in the BLC. While theologically defensible (God's providence cannot be defeated), "invincible" is not a term used in the WLC, 1689, or Shorter Catechism to describe providence. The 1689 uses "wise and holy providence" (Ch. 5.1); the WLC uses "overruling." "Invincible" introduces new vocabulary that, while not contradictory, lacks confessional precedent and shifts the emphasis from God's sovereign governance over all things ("overruling") to the irresistibility of His power ("invincible"). "Overruling" conveys that God directs and disposes all things despite contrary forces; "invincible" focuses on raw power.
  - The WLC's "prevent and remove" becomes "thwart and do away with" in the BLC. This is a straightforward modernization. The WLC's "prevent" carries the older English sense of "preclude/forestall" (not merely the modern sense of "stop from happening"), which "thwart" captures reasonably well.
  - The WLC's "direct and dispose of all things" becomes "direct and regulate everything" in the BLC. The WLC's "dispose" carries the sense of arranging and ordering all things according to God's purpose; "regulate" is close but may carry a more mechanical/legalistic connotation than the WLC intends.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - No major WLC phrases are dropped entirely. All theological elements are present.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Spiritual ignorance" vs. "ignorance"**: This is the most notable narrowing. The WLC's list of evils—atheism, ignorance, idolatry, profaneness—follows a logical progression from total denial (atheism) to mere lack of knowledge (ignorance) to misdirected worship (idolatry) to disrespect for what is known (profaneness). By qualifying ignorance as "spiritual," the BLC subtly implies that only religiously-coded ignorance is in view, potentially excluding prayer against the broader cultural, intellectual, and willful ignorance of God that the WLC encompasses. This is a minor concern—the BLC clearly intends to cover the same ground—but precision is weakened.

2. **"Invincible providence"**: While not contradictory to any standard, this phrase introduces novel terminology. The Reformed confessional tradition consistently describes God's providence as "wise," "holy," "powerful," and "overruling," but not "invincible." The term risks shifting from the nuanced Reformed concept of providence (God wisely ordering all things, including sin, to His own glory) toward a more blunt affirmation of irresistible power. The WLC's "overruling" better captures the idea that God's providence operates in, through, and even despite second causes.

3. **"Completely incapable" and "even disinclined"**: The BLC's rendering of the WLC's "utter inability and indisposition" is an excellent modernization. "Completely incapable" captures "utter inability" and "even disinclined" captures "indisposition." The word "even" is a helpful addition that emphasizes the depth of human fallenness—not merely unable but also unwilling.

4. **"Regulations" for "ordinances"**: This is a defensible modernization but involves some semantic loss. "Ordinances" in Reformed usage covers God's appointed means of grace and worship (preaching, sacraments, prayer) as well as His moral commands. "Regulations" more naturally suggests rules and commands, potentially losing the worship-centered connotation.

5. **Section structure**: The BLC's sixteen TOML sections effectively mirror the WLC's single-paragraph answer, breaking it into catechetically manageable pieces. The structural fidelity is commendable.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts closely track the WLC's references. The major proof texts are preserved: 2 Cor. 3:5 and Ps. 51:15 for human inability; Ps. 67:2-3 for knowing God; Ps. 83:18 for titles; Ps. 86:10-15 for attributes; Ps. 67:1-4 and Ps. 79:10 for opposing atheism; Eph. 1:17-18 for opposing ignorance; Ps. 97:7 for opposing idolatry.
- The BLC adds several proof texts not in the WLC: Ps. 72:19, Eph. 3:20-21, Ps. 145:6-8, 2 Thess. 3:1, Ps. 107:32, Ps. 147:19-20, Ps. 138:1-3, 2 Cor. 2:14-15, Ps. 145, Ps. 8, Ps. 103:1, Phil. 1:9-11. These additions generally strengthen the proof text support without introducing problematic references.
- For "invincible providence," the BLC cites 2 Chron. 20:6, 10-12; Ps. 83; Ps. 140:4, 8; Isa. 64:1-2. These texts emphasize God's sovereign power and human appeal to His might, which fits "invincible" but would equally support "overruling." The WLC's proof texts for this clause are not directly referenced.
- The shift from "profaneness" to "desecration" is supported by Ps. 74:18, 22-23, which speaks of the enemy reproaching God's name—a reasonable reference for desecration.
