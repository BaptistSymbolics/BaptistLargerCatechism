# Q85: In the beginning what did God reveal unto man as the rule of His obedience?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q85 closely tracks the WLC Q92 and Baptist Shorter Catechism, correctly identifying the moral law as the rule of obedience revealed to Adam in innocence, with the special command regarding the tree as an additional precept. However, the BLC's question wording subtly shifts from "at first" to "in the beginning," which is a minor stylistic change, and the answer adds "from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil" (specifying "from the tree" rather than "of the fruit of the tree"), which drops "the fruit of" — a minor precision loss relative to the WLC though not theologically significant. The more notable observation is that the BLC adds the proof text Genesis 1:26-27 (image of God) which the Shorter does not cite, drawing a connection to the 1689 Confession 4.2's teaching that man was made in God's image with the law written on his heart.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What did God at first reveal to man for the rule of his obedience?
> A. The rule which God at first revealed to man for his obedience, was the moral law (Rom. 2:14,15, and 10:5).

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter's answer is very concise and the BLC actually expands upon it with the parenthetical about the special command (the tree of knowledge), which mirrors the WLC's fuller treatment rather than the Shorter's brevity.
- Structural changes: The BLC restructures the question slightly — "In the beginning what did God reveal unto man as the rule of His obedience?" vs. the Shorter's "What did God at first reveal to man for the rule of his obedience?" The change from "at first" to "In the beginning" is stylistic. The BLC answer adds the clause about "Adam in the estate of innocence, and to all mankind in him" and the parenthetical about the tree — all drawn from the WLC, not the Shorter.
- The BLC adds proof texts Genesis 1:26-27 and Genesis 2:17 beyond the Shorter's Romans 2:14-15 and Romans 10:5. This is appropriate expansion.
- The Shorter's core theological content — that the moral law was the rule first revealed — is fully preserved.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 4, Paragraph 2:
> "After God had made all other Creatures, he Created man, male and female, with reasonable and immortal souls, rendring them fit unto that life to God; for which they were Created; being made after the image of God, in knowledge, righteousness, and true holyness; having the Law of God written in their hearts, and power to fulfill it; and yet under a possibility of transgressing, being left to the liberty of their own will, which was subject to change."

Chapter 4, Paragraph 3:
> "Besides the Law written in their hearts, they received a command not to eat of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; which whilst they kept, they were happy in their Communion with God, and had dominion over the Creatures."

Chapter 19, Paragraph 1:
> "God gave to Adam a Law of universal obedience, written in his Heart, and a particular precept of not eating the Fruit of the tree of knowledge of good and evil; by which he bound him, and all his posterity to personal entire exact and perpetual obedience; promised life upon the fulfilling, and threatned death upon the breach of it; and indued him with power and ability to keep it."

- Omissions: The 1689 19.1 specifies that the law bound Adam and his posterity to "personal entire exact and perpetual obedience," "promised life upon the fulfilling, and threatened death upon the breach of it," and that God "endued him with power and ability to keep it." The BLC does not include these details, but this is expected — the BLC is answering a narrower question about *what* the rule was, not its binding force, sanctions, or enabling power. These elements belong to subsequent questions about the nature of the moral law and the covenant of works.
- Structural changes: None. The BLC correctly reflects the 1689's two-part structure: (1) the moral law written in the heart, and (2) the special command about the tree.
- The 1689's language about "Law of universal obedience, written in his Heart" is captured by the BLC's identification of the moral law plus Genesis 1:26-27 and Romans 2:14-15 proof texts.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. 92. What did God at first reveal unto man as the rule of his obedience?
> A. The rule of obedience revealed to Adam in the estate of innocence, and to all mankind in him, besides a special command not to eat of the fruit of the tree knowledge of good and evil, was the moral law.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC answer is nearly verbatim from the WLC Q92. The BLC question is a close paraphrase ("In the beginning" for "at first").
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None in this question. No baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology differences are at play here.
- Unexpected divergences: The BLC says "from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil" where the WLC says "of the fruit of the tree knowledge of good and evil." The BLC drops "the fruit of" and adds "from." This is a minor wording change — the WLC's "fruit of the tree" is more precisely biblical (Genesis 2:17 refers to eating "of the fruit of the tree" in KJV, though later translations vary). The BLC also adds "from" which is a modernization.
- Key phrases dropped: "of the fruit of" — minor; the theological point (the special prohibition) is preserved.

## Detailed Notes
1. The BLC's question wording change from "at first" to "In the beginning" is a defensible modernization but loses a shade of meaning. "At first" emphasizes temporal priority and initial revelation; "In the beginning" could be read as referencing Genesis 1:1 and creation itself rather than the first act of revelation to man specifically.
2. The BLC's proof texts are a thoughtful expansion: Genesis 1:26-27 connects the moral law to the image of God (aligning with 1689 4.2), Genesis 2:17 grounds the special command, Romans 2:14-15 supports the law written on the heart, and Romans 10:5 supports the moral law as the rule of obedience.
3. The answer's structure — identifying the moral law as the primary rule, with the tree-prohibition as an additional "special command" — correctly reflects the Reformed distinction between natural/moral law and the positive command, as taught in all the standards.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Genesis 1:26-27** (BLC addition, not in Shorter or WLC): Appropriately added; connects to the 1689 4.2 teaching that man was made in God's image with the law written on his heart.
- **Romans 2:14-15** (shared with Shorter and WLC): The classic proof that the moral law is written on the heart of man by nature — directly supports the answer.
- **Romans 10:5** (shared with Shorter and WLC): "The man which doeth those things shall live by them" — supports the moral law as the rule of obedience with the promise of life.
- **Genesis 2:17** (BLC addition): Directly grounds the "special command not to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil" — a fitting addition that the Shorter and WLC proof texts surprisingly lack explicitly.
