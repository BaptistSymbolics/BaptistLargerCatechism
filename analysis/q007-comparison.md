# Q7: How may we know that the Scriptures are the Word of God?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q7 closely follows WLC Q4 in both structure and substance, and faithfully reflects 1689 Confession 1.5. The Shorter Catechism has no direct parallel question, so the BLC is appropriately expanding the catechetical framework here. The concern is minor: the BLC omits the 1689's and WLC's reference to the Scriptures' "light" (alongside "power"), and the 1689's phrase about "the full discovery it makes of the only way of man's salvation" — a soteriological argument for Scripture's self-authentication that is theologically significant. The BLC also separates the WLC's combined "majesty and purity" into "majesty of its style" and "purity of its doctrines," which is a reasonable interpretive expansion but introduces a stylistic/doctrinal distinction not present in the standards.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

No direct parallel. The Shorter Catechism does not have a question on how we may know the Scriptures are the Word of God. The closest related questions are:

"Q. What is the word of God? A. The holy scriptures of the Old and New Testament are the word of God, and the only certain rule of faith and obedience (2 Tim. 3:16; Eph. 2:20)."

"Q. How is the word made effectual to salvation? A. The Spirit of God maketh the reading, but especially the preaching of the word, an effectual means of convincing and converting sinners, and of building them up in holiness and comfort through faith unto salvation (Neh. 8:8; Acts 26:18; Ps. 19:8; Acts 20:32; Rom. 1:15,16, 10:13,14,15,16,17; 15:4; 1 Cor. 14:24,25; 1 Tim. 3:15,16,17)."

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel question to compare)
- Structural changes: N/A
- The BLC's Q7 appropriately fills a gap in the Shorter Catechism's coverage by providing a distinct question on Scripture's self-authentication, a topic the Shorter does not address directly but which is treated at length in the 1689 Confession (1.5).

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 1, Paragraph 4 and Paragraph 5

Paragraph 4: "The Authority of the Holy Scripture for which it ought to be believed dependeth not upon the testimony of any man, or Church; but wholly upon God (who is truth it self) the Author thereof; therefore it is to be received, because it is the Word of God."

Paragraph 5: "We may be moved and induced by the testimony of the Church of God, to an high and reverent esteem of the Holy Scriptures; and the heavenliness of the matter, the efficacy of the Doctrine, and the Majesty of the stile, the consent of all the parts, the scope of the whole (which is to give all glory to God) the full discovery it makes of the only way of mans salvation, and many other incomparable Excellencies, and intire perfections thereof, are arguments whereby it doth abundantly evidence it self to be the Word of God; yet notwithstanding; our full perswasion, and assurance of the infallible truth, and divine authority thereof, is from the inward work of the Holy Spirit, bearing witness by and with the Word in our Hearts."

**Key comparison findings:**

1. **Testimony of the Church omitted:** The 1689 begins paragraph 5 with the testimony of the Church as a legitimate (though not ultimate) inducement to esteem Scripture. The BLC omits this entirely. This is arguably acceptable since it is not itself an argument from Scripture's own self-evidencing character, and the question asks how the Scriptures "manifest themselves." However, the Confession's inclusion of this ecclesial point provides a fuller epistemological picture.

2. **"Heavenliness of the matter" and "efficacy of the Doctrine" omitted:** The 1689 lists "the heavenliness of the matter, the efficacy of the Doctrine, and the Majesty of the stile." The BLC renders this as "the majesty of its style and purity of its doctrines." The Confession's "heavenliness of the matter" (the sublime, transcendent character of Scripture's content) and "efficacy of the Doctrine" (the operational power of its teaching) are distinct theological concepts. The BLC's "purity of its doctrines" captures something of these but is not a precise equivalent — "purity" is narrower than "heavenliness" and "efficacy" combined.

3. **"The full discovery it makes of the only way of man's salvation" omitted:** This is the most notable omission. The 1689 lists this as a distinct argument for Scripture's self-authentication — that Scripture uniquely reveals the way of salvation. The BLC does not include this soteriological argument. This is theologically significant because it connects the doctrine of Scripture to soteriology in a way that strengthens the self-evidencing case.

4. **"Many other incomparable Excellencies, and intire perfections thereof" omitted:** The 1689's open-ended acknowledgment that the listed arguments are not exhaustive is absent from the BLC. This is a minor point but reflects the Confession's humility about the fullness of Scripture's self-evidencing character.

5. **"Light and power" reduced to "power" alone:** The WLC (and implicitly the Confession's "efficacy") includes both "light" and "power" in describing Scripture's effect on sinners. The BLC speaks only of Scripture's "power to convert sinners and to edify saints," omitting "light." Light and power are not identical — light illumines the mind while power transforms the will and heart. The omission of "light" narrows the description.

6. **"Comfort" omitted from believers' experience:** The WLC says Scripture has power "to convince and convert sinners, to comfort and build up believers unto salvation." The BLC says "to convert sinners and to edify saints unto salvation." The BLC drops "comfort," which in the WLC/Confession tradition carries specific pastoral significance as a distinct ministry of Scripture alongside edification.

7. **Final clause on the Spirit closely follows the standards:** The BLC's concluding section — "However, the Spirit of God alone, bearing witness by and with the Scriptures in the heart of man, is able fully to persuade it that they are the very Word of God" — closely mirrors both 1689 1.5 ("our full perswasion, and assurance of the infallible truth, and divine authority thereof, is from the inward work of the Holy Spirit, bearing witness by and with the Word in our Hearts") and the WLC. The BLC uses "alone" where the Confession says "notwithstanding" and the WLC says "alone," faithfully conveying the exclusive sufficiency of the Spirit's internal testimony.

8. **"Full persuasion and assurance of the infallible truth and divine authority" simplified:** The Confession's rich phrase about "full perswasion, and assurance of the infallible truth, and divine authority thereof" is reduced in the BLC to "fully to persuade it that they are the very Word of God." The concepts of "infallible truth" and "divine authority" as distinct objects of persuasion are collapsed. This is a compression rather than a contradiction, but the Confession's precision is notable.

- Omissions: (a) testimony of the Church, (b) heavenliness of the matter, (c) efficacy of the Doctrine, (d) the full discovery of the only way of man's salvation, (e) "many other incomparable Excellencies, and intire perfections," (f) "light" alongside power, (g) "comfort" for believers, (h) "infallible truth and divine authority" as distinct objects of assurance
- Structural changes: The BLC's "purity of its doctrines" replaces the Confession's "heavenliness of the matter" and "efficacy of the Doctrine" — a substitution that changes the character of the argument. The Confession's "convert sinners" and "build up believers" dyad becomes "convert sinners" and "edify saints" — a close parallel.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q4: "How doth it appear that the Scriptures are of the Word of God? A. The Scriptures manifest themselves to be the Word of God, by their majesty and purity; by the consent of all the parts, and the scope of the whole, which is to give all glory to God; by their light and power to convince and convert sinners, to comfort and build up believers unto salvation: but the Spirit of God bearing witness by and with the Scriptures in the heart of man, is alone able fully to persuade it that they are the very word of God."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- The BLC follows the WLC's structure and language very closely. The question is a clear modernization of WLC Q4.

- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected or found. This is a doctrine of Scripture question, not touching baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology.

- Unexpected divergences:
  1. **"Majesty and purity" expanded to "majesty of its style and purity of its doctrines":** The WLC's compact "majesty and purity" is interpreted by the BLC as referring to style and doctrine respectively. This is a reasonable gloss, but it introduces specificity the WLC left open — "majesty" could refer to content as well as style, and "purity" could encompass more than doctrine alone. The Confession's fuller list ("heavenliness of the matter, the efficacy of the Doctrine, and the Majesty of the stile") shows that these were understood as at least three distinct attributes, not two.
  2. **"Light and power" reduced to "power" alone:** The WLC says "by their light and power to convince and convert sinners." The BLC drops "light" and retains only "power."
  3. **"Convince and convert" reduced to "convert" alone:** The WLC's "convince and convert" includes the illuminating work of conviction (convincing of sin and truth) as distinct from conversion. The BLC retains only "convert."
  4. **"Comfort and build up believers" becomes "edify saints":** The WLC's "comfort" is a distinct pastoral category (consolation of troubled consciences) alongside "building up" (progressive sanctification and growth). The BLC's "edify saints" subsumes both under one term.

- Key phrases dropped:
  - "light" (Scripture's illuminating character)
  - "convince" (conviction as distinct from conversion)
  - "comfort" (consolation as distinct from edification)

## Detailed Notes

1. **The BLC adds "However" as a transitional word** before the final clause about the Spirit, where both the WLC uses "but" and the 1689 uses "yet notwithstanding." "However" carries essentially the same contrastive force. This is a stylistic modernization with no theological import.

2. **Number disagreement preserved from WLC:** Both the WLC and BLC use a grammatically interesting construction: "The Scriptures manifest themselves" (plural) followed by "the Word of God" (singular). The BLC's first section then switches to singular pronouns: "the majesty of *its* style and purity of *its* doctrines." The WLC uses "their majesty and purity." The BLC's shift from "Scriptures" (plural) to "its" (singular) is slightly awkward but reflects the theological unity of Scripture as a single Word.

3. **The question wording differs from the WLC:** The WLC asks "How doth it appear that the Scriptures are *of* the Word of God?" (passive, third-person). The BLC asks "How *may we know* that the Scriptures are the Word of God?" (first-person, active, experiential). This is a subtle but meaningful shift toward an experiential-epistemological framing that foregrounds the believer's knowledge rather than Scripture's objective self-presentation. Both are valid, but the WLC's framing keeps the focus on Scripture as subject ("The Scriptures manifest themselves"), while the BLC's question ("How may we know") puts the knower in the foreground.

4. **The cumulative effect of omissions:** While each individual omission ("light," "convince," "comfort," "the full discovery of the only way of man's salvation") is small, their combined effect is to flatten the richness of the standards' description. The 1689's paragraph 5 presents a multi-layered, cumulative case with at least seven distinct arguments; the BLC presents four. The WLC presents five distinct descriptors (majesty, purity, consent of parts, scope, light and power with fourfold effect); the BLC presents four descriptors with a twofold effect.

## Scripture Analysis

- **Psalm 12:6; Psalm 19:7-8; Psalm 119:18, 129, 140; 1 Corinthians 2:6-7** — Supporting "majesty of its style and purity of its doctrines." Good selections. Psalm 12:6 (pure words), Psalm 119:140 (very pure word), 1 Corinthians 2:6-7 (wisdom of God in a mystery) support both majesty and purity well.
- **Luke 24:25-27; Acts 10:43; Acts 26:22-23** — Supporting "consent of all the parts and the scope of the whole." Excellent choices showing Christ as the unifying scope of Scripture.
- **Romans 16:25-27; John 16:13-14** — Supporting "to give all glory to God." Appropriate. Romans 16:25-27 is a doxology; John 16:13-14 focuses on the Spirit glorifying Christ.
- **Acts 18:24-28; Hebrews 4:12-13; James 1:18; Psalm 19:7-9; Romans 15:4; Acts 20:32** — Supporting "power to convert sinners and to edify saints." Strong selection. Hebrews 4:12-13 (living and active word) is particularly apt. Romans 15:4 (comfort of the Scriptures) ironically supports the very "comfort" concept the BLC omitted from the text.
- **John 16:13-14; 1 John 2:20, 27; John 20:31** — Supporting the Spirit's testimony. Good selections. John 20:31 (written that you may believe) is an interesting addition not found in the WLC's or 1689's proof texts for this clause.

**Notable observation:** Romans 15:4 is cited as a proof text for the fourth section ("power to convert sinners and to edify saints unto salvation"), yet Romans 15:4 specifically says "that through patience and comfort of the scriptures we might have hope" — emphasizing precisely the "comfort" that the BLC text itself omits. The proof text witnesses against the omission.
