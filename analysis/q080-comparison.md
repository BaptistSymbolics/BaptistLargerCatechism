# Q80: What must we believe about the resurrection?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q80 is a faithful modernization of WLC Q87 and closely follows the 1689 Confession Chapter XXXI, paragraphs 2-3. It preserves all the major doctrinal elements: the general resurrection, the change of the living, the reuniting of the selfsame bodies with souls, Christ's power in raising the dead, the glorification of the righteous, and the dishonor of the wicked. The minor concern relates to the omission of the 1689's explicit phrase "and none other" regarding the selfsame bodies, and the addition of "who offend Him" in the final section which subtly shifts the reason for the wicked's dishonor from Christ's judicial office to the wicked's offense.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** The Shorter Catechism does not have a single question paralleling BLC Q80. The relevant content is distributed across three questions:

> **Q38.** What benefits do believers receive from Christ at their death?
> **A.** The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness (Heb. 12:23), and do immediately pass into glory (2 Cor. 5:1, 6, 8; Phil. 1:23; Luke 23:43); and their bodies being still united to Christ (1 Thess. 4:14), do rest in their graves (Is. 57:2) till the resurrection (Job 19:26, 27).

> **Q39.** What benefits do believers receive from Christ at the resurrection?
> **A.** At the resurrection believers, being raised up in glory (1 Cor. 15:43), shall be openly acknowledged, and acquitted in the day of judgment (Mt. 25:23; Mt. 10:32), and made perfectly blessed, both in soul and body, in the full enjoyment of God (1 John 3:2; 1 Cor. 13:12) to all eternity (1 Thess. 4:17, 18).

> **Q41.** What shall be done to the wicked, at the day of judgment?
> **A.** At the day of judgment the bodies of the wicked, being raised out of their graves, shall be sentenced, together with their souls, to unspeakable torments with the devil and his angels for ever (John 5:28, 29; Mt. 25:41, 46; 2 Thes. 1:8, 9).

- Omissions: The Shorter's Q39 includes the believers being "openly acknowledged, and acquitted in the day of judgment" and "made perfectly blessed, both in soul and body, in the full enjoyment of God to all eternity." The BLC Q80 omits these post-resurrection blessings entirely, focusing only on the nature of the resurrection body. However, this is likely because the BLC, like the WLC, treats the resurrection and the last judgment as separate questions, so these elements would belong in a subsequent question on judgment.
- Structural changes: The Shorter treats the resurrection as one component of believers' benefits (Q39) and the wicked's condemnation (Q41), while the BLC follows the WLC pattern of a standalone doctrinal question about the resurrection event itself. This is an appropriate structural choice for a larger catechism format.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XXXI, Paragraphs 2-3:

> **Paragraph 2:** "At the last day such of the Saints as are found alive shall not sleep but be changed; and all the dead shall be raised up with the self same bodies, and none other; although with different qualities, which shall be united again to their Souls for ever."

> **Paragraph 3:** "The bodies of the unjust shall by the power of Christ, be raised to dishonour; the bodies of the just by his spirit unto honour, and be made conformable to his own glorious Body."

- Omissions:
  - **"and none other"**: The 1689 explicitly emphasizes that the resurrection bodies are "the self same bodies, and none other." The BLC says "the very same bodies of the dead that were buried" which captures "self same" but drops the reinforcing "and none other." This phrase serves an anti-heretical function, ruling out the notion that resurrection involves receiving a different body. The BLC's "very same bodies...that were buried" is reasonably strong but lacks the emphatic denial.
  - **"although with different qualities"**: The 1689 includes this important qualifier that the selfsame bodies will have "different qualities." The BLC addresses this for the righteous (raised "in power, spiritual and imperishable") but does not state the general principle that applies to all the dead.
  - **"united again to their Souls for ever"**: The 1689 states the soul-body reunion is permanent ("for ever"). The BLC says "reunited with their souls" but omits the perpetuity. This is a minor loss; the permanence of the resurrection state is an important doctrinal point.
- Structural changes:
  - The 1689's paragraph 3 attributes the raising of the unjust to "the power of Christ" and the just to "his Spirit." The BLC attributes the raising of the righteous to "the Spirit of Christ and by virtue of His resurrection" and says Christ will raise the wicked "who offend Him as judge." The 1689's structure credits Christ's power for both raisings but distinguishes the manner (power vs. Spirit) and result (dishonor vs. honor). The BLC's restructuring is substantively faithful but introduces "who offend Him" as a characterization not found in the 1689 or WLC.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> **WLC Q87.** What are we to believe concerning the resurrection?
> **A.** We are to believe that at the last day there shall be a general resurrection of the dead, both of the just and unjust: when they that are then found alive shall in a moment be changed; and the selfsame bodies of the dead which were laid in the grave, being then again united to their souls forever, shall be raised up by the power of Christ. The bodies of the just, by the Spirit of Christ, and by virtue of his resurrection as their head, shall be raised in power, spiritual, incorruptible, and made like to his glorious body; and the bodies of the wicked shall be raised up in dishonour by him, as an offended judge.

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. The resurrection is not an area of Baptist-Presbyterian disagreement.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"in a moment"** (WLC) vs. "instantly" (BLC): This is a reasonable modernization of the same concept, and the WLC's phrase is drawn from 1 Cor. 15:52 ("in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye"). No concern.
  - **"which were laid in the grave"** (WLC) vs. "that were buried" (BLC): Reasonable modernization, no concern.
  - **"being then again united to their souls forever"** (WLC) vs. "reunited with their souls" (BLC): The BLC drops **"forever"**, which is present in both the WLC and the 1689. This is a minor but notable omission of the permanence of the body-soul reunion.
  - **"as an offended judge"** (WLC) vs. "who offend Him as judge" (BLC): The WLC describes Christ as "an offended judge" — a compound descriptor of Christ's role. The BLC restructures this as "who offend Him as judge," making the wicked the grammatical subject of offense rather than describing Christ's judicial disposition. This subtly shifts emphasis from Christ's righteous indignation in His judicial office to the wicked's act of offending. While not a contradiction, it is a less precise rendering of the WLC's Christological focus.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"forever"** in the body-soul reunion — present in both WLC and 1689, absent in BLC.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Who offend Him as judge"**: This phrase is the most notable departure. The WLC's "an offended judge" is a description of Christ's character and office in the act of raising the wicked. The BLC's "who offend Him as judge" is a relative clause describing the wicked's behavior. Both are theologically defensible, but the WLC's construction is tighter and more traditional in its focus on Christ's judicial role rather than the wicked's conduct.

2. **"Forever" omission**: Both the 1689 ("united again to their Souls for ever") and the WLC ("united to their souls forever") include the permanence of the resurrection. The BLC's omission of this word is unexplained. While the doctrine of the eternal state is taught elsewhere, the perpetuity of the resurrection body-soul union is a meaningful doctrinal point within this specific locus.

3. **"And none other" omission**: The 1689's emphatic "and none other" serves an anti-heretical purpose. The BLC's "very same bodies...that were buried" is a good modernization of "selfsame" but the reinforcing denial is lost.

4. **"Although with different qualities"**: The 1689's qualifier that the same bodies will have "different qualities" is a careful theological balance — affirming identity while acknowledging transformation. The BLC addresses this for the righteous (raised "in power, spiritual and imperishable") but does not state it as a general principle.

5. **Overall structure**: The BLC follows the WLC's four-part structure faithfully: (1) general resurrection of just and unjust, (2) change of the living and raising of the dead, (3) glorification of the righteous, (4) raising of the wicked in dishonor. This is well-executed.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are largely appropriate and overlap substantially with both the WLC and 1689:
- **Daniel 12:2; Acts 24:15**: Standard texts for the general resurrection of just and unjust. Shared with WLC/1689.
- **Job 19:26**: Used for the selfsame body. Present in the 1689 (paragraph 2).
- **1 Cor. 15:51-53**: Covers the change of the living and the imperishable body. Core resurrection text.
- **1 Thess. 4:15-17**: The living not preceding the dead. Shared with WLC/1689.
- **John 5:28-29**: General resurrection and judgment. Shared with WLC/1689.
- **Rom. 8:11**: Christ's Spirit raising mortal bodies. An addition not in the WLC proofs but theologically appropriate and supports the BLC's "Spirit of Christ" language.
- **1 Cor. 15:21-23, 42-44**: Christ as firstfruits and the nature of the resurrection body. Solid choices.
- **Phil. 3:21**: Conformity to Christ's glorious body. Shared with WLC/1689.
- **Matthew 25:33**: Used for the wicked's judgment. The WLC cites John 5:28-29 and Matthew 25:33 similarly.
- **John 5:27-29**: Christ's authority as judge and the resurrection. Appropriate.

The scripture support is strong and well-chosen, with Rom. 8:11 being a helpful addition that grounds the pneumatological dimension of the resurrection.
