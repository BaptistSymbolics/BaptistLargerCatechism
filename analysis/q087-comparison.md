# Q87: Is there any use of the moral law to man since the Fall?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q87 faithfully preserves the core doctrine that the moral law cannot justify but remains useful for all people after the fall. However, it flattens the WLC's careful threefold structure (uses common to all men + uses peculiar to the unregenerate + uses peculiar to the regenerate) into a simpler twofold statement ("applies to all men, whether unregenerate or the regenerate"), which treats these merely as an exhaustive division of humanity rather than identifying distinct categories of use. The 1689 Confession's rich articulation of the law's specific functions for believers (Ch. 19.6) is left entirely to subsequent questions.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not include a question on the use of the moral law since the fall. It moves from identifying the moral law ("The rule which God at first revealed to man for his obedience, was the moral law" — Rom. 2:14,15 and 10:5) and its summary in the ten commandments directly to exposition of the commandments themselves. The closest related content is the Shorter's affirmation: "No mere man since the fall is able in this life perfectly to keep the commandments of God (Ecc. 7:20; 1 John 1:8, 10; Gal. 5:17), but doth daily break them in thought, word, or deed."

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel to compare against)
- Structural changes: N/A

Since the Shorter has no parallel question, this is a case where the BLC is functioning as a genuine expansion of the Shorter Catechism's treatment, which is theologically appropriate for a Larger Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraph 6: "Although true Believers be not under the Law, as a Covenant of Works, to be thereby Justified or condemned; yet it is of great use to them as well as to others: in that, as a Rule of Life, informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty, it directs and binds them, to walk accordingly; discovering also the sinfull pollutions of their Natures, Hearts and Lives; so as Examining themselves thereby, they may come to further Conviction of, Humiliation for, and Hatred against Sin; together with a clearer sight of the need they have of Christ and the perfection of his Obedience: It is likewise of use to the Regenerate to restrain their Corruptions, in that it forbids Sin; and the Threatnings of it serve to shew what even their Sins deserve; and what afflictions in this Life they may expect for them, although free'd from the Curse and unallayed Rigor thereof. The Promises of it likewise shew them Gods approbation of Obedience, and what blessings they may expect upon the performance thereof, though not as due to them by the Law as a Covenant of Works; so as mans doing Good and refraining from Evil, because the Law incourageth to the one and deterreth from the other, is no Evidence of his being under the Law and not under Grace."

- Omissions: BLC Q87 is an introductory/framing question, so the detailed content of 19.6 would be expected in subsequent BLC questions. However, the 1689's important qualifier that believers are "not under the Law, as a Covenant of Works, to be thereby Justified or condemned" is stronger and more precise than the BLC's "no one can attain to righteousness and life by means of the moral law." The 1689 specifically addresses believers' freedom from condemnation under the law, while the BLC makes a more general statement about inability.
- Structural changes: The 1689 speaks specifically of "true Believers" not being under the law as a covenant of works, then affirms its usefulness "to them as well as to others." The BLC reverses the framing: it starts with a general statement about post-fall inability and then affirms usefulness to "all men, whether unregenerate or the regenerate." This is not a contradiction but shifts emphasis from the believer's specific covenantal relationship to a general anthropological observation.

Also relevant — Chapter 19, Paragraph 5: "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it: Neither doth Christ in the Gospel any way dissolve, but much strengthen this obligation." The BLC's phrase "still applies to all men" captures the binding nature affirmed here, though without the 1689's important distinction between the "matter contained in it" and the "authority of God the Creator."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q94: "Is there any use of the moral law to man since the fall? A. Although no man, since the fall, can attain to righteousness and life by the moral law: yet there is great use thereof, as well common to all men, as peculiar either to the unregenerate, or the regenerate."

- Solidarity: **MODERATE**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None apparent — this is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement on the third use of the law.
- Unexpected divergences: The WLC carefully articulates a **threefold categorization** of the law's post-fall uses: (1) uses common to all men, (2) uses peculiar to the unregenerate, and (3) uses peculiar to the regenerate. This structure sets up WLC Q95 (use to all men), Q96 (use to unregenerate), and Q97 (use to regenerate). The BLC replaces this with a simpler **twofold statement**: "all men, whether unregenerate or the regenerate" — treating unregenerate and regenerate merely as an exhaustive partition of "all men" rather than identifying distinct categories of use alongside uses common to both.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"as well common to all men, as peculiar either to"** — This is the most significant omission. The WLC distinguishes between uses *shared by all* and uses *specific to each group*. The BLC's "applies to all men, whether unregenerate or the regenerate" loses this common-vs-peculiar distinction.
  - **"great use thereof"** vs. BLC's "great use and still applies" — The BLC adds "still applies" which is not in the WLC. While not theologically problematic, it shifts from the WLC's focus on *usefulness* to a dual assertion of *usefulness + applicability*, which are related but distinct concepts.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Structural flattening:** The WLC's answer is architectonic — it sets up the next three questions (Q95-97) by identifying three distinct categories of use. If the BLC intends to follow with parallel questions about uses for all men, the unregenerate, and the regenerate, the introductory framing of Q87 does not clearly prepare for this threefold treatment. The BLC's phrasing more naturally sets up only a twofold treatment (unregenerate vs. regenerate).

2. **"By means of the moral law" vs. "by the moral law":** The BLC's addition of "by means of" is a minor modernization that does not alter meaning.

3. **"Still applies" addition:** The BLC adds the phrase "still applies to all men" which goes beyond the WLC's claim of usefulness. The WLC says the law has "great use" for all; the BLC says it "still applies" to all. The 1689 Confession 19.5 does support this addition ("The moral Law doth for ever bind all"), so this is a legitimate expansion drawing on confessional language, though it introduces a concept (binding applicability) not present in the WLC parallel.

4. **"After the fall" vs. "since the fall":** Minor stylistic modernization; no theological difference.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Romans 8:3** — "For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son..." Appropriate proof for the law's inability to justify after the fall.
- **Galatians 2:16** — "...a man is not justified by the works of the law..." Directly supports the opening clause. Also used in the WLC's footnotes for Q94.
- **1 Timothy 1:8** — "But we know that the law is good, if a man use it lawfully." Supports the law's continuing usefulness. This is a reasonable proof text, though it does not itself distinguish between uses common to all and uses peculiar to different groups as the WLC's structure requires. The WLC cites different proof texts: 1 Tim 1:8 for Q94 (footnote 403), matching the BLC's citation here.

The proof texts are adequate for the doctrinal claim being made, though the flattened structure means they need not carry as much theological weight as the WLC's more differentiated proof text apparatus across Q94-97.
