# Q90: Of what particular use is the moral law to regenerate men?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q90 tracks the WLC Q97 structure closely but omits several major elements present in the 1689 Confession 19.6. The 1689 provides a richly detailed account of the law's use for regenerate believers — including its function as a "Rule of Life" informing them of God's will, its role in discovering sinful pollutions and producing conviction/humiliation/hatred of sin, its restraint of corruptions through forbidding sin, its threatenings showing what sins deserve, and its promises showing God's approbation of obedience. The BLC reduces this to three narrow functions: (1) showing debt to Christ, (2) provoking thankfulness, and (3) motivating conformity. The omissions are extensive and theologically significant, dropping the law's role as a rule of life, its sin-discovering function, its restraining function, and its promissory function — all present in the Confession and most present in the WLC.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism (Charleston 1813) does not include a question on the particular use of the moral law to regenerate men. Its treatment of the moral law begins with "What did God at first reveal to man for the rule of his obedience?" and proceeds directly to the Ten Commandments without a series on the uses of the law.

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel)
- Structural changes: N/A
- The BLC here expands beyond the Shorter's scope, which is appropriate for a Larger Catechism. The primary standard for comparison is therefore the 1689 Confession.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 19, Paragraph 6:

> "Although true Believers be not under the Law, as a Covenant of Works, to be thereby Justified or condemned; yet it is of great use to them as well as to others: in that, as a Rule of Life, informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty, it directs and binds them, to walk accordingly; discovering also the sinfull pollutions of their Natures, Hearts and Lives; so as Examining themselves thereby, they may come to further Conviction of, Humiliation for, and Hatred against Sin; together with a clearer sight of the need they have of Christ and the perfection of his Obedience: It is likewise of use to the Regenerate to restrain their Corruptions, in that it forbids Sin; and the Threatnings of it serve to shew what even their Sins deserve; and what afflictions in this Life they may expect for them, although free'd from the Curse and unallayed Rigor thereof. The Promises of it likewise shew them Gods approbation of Obedience, and what blessings they may expect upon the performance thereof, though not as due to them by the Law as a Covenant of Works; so as mans doing Good and refraining from Evil, because the Law incourageth to the one and deterreth from the other, is no Evidence of his being under the Law and not under Grace."

- **Omissions (major):**
  1. **"Rule of Life" language**: The 1689 explicitly states the law is "a Rule of Life, informing them of the Will of God, and their Duty" which "directs and binds them, to walk accordingly." The BLC says believers should "conform themselves to the moral law as the rule of their obedience" — preserving "rule" language but only as a closing motivation, not as the primary framework for the law's usefulness. The informing/directing/binding function is absent.
  2. **Sin-discovering function**: The 1689 says the law discovers "the sinfull pollutions of their Natures, Hearts and Lives" leading to "Conviction of, Humiliation for, and Hatred against Sin." This entire function is absent from the BLC.
  3. **Restraining function**: The 1689 states the law is "of use to the Regenerate to restrain their Corruptions, in that it forbids Sin." Completely absent from the BLC.
  4. **Threatenings function**: The 1689 says "the Threatnings of it serve to shew what even their Sins deserve; and what afflictions in this Life they may expect for them, although free'd from the Curse and unallayed Rigor thereof." Completely absent from the BLC.
  5. **Promises function**: The 1689 says "The Promises of it likewise shew them Gods approbation of Obedience, and what blessings they may expect upon the performance thereof, though not as due to them by the Law as a Covenant of Works." Completely absent from the BLC.
  6. **Anti-antinomian safeguard**: The 1689 concludes with the important clarification that "mans doing Good and refraining from Evil, because the Law incourageth to the one and deterreth from the other, is no Evidence of his being under the Law and not under Grace." This pastoral safeguard against antinomian objections is absent.

- **Structural changes:**
  - The 1689 presents the law's use to regenerate men as multifaceted: rule of life, sin-discoverer, corruption-restrainer, threatener, and promiser. The BLC collapses these into a single trajectory: shows debt to Christ → produces thankfulness → motivates obedience. This restructuring reduces the law's ongoing, active role in the believer's life to essentially a Christological reflection exercise, losing the law's direct pedagogical and sanctifying functions.

- **Agreement:**
  - Both affirm believers are freed from the law as a covenant of works, neither justified nor condemned by it.
  - Both affirm the law shows what believers owe to Christ for fulfilling it and enduring its curse.
  - Both affirm this provokes thankfulness and care for conformity.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q97:

> "Q. 97. What special use is there of the moral law to the regenerate? A. Although they that are regenerate, and believe in Christ, be delivered from the moral law as a covenant of works, so as thereby they are neither justified nor condemned; yet, besides the general uses thereof common to them with all men, it is of special use, to show them how much they are bound to Christ for his fulfilling it, and enduring the curse thereof in their stead, and for their good; and thereby to provoke them to more thankfulness, and to express the same in their greater care to conform themselves thereunto as the rule of their obedience."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC follows the WLC Q97 very closely in structure and phrasing.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent (this topic does not involve baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology distinctives).
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  1. **"besides the general uses thereof common to them with all men"**: The WLC explicitly includes this phrase, directing the reader back to Q95 (the general uses: informing of God's will, binding to walk accordingly, discovering sinful pollution, humbling in sense of sin, showing need of Christ). The BLC drops this cross-reference entirely. This is significant because the WLC's general uses (Q95) contain much of the content that the 1689 includes in its paragraph 6 — sin-discovery, conviction, humiliation. By dropping this phrase, the BLC loses access to those doctrinal elements without ever teaching them.
  2. **"and believe in Christ"**: The WLC specifies "they that are regenerate, and believe in Christ." The BLC says only "those who are regenerate." Minor, since regeneration and faith are inseparable in Reformed theology, but the WLC's inclusion emphasizes the believer's active faith relationship.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "besides the general uses thereof common to them with all men" — This is the most consequential omission from the WLC. The WLC system teaches the general uses in Q95 (rule of life, sin-discovery, humiliation, need of Christ) and then adds the special uses in Q97. The BLC appears to teach only the special uses without ever covering the general uses, leaving a doctrinal gap.
  - "and believe in Christ" — minor precision loss.

## Detailed Notes
1. **The core structural problem**: The WLC wisely distinguishes between general uses of the law (Q95, applicable to all including the regenerate) and special uses for the regenerate (Q97). The 1689 collapses both into paragraph 6 but retains all the content. The BLC follows the WLC's Q97 structure but without a corresponding Q that covers the general uses that apply to believers. If the BLC has a parallel to WLC Q95, this concern is mitigated; but Q90 standing alone presents an impoverished view of the law's role in the believer's life.

2. **Christological reduction**: The BLC makes the law's primary function for believers essentially retrospective and Christological — showing what Christ has done. While true and important, the 1689 also gives the law a prospective and sanctifying function: informing of duty, discovering sin, restraining corruption, threatening consequences, and promising blessings. The BLC's framing could inadvertently support a view that the law's only value for believers is as a mirror reflecting Christ's work, rather than an ongoing instrument in sanctification.

3. **Missing pastoral safeguards**: The 1689's concluding statement that obedience motivated by the law's encouragements and deterrents is not evidence of being "under the Law and not under Grace" is a carefully crafted anti-antinomian safeguard. Its absence leaves the BLC's teaching on this point less robust against antinomian misreadings.

4. **The thankfulness-obedience trajectory is too narrow**: The BLC presents: debt to Christ → thankfulness → conformity. The 1689 presents: rule of life + sin-discovery + conviction/humiliation/hatred of sin + need of Christ + restraint of corruption + threatenings showing what sin deserves + promises showing God's approbation. The BLC captures roughly one-quarter of the Confession's content.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Romans 6:14; 7:4-6; Galatians 4:4-5; Colossians 2:13-14**: Appropriately cited for freedom from the law as covenant of works.
- **Romans 3:20**: Cited for "nor condemned" — this verse actually speaks to the law's inability to justify ("by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified"), not to condemnation specifically. The WLC cites different proof texts here. Minor concern.
- **Galatians 5:22-23; Romans 8:1**: Cited for the law showing debt to Christ. Galatians 5:22-23 (fruit of the Spirit) is an unusual choice here — the WLC does not cite it at this point, and it speaks more to the Spirit's work than to the law showing debt to Christ.
- **Romans 7:24-25; Galatians 3:13-14; Romans 8:3-4; Acts 13:38-39**: Cited for thankfulness. These are strong choices, particularly Galatians 3:13-14 on Christ enduring the curse.
- **Luke 1:68-75; Colossians 1:12-14; Romans 6:14**: Cited for conformity to the law as rule of obedience. Luke 1:68-75 is excellent — Zacharias's prophecy of serving God "in holiness and righteousness" is precisely about redemption motivating obedience.
- **Deuteronomy 30:19-20; Romans 7:22; Romans 12:2; Titus 2:11-14; James 1:25**: Final section verses. Titus 2:11-14 (grace teaching us to live godly) and James 1:25 (the perfect law of liberty) are good texts but could have supported a richer treatment of the law's sanctifying role if the BLC had included it.
- **Notable absence**: The 1689 cites Romans 3:20 and Romans 7:7ff for the sin-discovering function. The BLC uses Romans 3:20 but only for the "not condemned" clause, missing its application to sin-discovery in the believer.
