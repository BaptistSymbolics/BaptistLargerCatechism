# Q167: How do those who receive the Lord's Supper in the right way feed on the body and blood of Christ?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q167 faithfully reproduces the core theological content of 1689 30.7 and WLC Q170, correctly teaching the spiritual (not corporeal) presence of Christ in the Supper and spiritual reception by faith. However, it omits the important qualifications "really and indeed" / "truly and really" that the 1689, WLC, and Shorter all employ to guard against a bare memorialist reading, and it drops the comparative clause ("no less truly and really than the elements themselves are to their outward senses") that gives the doctrine its distinctive Reformed force. It also replaces the traditional "carnally and corporally" with the weaker "bodily or physical," losing connection to the historic anti-Roman Catholic and anti-Lutheran precision of the confessional language.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is the Lord's supper?
> A. The Lord's supper is an ordinance of the New Testament, instituted by Jesus Christ; wherein by giving and receiving bread and wine, according to his appointment, his death is shown forth, and the worthy receivers are, not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith, made partakers of his body and blood, with all his benefits, to their spiritual nourishment and growth in grace (Mt. 26:26, 27, 28; 1 Cor. 11:23-26; 10:16).

- **Omissions:**
  - The Shorter says worthy receivers are made "partakers of his body and blood, **with all his benefits, to their spiritual nourishment and growth in grace**." The BLC omits "to their spiritual nourishment and growth in grace," which names the *telos* (purpose/fruit) of worthy reception.
  - The Shorter's phrase "not after a corporal and carnal manner" uses the traditional confessional vocabulary; the BLC substitutes "not in a bodily or physical way," which is less precise (see Detailed Notes).
- **Structural changes:** The Shorter integrates the definition of the Supper with the mode of reception in a single answer. The BLC (as a Larger Catechism) appropriately separates these into distinct questions (Q164 for definition, Q167 for mode of feeding). This structural separation is expected and appropriate.
- **Agreement:** Both teach spiritual reception by faith, denial of corporeal presence, and reception of Christ crucified with all benefits of his death.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 30, Paragraph 7:
> "Worthy receivers, outwardly partaking of the visible Elements in this Ordinance, do then also inwardly by faith, **really and indeed**, yet not carnally, and corporally, but spiritually receive, and feed upon Christ crucified & all the benefits of his death: the Body and Blood of Christ, being then not corporally, or carnally, but **spiritually present to the faith of Believers**, in that Ordinance, **as the Elements themselves are to their outward senses**."

- **Omissions:**
  - **"really and indeed"**: The 1689 says worthy receivers "really and indeed, yet not carnally, and corporally, but spiritually receive." The BLC says they "truly and actually feed on the body and blood of Christ, not in a bodily or physical way, but spiritually." The BLC does have "truly and actually," which partially corresponds, but it attaches these adverbs to the feeding rather than to the mode of spiritual reception, subtly shifting emphasis.
  - **"as the Elements themselves are to their outward senses"**: The 1689 uses this comparative clause to establish the *equal reality* of spiritual presence to faith and physical presence to senses. The BLC renders this as "just as truly as the external elements are obvious to the senses" in section 2, which captures the comparison but weakens it by replacing "to their outward senses" (the proper parallel: spiritual senses / outward senses) with the flatter "obvious to the senses."
  - **"outwardly partaking of the visible Elements"**: The 1689 pairs "outwardly...inwardly" to establish the sacramental parallel between outward eating and inward spiritual feeding. The BLC drops this pairing entirely.
  - **"inwardly by faith"**: The 1689's "inwardly by faith" is not preserved as a phrase; the BLC distributes the concept across sections 3-4 but loses the explicit outward/inward sacramental structure.
- **Structural changes:** The 1689's single paragraph is broken into four BLC sections. This is acceptable for catechetical purposes, but the separation of the denial of corporeal presence (section 1) from the affirmation of spiritual presence (section 2) slightly weakens their confessional unity as two sides of one coin.
- **Agreement:** The core theology is preserved: denial of corporeal/carnal presence, affirmation of spiritual presence to faith, reception of Christ crucified and benefits of his death by faith.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q170:
> Q. 170. How do they that worthily communicate in the Lord's supper feed upon the body and blood of Christ therein?
> A. As the body and blood of Christ are not corporally or carnally present in, with, or under the bread and wine in the Lord's supper, and yet are spiritually present to the faith of the receiver, no less truly and really than the elements themselves are to their outward senses; so they that worthily communicate in the sacrament of the Lord's supper, do therein feed upon the body and blood of Christ, not after a corporal and carnal, but in a spiritual manner; yet truly and really, while by faith they receive and apply unto themselves Christ crucified, and all the benefits of his death.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG - The BLC clearly follows the WLC's structure and content for this question. The four-section breakdown of the BLC maps directly onto the WLC's logical flow.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** The BLC replaces "sacrament" with the implicit "ordinance" framing (consistent throughout the BLC). This is expected and acceptable.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - **"no less truly and really"**: The WLC's emphatic "no less truly and really" is weakened to "just as truly" in the BLC. The WLC's phrasing ("no less...than") is a stronger comparative that guards against minimizing spiritual presence.
  - **"yet truly and really"**: The WLC says worthy communicants feed "not after a corporal and carnal, but in a spiritual manner; **yet truly and really**." This crucial qualification insists that spiritual feeding is *real* feeding. The BLC's "truly and actually" partially corresponds but is placed differently in the sentence structure.
  - **"corporally or carnally"**: The WLC uses both terms; the BLC substitutes "bodily or physical," losing the historic polemical precision of "carnally" (which targets the Roman Catholic doctrine of carnal eating) and "corporally" (which targets Lutheran ubiquity).
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "no less truly and really than" (comparative force weakened)
  - "corporally or carnally" (historic theological vocabulary replaced)
  - "not after a corporal and carnal, but in a spiritual manner; yet truly and really" (the "yet" pivot that insists on the reality of spiritual feeding is structurally altered)

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Bodily or physical" vs. "corporally or carnally"**: The BLC's modernization of "corporally or carnally" to "bodily or physical" is not merely a vocabulary update. "Carnal" (from Latin *carnalis*, pertaining to flesh) specifically targets the Roman Catholic doctrine that the flesh of Christ is eaten with the mouth. "Corporal" specifically targets the idea of Christ's body being locally present. "Bodily or physical" collapses these two distinct denials into a single, vaguer negation. The 1689, WLC, and Shorter all use the traditional terms.

2. **The comparative clause**: The 1689's "as the Elements themselves are to their outward senses" and the WLC's "no less truly and really than the elements themselves are to their outward senses" establish a precise Reformed doctrine: the spiritual presence of Christ to faith is *equally real* as the physical presence of bread and wine to the senses. The BLC's "just as truly as the external elements are obvious to the senses" preserves the comparison but "obvious" is a weaker term than the original's structural parallel between faith and senses.

3. **Loss of outward/inward pairing**: The 1689's "outwardly partaking...inwardly by faith" establishes the sacramental principle that external acts correspond to internal spiritual realities. This pairing is a key feature of Reformed sacramental theology and its absence in the BLC is a notable, though not critical, loss.

4. **"Receive and apply"**: Both the WLC and BLC retain the important phrase about faith receiving and applying Christ crucified with all benefits of his death. This is a strength of the BLC.

5. **Question framing**: The BLC's question asks about "those who receive the Lord's Supper in the right way" where the WLC asks about those who "worthily communicate." These are functionally equivalent modernizations.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Acts 3:21** (section 1): Used by the 1689 (30.6) to support the denial of corporeal presence (Christ's body is in heaven until the restoration). Appropriate use, though the 1689 deploys it specifically against transubstantiation rather than against presence generally.
- **Matthew 26:26-28** (section 2): The words of institution. Standard proof text shared with the Shorter Catechism.
- **Galatians 3:1** (section 2): An unusual choice for this context. It references Christ being "publicly portrayed as crucified" before the Galatians' eyes, which may intend to support the idea of spiritual apprehension, but it is not a standard proof text for the Lord's Supper doctrine and was not used by the 1689 or WLC here.
- **Hebrews 11:1** (section 2): Supports the concept of faith apprehending unseen realities. Not used by the 1689 or WLC for this specific doctrine, but theologically relevant.
- **1 Corinthians 11:24-29** (section 3): Core Pauline institution text. Used by both the 1689 and WLC.
- **John 6:51-53** (section 3): Jesus's discourse on eating his flesh and drinking his blood. Standard Reformed proof text for spiritual feeding, though its use here is notable since neither the 1689 nor WLC cite it for this specific paragraph/answer.
- **1 Corinthians 10:16** (section 4): The communion/participation text. Used by the 1689 (30.7) and WLC (Q170). Appropriate and well-placed.
