# Q152: What is baptism?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q152 significantly restructures the doctrine of baptism in ways that depart from both the Shorter Catechism and the 1689 Confession. It upgrades "ordinance" to "sacrament," adds theological content not found in either Baptist standard (regeneration, adoption, resurrection unto everlasting life), omits the Shorter's crucial language about baptism being a sign to "the party baptized" and drops the Shorter's key terms "death, burial, and resurrection" and "ingrafted into him," and introduces the novel qualifier "for believers" which subtly shifts baptism's signification from objective gospel realities to the subjective experience of the baptized.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What is baptism? A. Baptism is an ordinance of the New Testament instituted by Jesus Christ, to be unto the party baptized a sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, burial, and resurrection; of his being ingrafted into him; of remission of sins; and of his giving up himself unto God through Jesus Christ, to live and walk in newness of life." (Rom. 6:3,4,5; Col. 2:12; Gal. 3:27; Mk. 1:4; Acts 2:38, and 22:16; Rom. 6:3,4)

- **Omissions:**
  - "to be unto the party baptized" — The Shorter specifies baptism is a sign *to the party baptized*. The BLC drops this personal, directed language entirely.
  - "in his death, burial, and resurrection" — The Shorter (following the Baptist tradition's emphasis on immersion as picturing burial) explicitly names death, burial, and resurrection as the elements of Christ's work with which the believer has fellowship. The BLC reduces this to "union and fellowship with Christ" — a vaguer and less specific formulation that loses the death-burial-resurrection sequence central to Baptist baptismal theology.
  - "of his being ingrafted into him" — This classic Reformed/Baptist language of spiritual union (engrafting) is absent from the BLC. The BLC substitutes "union and fellowship with Christ," which is less precise.
  - "ordinance" replaced with "sacrament" — The Shorter uses "ordinance," consistent with Baptist tradition. The BLC uses "sacrament," which is the WLC's term. While the 1689 Confession's chapter heading uses "Baptism and the Lords Supper" without either term, and Ch. 28.1 calls them "ordinances of positive, and sovereign institution," the Shorter clearly uses "ordinance." The BLC's adoption of "sacrament" is a significant terminological departure from its primary Baptist standard.

- **Structural changes:**
  - The Shorter presents baptism as signifying: (1) fellowship with Christ in his death, burial, and resurrection, (2) ingrafting into him, (3) remission of sins, (4) giving up unto God to walk in newness of life. The BLC restructures this into a much longer list: (1) union and fellowship with Christ, (2) remission of sins through His blood, (3) regeneration by His Spirit, (4) adoption, (5) resurrection unto everlasting life, (6) giving up into God to walk in newness of life. Items 3-5 are imported from the WLC and are absent from the Shorter.

- **Additions beyond the Shorter:**
  - "of positive and sovereign institution" — This phrase comes from 1689 Ch. 28.1 and is a legitimate expansion.
  - "to be continued in His church to the end of the world" — Also from 1689 Ch. 28.1, a legitimate expansion.
  - "for believers" — This qualifier is novel. Neither the Shorter, the 1689, nor the WLC uses this language. The Shorter says "to be unto the party baptized a sign." The BLC's "for believers" subtly shifts the framing.
  - "regeneration by His Spirit" — Not in the Shorter or 1689 Ch. 29.1. Imported from WLC Q. 165.
  - "adoption" — Not in the Shorter or 1689 Ch. 29.1. Imported from WLC Q. 165.
  - "resurrection unto everlasting life" — Not in the Shorter or 1689 Ch. 29.1. Imported from WLC Q. 165.
  - "through His blood" (modifying remission of sins) — Not in the Shorter's answer to this question. Imported from WLC Q. 165.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 28, Paragraph 1: "Baptism and the Lords Supper are ordinances of positive, and soveraign institution; appointed by the Lord Jesus the only Law-giver, to be continued in his Church to the end of the world."

Chapter 29, Paragraph 1: "Baptism is an Ordinance of the New Testament, ordained by Jesus Christ, to be unto the party Baptized, a sign of his fellowship with him, in his death, and resurrection; of his being engrafted into him; of remission of sins; and of his giving up unto God through Jesus Christ to live and walk in newness of Life."

- **Omissions:**
  - "to be unto the party Baptized" — The 1689's specification that baptism is a sign directed *to the party baptized* is dropped.
  - "in his death, and resurrection" — The 1689 (like the Shorter) specifies fellowship *in his death, and resurrection*. The BLC's "union and fellowship with Christ" loses this specificity.
  - "of his being engrafted into him" — The engrafting language is omitted.
  - "Ordinance" — The 1689 consistently calls baptism an "Ordinance." The BLC uses "sacrament."
  - "the Lord Jesus the only Law-giver" — The 1689 Ch. 28.1 calls Christ "the only Law-giver" in the context of instituting the ordinances. The BLC simplifies to "ordained and appointed by Jesus Christ."

- **Structural changes:**
  - The 1689's four-part signification (fellowship in death/resurrection, engrafting, remission, giving up to God) is replaced with a six-part list that imports WLC elements foreign to the Baptist confessional tradition.
  - The BLC adds "regeneration by His Spirit," "adoption," and "resurrection unto everlasting life" — none of which appear in the 1689's chapter on baptism. While these are true doctrines taught elsewhere in the Confession, their inclusion in the definition of baptism goes beyond what the 1689 assigns to baptismal signification.

- **Agreement:**
  - "of positive and sovereign institution" is faithfully drawn from 1689 Ch. 28.1.
  - "to be continued in His church to the end of the world" matches 1689 Ch. 28.1.
  - "remission of sins" is retained (though expanded with "through His blood").
  - "giving up into God, through Jesus Christ, to live and walk in newness of life" closely follows 1689 Ch. 29.1.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. 165. What is baptism? A. Baptism is a sacrament of the New Testament, wherein Christ hath ordained the washing with water in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, to be a sign and seal of ingrafting into himself, of remission of sins by his blood, and regeneration by his Spirit; of adoption, and resurrection unto everlasting life; and whereby the parties baptized are solemnly admitted into the visible church, and enter into an open and professed engagement to be wholly and only the Lord's."

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC clearly uses the WLC as its primary structural template rather than the Shorter Catechism or 1689 Confession.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:**
  - "sacrament" is retained from the WLC (this is actually unexpected — Baptists typically prefer "ordinance").
  - "sign and seal" reduced to implied sign only — the BLC drops "seal," which is appropriate for Baptists who do not hold baptism to be a seal of the covenant of grace in the Westminster sense.
  - "washing with water in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost" — omitted, presumably moved to a subsequent question on mode/administration.
  - "whereby the parties baptized are solemnly admitted into the visible church" — appropriately omitted or deferred, as Baptist ecclesiology handles church membership differently.
  - "enter into an open and professed engagement to be wholly and only the Lord's" — this is partly captured in the BLC's "giving up into God" language, though the WLC's "wholly and only" emphasis is stronger.

- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - "ingrafting into himself" — This is present in the WLC AND in the Baptist Shorter and 1689, yet the BLC drops it. This is a surprising omission since all three standards agree on this language.
  - The BLC retains "sacrament" from the WLC rather than "ordinance" from the Baptist standards. This is solidarity with the WLC at the expense of the Baptist confessional tradition.

- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "sign and seal" — "seal" appropriately dropped for Baptist theology; but "sign" is also not explicitly used (the BLC simply lists what baptism signifies without using the word "sign").
  - "ingrafting into himself" — dropped despite being in all three standards.
  - "wholly and only the Lord's" — weakened to "giving up into God."

## Detailed Notes

1. **"Sacrament" vs. "Ordinance"**: This is perhaps the most conspicuous terminological issue. The 1689 Confession and the Baptist Shorter Catechism consistently use "ordinance." The WLC uses "sacrament." For a catechism whose primary standards are the 1689/Shorter, adopting "sacrament" is a departure that requires justification. While some Reformed Baptists are comfortable with "sacrament," the confessional term is "ordinance."

2. **"For believers" qualifier**: Neither the Shorter, the 1689, nor the WLC introduces the phrase "for believers" into the definition of what baptism *is*. The 1689 and Shorter say baptism is a sign "to the party baptized." The BLC's "for believers" is theologically similar but subtly different — it shifts the definition from describing what baptism signifies to the one receiving it, to what baptism signifies *for a particular class of people*. This is a novel framing. Baptist theology restricts baptism *to* believers (as the 1689 Ch. 29.2 addresses), but the definition of baptism itself in Ch. 29.1 does not use this qualifier.

3. **Loss of "death, burial, and resurrection"**: The Baptist emphasis on immersion as picturing death, burial, and resurrection is a distinctive mark of Baptist baptismal theology. The Shorter explicitly includes "death, burial, and resurrection" (the 1689 has "death, and resurrection"). The BLC's vague "union and fellowship with Christ" eliminates this vivid, concrete, and theologically loaded language. This is a significant loss.

4. **Loss of "ingrafted/engrafted"**: All three standards (Shorter, 1689, WLC) include the language of being ingrafted/engrafted into Christ. The BLC drops this unanimously attested term, replacing it with the vaguer "union and fellowship." This is an omission from all three standards simultaneously.

5. **Imported WLC content**: The BLC imports "regeneration by His Spirit," "adoption," and "resurrection unto everlasting life" from the WLC. These concepts are not assigned to baptismal signification in either the 1689 or the Shorter. While these are true doctrines, expanding what baptism signifies beyond what the Baptist confessional standards teach introduces content that the 1689's framers chose not to include.

6. **The BLC reads more like a Baptized WLC than a Larger exposition of the Shorter**: The structure and content of BLC Q152 follow WLC Q. 165 much more closely than the Shorter Catechism. A Baptist Larger Catechism should expand on the Shorter Catechism's content while maintaining its theological framework, not replace it with a lightly modified WLC answer.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Matthew 28:19-20**: Appropriately cited for Christ's institution and perpetuity. Present in 1689 Ch. 28.1.
- **Galatians 3:27; Romans 6:3-5; Colossians 2:12**: These support "union and fellowship with Christ" but notably Rom. 6:3-5 and Col. 2:12 specifically teach *death, burial, and resurrection* — the very language the BLC drops from its text while citing these verses.
- **Mark 1:4; Acts 22:16**: Appropriate for remission of sins. Mark 1:4 matches the Shorter; Acts 22:16 matches both Shorter and 1689 (the 1689 cites "Act. 26.16" which appears to be a printing error for Acts 22:16).
- **Titus 3:5; Ephesians 5:26; John 3:5**: These support "regeneration by His Spirit" — but this content is imported from the WLC, not the Baptist standards.
- **Galatians 3:26-27**: Supports "adoption" — again WLC-imported content.
- **1 Corinthians 15:29; Romans 6:5**: Supports "resurrection unto everlasting life." 1 Cor. 15:29 is a notoriously difficult text (baptism for the dead) and its use as a proof text for resurrection unto everlasting life is exegetically questionable.
- **Romans 6:4**: Appropriately cited for walking in newness of life, matching the Shorter and 1689.
- The proof texts for the WLC-imported material (regeneration, adoption, resurrection) match the WLC's own proof texts, confirming that this content was drawn from the WLC rather than developed from the Baptist standards.
