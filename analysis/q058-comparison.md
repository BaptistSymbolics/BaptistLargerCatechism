# Q58: Who are made partakers of redemption through Christ?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q58 combines material from WLC Q59 (partakers of redemption), WLC Q66 (union with Christ), and the Shorter's two application questions into a single answer. While much of the theology is preserved, the BLC introduces a significant structural change: it inserts the gospel and faith as mediating instruments between the Spirit's work and the believer in a way that subtly alters the Shorter's presentation, where the Spirit works faith directly rather than enabling belief "in Christ as presented in the gospel." More critically, the BLC drops the Shorter's key phrase "thereby uniting us to Christ" — replacing faith as the instrument of union with a looser construction where union follows faith but is described as a parallel effect rather than faith's own uniting function. The WLC's phrase "really" (in "spiritually and mystically, yet really and inseparably") is changed to "truly," which is defensible but weakens an important theological term.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> "Q. How are we made partakers of the redemption purchased by Christ?
> A. We are made partakers of the redemption purchased by Christ, by the effectual application of it to us by his Holy Spirit." (Eph. 1:13-14; John 6:37,39; Titus 3:5,6)

> "Q. How doth the Spirit apply to us the redemption purchased by Christ?
> A. The Spirit applieth to us the redemption purchased by Christ, by working faith in us, and thereby uniting us to Christ, in our effectual calling." (Eph. 1:13,14; John 6:37,39; Eph. 2:8; Eph. 3:17; 1 Cor. 1:9)

- Omissions:
  - **"effectual application of it to us"** — The Shorter's first answer emphasizes that redemption is *applied to us* by the Spirit. The BLC rephrases as "certainly applied and effectually communicated," which preserves the concept but reorders from the Shorter's emphasis on application *to* us.
  - **"thereby uniting us to Christ"** — This is the Shorter's critical phrase showing that faith is the *instrument* by which the Spirit unites us to Christ. The BLC restructures this: the Spirit "works faith in them to believe in Christ as presented in the gospel, and thereby unites them... to Christ." The "thereby" in the BLC attaches ambiguously — it could refer to faith or to the entire preceding process. The Shorter's construction is tighter: faith *is* the means of union. The BLC makes union appear as a subsequent effect of the faith-process rather than faith's own uniting function.

- Structural changes:
  - The Shorter presents a clean two-step: (1) Spirit works faith → (2) faith thereby unites to Christ → (3) this happens in effectual calling. The BLC expands this into a more complex sequence: (1) redemption applied to those for whom Christ purchased it → (2) through the Holy Spirit → (3) who works faith to believe in Christ as presented in the gospel → (4) thereby unites them to Christ → (5) this is done in effectual calling. The insertion of "to believe in Christ as presented in the gospel" between "works faith" and union with Christ is an expansion not present in the Shorter, and it shifts the emphasis from the Spirit's direct work of uniting via faith to a process mediated through the gospel presentation.
  - The BLC adds "in the process of time" (from WLC Q59's "in time"), which is a legitimate expansion from the WLC.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 10, Paragraph 1 (Of Effectual Calling):
> "Those whom God hath predestinated unto Life, he is pleased in his appointed, and accepted time, effectually to call by his word, and Spirit, out of that state of sin, and death, in which they are by nature, to grace and Salvation by Jesus Christ; inlightning their minds, spiritually, and savingly to understand the things of God; taking away their heart of stone, and giving unto them an heart of flesh; renewing their wills, and by his Almighty power determining them to that which is good, and effectually drawing them to Jesus Christ; yet so as they come most freely, being made willing by his Grace."

Chapter 10, Paragraph 2:
> "This Effectual Call is of God's free, and special grace alone, not from any thing at all foreseen in man, nor from any power, or agency in the Creature, coworking with his special Grace, the Creature being wholly passive therein, being dead in sins and trespasses, until being quickned & renewed by the holy Spirit, he is thereby enabled to answer this call, and to embrace the Grace offered and conveyed in it; and that by no less power, then that which raised up Christ from the dead."

Chapter 13, Paragraph 1 (Of Sanctification):
> "They who are united to Christ, Effectually called, and regenerated, having a new heart, and a new Spirit created in them, through the vertue of Christ's death, and Resurrection; are also farther sanctified..."

Chapter 27, Paragraph 1 (Of the Communion of Saints):
> "All Saints that are united to Jesus Christ their Head, by his Spirit, and Faith; although they are not made thereby one person with him, have fellowship in his Graces, sufferings, death, resurrection, and glory..."

- Omissions:
  - **Monergistic emphasis** — The 1689 10.2 stresses that effectual calling is "of God's free, and special grace alone, not from any thing at all foreseen in man, nor from any power, or agency in the Creature, coworking with his special Grace, the Creature being wholly passive therein." The BLC does not include any language about the creature's passivity or the exclusion of human cooperation. While a catechism answer need not reproduce every confessional detail, the complete absence of monergistic qualification in a question about the application of redemption is notable.
  - **Enlightening, heart of stone/flesh, renewal of will** — The 1689 10.1 describes the internal operations of effectual calling in rich detail (enlightening minds, removing heart of stone, renewing wills, almighty power determining them). The BLC reduces this to "works faith in them to believe in Christ." These details properly belong to a subsequent question on effectual calling itself (which the BLC likely addresses in Q59+), so this is not a direct omission from Q58's scope.
  - **"By his Spirit, and Faith"** — 1689 27.1 describes union as accomplished "by his Spirit, and Faith," which matches the Shorter's construction. The BLC preserves both Spirit and faith but restructures their relationship.

- Structural changes:
  - The BLC's section on union ("spiritually and mystically yet truly and inseparably, to Christ as their head and husband") draws from WLC Q66 rather than from the 1689 directly. The 1689 does not use the "head and husband" formula in its effectual calling chapter, though it does identify Christ as "head" of the church (26.1) and describes the church as Christ's "spouse" (26.1). The combination is theologically sound, drawing on WLC language that itself draws on Ephesians 5.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

WLC Q59:
> "Q. 59. Who are made partakers of redemption through Christ?
> A. Redemption is certainly applied, and effectually communicated, to all those for whom Christ hath purchased it; who are in time by the Holy Ghost enabled to believe in Christ according to the gospel."

WLC Q66:
> "Q. 66. What is that union which the elect have with Christ?
> A. The union which the elect have with Christ is the work of God's grace, whereby they are spiritually and mystically, yet really and inseparably, joined to Christ as their head and husband; which is done in their effectual calling."

- Solidarity: **MODERATE**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected. This question involves no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology distinctives.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - **"really" changed to "truly"** — WLC Q66 uses "really" (in the philosophical sense of "in reality") to affirm the objective reality of mystical union, over against any merely metaphorical reading. The BLC substitutes "truly," which conveys truthfulness but loses the ontological weight of "really." This is a weakening of precision in an area where Baptists and Presbyterians share identical theology.
  - **"joined to" changed to "unites them... to"** — WLC Q66 says believers are "joined to Christ" (passive — God does the joining). The BLC says the Spirit "unites them... to Christ" — the passive posture of the believer is preserved but the construction shifts the subject from God's grace to the Spirit's action, which is theologically equivalent but structurally different.
  - **"the work of God's grace"** — WLC Q66 identifies union as "the work of God's grace." The BLC drops this phrase entirely, instead making union a consequence of the Spirit working faith. This omits the WLC's explicit attribution of union to grace as such.
  - **"enabled to believe"** vs. **"works faith in them to believe"** — WLC Q59 says the elect are "enabled to believe in Christ according to the gospel." The BLC says the Spirit "works faith in them to believe in Christ as presented in the gospel." The WLC's "enabled to believe" is more concise; the BLC's "works faith in them to believe" is slightly redundant (working faith and believing are the same act described twice). The BLC's "as presented in the gospel" substitutes for the WLC's "according to the gospel" — a subtle shift from faith operating *according to* the gospel's content to faith directed at Christ *as presented in* the gospel, which is defensible but introduces a mediating emphasis on gospel presentation.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - **"the work of God's grace"** (WLC Q66) — dropped without replacement. This is a significant attribution.
  - **"really"** (WLC Q66) — replaced with the weaker "truly."

## Detailed Notes

1. **Combination of WLC Q59 and Q66**: The BLC merges what the WLC treats in two separate questions (Q59 on partakers of redemption, Q66 on union with Christ) into a single answer. This is a legitimate structural choice for a catechism, but it compresses the WLC's careful progression from application → visible/invisible church → union → effectual calling into a single statement that moves directly from application to union to effectual calling.

2. **"In the process of time"**: This phrase modernizes the WLC's "in time," which itself echoes the 1689's "in his appointed, and accepted time." The expansion to "in the process of time" is a faithful rendering that preserves the temporal aspect of effectual calling (against any hyper-eternal view of salvation).

3. **The gospel-mediation emphasis**: The BLC's "to believe in Christ as presented in the gospel" adds an emphasis on the gospel as the medium through which Christ is apprehended by faith. This is theologically correct (cf. Rom. 10:17, which the BLC cites) but represents an expansion beyond both the WLC ("according to the gospel") and the Shorter (which simply says "working faith in us"). The Shorter and WLC keep the focus on the Spirit's direct work of enabling/working faith; the BLC shifts some emphasis to the instrumental role of gospel proclamation.

4. **"Head and husband" imagery**: Both the WLC Q66 and the BLC use this dual image. The "head" imagery comes from Ephesians 1:22, 5:23; the "husband" imagery from Ephesians 5:25-32 and the bridal theology of Song of Solomon/Revelation. This is fully consistent with Reformed theology across Baptist and Presbyterian traditions.

5. **Missing "work of God's grace" attribution**: The WLC Q66 begins by identifying union as "the work of God's grace." This is a theologically important attribution — it grounds union with Christ explicitly in divine grace, not human effort. The BLC's omission of this phrase means that while the Spirit is identified as the agent, the explicit grace-attribution is lost. Given that the 1689 10.2 emphasizes that effectual calling is "of God's free, and special grace alone," this omission is notable.

## Scripture Analysis
**BLC Proof Texts by Section:**

- **Section 1** (Redemption applied to those for whom Christ purchased it): Eph. 1:13-14; John 6:37-39; John 10:15-16
  - Eph. 1:13-14 — sealing with the Holy Spirit of promise; strongly supports application of redemption
  - John 6:37-39 — all the Father gives will come; none will be lost; excellent particular redemption text
  - John 10:15-16 — "I lay down my life for the sheep"; strong particular redemption/application text

- **Section 2** (Through the Holy Spirit): Titus 3:5-6; John 3:5-6; John 1:11-13
  - Titus 3:5-6 — washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit; excellent
  - John 3:5-6 — born of water and Spirit; supports Spirit's role in regeneration
  - John 1:11-13 — born not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God; supports monergism even though the answer text does not explicitly state it

- **Section 3** (Works faith to believe): Rom. 10:17; John 6:37,39; John 10:15-16; 1 Cor. 2:12-16; Rom. 8:29-30
  - Rom. 10:17 — faith comes by hearing; supports gospel-mediation emphasis
  - 1 Cor. 2:12-16 — Spirit reveals things of God; supports Spirit's work in understanding
  - Rom. 8:29-30 — golden chain of salvation; strong support for effectual calling sequence
  - Note: John 6:37,39 and John 10:15-16 are repeated from section 1

- **Section 4** (Union with Christ): 1 Cor. 6:17; John 10:28; John 15:1-5; Eph. 5:23,30
  - 1 Cor. 6:17 — joined to the Lord, one spirit; strong union text
  - John 10:28 — no one shall snatch them; supports inseparability
  - John 15:1-5 — vine and branches; strong union imagery
  - Eph. 5:23,30 — Christ head of the church/members of his body; supports head and husband imagery

- **Section 5** (Done in effectual calling): 1 Peter 5:10; 1 Cor. 1:9; Eph. 2:8; Eph. 3:17
  - 1 Peter 5:10 — God who called you; supports effectual calling
  - 1 Cor. 1:9 — called into fellowship of His Son; strong effectual calling text (also Shorter proof text)
  - Eph. 2:8 — by grace through faith, not of yourselves; supports grace-attribution (interestingly, this proof text contains the grace emphasis that the answer text omits)
  - Eph. 3:17 — Christ dwelling in hearts through faith; supports union via faith (also Shorter proof text)

The proof texts are generally well-chosen and appropriate. Notably, the proof texts for section 5 (Eph. 2:8) actually supply the grace-attribution that the answer text itself omits — the proof text teaches "by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves," but the BLC answer does not explicitly identify union or effectual calling as a work of grace.
