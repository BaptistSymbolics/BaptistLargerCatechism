# Q73: Can true believers fall from their state of grace?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q73 is a remarkably faithful exposition of the doctrine of perseverance as taught in the 1689 Confession (Chapter 17) and the Baptist Shorter Catechism (Q79). It closely mirrors the WLC Q79 in both structure and substance, demonstrating strong solidarity. The BLC preserves all essential grounds of perseverance and faithfully renders the "neither totally nor finally" formula from the confessional tradition.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** The Shorter Catechism does not have a standalone question on perseverance. The relevant content appears in Q79:

> Q. What are the benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification?
> A. The benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification, are assurance of God's love, peace of conscience (Rom. 5:1, 2, 5), joy in the Holy Spirit (Rom. 5:5, 17), increase of grace (Pr. 4:18), and perseverance therein to the end (1 John 5:13; 1 Pet. 1:5).

- Omissions: None of substance. The Shorter simply lists perseverance as one benefit among several; the BLC expands this into a full treatment with grounds — which is precisely the function of a Larger Catechism.
- Structural changes: The Shorter treats perseverance as a benefit flowing from the application of redemption. The BLC treats it as a standalone doctrine with theological grounds, which is appropriate expansion for a larger catechism format and consistent with the Shorter's affirmation.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 17 — Of Perseverance of the Saints

Paragraph 1:
> "Those whom God hath accepted in the beloved, effectually called and Sanctified by his Spirit, and given the precious faith of his Elect unto, can neither totally nor finally fall from the state of grace; but shall certainly persevere therein to the end and be eternally saved, seeing the gifts and callings of God are without Repentance, (whence he still begets and nourisheth in them Faith, Repentance, Love, Joy, Hope, and all the graces of the Spirit unto immortality) and though many storms and floods arise and beat against them, yet they shall never be able to take them off that foundation and rock which by faith they are fastned upon..."

Paragraph 2:
> "This perseverance of the Saints depends not upon their own free will; but upon the immutability of the decree of Election flowing from the free and unchangeable love of God the Father; upon the efficacy of the merit and intercession of Jesus Christ and Union with him, the oath of God, the abiding of his Spirit & the seed of God within them, and the nature of the Covenant of Grace from all which ariseth also the certainty and infallibility thereof."

Paragraph 3:
> "And though they may through the temptation of Satan and of the world, the prevalency of corruption remaining in them, and the neglect of means of their preservation fall into grievous sins, and for a time continue therein; whereby they incur Gods displeasure, and grieve his holy Spirit, come to have their graces and comforts impaired have their hearts hardened, and their Consciences wounded, hurt, and scandalize others, and bring temporal judgements upon themselves: yet they shall renew their repentance and be preserved through faith in Christ Jesus to the end."

- Omissions: The BLC does not include the Confession's extensive treatment of how believers may fall into grievous sins and the consequences thereof (paragraph 3), nor the elaborate description of subjects in paragraph 1 ("accepted in the beloved, effectually called and Sanctified by his Spirit, and given the precious faith of his Elect"). However, these are matters of scope rather than omission of core doctrine — a catechism answer appropriately condenses confessional content. The essential grounds from paragraph 2 are all present.
- Structural changes: None that alter meaning. The BLC organizes the grounds of perseverance in a clear enumerated sequence that faithfully tracks the Confession's paragraph 2.
- The BLC's phrase "God's unchangeable love" corresponds to the Confession's "free and unchangeable love of God the Father."
- "His decree and covenant to grant them perseverance" corresponds to "immutability of the decree of Election... the nature of the Covenant of Grace."
- "Their inseparable union with Christ" corresponds to "Union with him."
- "His continual intercession for them" corresponds to "efficacy of the merit and intercession of Jesus Christ."
- "The presence of His Spirit and seed of God abiding in them" corresponds to "the abiding of his Spirit & the seed of God within them."
- "Preserved by the power of God through faith unto salvation" corresponds to "kept by the power of God unto Salvation" (paragraph 1) and "preserved through faith in Christ Jesus to the end" (paragraph 3).
- The Confession's mention of "the oath of God" (from Hebrews 6:17-18) is not explicitly present in the BLC, though it may be subsumed under "decree and covenant." This is a very minor point as the oath and covenant are closely related concepts.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q79:

> Q. 79. May not true believers, by reason of their imperfections, and the many temptations and sins they are overtaken with, fall away from the state of grace?
> A. True believers, by reason of the unchangeable love of God, and his decree and covenant to give them perseverance, their inseparable union with Christ, his continual intercession for them, and the Spirit and seed of God abiding in them, can neither totally nor finally fall away from the state of grace, but are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation.

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC is essentially a modernized rendering of the WLC answer with only minor verbal differences.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None in this question. Perseverance is an area of full Baptist-Presbyterian agreement.
- Unexpected divergences: The BLC question form ("Can true believers fall from their state of grace?") is simpler than the WLC's more elaborate question ("May not true believers, by reason of their imperfections, and the many temptations and sins they are overtaken with, fall away from the state of grace?"). The WLC question explicitly names the grounds for the objection (imperfections, temptations, sins), which sets up the pastoral force of the answer. The BLC's shorter question is a reasonable modernization but loses some rhetorical nuance.
- Key phrases dropped: The WLC question's "by reason of their imperfections, and the many temptations and sins they are overtaken with" is omitted from the BLC question. In the answer itself, the BLC faithfully preserves virtually all WLC content:
  - "unchangeable love of God" — preserved
  - "decree and covenant to give them perseverance" — preserved (minor rewording: "grant" for "give")
  - "inseparable union with Christ" — preserved
  - "continual intercession for them" — preserved
  - "Spirit and seed of God abiding in them" — preserved (BLC adds "presence of His" before "Spirit")
  - "neither totally nor finally fall away/out of" — preserved ("fall out of" vs. "fall away from" — equivalent)
  - "kept/preserved by the power of God through faith unto salvation" — preserved ("preserved" for "kept")

## Detailed Notes
- The BLC's replacement of "fall away from the state of grace" with "fall out of their state of grace" is a trivial verbal difference with no theological significance.
- The BLC adds "His" before several clauses (e.g., "His decree," "His continual intercession"), making the divine agency more explicit — this is a slight improvement in clarity.
- The BLC's use of "preserved" rather than "kept" (1 Peter 1:5 uses φρουρουμένους, which can be rendered either way) is faithful.
- The addition of "the presence of" before "His Spirit" is a minor expansion that doesn't alter the theological content.
- The BLC assigns individual proof texts to each ground of perseverance, which is more granular than the WLC's numbered footnotes. The proof texts align well with both the WLC footnotes and the 1689 Confession's citations.

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are well-chosen and closely mirror those of the WLC:
- **Jeremiah 31:3** (unchangeable love) — matches WLC footnote 342
- **2 Timothy 2:19; Hebrews 13:20-21; 2 Samuel 23:5** (decree and covenant) — matches WLC footnote 343
- **1 Corinthians 1:8-9** (union with Christ) — matches WLC footnote 344
- **Hebrews 7:25; Luke 22:32** (continual intercession) — matches WLC footnote 345
- **1 John 3:9; 1 John 2:27** (Spirit and seed of God) — matches WLC footnote 346
- **Jeremiah 32:40; John 10:28** (neither totally nor finally) — matches WLC footnote 347
- **1 Peter 1:5** (preserved by the power of God) — matches WLC footnote 348

The proof text alignment is exact — every WLC proof text for Q79 is preserved in the BLC. This is the strongest possible indicator of deliberate WLC solidarity.
