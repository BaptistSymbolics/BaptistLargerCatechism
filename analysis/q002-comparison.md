# Q2: Ought everyone to believe that there is a God?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
The Larger Catechism faithfully expands the Shorter Catechism's answer by adding two theological reasons for why everyone ought to believe in God: (1) the being of God is the foundation of all practical religion, and (2) glorifying God is the chief purpose of man's creation. These expansions are well grounded in the 1689 Confession and supported by appropriate proof texts. The core assertion and its negative corollary ("great sin and folly") are preserved intact.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** Q2 — "Ought every one to believe there is a God? A. Everyone ought to believe there is a God (Heb. 11:6); and it is their great sin and folly who do not (Ps. 14:1)."

The Larger Catechism preserves the Shorter's entire substance:
- The affirmation that everyone ought to believe there is a God is retained verbatim.
- The negative judgment ("great sin and folly") is retained, though the Larger separates "sin" and "folly" into distinct clauses with separate proof texts rather than combining them.
- Hebrews 11:6 is retained as a primary proof text.
- Psalm 14:1 is retained (with Psalm 53:1 added as its near-duplicate).

The Larger expands the Shorter by supplying two reasons for the obligation:
1. **"The being of God is the foundation of all practical religion"** — This is a theological rationale not present in the Shorter but fully consistent with it. The Shorter assumes this without stating it.
2. **"The chief and highest purpose for which man was created"** — This connects Q2 to the Shorter's own Q1 ("What is the chief end of man? ... to glorify God and enjoy Him forever"), making explicit what the Shorter leaves implicit.

These are legitimate expansions appropriate for a "larger" catechism: they explain *why* the obligation exists rather than merely asserting it.

**Proof text differences:**
- The Shorter cites only Heb. 11:6 and Ps. 14:1.
- The Larger adds: Rom. 10:14 (necessity of belief for calling on God), Rom. 11:36; 1 Cor. 6:20; 1 Cor. 10:31; Ps. 86:9, 12 (God's glory as purpose of creation/life), Jer. 4:22; 1 Cor. 15:34 (sin of not knowing God), and Ps. 53:1 (parallel to Ps. 14:1).

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 1, Paragraph 1; Chapter 2, Paragraphs 1–2; Chapter 22, Paragraph 1

**Chapter 1.1** teaches that "the light of Nature, and the works of Creation and Providence do so far manifest the goodness, wisdom and power of God, as to leave men unexcusable." This aligns with the Larger Catechism's assertion that everyone ought to believe there is a God — the Confession grounds this obligation in natural revelation, while the catechism focuses on the theological and moral reasons for the obligation. These are complementary, not contradictory.

**Chapter 2.1** describes God as working all things "for his own glory" (citing Rom. 11.36), which directly supports the Larger's claim that God's glory is the chief purpose of creation. The Confession's citation of Hebrews 11:6 in this chapter ("the rewarder of them that diligently seek him") also matches the Larger's use of the same text.

**Chapter 2.2** states that God is "the alone fountain of all Being, of whom, through whom, and to whom are all things" (citing Rom. 11:34-36), and that "to him is due from Angels and men, whatsoever worship, service, or obedience as Creatures they owe unto the Creator." This strongly supports the Larger's claim that believing in God is foundational to all practical religion and is man's chief purpose.

**Chapter 22.1** states: "The light of Nature shews that there is a God, who hath Lordship, and Soveraigntye over all; is just, good, and doth good unto all; and is therefore to be feared, loved, praised, called upon, trusted in, and served, with all the Heart, and all the Soul, and with all the Might." This directly parallels the Larger's assertion that belief in God is the foundation of all practical religion — the Confession teaches that natural knowledge of God's existence grounds all duties of worship.

The Larger Catechism does not contradict, narrow, or go beyond the Confession on any point. Its two supplied reasons (foundation of practical religion; chief purpose of man) are well-attested in the confessional text.

## Detailed Notes
- The Larger's phrase "the being of God is the foundation of all practical religion" is a well-chosen theological summary. It echoes Hebrews 11:6 ("he that cometh to God must believe that he is") and draws out the logical implication: without belief in God's existence, no worship, prayer, or obedience is possible.
- The separation of "sin" and "folly" into distinct clauses with separate proof texts is a helpful pedagogical expansion. The Shorter combines them; the Larger distinguishes them, assigning Jeremiah 4:22 and 1 Corinthians 15:34 to the moral dimension (sin of not knowing God) and Psalms 14:1/53:1 to the intellectual dimension (folly of denying God).
- The phrase "chief and highest purpose for which man was created" effectively cross-references the catechism's own Q1 and the Confession's teaching on creation for God's glory (Chapter 4.1: "for the manifestation of the glory of his eternal power, wisdom, and goodness").

## Scripture Analysis
- **Hebrews 11:6** — Shared with the Shorter. Core text: "he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him." Also cited in 1689 Confession 2.1. Excellent and appropriate use.
- **Romans 10:14** — New in the Larger. "How shall they call on him in whom they have not believed?" This supports the "foundation of all practical religion" claim by showing that belief in God's existence is logically prior to prayer and worship. Good addition.
- **Romans 11:36** — New in the Larger, but cited in 1689 Confession 2.1–2. "For of him, and through him, and to him, are all things: to whom be glory for ever." Supports the purpose-of-creation claim.
- **1 Corinthians 6:20; 10:31** — New in the Larger. Both teach that man exists to glorify God. Appropriate and well-chosen.
- **Psalm 86:9, 12** — New in the Larger. Teaches that all nations shall worship and glorify God. Supports the universality of the obligation.
- **Jeremiah 4:22; 1 Corinthians 15:34** — New in the Larger. Both address the sinfulness of not knowing God. Appropriate for the "great sin" clause.
- **Psalm 14:1; 53:1** — Shared with the Shorter (Ps. 14:1). These twin psalms both declare "The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God." Adding Ps. 53:1 alongside 14:1 is a natural and unobjectionable expansion.
- No proof texts appear misused or taken out of context.
