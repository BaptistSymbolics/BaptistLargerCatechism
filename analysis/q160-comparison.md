# Q160: What is the visible church?

## Verdict: SIGNIFICANT CONCERN

## Summary
The BLC answer introduces a marks-of-the-church definition ("wherein the gospel is truly preached and the sacraments of baptism and Lord's Supper are rightly administered") that is absent from all three standards and shifts the definition from the people who compose the church to the activities that characterize it. Meanwhile, it omits the 1689's key qualifications about visible sainthood—professing "the faith of the Gospel, and obedience unto God by Christ" while "not destroying their own profession by any Errors everting the foundation, or unholyness of conversation"—and replaces the WLC's universal ecclesiology ("a society made up of all such as in all ages and places") with the more restrictive "organized gathering," which alters the theological scope.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** No direct parallel. The Baptist Shorter Catechism (1813 Charleston) does not include a question defining the visible church. The closest related material is:
- Q. "What is the duty of such who are rightly baptized?" A. "It is the duty of such who are rightly baptized to give up themselves to some particular and orderly church of Jesus Christ, that they may walk in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless" (Acts 2:41, 42; 5:13, 14; 9:26; 1 Pet. 2:5; Lk. 1:6).
- Q. "What are the outward means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption?" A. "The outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption are his ordinances, especially the word, baptism, the Lord's supper, and prayer; all which means are made effectual to the elect for salvation" (Mt. 28:19, 20; Acts 2:42, 46, 47).

- Omissions: N/A (no direct parallel to compare)
- Structural changes: N/A
- Note: Since the BSC does not define the visible church, the BLC here is an expansion without a direct Shorter baseline. This makes the 1689 Confession the primary standard for evaluating this answer.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter XXVI, Paragraphs 2, 5-6

Paragraph 2: "All persons throughout the world, professing the faith of the Gospel, and obedience unto God by Christ, according unto it; not destroying their own profession by any Errors everting the foundation, or unholyness of conversation, are and may be called visible Saints; and of such ought all particular Congregations to be constituted."

Paragraph 5: "In the execution of this power wherewith he is so intrusted, the Lord Jesus calleth out of the World unto himself, through the Ministry of his word, by his Spirit, those that are given unto him by his Father; that they may walk before him in all the ways of obedience, which he prescribeth to them in his Word. Those thus called he commandeth to walk together in particular societies, or Churches, for their mutual edification; and the due performance of that publick worship, which he requireth of them in the World."

Paragraph 6: "The Members of these Churches are Saints by calling, visibly manifesting and evidencing (in and by their profession and walking) their obedience unto that call of Christ; and do willingly consent to walk together according to the appointment of Christ, giving up themselves, to the Lord & one to another by the will of God, in professed subjection to the Ordinances of the Gospel."

- Omissions:
  1. **"Professing the faith of the Gospel, and obedience unto God by Christ"** — The 1689 defines visible saints by a double profession: faith in the gospel AND obedience unto God by Christ. The BLC reduces this to merely "professing believers," which drops the explicit requirement of professed obedience.
  2. **"Not destroying their own profession by any Errors everting the foundation, or unholyness of conversation"** — This critical negative qualification is entirely absent from the BLC. The 1689 teaches that profession alone is insufficient; it must not be contradicted by fundamental doctrinal error or unholy living. This is a significant omission.
  3. **"Visible Saints"** — The 1689's precise terminology "visible Saints" is dropped. The BLC uses "professing believers" which carries a weaker connotation—one can profess belief without manifesting sainthood.
  4. **"Willingly consent to walk together"** — The voluntary covenantal character of church membership (1689 26.6) is absent.
  5. **"Mutual edification" and "due performance of publick worship"** — The 1689's purposes for gathering (26.5) are replaced by marks-of-the-church language.

- Structural changes:
  1. **People → Activities**: The 1689 defines the visible church in terms of the *people* who compose it (visible saints professing faith and obedience). The BLC shifts the definition to the *activities* that characterize a church (preaching the gospel and administering sacraments). This is a fundamental reorientation from a confessional Baptist ecclesiology (who the members are) to a more Reformed/Protestant marks-of-the-church ecclesiology (what the institution does).
  2. **"Organized gathering" vs. universal scope**: The 1689 speaks of "all persons throughout the world" who meet the criteria—a universal category. The BLC's "organized gathering" implies a local institutional entity, which confuses the visible church as a theological category with the local congregation.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):** WLC Q62: "What is the visible church? A. The visible church is a society made up of all such as in all ages and places of the world do profess the true religion, and of their children."

- Solidarity: WEAK
- Intentional Baptist divergences:
  1. **"and of their children"** — Correctly omitted. This reflects the Baptist rejection of paedobaptist ecclesiology. Expected and acceptable.
- Unexpected divergences:
  1. **"a society made up of all such as... do profess the true religion"** — The WLC defines the visible church as a *society* composed of *persons* who profess the true religion. The BLC replaces this person-centered definition with an activity-centered definition about preaching and sacraments. This is not a Baptist-Presbyterian difference; it is an unexpected ecclesiological shift.
  2. **"in all ages and places of the world"** — The WLC's universalizing language ("of the world") emphasizes the visible church as a worldwide, trans-historical reality. The BLC includes "in all ages and places" but applies it to "the organized gathering" rather than to the persons, which subtly narrows the scope.
  3. **"profess the true religion"** — This comprehensive WLC phrase (encompassing doctrine AND life) is replaced by "professing believers," which is narrower. The WLC phrase implies profession of the whole true religion, not merely belief.
- Key phrases dropped:
  1. **"a society made up of all such as"** — The compositional, person-centered structure.
  2. **"profess the true religion"** — Replaced with the weaker "professing believers."
  3. **"of the world"** — The explicit universalizing scope.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Marks-of-the-church language is novel**: The phrase "wherein the gospel is truly preached and the sacraments of baptism and Lord's Supper are rightly administered" echoes the Belgic Confession (Art. 29) and the Augsburg Confession (Art. VII), not the 1689 or WLC. Neither the 1689, the BSC, nor the WLC uses marks-of-the-church language in defining the visible church. This is a significant theological import from a different confessional tradition that does not appear in any of the BLC's authoritative standards.

2. **The term "sacraments"**: While the 1689 does use "Sacraments" in Chapter 28 paragraph 2, Baptist catechetical tradition has often preferred "ordinances." The BLC's use of "sacraments" is not itself problematic but is worth noting.

3. **"Organized gathering" is restrictive**: The 1689 speaks of "all persons throughout the world" meeting certain criteria, and then says "of such ought all particular Congregations to be constituted." The 1689 distinguishes between the universal visible church (all visible saints everywhere) and particular congregations. The BLC's "organized gathering" collapses this distinction.

4. **Comparison with BLC Q161**: The BLC Q161 (privileges of the visible church) faithfully follows the WLC Q63, including mentioning "the ordinary means by which people are saved" and gospel ministry. This makes Q160's introduction of marks-of-the-church language somewhat redundant—the preaching and sacraments belong more naturally to the *privileges* of the church (Q161) than to its *definition* (Q160).

5. **The 1689's negative qualification matters theologically**: By omitting "not destroying their own profession by any Errors everting the foundation, or unholyness of conversation," the BLC loses the Confession's teaching that mere profession without doctrinal integrity and holy living is insufficient for visible church membership. This has practical implications for church discipline and membership standards.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Acts 2:42** — "And they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and the prayers." This supports church activities but is primarily descriptive of church practice, not definitional of what makes the visible church.
- **Acts 20:7** — "On the first day of the week, when we were gathered together to break bread..." Describes a local gathering practice. Supports "organized gathering" language but not the universal visible church concept.
- **Acts 7:38** — Stephen's reference to Moses and "the congregation in the wilderness." This supports the trans-historical scope of the visible church but is used in a very different context (Old Testament Israel as church).
- **Ephesians 4:11-12** — "And he gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the shepherds and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ." Supports church offices and ministry but is about the gifts Christ gives to the church, not a definition of the visible church's composition.
- Overall, the proof texts tend to support church *activities and practices* rather than the *definition* of the visible church in terms of its membership. The WLC's proof texts (1 Cor. 1:2; Acts 11:26; Rom. 1:7) focus more appropriately on the *persons* who compose the visible church. The BLC's proof-text selection reflects its activity-centered rather than person-centered definition.
