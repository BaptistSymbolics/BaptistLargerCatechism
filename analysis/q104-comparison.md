# Q104: What is required in the third commandment?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
The BLC faithfully expands the Shorter Catechism's treatment of the third commandment in close structural solidarity with WLC Q112. However, it replaces the WLC's precise phrase "holy profession, and answerable conversation" with the looser paraphrase "holy affirmations of our faith and conduct that matches our affirmations," and substitutes "attributes" (Shorter) / "attributes" (WLC) with "qualities," which is a less precise theological term. The BLC also replaces "ordinances" (Shorter/WLC) with "regulations," a term that loses the specific liturgical connotation. These substitutions weaken terminological precision without clear theological gain.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. What is required in the third commandment?
> A. The third commandment requireth the holy and reverent use of God's names (Mt. 6:9; Deut. 23:58), titles (Ps. 68:4), attributes (Rev. 15:3, 4), ordinances, (Mal. 1:11, 14), word (Ps. 136:1, 2) and works (Job 36:24).

- **Omissions:** None of substance. The Shorter's core list (names, titles, attributes, ordinances, word, works) is preserved, though with terminological substitutions (see below).
- **Structural changes:** The BLC rearranges the order of items and separates the list into multiple sections. Where the Shorter gives a compact list, the BLC expands into detailed subsections with proof texts for each item. The rearrangement does not alter the theological relationships.
- **Terminological substitutions:**
  - "attributes" → "qualities" — "Attributes" is the standard Reformed theological term for the perfections of God. "Qualities" is vaguer and could apply to creatures. This is a weakening of precision.
  - "ordinances" → "regulations" — The Shorter's "ordinances" has a specific liturgical/worship connotation (God's appointed ordinances of worship). "Regulations" loses that specificity and sounds more administrative/legal.
- **Expansions:** The BLC appropriately expands the Shorter by adding "sacraments and prayer," "oaths and vows," "casting lots," and the concluding application regarding holy profession and conduct — all drawn from the WLC's fuller treatment.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 22, Paragraph 1:
> "The light of Nature shews that there is a God, who hath Lordship, and Soveraigntye over all; is just, good, and doth good unto all; and is therefore to be feared, loved, praised, called upon, trusted in, and served, with all the Heart, and all the Soul, and with all the Might. But the acceptable way of Worshipping the true God, is instituted by himself; and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be Worshipped according to the imaginations, and devices of Men..."

Chapter 22, Paragraph 5:
> "The reading of the Scriptures, Preaching, and hearing the word of God, teaching and admonishing one another in Psalms, Hymns and Spiritual songs, singing with grace in our Hearts to the Lord; as also the Administration of Baptism, and the Lords Supper are all parts of Religious worship of God, to be performed in obedience to him, with understanding, faith, reverence, and godly fear..."

Chapter 23, Paragraphs 1-2 (Of Lawful Oaths and Vows):
> "A lawful Oath is a part of religious worship, wherein the person swearing in Truth, Righteousness, and Judgement, solemnly calleth God to witness what he sweareth..."
> "The Name of God only is that by which men ought to swear; and therein it is to be used, with all Holy Fear and reverence..."

Chapter 23, Paragraph 5:
> "A Vow which is not to be made to any Creature, but to God alone, is to be made and performed with all Religious care, and faithfulness..."

- **Omissions:** No significant doctrinal omissions from the 1689. The BLC appropriately touches on worship, the word, sacraments, prayer, oaths, and vows — all elements addressed in Chapters 22-23.
- **Structural changes:** None of concern. The BLC condenses material from two chapters into a catechetical answer, which is appropriate.
- **Agreement:** The BLC's treatment of oaths and vows as part of the reverent use of God's name aligns well with the 1689's positioning of oaths as "part of religious worship" (Ch. 23.1). The BLC's emphasis on "sacraments and prayer" reflects the 1689's listing of Baptism, the Lord's Supper, and prayer as parts of religious worship (Ch. 22.5).
- **Note:** The 1689 emphasizes that oaths must be taken "with all Holy Fear and reverence" and that vows must be performed "with all Religious care, and faithfulness." The BLC simply lists "oaths and vows" without this qualifying language, but this is acceptable in a catechetical format where the positive requirement ("holy and reverent use") governs the entire answer.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

> Q. 112. What is required in the third commandment?
> A. The third commandment requires, That the name of God, his titles, attributes, ordinances, the Word, sacraments, prayer, oaths, vows, lots, his works, and whatsoever else there is whereby he makes himself known, be holily and reverently used in thought, meditation, word, and writing; by an holy profession, and answerable conversation, to the glory of God, and the good of ourselves, and others.

- **Solidarity:** STRONG — The BLC closely follows the WLC's structure and content, preserving the same enumerated list, the same modes of use (thought, meditation, word, writing), and the same concluding teleological framework (glory of God, good of ourselves and others).
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** None apparent. This question does not touch on baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology.
- **Unexpected divergences:**
  - "attributes" → "qualities" — No theological reason to depart from the standard Reformed term shared by both the Shorter and WLC. This is an unexpected and unnecessary substitution.
  - "ordinances" → "regulations" — Similarly, "ordinances" is the term used in both the Shorter and WLC. "Regulations" loses the specific worship/liturgical connotation.
  - "sacraments" → "sacraments" — Retained without change, which is notable since some Baptist catechisms prefer "ordinances" for baptism and the Lord's Supper. Here the BLC follows the WLC directly.
- **Key phrases dropped:**
  - "holy profession, and answerable conversation" → "holy affirmations of our faith and conduct that matches our affirmations" — The WLC's phrase "holy profession" carries the weight of a public confession of faith, and "answerable conversation" means a manner of life that corresponds to that profession. The BLC's "holy affirmations of our faith" captures the idea of profession but "affirmations" is a weaker term. "Conduct that matches our affirmations" is a reasonable modernization of "answerable conversation," though "answerable" has a covenantal directness that "matches" softens.
  - The WLC's phrasing "whatsoever else there is whereby he makes himself known" is preserved in the BLC as "anything else by which He makes Himself known" — a faithful modernization.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Qualities" vs. "Attributes"**: This is the most notable terminological concern. "Attributes" is the received theological term in Reformed theology for the divine perfections (simplicity, immutability, omnipotence, etc.). The WLC, the Shorter, and the 1689 all use "attributes" (or in the Shorter's case, "attributes" is the standard reading). "Qualities" is a philosophically weaker term — qualities can be accidental or non-essential, while attributes in theological usage denotes essential, necessary perfections. For a catechism intended to exposit the 1689, departing from the standard terminology without cause is a precision loss.

2. **"Regulations" vs. "Ordinances"**: "Ordinances" in the Reformed theological vocabulary has a dual meaning — both the appointed ordinances of worship (preaching, sacraments, prayer) and the broader divine appointments. "Regulations" suggests rules or laws, which is a narrower and more administrative meaning. The BLC then separately lists "word, sacraments and prayer," which in the WLC and Shorter fall under the umbrella of "ordinances." This creates a mild redundancy: "regulations" partially overlaps with the subsequent listing of specific ordinances.

3. **Structural reordering**: The BLC moves "thought, meditations, words, and writing" to the beginning of the answer rather than positioning it after the full enumeration as in the WLC. This front-loads the modes of use before listing what is to be reverently used, creating a slightly different emphasis but not altering the theological content.

4. **"Sacraments" retained**: Interestingly, the BLC retains "sacraments" rather than substituting "ordinances" — even though Baptists often prefer "ordinances" for the rites of baptism and the Lord's Supper. This shows fidelity to the WLC's language in this context, though it could be confusing given that the BLC has already used "regulations" where the WLC has "ordinances."

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC's proof texts are generally well-chosen and align with the WLC's citations. Key observations:
  - **Malachi 1:14; Ecclesiastes 5:1** for "regulations" — These verses concern the reverencing of God's appointed worship, supporting the idea that "ordinances" would have been the more natural term.
  - **Psalm 138:2** for "word" — Good parallel to WLC's Word citations.
  - **1 Corinthians 11:24-25, 28-29** for "sacraments" — Specific Lord's Supper texts, appropriate.
  - **1 Peter 3:15; Philippians 1:27** for the holy profession/conduct section — 1 Peter 3:15 ("always being prepared to make a defense") supports "affirmations of our faith," and Philippians 1:27 ("let your manner of life be worthy of the gospel") supports "conduct that matches our affirmations." These are solid modernizing choices.
  - **Acts 1:24-26** for "casting lots" — Same Matthias passage used by the WLC.
  - **Jeremiah 32:39** for "the good of ourselves and others" — An interesting addition not in the WLC's citations; it concerns God giving his people "one heart and one way" for their good and their children's good.
  - The proof texts for section 1 (Malachi 3:16; Psalm 8; Colossians 3:17; Psalm 105:2,5; Psalm 102:18) cover the modes of reverent use well, with Psalm 102:18 specifically supporting "writing."
