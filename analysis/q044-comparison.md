# Q44: How does Christ execute the office of a priest?

## Verdict: CONSISTENT

## Summary
BLC Q44 faithfully combines the content of both the Shorter Catechism and the Westminster Larger Catechism into a single, enriched answer. It retains the Shorter's "satisfy divine justice" while also incorporating the WLC's "sacrifice without spot to God," producing a comprehensive statement that is well-grounded in 1689 Chapter 8.5. No key doctrinal elements are omitted; the BLC achieves a genuine synthesis of its sources.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. How doth Christ execute the office of a priest? A. Christ executeth the office of priest in his once offering up himself a sacrifice to satisfy divine justice (Heb. 9:14, 28) and reconcile us to God (Heb. 2:17), and in making continual intercession for us (Heb.7:24, 25)."

- Omissions: None. The Shorter's key phrase "satisfy divine justice" is preserved in the BLC.
- Structural changes: The BLC reorders slightly to place "satisfy divine justice" and "reconciliation for the sins of His people" as coordinate purposes, both governed by the sacrificial offering. This preserves the Shorter's theological content while incorporating additional WLC language.
- The Shorter says "reconcile us to God"; the BLC says "to be reconciliation for the sins of His people" — following the WLC's phrasing. Both express the same doctrine; the BLC's version more precisely echoes Hebrews 2:17 ("to make reconciliation for the sins of the people").
- The Shorter says "making continual intercession for us"; the BLC says "making continual intercession for His people" — again following the WLC's "them/people" phrasing rather than "us." Both are doctrinally equivalent.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** Chapter 8, Paragraph 5 — "The Lord Jesus by his perfect obedience and sacrifice of himself, which he through the Eternal Spirit once offered up unto God, hath fully satisfied the Justice of God, procured reconciliation, and purchased an Everlasting inheritance in the Kingdom of Heaven, for all those whom the Father hath given unto him."

Chapter 8, Paragraph 4 — "...and there sitteth at the right hand of his Father, making intercession."

Chapter 8, Paragraph 10 — "...in respect of our alienation from God, and imperfection of the best of our services, we need his Priestly office, to reconcile us, and present us acceptable unto God."

- Omissions: The 1689 mentions "perfect obedience" alongside "sacrifice of himself" (8.5), and the BLC does not include Christ's active obedience here. However, the Shorter Catechism likewise does not include it in the priestly office question, focusing only on sacrifice and intercession. The BLC appropriately follows the catechetical tradition rather than attempting to compress all of 8.5 into this answer.
- The 1689's "purchased an Everlasting inheritance in the Kingdom of Heaven" (8.5) is not reflected in the BLC, but again this is not part of either the Shorter or the WLC's treatment of the priestly office question. This material is better suited to questions on redemption applied.
- Structural changes: None of concern. The BLC's three elements — sacrifice without spot, satisfaction of divine justice/reconciliation, and intercession — map directly onto 8.5 and 8.4.
- The 1689's "fully satisfied the Justice of God" is preserved as "satisfy divine justice." The 1689's "procured reconciliation" is preserved as "to be reconciliation for the sins of His people."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q44: "How doth Christ execute the office of a priest? A. Christ executeth the office of a priest, in his once offering himself a sacrifice without spot to God, to be reconciliation for the sins of his people; and in making continual intercession for them."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC is nearly verbatim with the WLC, adding only the Shorter's "satisfy divine justice" clause.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This doctrine has no baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology divergence points.
- Unexpected divergences: None. The BLC follows the WLC extremely closely.
- Key phrases dropped: None. Every phrase from the WLC is preserved in the BLC: "sacrifice without spot to God," "reconciliation for the sins of his people," "making continual intercession."

## Detailed Notes
The BLC achieves something commendable here: it merges the strengths of both its Baptist source (the Shorter Catechism) and the WLC into a richer answer than either alone. The Shorter Catechism contains "satisfy divine justice" but lacks "without spot"; the WLC contains "without spot" but lacks "satisfy divine justice." The BLC includes both, and both are grounded in the 1689 Confession (8.5).

The shift from first-person pronouns ("us") to third-person ("His people") follows the WLC pattern consistently. This is a stylistic difference without theological consequence — both refer to the elect for whom Christ mediates.

The BLC's proof texts are well chosen:
- Hebrews 9:14, 28 and 10:12 for the sacrifice — expanding the Shorter's citations
- Isaiah 53 added for the sacrificial offering — appropriate OT background
- Hebrews 2:17 for reconciliation — shared with both Shorter and WLC
- 2 Corinthians 5:18 and Colossians 1:21-22 added for reconciliation — strengthening the reconciliation theme
- Hebrews 7:25 and 9:24 for intercession — shared with both sources

## Scripture Analysis
The BLC's proof texts are a superset of those in the Shorter and WLC, with appropriate additions:
- **Hebrews 9:14, 28** — shared across all three sources; foundational for "sacrifice without spot" and "once offered"
- **Hebrews 10:12** — BLC addition; reinforces the once-for-all nature of the sacrifice ("one sacrifice for sins forever")
- **Isaiah 53** — BLC addition; broad OT reference to the suffering servant as sacrifice, well-suited to the priestly office
- **Hebrews 2:17** — shared across all three; "to make reconciliation for the sins of the people"
- **2 Corinthians 5:18** — BLC addition; "God, who hath reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ"
- **Colossians 1:21-22** — BLC addition; reconciliation through Christ's physical body through death
- **Hebrews 7:25** — shared across all three; "he ever liveth to make intercession for them"
- **Hebrews 9:24** — BLC addition (also in 1689 8.4 proof texts); Christ appears in the presence of God for us — strengthens the intercession theme

All proof text additions are appropriate and well-grounded in the doctrinal content of the answer.
