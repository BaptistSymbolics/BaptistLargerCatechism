# Q119: What are the particular sins of those in authority?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q119 closely follows WLC Q130 in structure, content, and proof texts, providing a faithful modernization of the Westminster treatment of the sins of superiors under the fifth commandment. The core theological content is preserved. However, several WLC terms are narrowed rather than merely modernized: "favouring" evil becomes "rewarding" evil, and "discountenancing" good becomes "not rewarding" good — both reductions from broader concepts to more specific ones. The phrase "inordinate seeking of themselves" is also collapsed into the adjacent concept.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
The Baptist Shorter Catechism does not have a direct parallel that specifically addresses the sins of those in authority. The closest questions are:

Q: "What is required in the fifth commandment?"
A: "The fifth commandment requireth the preserving the honour and performing the duties belonging to every one in their several places and relations, as superiors (Eph. 5:21), inferiors (1 Pet. 2:17), or equals (Rom. 12:10)."

Q: "What is forbidden in the fifth commandment?"
A: "The fifth commandment forbiddeth the neglect of, or doing any thing against the honour and duty which belongeth to every one in their several places and relations (Mt. 15:4-6; Ez. 34:24; Rom. 13:8)."

- Omissions: N/A — The Shorter Catechism treats the fifth commandment at a general level without distinguishing the specific sins of superiors, inferiors, and equals. BLC Q119 is a legitimate expansion of the Shorter's general principles.
- Structural changes: None. BLC Q119 appropriately elaborates what the Shorter treats summarily.
- The BLC's opening phrase "In addition to failing to do what is required of them" properly references the general duty framework of the Shorter Catechism.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):** The 1689 does not contain a chapter specifically addressing the sins of those in authority under the fifth commandment. The most relevant material is:

Chapter 24 (Of the Civil Magistrate), Paragraph 1: "God the supream Lord, and King of all the World, hath ordained Civil Magistrates to be under him, over the people for his own glory, and the publick good; and to this end hath armed them with the power of the Sword, for defence and encouragement of them that do good, and for the punishment of evil doers."

Chapter 24, Paragraph 3: "Civil Magistrates being set up by God, for the ends aforesaid; subjection in all lawful things commanded by them, ought to be yeilded by us, in the Lord; not only for wrath but for Conscience sake."

Chapter 19 (Of the Law of God), Paragraph 5: "The moral Law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof, and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator; who gave it."

- Omissions: None. The 1689 does not elaborate the fifth commandment's application to superiors in the way a catechism does; BLC Q119 fills this gap appropriately.
- Structural changes: None.
- The 1689's emphasis that authority is ordained "for his own glory, and the publick good" is consistent with BLC's identification of self-serving use of authority as sinful. The 1689's qualification that subjection is owed "in all lawful things" is the mirror of BLC's point that commanding unlawful things is a sin of superiors.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
WLC Q130: "What are the sins of superiors?"
"The sins of superiors are, besides the neglect of the duties required of them, and inordinate seeking of themselves, their own glory, ease, profit, or pleasure; commanding things unlawful, or not in the power of inferiors to perform; counseling, encouraging, or favouring them in that which is evil; dissuading, discouraging, or discountenancing them in that which is good; correcting them unduly; careless exposing, or leaving them to wrong, temptation, and danger; provoking them to wrath; or any way dishonouring themselves, or lessening their authority, by an unjust, indiscreet, rigorous, or remiss behaviour."

- Solidarity: **STRONG** — The BLC follows the WLC's structure point by point, preserves the same sequence of sins, and uses substantially overlapping proof texts.
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None. This question does not touch on baptism, ecclesiology, or covenant theology.
- Unexpected divergences:
  1. **"inordinate seeking of themselves" dropped**: The WLC distinguishes between (a) "inordinate seeking of themselves" and (b) seeking "their own glory." The BLC collapses both into "using their position for their own glory," losing the distinct concept of general self-seeking apart from glory-seeking. The WLC's "inordinate seeking of themselves" captures a broader pattern of self-centeredness (cf. Phil. 2:21, "all seek their own"), while the BLC limits it to positional misuse for glory.
  2. **"counseling" → "persuading"**: The WLC uses "counseling" (giving evil counsel/advice); the BLC substitutes "persuading." This is a defensible modernization, though "counseling" and "persuading" have different nuances — counsel can be more subtle and advisory, while persuading implies more active pressure.
  3. **"favouring them in that which is evil" → "rewarding those under them for doing evil"**: This narrows the WLC's concept. "Favouring" encompasses a broader range of partiality toward evil (showing preference, turning a blind eye, giving social approval) than "rewarding," which implies material or tangible compensation. The WLC proof text (1 Sam. 3:13 — Eli failing to restrain his sons) is more naturally described as "favouring" than "rewarding."
  4. **"discountenancing them in that which is good" → "not rewarding them for doing good"**: Similarly narrows the concept. "Discountenancing" means actively showing disapproval or frowning upon good behavior — a stronger and broader concept than merely failing to reward it. The WLC proof texts (John 7:46-49, the Pharisees' contempt for the officers who appreciated Jesus' teaching) illustrate active discountenancing, not merely non-reward.

- Key phrases dropped:
  - "inordinate seeking of themselves" — a distinct concept from glory-seeking
  - "favouring" evil — broader than "rewarding" evil
  - "discountenancing" good — stronger than "not rewarding" good

## Detailed Notes
1. **Terminology shifts that narrow meaning**: The three changes noted above ("favouring" → "rewarding," "discountenancing" → "not rewarding," dropping "inordinate seeking of themselves") share a common pattern: they replace broader, more encompassing WLC terms with narrower, more specific BLC terms. While the BLC terms are valid sins of superiors, they do not capture the full range of the WLC's original concepts. A superior who shows social favor to evildoers but does not materially reward them, or who frowns upon the righteous without withholding tangible rewards, would fall under the WLC's condemnation but potentially escape the BLC's narrower language.

2. **"counseling" → "persuading"**: This is the most defensible change. In modern English, "counseling" has shifted toward therapeutic connotations, making "persuading" a reasonable modernization. However, the WLC proof texts (Matt. 14:8; Mark 6:24 — Herodias advising her daughter) describe counsel/instruction rather than persuasion per se.

3. **Additional proof texts**: The BLC adds several proof texts not in the WLC: Jeremiah 5:30-31 and Jeremiah 6:13-14 for encouraging evil; Ezekiel 13:9-10 for rewarding evil; John 9:28 for discouraging good; Leviticus 19:29 and Isaiah 58:7 for careless exposure to danger. These additions strengthen the scriptural support.

4. **Matthew 23:2-4 addition**: The BLC adds Matthew 23:2-4 alongside the WLC's Exodus 5:10-18 for "commanding things that cannot be accomplished." This is a strong addition, directly illustrating religious leaders binding heavy burdens on others.

5. **The opening framing**: BLC's "In addition to failing to do what is required of them" faithfully modernizes WLC's "besides the neglect of the duties required of them."

6. **Closing clause**: BLC's "anything that dishonors themselves or undermines their authority by being unjust, imprudent, too severe, or lax" is a faithful modernization of WLC's "any way dishonouring themselves, or lessening their authority, by an unjust, indiscreet, rigorous, or remiss behaviour." The equivalences are clear: imprudent = indiscreet, too severe = rigorous, lax = remiss.
