# Q153: How should baptism be administered?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q153 faithfully reproduces the substance of 1689 Confession 28.2 and 29.3-4, and closely mirrors the Shorter Catechism's Q&A on right administration. However, section 1 on who administers baptism derives from 1689 28.2 rather than from the Shorter's parallel question, and the BLC contains a notable textual error ("parson" for "person") that should be corrected. The BLC also drops the Shorter Catechism's explicit anti-sprinkling/pouring clause and the phrase "the whole body," both of which strengthen the distinctively Baptist position on mode.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**
> Q. How is Baptism rightly administered?
> A. Baptism is rightly administered by immersion, or dipping the whole body of the party in water, into the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, according to Christ's institution, and the practice of the apostles (Mt. 3:16; Jn. 3:23; 4:1, 2; Mt. 28:19, 20; Acts 8:38; Rom. 6:4; Col. 2:12), and not by sprinkling or pouring of water, or dipping some part of the body, after the tradition of men.

- **Omissions:**
  - The Shorter specifies "the whole body of the party" — the BLC says only "the parson [person] in water," dropping the explicit "whole body" qualification that strengthens the immersion requirement.
  - The Shorter includes the critical anti-clause: "and not by sprinkling or pouring of water, or dipping some part of the body, after the tradition of men." The BLC omits this entirely. This is a significant Baptist distinctive that the Shorter explicitly preserves.
  - The Shorter includes the phrase "according to Christ's institution, and the practice of the apostles," grounding immersion in both dominical command and apostolic practice. The BLC omits this.
  - The Shorter cites John 4:1-2, which the BLC drops.
- **Structural changes:**
  - The Shorter combines the element (water), the Trinitarian formula, and the mode (immersion) into one unified answer. The BLC splits these across three separate sections, adding a first section on who administers baptism. This fragmentation is not inherently problematic but does break the Shorter's unified treatment of the administration question.
  - The BLC's section 1 addresses who should administer (ministers of the gospel), which the Shorter does not address in this question at all. This content comes from 1689 28.2 and is theologically appropriate, but it represents an expansion rather than a modernization of the Shorter's answer.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section(s):**

Chapter 28, Paragraph 2:
> "These holy appointments are to be administred by those only, who are qualified and thereunto called according to the commission of Christ." (Mat. 28.19. 1 Cor. 4.1.)

Chapter 29, Paragraph 3:
> "The outward element to be used in this ordinance is water, wherein the party is to be baptized, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." (Mat 28.19,20. with Act. 8.38.)

Chapter 29, Paragraph 4:
> "Immersion, or dipping of the person in water, is necessary to the due administration of this ordinance." (Mat. 3.16. Joh. 3.23.)

- **Omissions:** None of substance. The BLC faithfully reproduces the content of 29.3 and 29.4 nearly verbatim.
- **Structural changes:** The BLC collapses 28.2's general sacramental principle (both baptism and the Lord's Supper administered only by qualified persons) into a baptism-specific statement. This is appropriate for a catechetical treatment.
- **Textual error:** BLC section 3 reads "parson" instead of "person," which appears in the Confession's 29.4 as "person." This is likely a typo that should be corrected.
- **Agreement:** The BLC's sections 2-3 are essentially direct quotations from 1689 29.3-4. Section 1 appropriately derives from 28.2. The BLC adds additional proof texts beyond the Confession (Romans 6:4; Colossians 2:12; Acts 8:38-39) which strengthen the biblical case.

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question(s):**

The WLC does not have a direct parallel question on how baptism is to be administered. The closest questions are:

> Q. 165. What is baptism?
> A. Baptism is a sacrament of the New Testament, wherein Christ hath ordained the washing with water in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, to be a sign and seal of ingrafting into himself, of remission of sins by his blood, and regeneration by his Spirit; of adoption, and resurrection unto everlasting life; and whereby the parties baptized are solemnly admitted into the visible church, and enter into an open and professed engagement to be wholly and only the Lord's.

> Q. 166. Unto whom is baptism to be administered?
> A. Baptism is not to be administered to any that are out of the visible church, and so strangers from the covenant of promise, till they profess their faith in Christ, and obedience to him, but infants descending from parents, either both, or but one of them, professing faith in Christ, and obedience to him, are in that respect within the covenant, and to be baptized.

- **Solidarity:** WEAK — The WLC does not address mode of administration at all (neither immersion nor sprinkling), so there is no direct parallel for BLC Q153's core content. The WLC's Q165 uses "washing with water" which is deliberately mode-neutral.
- **Intentional Baptist divergences:** The entire BLC Q153 section 3 on immersion as the necessary mode is a distinctively Baptist assertion with no WLC parallel. This is expected and appropriate — it is the most significant point of liturgical divergence between Baptist and Presbyterian practice.
- **Unexpected divergences:** None. The areas of divergence are all in the expected baptism/mode territory.
- **Key phrases dropped:** N/A — the WLC simply does not cover this topic in a way that would generate droppable phrases. The WLC's "sign and seal" language and expanded signification theology appear in other BLC questions (on the nature of baptism), not in this question on administration.

## Detailed Notes
1. **Typo — "parson" vs. "person":** BLC section 3 reads "dipping of the parson in water." The 1689 Confession reads "dipping of the person in water." This is almost certainly a typographical error and should be corrected to "person."

2. **Dropped anti-sprinkling clause:** The Shorter Catechism's most polemically distinctive phrase — "and not by sprinkling or pouring of water, or dipping some part of the body, after the tradition of men" — is entirely absent from the BLC. While the 1689 Confession itself does not include this clause (it simply states immersion is "necessary"), the Shorter Catechism explicitly adds it. Since the BLC is meant to mirror the Shorter in modernized form, this omission weakens the distinctively Baptist polemic that the Shorter deliberately includes. A modernized Larger Catechism might reasonably be expected to at least preserve this content if the Shorter contains it.

3. **Dropped "whole body":** The Shorter specifies "dipping the whole body," whereas both the BLC and the 1689 say only "dipping of the person." The BLC follows the Confession here rather than the Shorter, but the Shorter's "whole body" language explicitly rules out partial immersion in a way the simpler phrasing does not.

4. **Section 1 expansion:** The BLC's first section on who administers baptism is a legitimate expansion drawing from 1689 28.2. It is theologically sound and appropriately placed, though it represents content the Shorter does not include in its parallel question.

5. **Proof text enrichment:** The BLC adds Romans 6:4, Colossians 2:12, and Acts 8:38-39 to the immersion section, going beyond the 1689's citations of Matthew 3:16 and John 3:23. These additions are appropriate and strengthen the scriptural case for immersion.

## Scripture Analysis
- **Matthew 28:19** — Cited in sections 1 and 2. Covers both the commission to baptize (section 1) and the Trinitarian formula (section 2). Appropriate dual use.
- **Mark 16:15-16** — Cited in section 1 for who administers. Links preaching and baptizing as co-commissioned. Sound usage.
- **Matthew 28:19-20** — Cited in section 2 (overlapping with section 1's Matthew 28:19). The "20" adds the teaching command. Matches the Confession's citation.
- **Acts 8:38** — Cited in section 2 for the water element, and Acts 8:38-39 in section 3 for immersion mode (Philip and the Ethiopian going down into/coming up out of the water). Strong proof text for immersion.
- **Matthew 3:16** — Christ's own baptism, coming up out of the water. Classic immersion proof text, matching the Confession.
- **John 3:23** — "John was baptizing at Aenon near Salim, because there was much water there." Implies immersion requires substantial water. Matches the Confession.
- **Romans 6:4; Colossians 2:12** — Burial/resurrection imagery presupposes immersion. Added beyond the Confession's citations; strengthens the theological argument for mode.
