# Q191: What does the conclusion of the Lord's prayer teach us?

## Verdict: MINOR CONCERN

## Summary
BLC Q191 covers all the major theological content of the WLC Q196 and Shorter Catechism parallel, but weakens the opening concept by replacing "enforce our petitions with arguments" with "there must be substance to our requests," which loses the specific idea of argumentative pleading. The phrase "calmly to count on him to answer our prayers" also subtly shifts the WLC's "quietly to rely upon him, that he will fulfil our requests" from reliance on God's character to expectation of answered prayer. Additionally, the BLC's expansion into multiple sections, while clarifying, slightly dilutes the tightly woven logical structure of the WLC.

## Shorter Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** "Q. What doth the conclusion of the Lord's prayer teach? A. The conclusion of the Lord's prayer, which is, For Thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, forever. Amen (Mt. 6:13), teacheth us to take our encouragement in prayer from God only (Dan. 9:4, 7-9, 16-19), and in our prayers to praise Him, ascribing kingdom, power, and glory, to Him (1 Chron. 29:10-13). And in testimony of our desire and assurance to be heard, we say, Amen (1 Cor. 4:16; Rev. 11:20; 22:20, 21)."

- Omissions: The Shorter's phrase "to take our encouragement in prayer from God only" is not precisely mirrored. The BLC breaks this into two pieces — "substance to our requests" and "based not on any intrinsic worth in ourselves or any created being but on God himself" — but the word "encouragement" is lost. The Shorter teaches we derive our *encouragement* (i.e., boldness and confidence) from God alone; the BLC's "substance to our requests" is vaguer and could be read as referring to the content of prayer rather than its ground of confidence.
- Structural changes: The Shorter presents a compact three-part structure: (1) encouragement from God only, (2) praise ascribing kingdom/power/glory, (3) Amen as testimony of desire and assurance. The BLC expands this into nine sections, which is legitimate for a Larger Catechism but does fragment the unified logic.
- The Shorter's "ascribing kingdom, power, and glory, to Him" is preserved in the BLC's section on recognizing God as "eternally sovereign, omnipotent, and gloriously excellent."
- The Shorter's "testimony of our desire and assurance to be heard" is preserved in the BLC's final section on Amen.

## 1689 Confession Comparison
**Relevant Section:** Chapter 22, Paragraph 3 — "Prayer with thanksgiving, being one special part of natural worship, is by God required of all men. But that it may be accepted, it is to be made in the Name of the Son, by the help of the Spirit, according to his Will; with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance; and when with others, in a known tongue."

- Omissions: The 1689 does not specifically treat the conclusion of the Lord's Prayer, so direct comparison is limited. However, the Confession's emphasis on prayer being made "in the Name of the Son" and "by the help of the Spirit" as conditions for acceptance is not reflected in BLC Q191, which speaks only of God generically. This is not necessarily an omission since the BLC addresses the doxology specifically, and the Trinitarian dimensions of prayer are treated elsewhere in the catechism.
- Structural changes: None significant. The BLC's treatment of the doxology naturally focuses on different aspects than the Confession's general theology of prayer.
- The Confession's emphasis on "faith" in prayer is echoed in the BLC's "our faith makes us bold to plead with him."

## Westminster Larger Catechism Comparison
**Parallel Question:** WLC Q196: "What doth the conclusion of the Lord's Prayer teach us? A. The conclusion of the Lord's Prayer, (which is, For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen.) teacheth us to enforce our petitions with arguments, which are to be taken, not from any worthiness in ourselves, or in any other creature, but from God; and with our prayers to join praises, ascribing to God alone eternal sovereignty, omnipotency, and glorious excellency; in regard whereof, as he is able and willing to help us, so we by faith are emboldened to plead with him that he would, and quietly to rely upon him, that he will fulfil our requests. And, to testify this our desire and assurance, we say, Amen."

- Solidarity: **STRONG**
- Intentional Baptist divergences: None expected or observed. This is common ground on prayer theology.
- Unexpected divergences:
  - "Enforce our petitions with arguments" → "there must be substance to our requests." The WLC's language of *enforcing* petitions with *arguments* is a theologically rich concept rooted in the Reformed tradition of pleading God's own promises and attributes back to him (as Daniel 9 exemplifies). The BLC's "substance to our requests" is weaker and less precise — it could mean merely that prayers should be serious or meaningful, rather than the specific practice of argumentative pleading grounded in God's character.
  - "Quietly to rely upon him, that he will fulfil our requests" → "calmly to count on him to answer our prayers." This is close but introduces a subtle shift. "Quietly to rely" emphasizes a disposition of trust in God's person; "count on him to answer" shifts toward expectation of specific outcomes. The WLC's "fulfil our requests" is moderated by context (God fulfils according to his will); "answer our prayers" in the BLC is slightly more open to a prosperity-adjacent reading, though this may be an overly fine distinction.
- Key phrases dropped:
  - "Enforce our petitions with arguments" — the concept of argumentative prayer is a significant element of Reformed piety, and its dilution to "substance" is the most notable loss.
  - "In regard whereof" — the WLC ties God's ability and willingness to help directly to his sovereignty, omnipotence, and glorious excellency. The BLC preserves this logical connection ("In that respect, insofar as he is able and willing to help us") but "insofar as" introduces a conditional tone not present in the WLC, which treats God's ability and willingness as settled facts flowing from his attributes.

## Detailed Notes
1. **"Substance to our requests"** is the most problematic phrase. The WLC proof text for this clause is Romans 15:30 ("strive together with me in your prayers"), which supports the idea of earnest, vigorous prayer — not merely having "substance." The BLC cites the same verse (Romans 15:30) plus Job 23:3-4 and Jeremiah 14:20-21, which are appropriate but don't clarify what "substance" means in this context.

2. **"Insofar as he is able and willing"** — The WLC's "as he is able and willing to help us" is a statement of confident fact (God *is* able and willing). The BLC's "insofar as he is able and willing to help us" could be read as introducing qualification (to the extent that he is able and willing), though it likely intends the same meaning. This phrasing could be improved.

3. The BLC's overall structure successfully expands the WLC's single-paragraph answer into a multi-section catechetical form that walks through the logical sequence: doxology text → substance of petitions → ground in God alone → joined with praise → God's attributes → God's ability and willingness → faith's boldness to plead → quiet reliance → Amen. This is a legitimate pedagogical expansion.

4. The BLC adds "or any created being" to the WLC's "or in any other creature" — functionally identical in modern English.

## Scripture Analysis
- The BLC proof texts closely mirror the WLC's references: Matthew 6:13, Romans 15:30, Daniel 9:4/7-9/16-19, Philippians 4:6, 1 Chronicles 29:10-13, Ephesians 3:20-21, Luke 11:13, 2 Chronicles 20:6/11, 2 Chronicles 14:11, 1 Corinthians 14:16, Revelation 22:20-21.
- Additional BLC proof texts not in the WLC: Job 23:3-4, Jeremiah 14:20-21, Psalm 84:11, Ephesians 3:11-12, Hebrews 10:19-22, John 5:14, Romans 8:32. These are all appropriate and support the theological claims made.
- The Shorter Catechism cites "1 Cor. 4:16" for the Amen, which appears to be a misprint for 1 Cor. 14:16 (the same verse the BLC and WLC correctly cite). The Shorter also cites "Rev. 11:20" which is likely a misprint for Rev. 22:20.
- Overall, the proof text selection is sound and well-aligned with the WLC tradition.
